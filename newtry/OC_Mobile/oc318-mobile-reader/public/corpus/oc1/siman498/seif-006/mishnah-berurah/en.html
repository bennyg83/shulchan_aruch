In order to eat from it. After its soul departs, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 27. Even if he does not intend to eat anything from it, the reason is that in this manner, where there is time to eat, there is no Torah prohibition to slaughter it on Yom Tov. If guests who had not yet eaten would happen to come and now want to eat from this animal, the slaughter would permit it for them; therefore this slaughter is called food preparation labor. Thus, even though no guests came, the slaughter has no Torah prohibition, only the Sages forbade it when it has no Yom Tov need; in a case of loss, the Sages did not uphold their words. But a healthy animal may not be slaughtered unless he needs it on Yom Tov itself.
An olive-bulk roasted. This is the lightest of cooking processes. An olive-bulk is enough, because it is impossible to have an olive-bulk of meat without slaughtering, so the entire slaughter is for Yom Tov need.
Even if there is not time, etc. Since according to the law it is permitted without checking the lung, only that initially they were stringent to check it, here, because of financial loss, one need not be stringent in this.
First. Several later authorities hold that there must be time to skin and examine it. Yeshuos Yaakov wrote that specifically with kids and lambs, where adhesions are uncommon, one may be lenient, but with a large animal there must also be time to skin and examine it.
He does not take its hide. Meaning, to move it and put it in its place after he skinned it from the animal, as above in siman 308, seif 25. Although below in siman 499 we permit moving the hide after he slaughtered the animal on Yom Tov, that is with a healthy animal, because if you do not permit it he will not slaughter and will refrain from the joy of Yom Tov. Here, however, he is forced to slaughter so that it not die.
One limb. Then the hide is nullified to the limb, and it is permitted to move it with it.
Some say that one may not, etc. Skinning is also included in labor and is forbidden, since it is not for the need of eating. Although there is no Torah prohibition, for since if guests would happen to come who needed the meat to eat it would certainly be permitted to skin it, now too it is permitted at least according to the Torah, nevertheless D'Rabbanan it is forbidden. The Mechaber's view is that just as they permitted slaughter because of financial loss, so too they permitted skinning because of financial loss, so that the meat not spoil. See Magen Avraham, Eliyah Rabbah, and other later authorities, who rule that the main law follows the Mechaber, only that initially one should be careful. This is specifically where there is concern that if he waits until evening the meat will spoil; otherwise it is certainly forbidden, at least D'Rabbanan, to skin. The Peri Megadim wrote that all this is where there is much time, but if he slaughtered close to evening and it is impossible to eat more than an olive-bulk, it is forbidden according to the Torah to skin the hide, since the rationale of possible guests does not apply when there is no time to eat more.
At all. Similarly, one who slaughters a bird because it is dangerously ill and he does not intend to eat it on Yom Tov may not pluck its feathers.
