In order to eat. After its life departs, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 27. Even if he does not eat from it, it is permitted, Maggid Mishneh, Yam Shel Shlomo.
There is not enough time. For it implies that the first Tanna agrees with Rabbi Akiva in this, that it does not need inspection, as stated in the Gemara. Furthermore, Rama wrote that it is forbidden to skin it. But Yam Shel Shlomo ruled that there must be time to eat after skinning and cutting it up, and this is not compelling.
And some say that one may not skin, etc. Since it is forbidden to move the hide, it is forbidden to skin it. Nevertheless this requires study, for since the Yerushalmi explicitly wrote to permit, and the Ran and Rashba brought it, how did Shiltei Giborim disagree with them? Furthermore, we learned: he brings it in his hand, limb by limb, which implies that he skins it. Perhaps he holds that the Yerushalmi and the Mishnah discuss where he wants to eat from it on Yom Tov, but otherwise it is forbidden to skin it. According to him, by a bird it is forbidden to pluck the feathers; see seif 13. As for those who permit, it seems to me that the reason is that since there is no Torah prohibition in skinning because of ho'il, that if guests happen to come, etc., the Sages permitted it because of loss, so that the meat should not spoil; see the end of siman 497.
