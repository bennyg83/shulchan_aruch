6) Regarding the rubbing practiced by shochtim, in which they rub the knife against the hand or arm when a nick is found after slaughter and declare the animal valid if the nick disappears, see Chochmas Adam, rule 3, subsection 11. He rules very stringently that this is forbidden and writes that the gaon Beis Meir and the people of his city agreed. See there. The gaon Rabbi David Chazan likewise writes in Toras Zevach, siman 89, subsection 43, in the name of Bris Avraham, siman 12, who rules that it is forbidden. Rabbi David Chazan himself writes, however, that the custom is to declare it valid, provided the rubbing is done gently and not forcefully. See there. Zivchei Tzedek on Yoreh Deah, siman 18, subsection 19, cites this and writes that the custom in the city of Baghdad, may God protect it, is to permit it. See there. This has practical application in a place where the permissive custom is followed and such a case occurs on Yom Tov. It is permitted to rub the knife for the reason given by Shulchan Gavoah, since this is done only against the hand. Moreover, it is needed for food preparation, to render the animal or bird valid for Yom Tov.
