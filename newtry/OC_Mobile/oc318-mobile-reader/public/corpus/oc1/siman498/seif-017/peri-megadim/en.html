He brought in. See Taz, who emended like the Rashba that there are two distinctions: if he brought it into a garden or ruin, it is permitted to slaughter initially and cover with it. But if he brought it to cover his roof, it is muktzeh, because he is more particular about it, and it is permitted only after the fact, like an inserted spade, for initially it is forbidden even if he designated it because there is a D'Rabbanan prohibition of making a pit. The same applies to muktzeh without a pit; initially it is forbidden. In Yam Shel Shlomo, first chapter, siman 22, although he copied the text as in the Beis Yosef, it must be explained so. Yam Shel Shlomo had difficulty: what distinction is there between a ruin or garden and his roof? He answered that it always speaks of loose earth, and with his roof he is particular and it is muktzeh; after the fact it is permitted like an inserted spade, for they permit a pit where he does not need the pit, and so too muktzeh. The Taz's words following Rabbeinu Tam mean that ordinarily the roof needs crushing, and even so in such a case it is permitted because the positive commandment overrides the negative commandment. See above letter 12. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 32, and with Hashem's help the Mechaber's words will be explained further in the Magen Avraham.
