(Seif katan 17) A sage may not, etc. Because it appears as though he is repairing, etc. The Taz wrote that nevertheless another ruling is permitted, for specifically with a firstborn, which had a presumption of prohibition, it is considered repairing. But with other tereifos, not only an ordinary doubtful tereifah, where it had a presumption of permissibility before the doubt arose, but even a doubtful tereifah because of an extra limb, which existed from birth so that it never had a presumption of permissibility, nevertheless it also did not have a presumption of prohibition, unlike a firstborn.
He further wrote: nevertheless, if liquid became mixed with liquid before Yom Tov in less than sixty, and we do not say that it became neveilah. One must say that this discusses a case of great loss, where we do not say that it became neveilah even with liquid mixed with liquid. Or in truth, on Yom Tov enough permitted matter was added to make sixty against the entire mixture. Regarding whether it became known or did not become known, see Shulchan Aruch Yoreh Deah and the Shach there. Or dry mixed with dry in less than a majority, and on Yom Tov more was added, making it similar to a firstborn, for it had a presumption of prohibition; it is forbidden to rule on Yom Tov. These are his words.
See Magen Avraham in siman 323, seif katan 14, who wrote in the name of Terumas HaDeshen, siman 54, that it is permitted to estimate sixty, even if it became mixed before Shabbos and he did not know until Shabbos. See siman 498, seif 9. These are Magen Avraham's words. Magen Avraham alluded to what Terumas HaDeshen wrote there, for he wished to permit estimating on Shabbos whether the permitted matter is sixty, as Magen Avraham wrote in his name. Terumas HaDeshen wrote: as for the case of inspecting a firstborn, it can be resolved as Rashi explained in the chapter Ein Tzadin, where he distinguishes between inspecting blemishes of a firstborn and seeing a slaughtered animal to inspect it from tereifah status, which is permitted on Yom Tov. These are his words. Rashi's words are on 27, where the Gemara thought that Rabbi Shimon ben Menasya proved that Rabbi Meir holds that one may not inspect blemishes on Yom Tov from the Mishnah: one who slaughtered the firstborn and afterward showed its blemish to a sage, Rabbi Yehudah permits; Rabbi Meir says that since it was slaughtered not by instruction of an expert, it is forbidden. This shows that Rabbi Meir holds that inspecting a firstborn is not like inspecting a tereifah: inspecting a firstborn is before life, while inspecting a tereifah is after slaughter. From this, inspecting a tereifah may be even on Yom Tov, while inspecting a firstborn must be before Yom Tov. Rashi wrote: this shows that Rabbi Meir holds that inspecting a firstborn is not as light as inspecting a tereifah, for the Sages gave it importance, and the entire permissibility of the firstborn depends on it, since even its slaughter is forbidden before he shows it. It is not comparable to inspecting a tereifah, which they permitted to inspect after slaughter. From it Rabbi Shimon ben Menasya learned and said that since it is so significant, it is like repairing and judging a case, and it is forbidden to inspect it on Yom Tov, even though a tereifah may be inspected on Yom Tov. These are Rashi's words. It appears from this that although inspecting blemishes of a firstborn is forbidden, nevertheless inspecting tereifos is lighter and is permitted on Shabbos and Yom Tov. One must say that although Abaye rejected this proof and said that Rabbi Meir's law does not depend at all on the law of inspecting blemishes of a firstborn, but Rabbi Meir's reason is because of a penalty, decreeing blemishes that are not liable to change after slaughter on account of blemishes that are liable to change after slaughter, and were it not for the penalty Rabbi Meir would agree that after the fact, if it was slaughtered not by instruction of an expert, it is permitted, and if so inspecting a firstborn is not so significant, and perhaps he holds that one may inspect blemishes on Yom Tov, nevertheless Terumas HaDeshen holds that although Abaye rejected that there is no proof from here that Rabbi Meir holds one may not inspect blemishes on Yom Tov, the reasoning remains that according to us, who rule that one may not inspect blemishes on Yom Tov, nevertheless inspecting a tereifah is permitted on Yom Tov. For in any case, inspecting a firstborn is more significant than inspecting tereifos, since initially everyone agrees that even with blemishes that do not change after slaughter, one may not slaughter initially except by instruction of an expert who saw the blemish before slaughter, unlike by tereifah. If so, at least inspecting a firstborn is more significant than inspecting a tereifah. Therefore, although we do not inspect blemishes on Yom Tov, inspecting a tereifah is permitted on Yom Tov. So one must say according to Terumas HaDeshen. In any case, according to Terumas HaDeshen, the above law of the Taz is not correct, so it appears to me.
