A basketful, etc. From before Yom Tov, and he pours it onto the ground.
Without specification. Meaning, he did not think explicitly for the need of the garden, but brought it without specification, as above.
See above siman 308, etc. Meaning, it is explained there that it is permitted even to cover excrement with it. One might have said that his intention was only for something whose use is certainly frequent, but not for this, since perhaps the child will not relieve himself in the house. Specifically where he brought it into the house without specification, we say that his mind is on whatever he will need. But if he brought it for something whose use is certain, such as bringing it to cover the blood of a bird that he intends to slaughter on Yom Tov, it is forbidden to cover excrement with it. Some are lenient also in this.
Incidentally to the ground of the house. Because of its small amount, as in the previous seif.
That he designated a corner for it. He must also be careful to take from it evenly and not make a pit, or it speaks where it is very loose, so that as soon as he takes the sand it falls into the pit and closes it, later authorities.
Since he did not spread it, etc. See above in siman 308, Mishnah Berurah seif katan 44, that if he spread it, it becomes nullified incidentally to the floor of the house, even if he brought it in explicitly for his needs.
And if it is loose earth, etc. Otherwise it requires crushing, and that is forbidden. If it speaks where he has not yet slaughtered, the earth must be so loose all around that taking it will not make a pit, or he must take it evenly, for example where it is sloped and somewhat high, as above. Otherwise it is forbidden at least initially to slaughter relying on this, as explained above. The later authorities wrote that making a pit applies even in detached earth, as here, where the earth was already detached and dug, and the same applies to earth in utensils. Accordingly, one should not insert a candle into a utensil full of earth if the earth is somewhat hard, so that a pit is made by inserting it. If the earth is loose, so that when he removes it, it will return and fall, this is not considered making a pit. Nevertheless, removing a candle from this earth, even though a pit is made automatically, is of no concern.
