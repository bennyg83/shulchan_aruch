It is difficult: why did he not truly attach the prohibition to this, that one may not toil on Yom Tov for a matter that is a doubt; and why did he attach it to appearance because of chelev? One must say that in truth there is no prohibition of toil regarding a doubt, for he may trouble himself to fulfill a doubtful obligation of a mitzvah; only that the onlooker may err in this. And there is a further practical difference regarding what the Gemara asks: What is the reason one does not cover the blood of a koi? Let him cover it with stove ash, which is prepared! This shows that because of toil there is no prohibition so long as there is no prohibition from moving the earth; therefore they had to give the reason of appearance because of chelev. But against this is difficult from what is stated in the Gemara: R' Zeira taught: Not only regarding a koi did they say that one does not cover on Yom Tov, but even if he slaughtered a domestic animal, a wild animal, and a bird and their blood mixed together, it is forbidden to cover it on Yom Tov. Rashi explained: not only a koi, where by law one should not cover it lest it be a domestic animal and he toils a toil that is not a mitzvah, and even with stove ash; but even etc., where there is one that requires covering, nevertheless they do not cover, for he toils for the sake of the domestic animal. This implies that regarding a koi there is a prohibition because of toil alone, even if there is no prohibition from moving the earth. One must say that they disagree on this: the view that a koi is forbidden because of permitting its chelev holds that there is no prohibition from the aspect of toil, and so held the questioner. And do not say that R' Zeira taught in a baraita because of toil, and one may not dispute a baraita: one may say that that view because of chelev holds that that Mishnah, "one does not slaughter a koi, and if one slaughtered it one does not cover its blood," does not hold the reason of toil; for if so why mention a koi, and why mention a mixture of domestic animal and wild animal? As appears above, this is necessary according to Rashi, and likewise according to the Rambam as written nearby.
