(חי) In the gloss: he shall check his knife from erev Yom Tov etc., and hide it where it will not be flawed, and slaughter with it on Yom Tov or lend it to others for clothing. It implies one may slaughter relying on the erev Yom Tov inspection; Darkei Moshe note 1: although on a weekday it is forbidden to slaughter without knife inspection relying on post-slaughter inspection as Yoreh De'ah 18 — on Yom Tov it is permitted since it is not possible otherwise. Chayei Adam general 89:2 relies on this presumption and slaughters without inspection — specifically a knife presumed not to become flawed on its own. Mateh Aharon siman 485 seif 23: nevertheless when he comes to slaughter on Yom Tov he should check the knife near slaughter as Yoreh De'ah 18 — one need not then fear it will not be found good and he will sharpen, since they checked erev Yom Tov and hid it — presumably it will be found good; he only checks from general concern lest it became flawed, which is a minority; one need not fear so much lest it not be found good, lest he forget it is Yom Tov and sharpen — unlike when they did not check erev Yom Tov, when we are concerned for this — see there; Zivchei Ratzon, Likutei Rima, Torat Chayim on Chayei Adam note 4: but if he did not prepare erev Yom Tov and did a good inspection, he should not begin inspection on Yom Tov except in pressing need as stated afterward:
