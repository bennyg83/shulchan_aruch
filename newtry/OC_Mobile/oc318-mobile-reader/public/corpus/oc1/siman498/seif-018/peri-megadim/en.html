And if. See Taz. It requires some explanation. Certainly for us, in seif 14, an inserted spade is needed because of muktzeh, for there are two prohibitions, as in Magen Avraham letters 25 and 26, and making a pit where he does not need it is D'Rabbanan and was permitted because of Yom Tov and covering. The Beis Yosef asked according to one explanation that it is like digging, so even in loose earth it should be forbidden without an inserted spade. He answered that one can distinguish between loose earth in the ground of the world, where even if he verbally designated it before Yom Tov he still needs an inserted spade so there should not be a trace of digging, and earth that was moved and brought into a garden, where there is no trace of digging and no inserted spade is needed; even initially it is permitted in such a case when he designated it. The Taz holds that even where it is moved, detached, and brought into his house, wherever a pit remains, it is obvious that initially it is forbidden; here it speaks of a mound from which he takes from the top and no pit is made. But wherever a pit remains and there is no inserted spade, even if he designated it verbally, one may say it is forbidden after the fact, for it also appears like digging, besides the pits. See Magen Avraham, letter 32.
