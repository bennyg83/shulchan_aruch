18) There, in the gloss. He should inspect his knife on erev Yom Tov, etc. He should conceal it in a place where it will not become nicked, and slaughter with it on Yom Tov or lend it to others. Levush. This implies that one may slaughter with it in reliance upon the inspection performed on erev Yom Tov. Darkei Moshe, subsection 1, writes likewise. Although on a weekday it is forbidden to slaughter without first inspecting the knife in reliance upon inspecting it after slaughter, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 18, nevertheless, on Yom Tov it is permitted because no other course is possible. These are his words. Chayei Adam, rule 89, subsection 2, likewise writes that one relies upon this presumption and slaughters without inspection, specifically when it is a knife whose established nature is not to become nicked by itself. These are his words. Mateh Ephraim, siman 685, se'if 23, however, writes that when one comes to slaughter on Yom Tov he should nevertheless inspect the knife immediately before slaughter, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 18. There is then no concern that he might find it unsatisfactory and sharpen it. Since he inspected it on erev Yom Tov and concealed it, it will presumably be found satisfactory. His inspection is merely to address the remote possibility that it became nicked. We do not have so great a concern that he might find it unsatisfactory and also forget that it is Yom Tov and sharpen it. When he did not inspect it on erev Yom Tov, however, this concern does apply. See there. Zivchei Ratzon writes likewise, as do Likutei Rima there and Tosafos Chaim on Chayei Adam there, subsection 4. If he did not designate it on erev Yom Tov and inspect it thoroughly, he should not perform its initial inspection on Yom Tov except in pressing circumstances, as stated afterward.
