Animals known, etc. This is the same as seif 3, but it teaches a further point: even the animals that we see today with a non-Jew are known to us to lodge outside the techum, and on Erev Yom Tov they were not yet in the city. One might have thought that now too it was so, only that the non-Jew brought them into the city today for the meat market. It teaches that even in this case it does not leave the category of doubt, for perhaps he indeed brought them yesterday and they only lodged outside the wall, and in the morning he brought them into the city.
On the second day. The commentators wrote that this is a scribal error, and it should read: on Yom Tov.
Those slaughtered in the morning. Meaning, the non-Jew brings them to the meat market to slaughter them in the morning, and we say that he surely brings them before day so as not to delay the time, and also because it is not so common to walk with the animals at night. All this follows his view in seif 3 that with techumin we attribute leniently as long as it is not clear to us. We already wrote that the Mechaber himself is stringent in this in several places. The Magen Avraham's view is that one may be lenient only where the non-Jew lives in the city and animals are available to him within the techum, for in that case we do not establish a prohibition, or on the second day of Yom Tov, because of a double doubt, as above in siman 497, seif 4.
