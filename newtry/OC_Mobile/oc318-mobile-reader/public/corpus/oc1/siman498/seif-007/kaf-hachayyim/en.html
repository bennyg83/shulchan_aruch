Rashi's explanation implies that only a butcher who sells meat in a shop may not show his knife on Yom Tov, because the matter becomes public, but any other person may do so. According to Halachos Gedolos, the concern is that he might go beyond the techum. According to Razah, this resembles inspecting a firstborn animal for a blemish, but inspecting the knife for oneself is permitted. See there. Maggid Mishneh writes there that Rambam's explanation appears primary. Beis Yosef also cites him and therefore rules accordingly in Shulchan Aruch. Ramban, in Milchamos, likewise mentions Rambam's explanation and says that it is the most correct of all the explanations. Bach cites him. Nevertheless, Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, subsection 1, writes that in practice one should be concerned for all the reasons. A knife is therefore not shown to a scholar even by one who is not a butcher and not an am haaretz. See there. This is also the view of Erech HaShulchan, subsection 1. Pri Chadash, however, agrees with Rambam's reason as written by Shulchan Aruch. Levush likewise follows Shulchan Aruch, as do Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1; and Mikraei Kodesh, siman 43, subsection 1. See the following subsection.
