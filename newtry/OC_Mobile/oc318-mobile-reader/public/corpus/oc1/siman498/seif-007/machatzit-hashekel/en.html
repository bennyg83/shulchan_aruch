(Seif katan 7) On the day, etc. His words are true according to the Rashba, etc. It states in Shabbos 151 that if a non-Jew brought flutes on Shabbos, a Jew may not eulogize with them unless they came from a nearby place, which Rashi explains as within the techum. The Gemara asks what nearby place means, and Shmuel says, and we rule like him, that we are concerned lest they lodged outside the wall. Rashi explains that concerned here means leniently, and he brings proof that sometimes concern is for leniency. Shmuel holds that even if they were brought from outside the city, we attribute it leniently and say that already the previous night they were within the techum and lodged outside the wall, and therefore they are permitted immediately after Shabbos. The Mishnah means: unless they came from a nearby place, meaning unless one can attribute them to having come from a nearby place, excluding where it is known that they came from outside the techum, in which case they are forbidden. According to this, whenever there is a doubt whether something was brought from outside the techum, we follow the doubt leniently. But the Rif explained Shmuel's words, that we are concerned lest they lodged outside the wall, stringently. Meaning, even if they came into the city on Shabbos morning, we do not say that they certainly came from within the techum, for if they were from outside the techum it would have been impossible for them to arrive in the city immediately in the morning. Even so, we are concerned that they came from outside the techum, except that they walked Friday night until they reached the wall and lodged there until morning. According to this, when the Mishnah teaches, unless they came from a nearby place, it means that we certainly know they came from a nearby place. But a doubt whether they came from outside the techum is forbidden according to the Rif. The Rashba ruled like Rashi's approach, that in a doubt of outside the techum we rule leniently. Therefore Magen Avraham wrote that the Shach's words, that even on the first day of Yom Tov it is permitted, are true according to the Rashba, who holds that a doubt whether it was brought from outside the techum is permitted. But the Shulchan Aruch, which ruled in siman 515 like the Rif to be stringent, properly wrote here, on the second day, for on the first day it is forbidden since it is a doubt. Nevertheless, on the second day it is permitted even according to us, because it is a double doubt, as written in siman 496, seif 4. See there in Magen Avraham, who disagrees, and see what I will write, with Hashem's help, below in siman 515. Know that the Rashba wrote that even according to the Rif, who is stringent regarding a doubt whether it came from outside the techum, nevertheless if the non-Jew lives with him in the city and has that item in his city, we rule leniently and say that he brought it from what he has within the city, not from what he has outside the techum. So we rule below in siman 515, seif 7.
Magen Avraham intended this in seif katan 6: according to us, who rule like the Rif, it must be that what he permits in seif 6 in a case of doubt refers to a non-Jew who lives with him, etc., for in such a case even the Rif permits.
Also, etc. He holds this way, meaning that Orchos Chaim also wrote on the second day of Yom Tov, because Orchos Chaim also ruled like the Rif, and according to him it is forbidden on the first day. Although Orchos Chaim wrote this in the name of the Rashba and brought the language of the Rashba, as explained in Beis Yosef, and according to the Rashba it is permitted even on the first day, one must say that Orchos Chaim copied the Rashba's law according to his own view, since he rules like the Rif.
