It is permitted. To slaughter initially and cover with it.
It is forbidden. Even according to Rabbi Shimon, for it is a new act, as initially it was wood and now it is ash. If he kindled wood on top of yesterday's ash and today's ash became mixed with yesterday's ash in such a way that it was never recognizable, it is nullified in the majority, as written in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 102 and above in siman 320, seif 2. Although flour mixed with flour is considered liquid with liquid and requires sixty, as written in siman 453, nevertheless here, since the main prohibition is from the words of the Sages, it seems to me that it is nullified in the majority; see Yoreh Deah, siman 98.
That is fit for roasting. For then it stands for its original use. It seems to me that dim coals may not be moved on Yom Tov.
It is permitted to cover with it. Since there is only one prohibition, namely muktzeh, they permitted moving it for the need of covering; see seif 14, Rosh.
Nevertheless, it is preferable, etc. Beis Yosef wrote this because it appears so from the Rosh and Tur. In my humble opinion, even the Rosh and Tur hold that it is better to cover with ash from the stove than to cover with a spade that was inserted, as Kol Bo wrote, for with a spade inserted he commits the prohibition of making a pit, whereas muktzeh may be moved for the need of Yom Tov, as Tosafot wrote explicitly. See the end of siman 509. So ruled the Bach, except that he has a different approach; see there. As for Beis Yosef's proof from the Yerushalmi, see there, on the contrary, the Yerushalmi says that it is better that he take ash and not dig with a spade and cover. The Yerushalmi refers to the Mishnah; see there. The Mishnah discusses where he has a spade inserted, and even so ash is preferable. This seems to me primary.
