If it was heated on Yom Tov it is forbidden. For it is nolad, and it is forbidden even according to R' Shimon who permits regarding muktzeh.
That is fit to roast an egg in it. From here [it follows] that it is permitted to cover with hot ash, and not like those who err and forbid covering with hot ash. And Tosafos wrote: And if you say, how do we remove stove ash to bake a pastry there? And R' Yitzchak answered that muktzeh is not forbidden to move for the needs of food and the joy of Yom Tov.
And if he slaughtered it is permitted, etc. In Beis Yosef in the name of Kol Bo he wrote in the name of R' Meshullam that if he slaughtered it is permitted to cover with stove ash that was heated on Yom Tov, and he should not dig with a digging-stick; end quote. Beis Yosef explained: he should not dig with a digging-stick and cover; but if he had a digging-stick stuck in, that is preferable; and so we say in the Yerushalmi: better that he take ash that was heated on Yom Tov and not dig with a digging-stick and cover; thus it speaks of where he has no stuck digging-stick; end quote. And he explained well: digging with a digging-stick is an av melacha, and nolad is forbidden only by rabbinic law; and so long as we can take care regarding "you shall not do any melacha," we do not say that the negative should be overridden. Even though according to Beis Hillel in Rabbeinu Tam's view it is permitted in every way, even to dig, as the law implies according to Beis Shammai before we knew of the reversed attribution: one who comes to ask is told, go dig and slaughter and cover, implying even without a stuck digging-stick; and so Beis Hillel hold after the exchange according to Rabbeinu Tam, as I wrote. And now the Yerushalmi's wording is well: it said "better," etc., and did not say briefly "let him not dig with a digging-stick"; rather, certainly even digging with the stick is permitted by law, but it is more preferable to permit a lighter prohibition than that, namely nolad; and this supports our explanation, thank God.
