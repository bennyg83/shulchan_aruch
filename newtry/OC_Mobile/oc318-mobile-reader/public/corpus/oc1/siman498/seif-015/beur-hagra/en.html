Seif 15, and if he slaughtered, etc. Yerushalmi there, end of halacha 3: In what case were these words said? When he had not slaughtered. But if he slaughtered, it is better that he take from ash heated on Yom Tov and not dig with a spade and cover. The colleagues said: Learn from this that a positive commandment overrides a negative commandment.
Nevertheless, it is preferable, etc. From what is stated there, 'and not dig,' etc., it implies specifically where he has no spade inserted. This is the language of the Beis Yosef: The Kol Bo wrote in the name of Rabbi Meshulam that if he slaughtered, it is permitted to cover with ash of an oven heated on Yom Tov, and he should not cover with a spade. End quote. It appears to me that this means: he should not dig with a spade and cover, but if he had a spade inserted, it is certainly preferable. This is what we say in the Yerushalmi: if he slaughtered, it is better, etc., and not dig with a spade and cover; evidently it speaks where he does not have a spade inserted. But if he has a spade inserted, it is preferable. So too it appears from the words of the Rosh and Tur. End quote. His words are astonishing, for the Kol Bo wrote, and he should not cover with a spade, which implies even a spade inserted. Likewise, the Yerushalmi speaks of a spade inserted, for it refers to the Mishnah, which says: and they agree that if he slaughtered, he should dig, etc. Furthermore, if it speaks where it is not inserted, what is the meaning of 'it is better,' etc.? It means that if he has oven ash, even without this it is forbidden. The reasoning is also simple, for the prohibition of moving is lighter than labor that is not needed for its own sake, or destructive labor. For the Mishnah says that if a non-Jew comes to extinguish, etc., even in a loss through fire, whereas regarding moving muktzeh all the poskim permitted telling a non-Jew, as written above in siman 276, seif 3 in the gloss. All the more so on Yom Tov, where moving muktzeh for the need of food preparation is permitted, as written above in siman 518. There is no implication in the words of the Rosh and Tur. So wrote the Rashba explicitly, and this is his language in Avodas HaKodesh, section 40a: If he slaughtered and has no oven ash heated on Yom Tov with which it is permitted to cover because of the mitzvah of covering, not only that, but even if he has an inserted spade and oven ash heated on Yom Tov, he covers with the ash and does not cover with the inserted spade. End quote. So too the Bach and Magen Avraham challenged him.
