That it is fit to roast an egg upon it. See Magen Avraham, who writes that dimmed coals may not be moved on Yom Tov. Maamar Mordechai expresses great surprise: what muktzeh status could they have? Dimmed coals are still hot, although they have become somewhat covered with ash, as above in siman 253, and one can cook with them. Why should they be worse than hot ashes? See there. Indeed, Pesachim 26a explicitly states that cooking is possible with dimmed coals. Korban Nesanel also questions the ruling because they remain fit for their original function. He therefore writes that Magen Avraham contains a scribal error and should read that they may be moved. It therefore appears to me that “dimmed” is not precise, and the intention is coals that were extinguished. This is also evident from Peri Megadim, who writes that extinguished coals that are no longer hot constitute a new entity like ashes. He means that coals too are considered nolad like ashes, since initially they were wood and now they are coals. Machatzis HaShekel writes that if they are rekindled, they again become permitted, just like ashes that cooled and were reheated, as explained below in siman 507 in Magen Avraham, because there is no muktzeh for half of Shabbos. The same applies that one may blow upon them when they lie beside glowing coals in order to ignite them. Although blowing causes them to move from their place, this is of no concern. The basic ruling requires consideration. In my humble opinion, even if they cooled completely, they are not a new entity, because they remain fit for their original use. Just as one uses wood as fuel, one also uses coals for roasting, especially in our time when a water urn is heated only with coals. I later found that authorities cite Kehillas Yaakov as agreeing that small coals unfit to be ignited for cooking or roasting may not be moved, but large coals are permitted like firewood.
If he slaughtered, he may cover the blood. See Shaar HaTziyun, which gives Rosh's reason that there is only one prohibition involved. This requires explanation. The Gemara on page 8b asks, “What is different about a doubtful case, where he may make a depression?” This clearly implies that making a depression can apply even in stove ashes, for example where the ashes will not fall back after he takes some. It requires study why the authorities did not note this passage.
