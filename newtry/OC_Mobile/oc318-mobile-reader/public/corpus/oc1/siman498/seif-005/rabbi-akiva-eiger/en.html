Seif 5. A calf born on Yom Tov may be slaughtered if the mother stands for eating. At first glance the plain reading of the sugya is difficult. It says according to Rav: what difference is there between this and a calf, etc. This implies that only according to Rav do we need the reason that it is prepared incidentally to its mother. But according to the one who permits a chick, this reason is not needed at all. If so, since the Tur in siman 513 brought that regarding a chick born on Yom Tov some permit and the Geonim forbid, he should here also have brought the view of those who permit with a calf born where the mother stands for raising, for those who permit a chick and do not require the reason of preparation incidentally to its mother would permit even where she stands for raising. The Tur should not have ruled here plainly that if it stands for raising it is forbidden. Shaar HaMelech asked this as well. It appears to me by first considering Tosafot in the sugya, who ask why it does not answer that it was prepared because he could have inserted his hand into the animal's womb and slaughtered it. They answer that Rabbi Oshaya was uncertain in Chullin whether we say that the Torah made four signs valid, or not. They further answered that initially one certainly may not slaughter it in its mother's womb, for it is no better than slaughtering at night; see there. At first glance, even without their question they need their answer because of another question. Even according to Rabbi Yehudah it is difficult from a calf, for the reasoning that it permits itself through slaughter applies only to a chick, where birth causes the signs to become fit for slaughter, and therefore also causes the permission from muktzeh. But with a calf that was born, how does permitting itself through slaughter apply, since even before birth it was fit in its mother? This requires the explanation above.
