5) Shulchan Gavoah further writes there that at times something resembling a nick appears on a knife, and the shochet smooths it against the palm of his hand with saliva, as he would ordinarily do against a sharpening stone. This appearance comes upon the knife because he slaughtered with it on erev Yom Tov and some moisture from the blood or saliva remained upon it. When the night passes, the blood or saliva dries and appears like a nick. When he smooths the knife with saliva against the palm of his hand, that blood softens and comes off, after which he can determine whether it is truly a nick. Smoothing in this manner is permitted. The prohibition against sharpening applies only to its sharpening stone, but sharpening against wood, earthenware, or a stone is permitted, as stated below, siman 509, se'if 2. It is certainly permitted in this case. These are his words.
