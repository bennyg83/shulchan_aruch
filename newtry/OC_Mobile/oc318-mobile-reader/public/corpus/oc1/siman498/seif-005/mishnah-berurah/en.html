Standing for eating. Meaning, then it is permitted even according to what was ruled above in siman 495, seif 4, that muktzeh is forbidden on Yom Tov. The reason is that since its mother was prepared for eating, it is prepared incidentally to its mother, for if he wanted, he could have slaughtered the mother before the calf was born and eaten both on Yom Tov. Even if it is known that the mother is a treifah, nevertheless she is not muktzeh on Yom Tov, for she is already prepared for dogs, and therefore the calf is likewise not muktzeh. According to the practice of our lands, where we forbid on Yom Tov only nolad and not muktzeh, as the Rama ruled there in the gloss, one may permit a calf born on Yom Tov even if its mother stands for raising offspring, and it should not be forbidden as nolad, because the birth made no change in its body at all, for even before birth it was fit for eating just as after it was born.
And only if he knows, etc. Nowadays we are not expert in this, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 13, end of seif 3. Therefore we do not slaughter a calf born on Yom Tov, or on Shabbos that is Erev Yom Tov, before the eighth day, lest it be a nefel. Where one must wait seven days, such as for us who are not expert whether its months were completed, and the eighth day falls on the first day of Yom Tov, one may say that it is forbidden because it was set aside at twilight. The same applies when the eighth day falls on the second day of Yom Tov, Peri Megadim in Mishbetzos Zahav. But Yeshuos Yaakov permits this, since most births produce viable offspring and it will certainly become permitted, so it is not considered muktzeh.

And we also require that it walked, etc. Through this it leaves the concern that its limbs may have been crushed when emerging from the womb, which is a narrow place.
For we are concerned, etc. Meaning, although if it was born on a weekday, where we know that its months were completed, it is permitted to slaughter it that same day and we are not concerned that its limbs may have been crushed, and it does not require examination of its inner limbs, nevertheless on Yom Tov we are concerned lest he unintentionally find a concern of treifah in its inner limbs, and it turns out that he slaughtered on Yom Tov without need. The view of the Maharshal and Taz is that it need not walk, and one need not be concerned for all these concerns. But for us there is no practical difference, since we are not expert in whether its months were completed, as above.
