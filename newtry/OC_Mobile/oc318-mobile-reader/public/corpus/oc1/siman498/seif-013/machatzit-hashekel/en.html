(Seif katan 13) There is not enough time, which implies that the first Tanna agrees, etc. As stated in the Gemara, page 25: we learned that one may not slaughter a dangerously ill animal unless there is time in the day to eat from it a roasted kezayis. Rabbi Akiva says, even a raw kezayis from the place of slaughter. Rabbi Chiyya taught: from the actual place of slaughter, where skinning and cutting are unnecessary. This implies that one may eat from it before skinning and cutting, and we are not concerned lest it be found to be a tereifah. Afterward Rami bar Chama said that the skinning and cutting written regarding an olah teaches proper conduct for butchers, that a person should not eat meat before skinning and cutting. The conclusion is that Rami bar Chama is necessarily teaching proper conduct, but by strict law it is permitted before skinning, because we establish it in its presumption of permissibility. Otherwise there would be a difficulty from Rabbi Akiva, who said that it is enough that he eat a kezayis from the place of slaughter. Rather, necessarily, Rami bar Chama said it only as proper conduct. If it were not obvious to the Gemara that in this the first Tanna also agrees, and their dispute is only that according to the first Tanna we require a roasted kezayis while according to Rabbi Akiva it is enough if he can eat it raw, what difficulty would there be against Rami bar Chama from Rabbi Akiva? On the contrary, there would be support from the first Tanna. Rather, necessarily, it is obvious to the Gemara that we do not require skinning and cutting even according to the first Tanna. Therefore, although we rule like the first Tanna, who requires a roasted kezayis, nevertheless it is permitted even before skinning, for in this the first Tanna agrees.
Furthermore, Magen Avraham wrote that it is forbidden, etc. If so, necessarily the first Tanna also agrees in this, that it is permitted to eat before skinning. Although even before skinning it is possible to inspect it, as written at the end of siman 497, that one who slaughters an animal on Yom Tov should preferably not inspect it until after the skinning, implying that it can be inspected before skinning. One must say that this is by skinning part of the hide so that he can inspect through it, and he gives advice in siman 497 not to inspect until after completing the skinning.
