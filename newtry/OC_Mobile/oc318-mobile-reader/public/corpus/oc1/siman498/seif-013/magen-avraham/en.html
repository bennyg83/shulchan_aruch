He should not pluck. Although it is a melachah she'einah tzrichah legufah, since he does not need the feathers, nevertheless it is forbidden, for preparatory acts for food are permitted only after slaughter, but what he does before slaughter could have been done yesterday, Rosh. It is difficult, how is this different from carrying in seif 2? One can say that with regard to carrying we are lenient, as above. There is another difficulty, for in Beitzah 7b Beis Shammai hold that it is permitted to dig, slaughter, and cover, and the Gemara also says there, perhaps he will reconsider and not slaughter, and we say the same regarding carrying; see there. It does not say the reason is because it is permitted only after slaughter. Rather, necessarily, we hold that this is not called preparatory acts, but food itself. Furthermore, the Rosh himself wrote at the beginning of chapter 3 of Beitzah that trapping is permitted because food itself is permitted even if it could have been done yesterday, except that it is forbidden because it resembles weekday activity; see there. This implies that even something before slaughter is permitted. And what the Rosh wrote as proof from the fact that Beis Hillel forbade digging in order to cover is difficult, for if so, one who has loose earth to place below should first slaughter and afterward dig to cover. Rather, necessarily, Beis Hillel's reason is that initially it is forbidden to push aside Yom Tov because of this; see the Gemara. Maggid Mishneh in chapter 3 wrote the reason: since in its lifetime it stands in a presumption of prohibition, it does not override Yom Tov except for something necessary, such as slaughter. But detaching, which is not necessary to do before slaughter, does not override until it becomes complete food. These are his words. See Tosafot, Beitzah 3.
