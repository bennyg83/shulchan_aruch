[13] An olive-bulk roasted, etc. This follows the first Tanna of Rabbi Akiva in Beitzah, page 25, and so ruled the Rambam, Semag, Rashba, and Semak. The Taz wondered: do we not hold that the halacha follows Rabbi Akiva even against his colleagues, meaning even if many disagree with him? End quote. I did not understand the end of his reasoning, for in Eiruvin 46b it is explicit that the halacha follows Rabbi Akiva specifically against an individual, but not against many. So too it is written in Sefer Kerisus and in Knesses HaGedolah in the rules of the Gemara, siman 8. Also, against an anonymous Mishnah one should not rule like Rabbi Akiva, as Tosafot wrote in Beitzah 25, s.v. lachem, etc. However, in this case one can say that since Rabbi Akiva disputes in that very Mishnah, the words of the first Tanna are not considered an anonymous ruling.
