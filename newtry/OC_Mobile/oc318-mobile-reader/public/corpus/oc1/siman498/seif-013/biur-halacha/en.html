He should not pluck. See Mishnah Berurah. Plucking a bird's feathers is more severe than plucking wool. Plucking wool is considered an unusual manner, whereas feathers are normally removed this way, and it is a derivative of shearing. I saw in Pischei Teshuvah, Yoreh Deah 308, citing Shaar HaMelech, that he proves from Chullin 137a that goat hair has the same status as bird feathers because it is normally removed by plucking and is not considered an unusual manner. He therefore questions why the authorities did not distinguish between goat hair and sheep's wool regarding a firstborn animal. See Pischei Teshuvah. However, Tosafos on Shabbos 27a, s.v. “Noga,” write that hair and the fine fleece are distinct. The fine fleece is the thin material upon goats from which garments are made. It is concerning this that the Gemara says its normal method is plucking, like a bird's feathers. The goat's hair is the coarse material from which sacks are made. Accordingly, goat hair has the same status as animal wool. At minimum, the Gemara proves regarding the thin fleece that its normal method of removal is plucking, like a bird's feathers. Thus, even according to Rambam, who is lenient regarding animal wool and permits intentional plucking because it is only an unusual manner, as Beit Yosef explains, one who slaughters a goat must be very careful not to pluck the thin fleece from its neck, for according to Rambam this entails a Torah prohibition. Ramban's first reason for permitting plucking from a bird, namely, that this stage of the melachah will ultimately be displaced on Yom Tov when the feathers are removed after slaughter, does not apply here, because the goat's entire hide will ultimately be skinned.

Mordechai himself concludes, “In chapter Klal Gadol, he explained it as forbidden in the name of Rabbi Eliezer of Metz.” Yet there the case concerns intentional plucking, to which Rabbi Shimon's permission for an unintended act does not apply, whereas here he permitted it specifically when one does not intend to pluck, as is evident from his concluding words.
