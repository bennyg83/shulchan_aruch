And if. See Taz. He ruled like the Rama in the gloss, unlike the Bach and Magen Avraham, seif katan 31. Specifically with digging, which is a primary labor, muktzeh ash is preferable, for wherever one can fulfill both, the positive commandment does not override the negative commandment. This supports what I wrote in letter 12, that according to Rabbeinu Tam, even initially Beis Hillel permit actual digging because the positive commandment overrides the negative commandment and Yom Tov has only a negative commandment. However, with loose earth one may say it is not a primary labor, only a trace of digging; plowing is only in a field and where he needs it. Nevertheless, it may still be more severe than muktzeh. We already wrote in letter 12 that we rule that Yom Tov has both a positive and negative commandment, in siman 586 regarding shofar, in siman 446 regarding chametz, and in Rambam, Laws of Yom Tov, in the introduction and chapter 1. Know that an inserted spade without designation, only an inserted spade from long ago, has muktzeh and pit-making, two D'Rabbanan prohibitions. If there is an inserted spade and he designated it, one may say the inserted spade is preferable, for there is one D'Rabbanan prohibition of making a pit, whereas stove ash as muktzeh is more severe. The Magen Avraham holds that muktzeh may be lighter than a pit, since they were lenient with muktzeh for the joy of Yom Tov, and this is joy of Yom Tov. With Hashem's help it will be explained further.
