If he brought in ten, etc. In Beis Yosef in the name of Orchos Chaim in the name of the Rashba: if he brought earth into his garden and into his ruin to cover his roof with it, this is like a stuck digging-stick, and we do not rely on it l'chatchila on Yom Tov; and if he transgressed and slaughtered, we cover with it; end quote. And this is astonishing, for in the Gemara we say regarding this that it is permitted to cover, meaning even l'chatchila, as Rashi wrote, because since it is piled his mind is upon it, and automatically it is prepared. Also what he added, "to cover his roof with it," I do not know what benefit there is in that addition. And it appears to me that there is a scribal error here, and it should read: into his ruin it is permitted to cover with it; [but] to cover his roof with it, this is like a stuck digging-stick, etc. And this the Rashba innovated to distinguish between bringing it into a ruin or for the need of his roof. And so it is somewhat implied in Maharshal's words in the first chapter: even though he copied this law in the name of Orchos Chaim as it stands before us, nevertheless his words afterward imply that there are two clauses. For he wrote, and this is his language: And I did not delve to the end of his intent, whether it is earth to which digging applies and needs a digging-stick, and since he brought it in to cover with it, it is like a stick stuck from before Yom Tov. And the reason is that so long as it is piled it stands for all his needs, like one who brought earth into his garden, etc. If so, why must it say "to cover his roof with it," to distinguish from within itself: if he brought earth into his garden and it is not so soft that digging applies, it is forbidden l'chatchila; and if digging does not apply, why is it forbidden l'chatchila; how does it differ from bringing earth into his garden? One must say: since he brought it in to cover his roof with it, that is, regarding building he is particular about it and it is muktzeh; nevertheless after the fact it is permitted like a stuck digging-stick, as above regarding earth that is not prepared. And what he said that it is like a stuck digging-stick is not regarding "prepared," but regarding the after-the-fact permission to cover, which we learn from a stuck digging-stick as I explained above; end quote. The meaning of his words is that there are two clauses here, and so it appears to me: namely at first it is permitted even to cover, and at the end regarding his roof he wrote that it is permitted specifically after the fact because it is muktzeh, and even so it is permitted after the fact, for we rely on Rabbeinu Tam according to what I wrote in our words.
