(Seif katan 16) And we are not concerned, etc. This requires study, for Tosafot in Chullin 11a, s.v. ati, etc., asked against Rabbah bar Sheila, who derived that we follow the majority from the red heifer, since it had to be burned whole, and we should be concerned lest perhaps it is a tereifah and invalid, as derived there from a verse. Rather, necessarily, we follow the majority. Tosafot asked: since a red heifer is two years old, perhaps that is because we establish it on its presumption that it is not a tereifah.
According to the one who says that a tereifah cannot live, etc. One can answer that any presumption that was not clarified for even one moment, we do not follow. Meaning, every moment from its birth there is room to doubt, perhaps now it became a tereifah. Only when twelve months have passed do we know retroactively that then it was not a tereifah, but nevertheless it is possible that after the twelve months it became a tereifah. Only if another twelve months pass will it be revealed retroactively that then it was not a tereifah. In any case, there is no known moment when it is now not a tereifah, so presumption does not help. From this one can learn that if cheeses were made from several animals and one was slaughtered and found to be a tereifah, all are forbidden, for we do not establish the animal on its presumption and say that only now it became a tereifah, etc. These are the words of Tosafot. The Rosh concluded: furthermore, according to the conclusion that we follow majority, all animals are presumed kosher even within their first year, because most animals are kosher, and majority is stronger than presumption. These are his words. It is explicit that the presumption is only by force of the majority of animals being kosher. This presumption also exists for a calf on the day it is born, as the Rosh wrote, even within its first year, etc.
And in Yam Shel Shlomo he wrote that we rule, etc. Meaning, the case of a bird that was trampled is an unresolved question, and the poskim ruled leniently because no Torah prohibition is involved. But the Ran ruled stringently, and Yam Shel Shlomo agreed with the Ran and brought proof from Hagahos Ashri, that he too necessarily holds stringently.
All the more so where a defect has been established. This means a bird that was trampled.
See what I wrote in seif 5, meaning that he brought in the name of Darchei Moshe, who brought proof to be stringent with a calf, etc. Although we are lenient with a trampled bird. His proof is from Hagahos Ashri, who forbids slaughtering in a place where tereifos are common. What proof is this? That Hagahos Ashri necessarily holds that even with a trampled bird we are stringent. But according to us, who rule the unresolved question leniently by a trampled bird, the words of Hagahos Ashri do not apply, and there is no reason to be stringent with a calf, etc.
See what I wrote in siman 527, seif katan 21, where he brought in the name of the responsum of Maharshal that if he did not mention slaughtering in the eruv tavshilin, it is forbidden to slaughter from Yom Tov for Shabbos. One must say this refers to a place where tereifos are not common, for otherwise it is forbidden to slaughter in any case. And the custom is not to slaughter in any manner, so it must be referring to a time of need.
The same applies to a dangerously ill animal: even if he does not need it because of loss, one may permit.
