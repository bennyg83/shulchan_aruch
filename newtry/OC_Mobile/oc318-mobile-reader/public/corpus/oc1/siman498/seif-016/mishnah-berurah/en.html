To his house for the need of his garden. This means that his intention was explicitly for the need of his garden, to spread it there, and therefore only if there is much earth, because as long as it is piled, etc. But if it is only a basketful, no, and even if he placed it in a corner it does not help. This is unlike seif 17, where he brought it into the house without specification; even a basketful is permitted when he designated a corner for it, as below. Some poskim hold that this is specifically if he brought it into his actual garden, for then we say that if it is small, a basketful and the like, it is nullified to the ground of the garden. But if he brings it into the house or courtyard, even if he brought only a basketful and designated a place for it, it is not nullified.
And it is gathered in one place. Meaning, he has not yet spread it over his garden, but for now it is lying as he brought it, in one pile.
To cover with it. Even to slaughter initially relying on this earth, as he concludes, as long as, etc. The later authorities wrote that this seif also deals with earth that was loose, like the case in seif 14; otherwise it is forbidden to cover with it. For slaughtering initially, or for other needs, it must be so loose that taking it will not make any pit.
For whatever he will need. This implies that it is permitted for any use. Some poskim wrote that they permitted it only for covering, which is a mitzvah need, but not for other uses; and even for covering, only after the fact, but not to slaughter initially relying on this. It is not comparable to seif 17 below, because here the preparation is not complete, since its main purpose was for his garden. One should be stringent like this view.
