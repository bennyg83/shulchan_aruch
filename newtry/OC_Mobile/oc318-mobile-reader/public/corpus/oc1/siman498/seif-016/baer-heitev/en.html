Earth. This speaks of loose earth, which lacks neither digging nor crushing, but only preparation, as written in seif 17. Although above, in seif 14, he holds that if there is no spade inserted, digging applies, that is with ground of the earth itself. Here, however, where he brought earth from elsewhere and placed it in a corner, digging does not apply at all. Beis Yosef. The Olas Tamid [and the Taz] wrote that here it speaks where that loose earth is lying on the ground like a heap, and whatever he takes from above has no relevance to digging; while in seif 14, where an inserted spade is required, it speaks of level ground, and although it is loose earth, nevertheless whatever he takes from there is digging with respect to what is beneath it, for a pit remains beneath it; see there. The Magen Avraham wrote that it speaks of earth so loose that it does not make a pit, for as soon as he takes the earth, earth falls beneath it; see there. If he slaughtered on hard ground and there is much blood there, he should take some of it and place it in loose earth and cover it, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 28, seif 7. But if the blood dried somewhat, it is forbidden to scrape it on Yom Tov, for he levels holes in the house, and this is labor according to the Torah; see Magen Avraham. It is permitted to remove a candle from sand on Yom Tov, for the pit forms by itself, but it is forbidden to insert the candle into the sand, since he makes a pit. Magen Avraham; see there.
