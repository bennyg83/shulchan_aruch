[1] [Levush] For himself from before Yom Tov. This requires study, for in the Talmud, Beitzah 28, it is explained that even on Yom Tov it is permitted to inspect for himself, since, being a sage, he knows not to sharpen it. So too it is proven from the Beis Yosef according to all the explanations. What he wrote afterward, that nowadays one should check before Yom Tov like the Rama, is because in his time the law of a Torah scholar does not apply. What the Magen Avraham asked, that for several matters we judge the law of a Torah scholar, appears to me not comparable: here it is different because the matter is entrusted to himself, and one may err about himself, thinking that he is a sage. Similar to this I wrote in my work on Yoreh Deah, siman 1, regarding an expert in the slaughterhouse. I saw in Be'er Sheva that he is lenient because our practice is to take authorization, which is similar to a sage, but this is not clear, for even in the days of the Maharil and Rama this custom existed, and nevertheless they prohibited it. Furthermore, on the contrary, it is worse, for authorization is given even to one who is not a sage. See Yam Shel Shlomo, Perek Ein Tzadin, siman 20.
