1) [Se'if 1] A knife is not shown to a scholar, etc. This follows the practice in the era of the Sages of the Talmud, when an animal was not slaughtered with a knife until the knife was shown to a scholar to determine whether it was fit for slaughter and free of a nick. Levush. Nowadays, however, the custom throughout the Jewish Diaspora is not to trust butchers. Designated people are appointed over slaughter and inspection, and the Sages waived their honor for them because these appointees are diligent and careful. Consequently, inspection by a scholar was entirely discontinued, even for one who slaughters for his own household, as the Tur writes in Yoreh Deah, siman 18, in the name of his father, the Rosh. Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly there, se'if 17. See there. This is the meaning of Rama's statement here in the gloss, 'Nowadays, when every shochet, etc.' It follows the aforementioned position of the Rosh. Nevertheless, the inspection must be performed with great deliberation and with fear of Heaven upon one's face, as the later authorities there wrote in their explicit admonitions regarding this matter. This is not the place to elaborate.
