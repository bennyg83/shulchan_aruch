(א) One does not show etc. — even if the tavch already checked for himself on erev Yom Tov and it was found good:
(2) To see, etc. — as was the custom in the time of the Sages to show the knife after his inspection to a scholar due to the scholar's honor.
(3) Lest it be defective, etc. — b'dieved if he showed and it was found fine it is permitted to slaughter with it.
(4) That he showed a knife for himself — meaning in his house on Yom Tov to slaughter with it; see Peri Chadash who sides that even l'chatchila a sage may inspect his own knife to lend to others; reason they permitted the sage: for him there is no concern lest he sharpen since he knows it is forbidden; therefore permitted also when he does not wish to rely on his inspection to show his knife to another sage.
(5) He views a knife for himself — for they rely on a receipt he has.
(ו) He shall check his knife from erev Yom Tov — and not return the knife to his sheath, for if the point or sides of the knife touch the hardness of the sheath it is not called a checked knife, as Taz wrote Yoreh De'ah siman 18 s.k. 14. Acharonim wrote it is proper that each tavch have two or three knives checked from erev Yom Tov:
(ז) and not in the High Court - we were the first test, but the test that the wheat is permitted is a crystal that will be excavated if a flawed is found, the chest will be useful to train the wheat that was slaughtered before:
(8) He may check it on Yom Tov — such as when there is no other knife in the city checked before Yom Tov.
