(Seif katan 10) For we are concerned, etc. It is permitted, etc., for we rule like Rav Nachman, who says in Chullin 51 that the womb does not create a concern of crushed limbs. Meaning, regarding an offspring born today, we are not concerned that because it emerged from the womb, which is a narrow place, perhaps its limbs were crushed in its emergence. There in Chullin the Gemara brings support for Rav Nachman from the teaching that all agree, even according to the one who says that if a blemish fell into a firstborn one may not inspect it on Yom Tov to determine whether it is a permanent blemish permitting it, nevertheless if it was born on Yom Tov and its blemish was with it immediately, so that no prohibited status ever took effect upon it, one may slaughter it on Yom Tov. Let the concern of crushed limbs be a problem. Rather, necessarily, it follows Rav Nachman, that the womb does not create a concern of crushed limbs. The Gemara rejects this, saying that it discusses a case where it stood with its hooves on the ground, and thereby left the concern of crushed limbs, as explained in Yoreh Deah; really, Rav Nachman's principle would not be established from there. If so, according to the halachic ruling that we rule like Rav Nachman, we do not require that it stand on the ground. Nevertheless, on Yom Tov we require that it stand on the ground. See Peri Chadash, who questioned this from the above Gemara.
And this is the language of Darchei Moshe, etc. If we see, etc. Meaning, with regard to a calf that was born and slaughtered that same day, and we saw a change in its limbs. This is explained in siman 497, and Magen Avraham brought it in this siman, seif katan 16. If so, for this reason one should not slaughter a calf born on Yom Tov, lest a change be found and it will be a tereifah.
And for this reason they were uncertain, etc. Because perhaps it will be found to be a tereifah, since there is a defect because it was trampled. If so, the same applies here. Although with regard to the trampled bird itself we rule that it is permitted to slaughter it on Yom Tov, Magen Avraham already wrote in seif katan 16 to distinguish, that a calf born that day is worse, etc. The intent of Darchei Moshe is that since we see that at least with a trampled bird the Gemara was uncertain whether to forbid it on Yom Tov, from this we judge that a calf born on Yom Tov should certainly be forbidden to slaughter on Yom Tov, since it is worse than a trampled bird. And one can say that he relied, etc., and therefore a calf, etc. Meaning, the Tur relied on our understanding from reasoning: since a fallen animal requires inspection, at least a calf born today, which is lighter than it and one level lower, does not require inspection. But if he saw a change in the inner limbs, its law is like a fallen animal and it is forbidden. Also regarding a calf, it implies that he holds this even if the change does not itself render it a tereifah, because he does not distinguish between a calf and a fallen animal except that a fallen animal requires inspection while a calf does not. But if a change is seen, a calf is equal to a fallen animal, and by a fallen animal we rule that even if the change does not itself render it a tereifah, it forbids. Furthermore, if we required specifically a change that makes it a tereifah, what was difficult to Darchei Moshe, that the Tur should have explained that if he sees a change it is forbidden? What explanation would be needed for this? It is obvious that it is forbidden, since it is a change that makes it a tereifah. And if he holds that it must be in an organ on which tereifah status depends, so that even a change that does not itself make it a tereifah is forbidden, but in an organ on which tereifah status does not depend, a change does not make it a tereifah, it is difficult to distinguish this way; and by a fallen animal there is no distinction, and in every case it forbids. Nevertheless, this requires study, for wherever it says, etc., Rav Nachman also used this expression, that the womb has no concern of crushed limbs. Why should we say that the womb is more severe? Generally, where it teaches that there is no concern of crushed limbs, even if we see a change it is permitted.
And with regard to the womb he also taught this wording; we will explain that if a change is seen in it, it is forbidden. There is another difficulty: why did Rama write, etc.? For Maharam himself asked this against the Tur, that he should have explained it, and answered that he relied on the law of a fallen animal. If so, although that answer suffices to explain the Tur, for one can say that it was obvious to the Tur in his broad understanding that by reasoning we know that the womb is one level lower than a fallen animal, but when a change is seen we know that it is no better than a fallen animal. But for Darchei Moshe it was necessarily not obvious, since he raised it as a question and answered the Tur's words. If so, Rama should have explicitly explained the law in Yoreh Deah, that if a change is seen even with the womb, it is forbidden. See what I wrote in seif 8 to distinguish between here and a trampled bird.
In Yoreh Deah siman 15, the Taz there disagreed with Rama, and even on Yom Tov it does not require standing on the ground. He agreed with Rabbeinu Yerucham that what is written in the Rosh, requiring that it stand on the ground, is a textual error.
