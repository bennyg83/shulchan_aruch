Treifah. See Baer Heitev; and he wrote in Shevut Yaakov, part 1, siman 23, that if no gallbladder is found, it is permitted to roast the liver on Yom Tov in order to taste it, for we rely on the majority; see there. And see Shaagas Aryeh, siman 64: if most animals are treifos, even due to a chumra of the later authorities, it is forbidden to slaughter on Yom Tov; but if it is only a minority, since regarding that minority there is also [only] from a chumra of the later authorities, there is a heter to slaughter on Yom Tov; see there. And accordingly, what Magen Avraham wrote, that if he needs it one should be lenient, it appears that also in such a case one should be lenient; but where most are treifos, or where treifos are as common as kosher ones but they are absolute treifos by law, one should not be lenient. And see in Shevut Yaakov that one who has never slaughtered is forbidden to slaughter l'chatchila on Yom Tov, since he is not yet established as steady of hand and that he will not faint; we are concerned that he may ruin his slaughter, and it is unnecessary toil on Yom Tov, and it is forbidden, as we hold above in siman 331, and in Yoreh De'ah regarding one who has never performed milah, etc.; see there. And see in Responsa Chavos Yair in the name of the Gaon, author of Olas HaGolah, who raises a difficulty from the case of a mohel against this of a bird that was mauled, etc.; see there, siman 158, and what he wrote on this, and also to resolve this; see there.
