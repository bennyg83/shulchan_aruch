It appears in my humble opinion. A practical difference between the reason of the Rosh and the reason of the Ran is with one born from an animal that stands for raising offspring. According to the Ran, there is no preparation, for in any event he prepared it for himself: if he slaughters for a sick person, it is permitted for a healthy person raw, since on Shabbos we rule like Rabbi Shimon that muktzeh is permitted, and even nolad is permitted according to many poskim. But according to the Rosh it is forbidden. The root of the Ran and Rosh depends on the two explanations the Ramban wrote in Milchamos regarding an egg born on Yom Tov after Shabbos being forbidden because of preparation: even if it had not been completed and could not have been laid today, he could have eaten it by slaughtering the hen; nevertheless, now without slaughtering, he needs yesterday's completion so that the egg come to him through today's laying. Or, it is better and improved in taste through laying. It seems that the main reasoning of the Rosh is that with a calf, which is not improved more through birth, it does not need birth at all, for even without birth he could eat it the next day on Yom Tov by slaughtering the mother, and the birth does not function more to be called preparation. This is only where she stands for eating. But where she stands for raising, even according to the poskim that muktzeh is permitted on Yom Tov and if he slaughtered her she would be permitted, since she stands for raising and he does not want to slaughter her, he needs the birth, and it is preparation. The Ran follows his view, as is clear from his words regarding an egg born on one day being forbidden on the other, that he holds like the first reason of the Ramban, for now he did not slaughter her. This also applies to a calf; therefore he needed the reason of preparation incidentally to its mother.
