For if. See Taz, Beitzah 8a and 8b, and so it appears in the Gemara; this is how the text should read. What he wrote that the one who says it is because of permitting its fat holds that there is no concern of exertion, for one may exert oneself in a doubtful case, and he disagrees with the baraisa of Rabbi Zeira. He holds like the baraisa: one may not slaughter a koy, and if he slaughtered it one does not cover it. Since it did not teach a greater novelty, this implies that where the blood of a domestic animal and wild animal became mixed, one covers with two thrusts. With a koy, one who sees will err and think that because of a doubt the Sages did not trouble him on Yom Tov; this shows that the Sages established it as certainly a wild animal, and therefore we do not cover on Yom Tov. If so, it is difficult on the Mechaber why he mentions the observer with a koy, since he ruled in seif 19 that exertion is forbidden. One may say this depends on the dispute whether a Torah doubt is forbidden according to the Torah or only D'Rabbanan; see Pri Chadash, Yoreh Deah 110. According to the view that a doubt is D'Rabbanan, the prohibition with a koy is because of exertion; according to the view that a doubt is according to the Torah, he is obligated, and it is only because of permitting its fat. It still requires study.
