If he slaughtered a domestic animal and a wild animal, etc. These are the words of the Rambam in chapter 3; and we will first explain his view. At the end of chapter 2 he wrote: One who had a digging-stick stuck in from erev Yom Tov, and he pulled it out on Yom Tov and brought up earth: if that earth was soft, he covers with it and may move it. And the Maggid Mishneh wrote: It is implied from our master's language that it is not permitted to slaughter relying on a stuck digging-stick, for so he wrote "and he pulled it out," etc.; and so it appears from what he wrote at the beginning of chapter 3: "And if he has no prepared earth or stove ash," etc. And after the fact, what our master wrote that he covers with soft earth is explicit in the Gemara; end quote. And this is difficult, for the Rambam wrote at the beginning of chapter 3, and this is his language: One who had prepared earth or prepared ash that is permitted to move may slaughter a wild animal and a bird and cover their blood; and if he has no earth or ash fit to move, he should not slaughter; and if he transgressed and slaughtered, he should not cover their blood until evening; end quote. And according to the Maggid Mishneh based on what the Rambam wrote "prepared earth or prepared ash," a stuck digging-stick mentioned at the end of chapter 2 is not included, since there according to the Maggid Mishneh it is permitted only after the fact, while here it is permitted l'chatchila. And it is astonishing to me: since in any case after the fact it is permitted with a stuck digging-stick, why did he write that if he has no prepared earth or ash he should not slaughter, and if he slaughtered he should not cover until evening? Behold it is permitted after the fact even without these, namely with a stuck digging-stick. And if you wish to say that he also has no stuck digging-stick, and it is included in "earth or prepared ash" which he said he does not have: then also when the Rambam wrote that one who has earth and ash may slaughter l'chatchila, a stuck digging-stick is likewise included. Thus his words contradict one another. And furthermore, the Rambam attached the permission to cover to the permission to move; if so, even a stuck digging-stick is also permitted, since moving is permitted. Therefore it appears to me that certainly with a stuck digging-stick there is no l'chatchila prohibition to cover with it from the aspect that it is not prepared, for it is certainly prepared; rather there is a prohibition to take from there the earth that comes by sticking the digger, since he makes a depression if he takes enough to cover; but so long as he does not take so much that a depression is made, he may take for his need, since there is no muktzeh there, for it was already moved from its place yesterday, and it is like crushed and soft regarding covering. Beis Hillel permitted after the fact that he may make a depression; as we say in the Gemara, "but he is making a depression," and it answers as R' Abba said: one who digs a pit and needs only its earth is exempt; that is, but it is forbidden. Therefore we say that after the fact if he slaughtered he should dig and cover even though he makes a depression. And if so, all is well: at first he wrote at the end of chapter 2 that if he pulled it out on Yom Tov, that is after the fact, and the depression was already made, it is permitted to cover with it, meaning even l'chatchila he may slaughter after the pulling out and may move it for all his needs; but if it has not yet been pulled out, he should not l'chatchila slaughter with intent to pull it out and cover, for he will make a depression, and that is forbidden l'chatchila even though he needs only its earth; but if he already slaughtered he may even pull it out, and we are not concerned for the depression since he is exempt for it. And now he properly said at the beginning of chapter 3: one who had prepared earth and ash fit to move; included in this is also a stuck digging-stick already mentioned that was pulled out on Yom Tov; then l'chatchila he slaughters and relies on it. But if he has no earth and ash fit to move, meaning he also has no digging-stick at all (for if he had one it would be fit to move so long as he is not making a depression), then certainly he has no stuck digging-stick at all; in that case he should not cover until evening even if he transgressed and slaughtered. And if so, what is written here regarding the law of mixed bloods, and he has prepared earth: included in this is also a stuck digging-stick, namely if it was already pulled out and he brought up the earth; in that case it is permitted with one digging, implying that if it was not yet pulled out and he transgressed and slaughtered a wild animal and a bird and the bloods mixed, even one digging is forbidden, since he must make a depression, and making a depression after an after-the-fact slaughter was permitted only when he slaughtered a wild animal and a bird alone, not with a mixture. This is the Rambam's view correctly, with Heaven's help. And the Tur wrote on these words of the Rambam: And it appears that if he can cover it with one digging, since he has already slaughtered it is permitted, even if he has no prepared earth, provided he has a stuck digging-stick and soft earth; and if he has prepared earth he may cover it even with many diggings; end quote. He disagrees with the Rambam on two points: one, that after the fact he may cover also with a mixture even if it was not yet pulled out to earth; and where he has prepared earth it is permitted even with many diggings; whereas according to the Rambam that is forbidden here because it is toil for him, and he holds like Rashi as written, that from the aspect of toil it is forbidden with a mixture. And accordingly it is difficult at first glance: if so, why do we need the reason of a decree because of its chelev; behold it is forbidden because of toil as with a mixture? One may answer that regarding a koi specifically we are not concerned about toil, since there is no clear side of prohibition, only a doubt; which is not so with a mixture, where there is a clear side of prohibition, for in any case there is certainly blood of a domestic animal there; therefore the toil is forbidden. And accordingly, in what was said "not only regarding a koi did they say," meaning there the prohibition is from the decree of chelev, which implies that if not for this decree there would be no prohibition: that is not so, for even with a mixture there is in any case a prohibition because of toil. But the Tur is not satisfied with this, for one must equate the reason of mixtures with the reason of a koi, as the language implies: "not only regarding a koi did they say," etc.; and if so toil cannot apply in both, for regarding a koi it is proven that we are not concerned for toil; rather certainly from the aspect of prohibition, and likewise with mixtures from the aspect of prohibition, namely the prohibition of what is not prepared; if so, what is prepared is permitted even with a mixture. And now it is well that there is no dispute in the Gemara as one must say according to Rashi as we mentioned. And still clarification is needed regarding what is stated in the Gemara on this and in the poskim, that a mixture is forbidden with two diggings, implying that without a mixture many diggings are permitted; and so it is written in a manuscript; and the Rama already mentioned it in siman 14. And it is astonishing: why should this be permitted; how does it differ from where there was no stuck digging-stick, which was forbidden; and how does it differ if there is a stuck digging-stick but the earth in it does not suffice even for a bird alone without a mixture, and now he comes to dig with the stick anew: this is as if there was none at all, both from the aspect of preparation and from the aspect of digging or making a depression. And it appears to me that certainly in every place an additional digging on Yom Tov is forbidden; but here regarding a mixture we say that even if he had many sticks stuck from erev Yom Tov, which would permit covering a bird alone with many diggings, with a mixture only one digging was permitted; since regarding a bird alone it is permitted only after the fact even with one digging, automatically with a mixture we do not rely to be lenient with many diggings, since he is making an additional digging for the domestic animal; as appears correct to me. And I saw Maharshal on this, and it appears to me that where one digging will not suffice for him, then he should not cover at all, even with one digging, lest he not cover the blood that is obligated in covering, and he turns out to toil on Yom Tov unnecessarily; end quote. And so wrote my master, my father-in-law of blessed memory. And in my humble opinion this prohibition is not compelling at all, for we hold in Yoreh De'ah, siman 28, that one need not cover all the blood; rather we expound "its blood," even part of its blood. And if so here, where the bloods were mixed together, there is no room here for a split to say that perhaps in one place blood of a wild animal or bird alone remained without blood of a domestic animal, for everything was mixed and there is no portion of one without the other. And know this, for we hold regarding prohibitions in mixtures not wet that it is nullified in sixty; and why should we not be concerned that the prohibition remained in one place by itself? And if so, likewise here in every portion there is in any case blood of a wild animal or bird, and it is proper to cover part of its blood. And also one who slaughters a wild animal alone, and one digging does not suffice to cover all the blood, and he had only one stuck digging-stick: would it enter your mind that he should not cover at all? Certainly whatever he can cover with one digging he must at least cover; likewise so it is with a mixture. This appears clear and correct to me, with Heaven's help.
