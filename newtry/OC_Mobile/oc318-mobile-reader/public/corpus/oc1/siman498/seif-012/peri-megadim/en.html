He should not slaughter. See Taz at length. See Gemara Beitzah 7b, regarding what it says: he dug and covered; and Rav Yehudah said: this is where a spade was inserted before Yom Tov. The explanation may be that without an inserted spade there is digging initially, even with loose earth, a trace of digging. See Rashi there and on 9b. See Pri Chadash at length. Or it may be because of preparation and muktzeh that an inserted spade and designation for this are required. See Tosafot, Beitzah 8a, Rosh, and Tosafot 9b. Rabbeinu Tam explained that the text is reversed regarding 'one who slaughters,' and according to Beis Hillel it is permitted to slaughter even without any inserted spade. The teacher of the Semag ruled stringently at the outset, lest the halacha follow Rashi, but after the fact, once it was slaughtered, one may rely on Rabbeinu Tam that the text is reversed and that according to Beis Hillel one covers even when it was not prepared. The Taz answered the Tosafot's questions that according to Rabbeinu Tam, the concern applies only according to the initial assumption, but in the conclusion Beis Hillel permit initially even without an inserted spade and even actual digging without loose earth, for the positive commandment overrides the negative commandment. See Tosafot 8b, s.v. sof sof. Covering at the outset is not called a case where both can be fulfilled, for he wants to slaughter and eat for the joy of Yom Tov. According to Beis Shammai, however, initially it is forbidden, and after the fact it is not a case of overriding, for it is a positive commandment that came through negligence.
