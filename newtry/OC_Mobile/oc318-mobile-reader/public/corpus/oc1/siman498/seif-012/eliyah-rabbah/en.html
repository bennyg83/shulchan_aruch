[12] In order to eat, etc. Meaning after its soul departs. Even if he does not eat, and even thought to eat is not required. Yam Shel Shlomo. The Taz wrote that they permitted specifically an animal in mortal danger, because of loss, but a healthy animal is forbidden unless he actually eats an olive-bulk from it. What the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba, 'even if he eats,' is a scribal error, and it must read, 'unless he eats,' etc. End quote. This is astonishing from an author of his stature, for he fell into a great error by emending for no reason. Certainly eating does not help, since he does not need it, and he does it only in order to permit it; this is a device and is forbidden. So it is explained in the Maggid Mishneh cited by the Beis Yosef there. It is even more astonishing that he did not look at the source of the law in Responsa Rashba, siman 680, where it is explicit that his eating does not help. So too it is written explicitly in Responsa Rivash, simanim 16 and 254, and Sefer Tanya, siman 55. See Responsa Be'er Esek, siman 56; his words in this are not correct, see there.
