To pluck the wool. See Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote that plucking is removal in an unusual manner. This is evident from the Gemara in Bechoros 25a and Rashi there, and likewise from Tosafos there, s.v. “And this is,” for according to the Gemara's conclusion, plucking is considered removal in an unusual manner. Rosh also writes this there. For this reason Rambam permits intentional plucking initially for the sake of Yom Tov rejoicing. The Levush's wording on this matter is entirely imprecise.
With his hand. See Magen Avraham, who remains in difficulty regarding the Rama. Rabbi Yonasan in his novellae on Hilchos Yom Tov, Peri Megadim, and other authorities resolve his question and prove that Rosh supports the Rama. Know also that Magen Avraham writes that if one plucks with a utensil, it is shearing. This is not conclusive. It is possible that only cutting the wool with a utensil is shearing, while plucking it with a utensil remains uprooting it from its place of growth in an unusual manner. This is somewhat implied by Rashi there, s.v. “In an unusual manner,” who writes that the normal method is not to pluck wool but to shear it. This implies that all plucking is unusual. Nevertheless, the Sages prohibited it there regarding a firstborn animal, and here on Yom Tov, because plucking with a utensil resembles shearing. Tur, Yoreh Deah, siman 308, supports this understanding, for he writes that the prohibition of using a utensil is because it appears like shearing, not because it actually constitutes shearing. I later found that Shaar HaMelech, Hilchos Yom Tov, also inclines to our view. Rosh, however, in chapter Klal Gadol, siman 6, appears to support Magen Avraham, namely, that plucking wool with a utensil is the normal manner of shearing. Pri Chadash as presently printed likewise appears to say this. See there.
And if it becomes detached, it becomes detached. See Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote that he should not move it, etc. This is from Rambam and is cited by Tur. Although Rambam discusses one who intentionally plucks at the outset, it is obvious that the same applies according to Tosafos and Rosh, as is evident from the discussion there.
