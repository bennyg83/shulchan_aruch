To detach. For it is uprooting something from its growth.
With his hand. This requires study, for Beis Yosef wrote this only according to Rambam, who permits detaching initially because it is uprooting in an unusual manner. Therefore specifically by hand is permitted, but not with a utensil, for that is shearing. But in Shulchan Aruch he ruled that it is permitted only to clear away the hair, because it is an unintended act. If so, even with a utensil it should be permitted to clear it away. Perhaps Rama's intent is that it is forbidden to clear it away with a comb, for that is a psik reishei, as written in siman 523, seif 2. However, in the Gemara it implies that it is forbidden with any utensil; see Tosafot there regarding a firstborn. Lechem Chamudos asked why in Yoreh Deah, siman 308, he ruled that it is permitted to detach initially, since the Rosh wrote that Yom Tov and a firstborn are equal, and it is permitted only to clear away. If so, why did the Tur and Shulchan Aruch distinguish between them? These are his words. Some say that the Shulchan Aruch's view is that by a firstborn it is permitted initially to detach, for detaching is not the same as shearing, but on Yom Tov it is forbidden because it is uprooting something from its growth, as Tosafot wrote. See Yoreh Deah, siman 24, seif 8 in the gloss.
