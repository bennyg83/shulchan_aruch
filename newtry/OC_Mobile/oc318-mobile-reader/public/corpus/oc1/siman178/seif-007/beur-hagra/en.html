Seif 7: A person, etc. This rejects Raavad’s explanation of Taanis 12a, where one version states, “Rav said: this was taught only,” etc. Raavad understands it to refer even to the middle of a meal, requiring an after-blessing and a new opening blessing. Likewise, Pesachim 120b states, “Abaye said to him: you have dozed,” etc., treating sleep as an interruption of the meal. This is incorrect. There the statement concerns the end of the eating and is a leniency. The Pesachim passage follows Rashbam’s explanation regarding the afikoman, because of eating in two places; see there. Nor is it comparable to the Tosefta in Berachos cited above, “the homeowner,” etc., “went a considerable distance,” etc., for there there is a diversion of attention, whereas casual sleep does not constitute a diversion of attention. The proof is that Sukkah, chapter 2, permits casual sleep while wearing tefillin, although one may not divert his attention from them. Rabbeinu Yonah and Rosh in Berachos, chapter 3, likewise explain this way.
Likewise, etc. This is self-evident, as stated in the cited Tosefta, that only a diversion of attention interrupts.
Voluntary matters. Meaning, as stated in siman 474, once one has begun the matter, it is not considered an interruption unless it is a fixed obligation. Even prayer in seif 6 applies where there is insufficient time, etc. Although prayer is obligatory, it is not fixed specifically to that meal. See there.
