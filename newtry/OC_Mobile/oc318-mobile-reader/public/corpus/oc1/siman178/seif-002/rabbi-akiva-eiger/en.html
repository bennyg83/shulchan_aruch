Magen Avraham, seif katan 3. “Even with drinking.” This remains somewhat difficult, for the Mechaber does not mention that with drinking one recites a retroactive blessing over what he consumed. Nor can it merely be regarded as reconsidering, which would require only a new initial blessing and not a retroactive blessing. Ran indeed explains the Gemara's challenge from the wording “and they uprooted themselves” in precisely this way: if the case concerned foods that do not require an after-blessing in their place, the situation would merely be one of reconsideration and would not require a retroactive blessing. Perhaps Magen Avraham was dissatisfied with this explanation because according to Ran, where the retroactive blessing is required because the blessing must be recited in the original place, that requirement exists only before they leave. Once they left and returned and now wish to continue eating, they do not recite a retroactive blessing. What difference, then, would there be whether they recited it before eating again or afterward? Accordingly, once the Mechaber writes that one who left his place and returned must recite a retroactive blessing, it is evident that the reason is that the meal has ended. The same should therefore apply to drinking, and he must first recite the after-blessing over what he drank. In my humble opinion there is proof for Taz that leaving some companions behind is ineffective regarding fruit. In seif 5 the Mechaber cites an opinion that fruits of the seven species require an after-blessing in their place. At first glance it is difficult to understand why he mentions this here, in a siman concerning change of place, since the Mechaber rules that a change of place requires a new blessing for every type of food. The primary law of whether an after-blessing must be recited in the original place appears below in siman 184. In this siman, which concerns only whether a change of place requires a new blessing, there should be no practical difference. Necessarily, the difference concerns leaving some companions behind, which is ineffective regarding foods whose after-blessing need not be recited in their place. This still requires study. I am also uncertain, according to Taz, where people reclined over bread and left their place before eating a kezayis, whether leaving some companions behind is effective. Although according to Rabbeinu Chananel less than the required measure of bread appears to have the same status as foods that do not require an after-blessing in their place, Magen Avraham explicitly writes at the end of siman 210, seif katan 1, that this is because the person has not yet become obligated in an after-blessing and therefore has no obligation to return to his place; his departure constitutes a complete termination. Yet the reason leaving companions behind is ineffective for fruit may be that no formal meal is established over fruit. Their reclining therefore does not constitute a binding meal, just as zimun is not recited over fruit. One may therefore argue that whenever they reclined over food whose after-blessing is normally recited in its place, even if they have not yet eaten the required measure, leaving companions behind is effective. This requires study in practice. My ancestor, the gaon Rabbi Avraham Broda, of blessed memory, likewise agrees with Taz in his work Eshel Avraham.
