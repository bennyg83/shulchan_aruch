And likewise if they were reclining, etc. Here there is no distinction whether some members remained behind or not, for even if some remained, another blessing is required, since fruit does not create a fixed meal, as explained below. According to Rav Sheshes there is no distinction in this respect. The word “likewise” does not equate every detail of the two cases; it means only that a change of location likewise requires a new blessing. This applies in every case. Although it is more obvious regarding fruit than regarding the bread discussed in the first clause that a new blessing is required, the wording “likewise” is nevertheless appropriate because here one is liable even when some members remained behind.
From one corner, etc., he need not return and recite another blessing. The phrase “need not return” might appear to mean that he also eats in the other corner, and then need not return here to recite the after-blessing but may recite it in the other corner. This cannot be correct, for if he eats in the second place, even when moving from one house to another he need not return to the first place, as stated in seif 4. Rather, this case concerns one who did not eat in the second corner, but returns to the first corner to finish his meal. He need not return to recite Bircas HaMazon and then recite HaMotzi again. The wording is somewhat imprecise; it should have said that he need not recite an after-blessing before moving.
And some disagree. This is the view of Tosafos and the Rosh, who disagree on two points, as explained above. Therefore the Rama writes this qualification regarding everything stated in this siman.
Specifically when he did not leave, etc. This is difficult. Since we now rule like Rav Chisda, there should be no distinction of this kind. Regarding fruit, even if some remained behind, one must recite another blessing; regarding bread, even if none remained, no new blessing is required. This gloss is taken from Semak, as cited by Beit Yosef: “A group who were reclining and then rose to leave, if they left some members behind, do not require an after-blessing, neither Bircas HaMazon nor HaMotzi, even for foods that do not require an after-blessing in their original place; for foods that do require an after-blessing in their original place, even if they left none behind, a change of location does not require another blessing before eating.” These words are difficult, for the sugya implies that regarding foods not requiring an after-blessing in their original place, namely fruit, there is no distinction whether some remained or not. Beit Yosef also writes that Semak’s wording is imprecise, since his mention of Bircas HaMazon implies bread, while he then applies the ruling to foods that do not require an after-blessing in their place. There is a further difficulty: he first says “even foods that do not require,” implying that the ruling certainly applies to foods that do require, yet afterward he says the opposite. It appears to me that one word is missing, namely, “remained.” The correct structure is as follows. Semak’s main text, through the word “even,” follows Rav Sheshes and therefore discusses leaving some members behind with respect to bread. The gloss, written by Rabbeinu Peretz, disagrees with the main text and says that this is not the accepted ruling. Rather, “even if some remained behind, for foods that do not require an after-blessing in their original place,” another blessing is required; “whereas for foods that do require,” even if none remained, no new blessing is needed. In other words, we rule like Rav Chisda rather than Rav Sheshes. Thus, for foods not requiring an after-blessing in their place, another blessing is required even if some remained; for foods requiring it, no new blessing is required even if none remained. The general conclusion is that according to Tosafos and the Rosh, whether some remained is irrelevant. Semag, however, also distinguishes based on whether some remained, as will be explained below. Rama therefore has no basis here to distinguish between leaving some behind and leaving none. The distinction is instead between foods that require an after-blessing in their original place and those that do not.
