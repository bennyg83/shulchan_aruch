(Seif 7) A person who sleeps during his meal—casual sleep is not interruption. In margin: see Magen Avraham who wrote in any case we require netilat yadayim for it is hesech da'at, as Tur and he brought proof from oral law, etc. In my humble opinion this is not clear—room to distinguish per Tosafot Chagigah 21a s.v. the mourner and lacking atonement who wrote: R' Yitzchak answered hesech da'at to disqualify only similar to there when it was in his hand and he diverted mind from it; but when not in his hand such as lost—not applicable to disqualify; R' Eliezer brought proof from ch. 3 that limbs of olot and pieces of chatat were found in azarah—implies olah he sacrifices and chatat is eaten and not disqualified by hesech da'at, end quote. [Long discussion of Shekalim/Yerushalmi, R' Yitzchak vs R' Elazar on lost items and hesech da'at in azarah—concludes casual sleep during meal is not hesech da'at like loss, unlike speaking during meal or Hallel/aggadah; if he put himself to sleep knowingly it is hesech da'at; if sleep overcame him without lying down intentionally—not hesech da'at; for netilat yadayim rabbinic law follows lenient view when doubt.
