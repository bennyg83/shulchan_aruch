What he already ate. The after-blessing recited over his second eating covers both the first and second eating. Before eating again, however, he must recite a new blessing, as in the law of one who changed his mind.
He need not recite a blessing. In that case, even if he went elsewhere during the meal and drank wine or water, he need not recite a new blessing, for he returns to his original established meal. Beit Yosef; Magen Avraham.
To another house. What Rama wrote above, that the two places must be in one house, follows the opinion ruling there that one must recite an after-blessing retroactively. According to the dissenting opinion cited by Rama, this is not so. Even HaOzer concludes that if one changes location during a meal, even if he diverted his attention from further eating, when he returns to his meal he need not recite a new blessing over bread or over foods that come because of the meal. Foods that do not come because of the meal are different: even if he originally recited a blessing over them, if he diverted his attention from them he must recite a new blessing. See Eliyah Rabbah for his resolution of Bach’s question against Rama. Responsa Zichron Rabbi Yishmael HaKohen likewise rules like Rama, unlike Bach.
To recite a blessing. He must also wash his hands again, but without a blessing. See siman 164.
Elsewhere. Leaving some companions behind helps regarding other foods only if he returns to his original place. If he eats elsewhere, he must recite a new blessing. Magen Avraham; see there.
Because we are concerned that he may forget. According to this reason, if he is invited to eat elsewhere it is permitted, and this is the common practice. Bach, however, writes that this is permitted only when, at the time of HaMotzi, he intended to leave. Otherwise, he may not leave, for ideally he must recite the after-blessing in his place. Rama writes similarly in responsum 1. Magen Avraham. Taz discusses this at length and concludes that our custom is to eat part of the meal at home and then go to a wedding feast to complete the meal, provided that this was their intention from the outset. The same applies to travelers, who recite the after-blessing where they finish eating. If one went elsewhere without intending to eat there and then returned to his meal, then if he ate something requiring an after-blessing in its place, as described in seif 5, he need not recite a new opening blessing, and certainly need not first recite Bircas HaMazon. If he ate fruit or drank wine, went away, and returned, he must recite a new blessing before drinking again. If he diverted his attention from eating while away, he must in every case recite a new blessing over what he subsequently eats. See there and Yad Aharon. Bircas Avraham, folio 154, writes that if one recited a blessing over a fruit but did not eat it there, and carried it elsewhere, he must recite a new blessing in the second house; the first blessing was in vain.
