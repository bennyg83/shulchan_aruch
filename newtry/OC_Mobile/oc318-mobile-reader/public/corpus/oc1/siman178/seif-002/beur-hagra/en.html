Seif 2: Companions, etc. This is repeated here because of the novelty that leaving some companions behind is effective. Seif 1 taught that moving from house to house constitutes a change of location, and likewise returning to the original house, as explained above.
Some of them, etc. The Gemara’s reference to an elderly or sick person is not literal, as is clear from the second Baraisa there.
Likewise, etc. This teaches that even Rav Chisda agrees that leaving some companions behind is effective in this case as well. See what is written below in the gloss. The Mishnah’s case of moving from one corner, etc., is as Rav Chisda explains there, and Rav Chisda certainly discusses fruit, as is clear from the account there: he again sat, etc.
However, etc. This is from the Yerushalmi and is cited by Tosafos there, s.v. ella, etc.
And some disagree, etc. They derive this from Chullin 107b: the attendant recites a blessing, etc., yet the Gemara did not require an after-blessing before he left. See the beginning of siman 179 regarding one who said, etc. Rif, however, holds that this applies only to a diversion of attention, not to a change of location. Tosafos further proves it from the wording, “when they return they require,” etc. If the law were otherwise, it would be obvious. Rather, it means that ideally, before leaving, they must recite the after-blessing lest they remain away too long, as written below: nevertheless, ideally, etc. See Tosafos there, s.v. keshehen, and Chullin 86b, s.v. asur.
Specifically, etc., or, etc. This follows Rav Chisda, according to the Tosefta cited by Rashbam, Tosafos s.v. ella, Rosh, and the other poskim. Rif evidently did not have this Tosefta in his text. He writes, “specifically,” etc., and this is also Rambam’s and Shulchan Aruch’s view above, since they write, “and likewise if they were,” implying equality. Semak also rules this way, unlike Beit Yosef, who writes that with foods not requiring an after-blessing in their place there is no distinction whether some companions remained. That is incorrect, for otherwise why would the Gemara infer from the wording “they uprooted their feet” that, etc.?
Therefore, etc., for, etc. As stated in Berachos, chapter 8.
Nevertheless, if, etc. As stated in the Tosefta to Pesachim and in Chullin in the cited passage. The Tosefta says that one went a considerable distance, etc., and when he returns, etc. According to their explanation, he need recite a new blessing only over what he now wishes to eat. The meaning is that when leaving he should recite Bircas HaMazon. If he failed to do so, then upon returning he must nevertheless recite a new opening blessing, as explained in the Baraisa in Pesachim above.
There is no distinction, etc. As stated in seif 1 in the name of Ran. The same applies to foods requiring an after-blessing in their place, as newly stated in the gloss, in accordance with Rav Chisda’s language “from house to house” and the Baraisa concerning companions, etc., from which the Gemara asks, “they returned,” etc.
Nevertheless, etc. Tosafos there and in Chullin explain that this is the meaning of the Gemara’s statement, “when they leave they require,” etc.
Nevertheless, etc. As stated there concerning members of a group, etc., implying that this rule applies specifically in such a case. This is why the gloss above says, nevertheless, ideally, etc.
For example, etc. This is stated in the second Baraisa there.
