[2] Levush: In the one house, etc. This requires extensive study, for Rama writes that the two places must be in one house, and Rama rules likewise at the beginning of siman 273. Levush's words there in seif 2 likewise require study. In seif 5 there it is also evident that he holds that prior intent is effective even from one house to another. It is difficult to say that what he calls “another house” means another room, as I explained above, for he should still have stated that actual movement to another house is ineffective. Here, however, one may say that he wrote this according to the final conclusion in seif 2, that regarding foods requiring an after-blessing in their place no new blessing is required. Although we rule there that this applies even where he had no such intention when establishing the meal, nevertheless it is initially forbidden; Levush therefore teaches here that since he had this in mind, it is permitted even initially, as Bach writes. Yet all this is strained. Moreover, this seif follows those authorities who hold that even regarding bread a new blessing is required. Perhaps Levush reasons that even according to the opinion that makes no distinction between foods requiring an after-blessing in their place and other foods, all agree that where he had prior intent there is a distinction. According to this, perhaps this is also his intention below in siman 273, and he holds that for this purpose wine has the same law as bread. Yet Tosafos, the Rosh, and Ran in the chapter Arvei Pesachim, page 100, clearly imply that regarding Kiddush this cannot be said, and prior intent is entirely ineffective from one house to another. On the contrary, Kiddush should be more stringent by logic, since he has not yet eaten anything and has merely recited Kiddush. Taz was subsequently published, and I saw that in seif katan 9 he discusses this question at length but does not mention Levush's words, which is surprising.
