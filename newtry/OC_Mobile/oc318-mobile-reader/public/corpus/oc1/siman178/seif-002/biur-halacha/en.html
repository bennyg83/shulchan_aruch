(1) In one house—see in Mishna Berurah that this means one room, as Magen Avraham and other later poskim: from room to room even in one house is a change of place, as in siman 473; in seif 1 here [see Tur and Beit Yosef who plainly hold so], but if he sees his first place or had this intent at time of blessing one may be lenient as written above in seif 1; therefore if he ate fruits and beverages that do not require blessing in their place and entered in the middle another room where he does not see his first place—forbidden to eat there without blessing per all views [even per the gloss below on fruits and beverages he agrees]; and even if he returned to his place he requires blessing l'chatchila. Although this is written in several acharonim it is very difficult to be careful in practice and it appears the world does not practice so—I searched sources of the law and found this law is not clearly established; one who examines Pesachim 101 s.v. but and Rosh there will see this is only per their text in Gemara on kiddush "but from place to place" [and per them law of kiddush in meal place and change of place are the same]; but if we read "but from corner to corner" we need not say Gemara holds corner to corner and room to room are equal under one roof—and if we are strict room to room, we must be strict corner to corner [otherwise what proof from Rav Anan from roof to ground]; therefore when we are lenient corner to corner for change of place, room to room is included—only from house to house is called change for change of place; Gemara language "only from house to house"—per this text must distinguish change of place from kiddush in meal place, as Maharam Chalavah wrote [student of Rashba]: for kiddush we need actual meal place and place of bread causes—even corner to corner forbidden; but for change of place complete uprooting applies and corner to corner is not uprooting, see there. This text Rambam holds from it, etc., but corner to corner I did not find in many rishonim—explicitly Rabbenu Chananel, Bahag, Rif, and Maharam Chalavah [and one who wants to reject this proof and say at any rate we find no explicit permission in Gemara for change of place except corner to corner as Gemara concludes "but corner to corner"—I cannot answer him prohibition either, for we infer from opening language Gemara taught only house to house and room to room not in this category]. Rambam in Beit Yosef holds like Tosafot and Rosh that kiddush in meal place and change of place laws are equal—so we are strict room to room for kiddush and change of place, lenient only corner to corner in same room; but Maharam in pekudat Shabbat proves Rambam holds opposite—we distinguish kiddush in meal place from change of place in meal, and for change of place even room to room is not change—he also has Gemara text on kiddush "he holds from it" but corner to corner not, [like geonim's text] explaining this refers to room to room: corner to corner means one corner of house to another at end of house though many rooms divide in middle [proved from geonim, see there, settling Gemara proof Rav Anan roof to ground on corner to corner that intention is room to room, see there; possibly all geonim with text corner to corner hold so and sugya is plain]; therefore Rambam was lenient kiddush corner to corner—not Gemara's corner to corner; so change of place Gemara: only house to house, corner to corner permitted—also room to room called corner to corner as above [and Rambam who copied our matter change of place in Gemara language corner to corner in one house permitted—his intent also room to room since one house, and he did not mention forbidding except house to house [and precise that Rambam changed language: kiddush says corner to corner, here says corner to corner]]. We conclude: per Maharam Rambam holds room to room not change; also per Bahag, Rabbenu Chananel, Rif per their text; also Rashi plainly on change of place house to attic not change, same room to room—he has both texts but not place to place [and therefore we distinguish kiddush from change of place in meal]; Ohr Zaru'a copied his words to law, see there; therefore even though proper l'chatchila to be concerned per later poskim not to enter even room to room mid-meal for things not requiring blessing in their place unless he intended at blessing—nevertheless custom to be lenient should not be protested, for he has whom to rely as above; and b'dieved certainly no blessing required—for doubt does not extract and doubt in blessings to be lenient; and if he can see first place certainly lenient even l'chatchila as in seif 1 in Mishna Berurah.

It appears Rama's source is Tosafot and Rosh who wrote per their view in Hagahot birkat hamazon retroactively never needed per all views for change of place is only like hesech da'at requiring blessing l'chatchila; nevertheless they wrote from good advice lest he delay until hungry and lose birkat hamazon—must bless when uprooting to leave; Gra hinted source is Tosafot words—and therefore plausible to say this applies also to us who hold like Rabbenu Chananel; nevertheless possible this is only for Rav Sheshet there who requires first blessing at least by law on leaving as hesech da'at—therefore say bless birkat hamazon before leaving lest he delay since otherwise diverted mind from eating; but for one who holds no blessing l'chatchila when returning to bread meal—for him also no birkat hamazon when leaving even from good advice, not relevant to bless mid-meal as above.
