An interruption. Nevertheless, it appears to me that he must wash his hands again, because this constitutes a diversion of attention. Magen Avraham. Olas Tamid and Bircas Avraham write that this applies only if he stood and prayed in his place, but if he went to the synagogue to pray it constitutes an interruption. Eliyah Zuta writes that Levush implies that even going to the synagogue does not constitute an interruption. Taz concludes that in practice one should be careful, for prayer is considered no interruption only when they pray in the same room or when some companions remain behind. See there. Rama writes in siman 272 that moving from the house to the sukkah is considered like moving from one corner to another. Nechalas Tzvi challenges him, and Eliyah Rabbah reconciles Rama’s ruling at length. See there.
