There. “Rabbeinu Manoach and Ran.” I do not know where this is Ran's view. Ran says this only according to Rashi, under whose view a change of place also requires Bircas HaMazon. Thus, even if one left without reciting Bircas HaMazon and then returned, he must first recite Bircas HaMazon. This accords with Ramban in Milchamos. According to Rabbeinu Chananel, however, he certainly need not recite Bircas HaMazon, for Rabbeinu Chananel does not regard the departure as a change of place at all. An explicit Baraisa states: Companions who were reclining and uprooted themselves to go to the synagogue, when they leave they do not require a retroactive blessing. Thus even initially no blessing is required, and the authorities who follow Rabbeinu Chananel rule in accordance with this first Tanna. If one were to say that the first Tanna speaks of a time-sensitive mitzvah, why does the first Baraisa teach that when they went out to greet a groom, which is likewise a mitzvah, they require a retroactive blessing upon leaving? Necessarily, that case is different because that Baraisa regards it as a change of place. Since the meal has already ended and whatever they later eat constitutes another meal, they must recite Bircas HaMazon immediately upon leaving lest they forget. For this reason, even if they left and returned, they must first recite Bircas HaMazon, since they have already become obligated in it. See Ramban and you will find this explained. According to our ruling like Rabbeinu Chananel, however, the meal has not ended, and what they later eat completes the same meal. They therefore need not recite Bircas HaMazon. Examine this carefully.
