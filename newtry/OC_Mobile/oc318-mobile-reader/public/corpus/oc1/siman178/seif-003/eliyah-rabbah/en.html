[3] And he must recite a blessing, etc. This is because he cannot see his original place. Magen Avraham. This requires study, for Beit Yosef implies that even if he can see it, the law is lenient because the intervening area does not constitute a separation; he does not say that the reason is that he cannot see it. Raavad therefore explains that here the case is one in which he had no prior intention, whereas in seif 5, concerning moving from one tree to another, he did have such an intention. In any event, it appears to me that here he must recite a new blessing even if the place is enclosed by a partition. Examine this carefully, and see Bircas Avraham, page 47.
