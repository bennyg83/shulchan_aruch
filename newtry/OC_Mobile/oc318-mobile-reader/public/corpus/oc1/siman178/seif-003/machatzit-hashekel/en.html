(s.k. 3) And so, etc.—all agree, etc., in Pesachim 101a tanna teaches change of place requires blessing; Rav Chisda: we taught only things not requiring blessing after them in their place, but things requiring blessing after in their place—no blessing needed. Why? He returns to first establishment. Rav Sheshet: both this and that require blessing; Rif and Rambam ruled like Rav Sheshet that even things requiring blessing after in their place nevertheless require blessing. On this Kesef Mishneh challenged: since tanna teaches they were sitting to eat—implies bread—still requires blessing though per Rabbenu Chananel on bread requires blessing in its place (as seif 5)—nevertheless we hold like Rav Sheshet.

Therefore the end section on reclining to drink and fruits—even per Rabbenu Chananel must bless, for they need no blessing in their place (although Rambam holds all on which one blesses the equivalent of three need blessing in their place—and one can say drinking wine and fruits means of seven species, all the more so bread which all poskim hold requires blessing after in its place).

Do not say the first section's eating discusses, etc.—it teaches us the end section wine and fruits means even of seven species; and Rambam per his view holds they need blessing after in their place, nevertheless when he uproots he must bless, like Rav Sheshet, see there.

In my humble opinion, etc.—even for drinking and fruits meaning not of seven species, for which universally no blessing after in their place—nevertheless leaving some friends helps, unlike Bach, as said below.

And as in Hagahot Rama—not like Bach who disputes Rama; Rama's hagahot derive from Hagahot Semak and per his view needed different explanation than Rama understood; in my opinion Semak's words: group reclining, uprooted feet to go when left some friends—no blessing retroactively for birkat hamazon nor hamotzi; Hagahot Semak: even things not requiring blessing after in their place; for things requiring blessing even without leaving, change of place need not bless before; Rabbi on this: language not precise—Semak means no retroactive birkat hamazon/hamotzi implying bread, then Hagahot Semak on things not requiring blessing after in their place. Bach added difficulty: how say for things requiring even without leaving some friends—beraita explains only when left; Magen Avraham resolves: Pesachim challenges Rav Chisda—group drinking, left for groom—when leaving no retroactive blessing, when returning no l'chatchila blessing only when left some friends there; if did not leave, when leaving require retroactive, when returning l'chatchila; from "and uprooted" implies discusses things requiring blessing in place; reason of leaving some friends; if did not leave—require blessing; difficulty on Rabbenu Chananel—Gemara answers beraita is R' Yosi, Rabbenu Chananel like Rabbanan. Per Bach who holds reasoning: things not requiring blessing in place—leaving some does not help—why Semak says it helps? Therefore discusses bread, rules like Rav Sheshet not Rabbenu Chananel—difficulty on Hagahot Semak on things requiring in place without leaving; beraita discusses bread from "uprooted" yet requires leaving some—Rav Sheshet; Magen Avraham: Hagahot Semak like Rabbenu Chananel, also inside Semak rules like Rabbenu Chananel on bread and all requiring blessing after in place—no need leaving some; only for things not requiring blessing in place Rabbenu Chananel agrees change of place is interruption as beraita—still if left some even for things not requiring blessing in place must bless; resolves Rabbi's difficulty—Semak discusses things requiring in place mentioning birkat hamazon/hamotzi, still states leaving some for comprehensive rule on things not requiring in place; for things requiring in place truly no need to leave—rules Rabbenu Chananel; language "even" in Hagahot Semak; resolves Rabbi; no Bach difficulty from beraita—beraita per R' Yosi like Rav Sheshet; Rabbenu Chananel like Rabbanan—things requiring in place no need leaving; Semak and hagahot rule Rabbenu Chananel, still Rabbenu Chananel agrees for things not requiring in place leaving helps.

For Rabbenu Chananel there is no distinction—meaning for things requiring blessing in their place even without leaving some friends no blessing needed; for things not requiring blessing in their place leaving some does not help, whether Rabbenu Chananel or Rav Sheshet.

From teaching "and uprooted"—implies discusses things requiring blessing in place (Ran strained in explanation)—not precise inference from "uprooted" alone; rather as if he said from "uprooted" and inferred from end "when they leave require retroactive blessing"—if discusses things requiring in place, should immediately before leaving require birkat hamazon forbidden to uproot until bless in place; but if discusses things not requiring in place—why retroactive blessing? uprooting place is not like diverting mind requiring only blessing before what he will eat, not after what he ate—per disputing view Rama seif 2 and Ramban, see there.

Nevertheless also Rav Sheshet has distinction—for things not requiring blessing in place leaving some friends helps; therefore if not for inferring from "uprooted" no question on Rabbenu Chananel that beraita distinguishing leaving or not discusses things not requiring in place; beraita change of place requires blessing—Rabbenu Chananel establishes for things not requiring in place without leaving; therefore infers from "uprooted" discusses things requiring in place, yet requires specifically leaving some—for Rabbenu Chananel who says things requiring in place no blessing needed only when left some friends; amora must explain his words; main point: when left, Talmud holds no distinction between requiring blessing in place or not—even for things not requiring in place leaving some friends helps, unlike Bach; Rabbenu Chananel distinguishes.
