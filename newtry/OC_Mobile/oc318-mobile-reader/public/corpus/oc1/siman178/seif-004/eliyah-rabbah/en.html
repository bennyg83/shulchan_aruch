(4) Or even if he did not leave, etc.—implies even for things not requiring blessing in their place, if he left some friends no blessing needed; so Rama in name Semak and Ohr Zaru'a. But Bach lengthy dispute on Rama: universally for things not requiring blessing no distinction whether left some friends; Semak's words explained in another matter, see there at length; so Taz and Magen Avraham agreed to Rama and strained Semak's words. In my humble opinion to resolve comfortably and uphold Rama: for per Bach's explanation I wonder—for Bach wrote Semak ruled like Rif no distinction between things requiring blessing—yet Semak explicitly wrote and brought his own language: ate bread in one place, returned and ate bread in another place must return and bless birkat hamazon, need not bless in second place before eating; but wine and fruits must bless before—appears he literally holds like Rosh's view and there are those who say distinction between things requiring blessing. From this also Rama's explanation is correct: otherwise difficulty since he distinguishes things requiring blessing—why write law of leaving some friends, unless to teach even for things not requiring, if left some, no blessing needed—as Nachalat Tzvi and deduced from Talmud, see there. So appears Semag who also wrote law of leaving some friends though he ruled like Rosh; Bach seif 2 wondered on Semag—in my opinion as I explained. Also Rama wrote in name Ohr Zaru'a and Bach did not mention Ohr Zaru'a but wrote all hold no distinction. Also in Zichron leGraon R' Yitzchak HaKohen chapter Keitzad Mevarkhin implies like Rama; so I found explicitly in Birkat Avraham 73, 73; also proof from Pesachim 102 who asks on beraita leaving some friends: who taught uprooting—R' Yehuda; difficulty what language uprooting; Rashi and Rashbam strained who taught this beraita that even though things requiring blessing need blessing, R' Yehuda is author until here—and did not help, still difficult why word uprooting. Therefore appears he hints beraita entirely is not only R' Yehuda but comes even for Rabbanan—namely for things not requiring blessing in their place; but for what beraita taught language uprooting implying things requiring as Rashi explained before—that is R' Yehuda—requires study. From this: even things not requiring no blessing when left some friends; also implied from Shulchan Aruch copied from Rambam ch. 4 Laws of Blessings on distinction if left some there, and if reclining to drink or eat fruit—implies distinction leaving some applies also to fruit—universally no blessing in their place. Now Kesef Mishneh's difficulties on Rambam for needing to write "and if they were reclining" are settled—requires study. Also say we learn even things not requiring need blessing retroactively, unlike Ran Pesachim that retroactive blessing not needed per all; Kesef Mishneh answers earlier eating I explained from things not requiring, and comes to teach same for wine drinking and eating fruits of seven species requiring blessing after in their place, until here. I wonder: earlier eating surely things requiring blessing in their place—from "he established his meal" mentioned in Talmud and all poskim on bread; also "returns and blesses hamotzi first"—he sits at bread meal; Rambam did not mention drinking wine also fruits of seven species; leaving some helps when he returned to his place, but when eating elsewhere must bless as yachid. Magen Avraham siman 210: if ate less than satiating amount, went outside and returned—must bless again at start even on bread for no birkat acharonah; if returned and ate less than satiating amount—combine for birkat acharonah, until here—specifically when he did not wait kevedei achilat pras.
