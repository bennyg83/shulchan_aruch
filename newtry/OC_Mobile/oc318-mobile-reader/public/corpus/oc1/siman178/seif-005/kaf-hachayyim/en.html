5) There, gloss: Provided that both places are in one house. Bach writes that the gloss states this only according to the Rambam, who rules like Rav Sheshes that upon changing location one must recite both a retroactive blessing and a new initial blessing, even for foods that require a blessing in the place where they were eaten. According to Rav Chisda, however, for foods that require a blessing in their place, no blessing at all is required upon changing location, neither retroactively nor initially. Although one certainly should not initially leave his first location in order to eat elsewhere, as Kol Bo writes, nevertheless, if when he initially established himself to eat in the first location he intended to eat elsewhere, he may initially go elsewhere and finish his meal, even in two or more houses. Bach writes that this is the practice; see there. Levush, Olas Tamid, paragraph 1, and Taz, paragraphs 7 and 9, write the same. See below, paragraphs 8 and 23.
