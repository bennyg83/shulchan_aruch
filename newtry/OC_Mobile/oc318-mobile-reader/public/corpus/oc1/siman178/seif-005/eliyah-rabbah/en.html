[5] Lest he forget, etc. According to this, if during the meal he is invited to eat in another place, it is permitted. Magen Avraham writes that this is the common practice. Bach, however, writes that since at the time of HaMotzi he did not intend to eat elsewhere, it is forbidden, because initially one must recite the after-blessing in the place of the meal. Ran and Responsa Rama, siman 11, write likewise. One may further argue that even if he is invited to eat elsewhere, there remains a concern that something may occur and he will not eat there, as stated in Responsa Rama. It also appears to me from siman 184 that he need not recite the after-blessing in his first established place, but rather in the place where he eats the second time. Bircas Avraham, page 154, writes that if one recited a blessing over a fruit but did not eat it there, and then carried it to another place, he must recite the blessing again in the second house, while the blessing recited in the first place was in vain. This implies that the law applies even when there was no lapse of time.
