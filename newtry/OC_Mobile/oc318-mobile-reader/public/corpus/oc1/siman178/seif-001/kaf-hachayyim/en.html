1) [Seif 1] Since he changed his location, he must recite a blessing retroactively, etc. This means that initially he must recite Birkas HaMazon before leaving, as stated in seif 2. If he did not recite it before leaving, he must return to his place and recite Birkas HaMazon, and afterward he may eat wherever he wishes, provided that he first recites HaMotzi. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Taz, paragraph 2, writes the same. This follows Maran's words, of blessed memory. According to Rema, of blessed memory, however, in the gloss to seif 2 below, if he ate foods that require a blessing in the place where they were eaten, he need not recite a new blessing even over what he subsequently wishes to eat; see there. The later authorities agree with Rema, of blessed memory, because in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently, as stated below in paragraph 14; see there. Some nevertheless practice taking a piece of bread in their hand when they go elsewhere during the meal or go to speak with friends, so that they do not divert their attention from eating bread and also do not divert their attention from the washing, which would require them to wash again, as stated above in siman 170, paragraph 13; see there. See below, paragraph 20.
2) There: Although he changed his location from one corner to another, etc. This means specifically from one corner to another within a single room, but when moving from one room to another he must recite a blessing. Olas Tamid, paragraph 1; this is also implied by Beis Yosef. Levush, Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1, write the same. This has practical relevance for us regarding foods that do not require a blessing in the place where they were eaten, for he must recite a blessing over what he wishes to eat afterward, as stated below in the gloss to seif 2; see there.
3) There: From one corner to another, etc. Even if it is a large hall, for this law we consider the entire building as the place where he ate, and he need not recite a blessing. Even if, when he initially recited the blessing, he did not intend to move to another corner, he nevertheless need not recite a new blessing. Chayei Adam, rule 59, paragraph 4; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1.
4) There, gloss: If he intended, etc. The same applies when he can see his original place; this is not considered a change of location, as stated at the beginning of siman 273. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3, writes that even if he sees it through a window, or sees only part of his original place, this is not considered a change of location. Machatzis HaShekel, paragraph 2, Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1, and Chayei Adam there, paragraph 6, write the same. Even HaOzer, however, disagrees and writes that whenever one goes to another room, even if he can see his original place, it is considered a change of location, both with respect to the requirement to return to his original place and recite Birkas HaMazon there, and with respect to reciting a new blessing over foods that do not require a blessing in the place where they were eaten; see there. Zechor LeAvraham, under samech, and Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1, cite this. It appears that initially one should be careful, but after the fact one may rely upon Magen Avraham because of the rule that in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently.
5) There, gloss: Provided that both places are in one house. Bach writes that the gloss states this only according to the Rambam, who rules like Rav Sheshes that upon changing location one must recite both a retroactive blessing and a new initial blessing, even for foods that require a blessing in the place where they were eaten. According to Rav Chisda, however, for foods that require a blessing in their place, no blessing at all is required upon changing location, neither retroactively nor initially. Although one certainly should not initially leave his first location in order to eat elsewhere, as Kol Bo writes, nevertheless, if when he initially established himself to eat in the first location he intended to eat elsewhere, he may initially go elsewhere and finish his meal, even in two or more houses. Bach writes that this is the practice; see there. Levush, Olas Tamid, paragraph 1, and Taz, paragraphs 7 and 9, write the same. See below, paragraphs 8 and 23.
