Shulchan Aruch, seif 1: He was eating. Both seifim reproduce the language of the Rambam. The first seif appears superfluous, since it is included in the second seif, which states that moving to another house requires a new blessing, whereas moving from one corner to another does not. It may be answered that the second seif proves only that when they leave, they must recite the concluding blessing retroactively. It does not clarify the law where they left without reciting that blessing and then returned, namely, whether a new initial blessing is required. The first seif was therefore necessary. Although the Rambam wrote the first seif, he still needed the second in order to teach the law where some members of the group remained behind. The first seif concerns an individual eating alone, where no distinction can be made between whether some remained and whether none remained. The Rambam did not combine all these laws into one brief clause because the laws in the two seifim are taken from different baraitot, and the Rambam generally preserves the wording taught in each baraita. All this is explained in Beit Yosef.
