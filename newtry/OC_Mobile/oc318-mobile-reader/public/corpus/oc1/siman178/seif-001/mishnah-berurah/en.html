Since this siman has many details, I precede with a short introduction to ease the reader. Regarding change of place [one began eating, went elsewhere to eat or return to first place to eat there]—we hold he must return and bless; three matters to explain: (a) what is change of place—house to house or room to room; (b) which foods the law applies to; (c) if he changed place, what is the law. Change of place: if mid-meal he went from this house to another—certainly change of place; even if he only left outside his doorway mid-meal—included in change of place, does not help even if he intended at blessing to change place; even room to room—many poskim hold it is change of place [see Biur Halacha]; distinction: if at blessing he intended to change room to room—permitted since under one roof; corner to corner even in large hall—not change of place at all since one room, need no intent. Which foods: dispute in Gemara Pesachim 101b—Rav Sheshet: bread, fruits, beverages—same law, must return and bless; Rav Chisda: only if ate something not requiring blessing in its place, e.g. fruits and beverages not obligated to bless their birkat acharonah in first place—immediately upon uprooting his establishment is voided, must return and bless when he wants to eat more; but if ate bread [some say all seven species] requiring blessing in its place—even if uprooted, first establishment not voided; wherever he eats on first establishment it is as if sitting in his place, need not bless hamotzi again. Poskim divided: Rambam et al. like Rav Sheshet—all cases must bless—basis of first two seifim; Rosh et al. like Rav Chisda—Rama's hagahot at end seif 2. All this to return and bless; l'chatchila may not uproot per all views. If changed place—Rambam et al.: when he returns first must bless birkat hamazon [or birkat acharonah if ate something requiring it] on food eaten before, then bless first blessing on what he wants now—seif 1; other poskim: only first blessing on what he will eat, birkat acharonah fulfilled by blessing after second eating covering both—beginning of hagahot end seif 2. In practice we follow poskim in hagahot; further details in siman. Now we explain the seifim.
