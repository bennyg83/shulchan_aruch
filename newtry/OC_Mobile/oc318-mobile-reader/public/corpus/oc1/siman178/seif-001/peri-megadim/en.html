(1) Taz brought the sugya Pesachim 101b and his holy words need explanation. (a) That Rav Chisda and Rav Sheshet argue in two matters—Rosh and Tosafot say not; rather Rabbenu Chananel and Rav Sheshet hold uprooting, even when there is interruption, does not diminish from hesech da'at; the general principle is hesech da'at obligates a first blessing, not a final blessing retroactively; and two beraitot bless retroactively—good advice, or l'chatchila one must bless in their place before uprooting (Magen Avraham 8, and so Ran Pesachim there: uprooted must bless in their place l'chatchila before uprooting, for things requiring blessing in their place, as we will say below, IY"H). Rabbenu Chananel and Rav Sheshet argue only in one matter—things requiring blessing in their place: Rabbenu Chananel—no first blessing on change of place; Rav Sheshet—requires; but a final blessing retroactively, if he transgressed, went out, and returned—all agree unnecessary, both Rabbenu Chananel and Rav Sheshet; so too Rosh and Tosafot Pesachim and Chullin daf 86, see there. But Rambam—one depends on the other: for Rav Sheshet, things requiring blessing in their place—blessing and change of place when returning require retroactive blessing, and consequently a first blessing on what he will eat, for it diminishes from hesech da'at, since change of place is uprooting and removal, and also diminishes from "and we will bless" siman 179 seif 1. For Rabbenu Chananel, since it requires blessing in its place, it is not removal, and he returns to the first establishment—no retroactive blessing when returning, and likewise no first blessing on what he will eat; for him it is possible they are two matters. (b) Also what Rif and Rambam obligate for things requiring blessing retroactively—one studying Derisha wrote explicitly that Rif has no obligation of a final blessing, only Rambam. In truth, studying Rif and Ran Pesachim 101b, it is clear as Derisha, who explained "uprooted" and taught (per Rashi and Rashbam: if they did not uproot they must wait and bless in their place—so it discusses things requiring blessing in their place, and if uprooted quickly they bless when leaving while walking, see there). But Rif ruled there that bread and grain species specifically require blessing in their place (see Shaarei Teshuvah)—if so, "uprooted" cannot prove, for this language was taught—deduce only for things not requiring blessing in their place: why a retroactive blessing? See there—explicitly Ran like Rosh here, and it further proves Rif did not bring this tosefta that Rambam brought ch. 4 halacha 3 hagah (halacha 3 tosefta: returned requires retroactive blessing—how is this the law of Rav Sheshet; halacha 5 fruits and beverages—all agree—will be explained IY"H note 3)—so for Rif, when they leave they must bless retroactively, good advice, etc.; if he did not bless and returned, he blesses a first blessing and birkat hamazon covers both. The Mechaber does not hold so, for otherwise he would have ruled Rif and Rosh at least that halacha is like Rav Sheshet on a first blessing, but retroactively unnecessary—proves he holds Rif like Rambam. The beraita of Tosefekhta: friends reclining to drink wine, uprooted—when they returned must bless—is not like Ran caused for Rif and Tosefekhta R' Yochanan; rather Rif does not have this Tosefekhta at all, or he has it without "wine" per his view (bread and grain species require blessing in their place), and it discusses uprooting for things requiring blessing in their place, as appears from Rambam's flow there; nevertheless he ruled like Rav Sheshet. Now Rif and Rambam stand in one view, as we said. Beit Yosef finds it reasonable that Rif and Rambam stand in one view, as the explanations imply—this is what we wanted to explain; IY"H below and in Magen Avraham I will explain further. See note 3.
