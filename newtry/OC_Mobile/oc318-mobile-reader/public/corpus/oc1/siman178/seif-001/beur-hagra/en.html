Seif 1: If one was, etc. There, the law of changing location, etc., applies not only within a house, etc., in accordance with Rav Sheshes, for Rav Chisda was refuted. Although Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak answered the objections, we do not rely on that resolution. Rif. Although Rosh questions him and writes that the Gemara contains no conclusive refutation, since Rabbi Yehudah holds this view and the first Tanna disagrees, Rabbi Yehudah is a lone opinion. Kesef Mishneh writes that Rabbi Yehudah and Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi disagree in Eruvin, chapter 7, and Sanhedrin, chapter 3, whether the phrase “when does this apply” comes to explain or to distinguish. We rule everywhere like Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, as Tosafos writes at the beginning of Megillah, chapter 4, and the other poskim likewise rule. Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak’s resolution is therefore rejected.
Or if he was, etc. This is stated in the Tosefta and cited by Tosafos: if he went a considerable distance, etc. Ran explains there that a change of location includes both going elsewhere without returning and going elsewhere and then returning to the original place, as indicated by the statement there concerning members of a group.
To the entrance of his house, etc. Meaning, from the entrance of his house, and so is the reading in Kesef Mishneh. This is considered like moving from one house to another, as above, even if he returns, as stated in seif 2.
However, etc. As stated in seif 2 in the Mishnah and Gemara there, according to the reading of Rif and Rosh: but from one corner, etc. See Tosafos there, end of s.v. ella, and s.v. keshehen, citing the Tosefta in Berachos: the homeowner, etc., went a considerable distance, etc. According to Rambam, “went a considerable distance” means once he exited the entrance of his house.
Even though he changed, etc. There: but from one corner, etc. The halachah follows Rabbi Shimon. Although Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak explained, “Who is the Tanna,” etc., that answer was only according to Rav Chisda, who holds that the phrase “when does this apply” comes to distinguish. Rabbi Shimon follows Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, who holds that it comes to explain, as is his approach in Sanhedrin 24b, and this is the halachah.
See below, etc., if, etc. Meaning, as with Kiddush, for the law of changing location is the same in both matters. See Tosafos there 101a, s.v. aval mimakom, and Rosh there, siman 5, regarding moving from one corner, etc. Our text reads, etc., unlike Ran.
