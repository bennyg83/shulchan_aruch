He was eating in this house. To clarify the law we mention part of the sugya and dispute among poskim. In ch. "Ayin Peh" Rav Chisda sat and said: what we said—change of place requires blessing—we said only for things not requiring blessing after in their place; Rashbam explains, e.g. fruits—for when he stood and went elsewhere, this standing is end of his meal and that is another meal and must bless again; but things requiring blessing after in their place, e.g. bread, need not bless—why? He returns to first establishment, eating per mind of first meal. Rav Sheshet said: both this and that require blessing. They challenged: group members reclining to drink, uprooted feet to go for a groom—when leaving need no retroactive blessing; when returning need no l'chatchila blessing; stated only when they left an elder or sick person there (Tur: some friends); if they did not leave, when leaving require retroactive blessing, when returning require l'chatchila blessing—from teaching "uprooted" we infer it discusses things requiring blessing after in their place, yet if they did not leave an elder they require blessing—difficulty on Rabbenu Chananel. Ran in Beit Yosef: who is the tanna of uprooting? R' Yosi, as taught: friends reclining uprooted feet to go to synagogue—when leaving not required, etc.; R' Yosi stated only when they left, etc.—taught like Rabbenu Chananel. Before us they argue in two matters: Rabbenu Chananel on bread—no retroactive blessing, etc.; Rav Sheshet requires; further Rav Sheshet distinguishes bread between left some or not, Rabbenu Chananel no distinction except bread exempt even without leaving, fruits liable even with leaving—so Beit Yosef for Rabbenu Chananel, and Tosefekhta Rabbenu Chananel did not mention leaving at all. Rav Sheshet who distinguishes left or not—plain this is bread only; fruits where blessing in place not relevant—even if left must bless—as from beraita R' Yosi who taught uprooted, implying bread, and R' Yosi distinguishes left or not; fruits no distinction. Dispute between Rif and Rambam versus Tosafot and Rosh in two matters: first Rif and Rambam ruled like Rav Sheshet and do not have this Tosefekhta of Rabbenu Chananel above; Tosafot and Rosh like Rabbenu Chananel. Second: Rif and Rambam where they say require retroactive blessing—obligated in the thing; Tosafot and Rosh not obligated retroactively but must bless on what he will eat later—for birkat hamazon properly covers even first eating; what they said require retroactive blessing means good advice lest he delay until hungry and not go out then in birkat hamazon on one eating—thus explained views Mechaber and Rama bring: Shulchan Aruch in seif 1, and second is Rambam's view who rules like Rav Sheshet.
