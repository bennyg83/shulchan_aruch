And at their bases, where they are attached to the tree, etc. The same applies if they are not ten handbreadths high where they are attached to the tree, but reach a height of ten handbreadths in the middle because they form an arch. It appears to me that where they reach a height of ten handbreadths, the area must be four handbreadths wide, for otherwise it would be a karmelis. Magen Avraham, subsection 2. This means that the hollow space within them must be ten handbreadths high and four handbreadths wide, as required for a private domain. The areas at the sides are then also called holes of a private domain. If, however, there is no hollow space ten handbreadths high and four handbreadths wide, but the four-handbreadth-wide area is lower than ten handbreadths, it is a karmelis. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 2, explains the Magen Avraham's intent in this manner. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2, writes likewise. This resolves the Ma'amar Mordechai's question concerning the Magen Avraham's words.
