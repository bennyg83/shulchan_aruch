[8] [Levush] And people, etc. This includes even male and female servants, as stated by Rabbi Yonatan, and likewise minors.
[9] Less than three, etc. The Bach asks that an ordinary person is one amah wide, so the standing portion would exceed the gaps even if more than three handbreadths separate the people. The Magen Avraham answers that the legs are not three handbreadths wide and the body is not a full amah throughout. Only the seated width is an amah, and a person does not sit within an exact amah by amah.
[10] Provided that they did not know, etc. The Beit Yosef, in the name of Rabbi Yonatan, says the concern is where the partition was made intentionally. The Taz asks that Rav Chisda, who caused the arrangement, should likewise be considered intentional. He did not examine Rabbi Yonatan himself, who answers that the fact the matter came about through Rav Chisda does not harm us, because he did not personally arrange the people. They gathered on their own to watch him or console him. Nevertheless, the primary reason for prohibiting when they know appears to me to be that of Orchot Chaim in the name of the Rashba: if they know the purpose, they will become lenient and degrade Shabbos restrictions. So too it appears from Avodat HaKodesh. According to this, even in a circumstance like seif 3, where an intentional partition may otherwise be permitted, it may be prohibited here.
[11] And he should not make, etc. This implies that another person is the one arranging them into a partition.
[12] He should not stand, etc. It appears to me that the Rambam understands the Gemara's question in Eruvin 44, 'But the incident of Rabbi Nechemiah was with knowledge,' to mean Rabbi Nechemiah himself knew the purpose. The Rashba, who permits in that situation, follows Rashi and explains that the people forming the partition knew. This is the implication of the authorities and of siman 630, seif 12. This is the primary view. The Magen Avraham did not address this distinction.
