(30) With vessels and saddles — even though they will be removed after Shabbat:

(31) Such as ropes — stretched on stakes in ground:

(32) Up to se'atayim — not more even if enclosed for dwelling and use since weak partition of woof without warp or warp without woof — see above siman 340 Biur Halacha:

(33) Even for individual in settlement — even in town he could make better partition — we are lenient up to beit se'ah; all the more in field:

(34) Provided not between rope, etc. — thereby one may make ten tefachim partition with four ropes; with three ropes if thickness holds more than tefach — three ropes such as lower less than three near ground, same to second, same to third — combining thickness makes ten [Shulchan Aruch]:

(35) And between reed, etc. — we do not say here breach greater than standing — lavud is like closed:

(36) Provided bound — it binds itself and places it — then permitted to carry thereby on Shabbat; reason: binding required so they not flee; also without binding gap between animal leg hollow and ground; when bound it lies on body on ground — deals when ten tefachim height even lying:

(37) Within less than three — see Biur Halacha:

(38) Considered partition and between them reshut ha-yachid — such as enclosed from four directions and complete partition effective even for reshus ha-rabbim; see below seif 7 gloss; same such partition is interruption for speaking words of Torah opposite unclean place [later authorities]:

(39) And this is when they did not know, etc. — although no building-on-Shabbat concern, forbidden when they know lest they lighten Shabbat prohibition; even b'dieved forbidden — like partition made intentionally seif 3:

(40) He should not make again — if another may make partition from them — doubt lest they notice [Magen Avraham in Beit Yosef]; Eshel Avraham is lenient:

(41) Partition again — even another Shabbat — since likely they will notice it is like standing initially knowingly; unlike seif 6 where initially no concern they would know; even first time if he lacked people and stood them in one place then said go while he walks between — forbidden lest they notice; what we say even when walking permitted — when they walk on their own or large crowd walking and his walking between is not noticeable:
