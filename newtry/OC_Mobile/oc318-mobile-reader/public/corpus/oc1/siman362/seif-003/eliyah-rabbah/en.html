[4] But if there was there, etc. This is the Tur's wording. Tosafot and the Rosh, however, permit only when there are mats between the areas, because without them the whole area is already a private domain due to the mats. Although the Beit Yosef writes that the Rosh is to be explained like the Ran, that interpretation does not appear correct. The Rosh writes that the two groups made an eruv, and these partitions serve to remove the prohibition created by separate residents, not to create a private domain. Therefore Malbushei Yom Tov concludes that one should rule only like Tosafot and the plain meaning of the Rosh. The Bach, Nachalat Tzvi, and Magen Avraham agree, and the Magen Avraham adds that even if the partition fell on a weekday and was deliberately rebuilt on Shabbos, carrying remains prohibited.
[5] And they made an eruv together, etc. The Taz questions the Bach's emendation changing 'public domain' to 'private domain,' asking that if so, what is the meaning of 'and they made an eruv together'? In my humble opinion, the wording can be preserved by reading the connective as 'or': 'and they made an eruv' means 'or they made an eruv.' Examine carefully.
[6] [Levush] And not for temporary dwellings, etc. It is clear that even beneath it carrying is not permitted.
[7] [Levush] So that they do not move, etc. This is because animals move without intention. A person, however, need not be tied, as stated by the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 17 of Hilchot Shabbos. The reason is apparently that although a person moves, since he moves only intentionally the partition is considered capable of standing. This is unlike the Magen Avraham, who discussed the matter at length unnecessarily. It appears to me that in siman 363, seif 3, where a person requires tying, the purpose is only to prevent him from lying down and thereby becoming less than ten handbreadths high.
