A partition that stands on its own — meaning one not made for the purpose of being a partition — is valid. A partition made on Shabbat is valid, and this applies when it was made unintentionally (shogeg), but if done intentionally (meizid) it is a partition only to be stringent — to make one liable for throwing from a public domain into it — but not to permit carrying within it. This applies when there was no partition there previously; but if there was one, it was removed, and then re-formed, even intentionally, it reverts to its original permitted status. For example, two or three people who surrounded themselves with mats in a public domain and also divided between themselves with mats and made an eruv together — they are permitted to carry from one area to the other. If the mats were rolled up, it became forbidden; if they were spread out again, even intentionally, they revert to their original permitted status.
