1) [Se'if 1] Any partition that was not made for residing within it. This means for continual entry and departure. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1. See above, at the beginning of siman 358, and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
2) There. But rather for privacy. For example, the partition of builders, who make a partition around themselves on every side when they build in the sun. Beis Yosef; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there.
3) There. In order to guard the fields. It was not made for one to reside there by day and night. Magen Avraham, subsection 1. If one does reside there by day and night, Magen Avraham concludes above, at the beginning of siman 358, subsection 4, that according to both Tosafos and Hagahos Ashri it is called enclosed for residential use. Machatzis HaShekel. Eshel Avraham, subsection 1, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1, write likewise. We also wrote this above in siman 358, subsections 5 and 26. See there.
4) There. In order to guard the fields. The same applies to a portico built within an orchard. The use of this partition is likewise not fixed for lodging in its shade on even one full day and night, but only when the sun is intense. A partition made for one full day and night, however, constitutes a proper residence. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there.
5) There. It is a partition with regard to carrying, etc. Although all these are complete partitions under Torah law, and one who throws an object into them from a public domain is liable even if the enclosure extends for several mil, nevertheless, by Rabbinic law one may carry within them only four amos if they contain more than beis sasayim, because they are not considered enclosed for residential use. If they contain no more than beis sasayim, including exactly beis sasayim, it is permitted to carry throughout the enclosed area. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there.
6) There. Therefore, a tree whose branches descend downward. This means on every side around it, when they are less than three handbreadths above the ground, for we apply the principle of lavud. Rashi; Beis Yosef.
7) There. And at their bases, where they are attached to the tree, etc. The same applies if they are not ten handbreadths high where they are attached to the tree, but reach a height of ten handbreadths in the middle because they form an arch. It appears to me that where they reach a height of ten handbreadths, the area must be four handbreadths wide, for otherwise it would be a karmelis. Magen Avraham, subsection 2. This means that the hollow space within them must be ten handbreadths high and four handbreadths wide, as required for a private domain. The areas at the sides are then also called holes of a private domain. If, however, there is no hollow space ten handbreadths high and four handbreadths wide, but the four-handbreadth-wide area is lower than ten handbreadths, it is a karmelis. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 2, explains the Magen Avraham's intent in this manner. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2, writes likewise. This resolves the Ma'amar Mordechai's question concerning the Magen Avraham's words.
8) Tosefes Shabbos writes there in the name of Tosafos that we apply the principle of lavud only to something that constitutes a domain, meaning that it is ten handbreadths high and four handbreadths wide. Consequently, when there is no hollow space ten handbreadths high and four handbreadths wide, if its branches do not reach the ground it is not a karmelis, even if they reach within three handbreadths of the ground.
9) Tosefes Shabbos further writes that it has already been explained many times that with the measure of a handbreadth we proceed stringently. At times we measure with compressed handbreadths and at times with expansive handbreadths, always in the direction of stringency. It follows that the three-handbreadth distance from the branches to the ground is measured with compressed handbreadths, while the ten-handbreadth height from their bases to the ground is measured with expansive handbreadths. See below, siman 363, se'if 26.
10) There. And it is permitted to carry throughout it. This is because it is comparable to the holes of a private domain, whose status is that of a private domain, as stated in siman 345, se'if 4.
11) There. Provided that one fills the open space, etc. This is so that it is recognizable as a partition. One must also tie the branches so that the wind does not move them. Levush. If they are rigid, this is unnecessary except where there is a gap of more than three handbreadths between the branches, in which case one must fence the gaps. Eshel Avraham, subsection 3.
12) There. For any partition that cannot withstand, etc. The same applies to a lechi or to the posts of a tzuras hapesach. Hagahos Ashri, chapter 1 of Eruvin; Darkei Moshe, subsection 1.
