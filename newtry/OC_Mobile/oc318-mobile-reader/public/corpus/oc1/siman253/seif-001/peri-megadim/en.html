According to Rif and Rambam in chapter 3 of Hilchos Shabbos, the Mishnah addresses leaving food on the stove, and even leaving it there is permitted only under the conditions explained there. The rules governing leaving food and returning it on Shabbos are therefore identical. Nevertheless, placing a pot beside the stove is permitted for leaving food there, but forbidden for returning it, as Magen Avraham writes in se'if katan 5 and 6, because the Gemara's question there concerned leaving food. According to Rashi and Ri, the author of Tosafos, the Mishnah teaches the law of returning food, and the question concerning placing a pot beside the stove likewise concerns returning it. Leaving food, however, is permitted in every circumstance, even when it has reached only ma'achal ben Derusai or when further reduction improves it, as explained by Tosafos in the chapter Kirah. The only prohibition applies where it has not yet reached ma'achal ben Derusai. The Mechaber's wording, which cites a view permitting food that has reached ma'achal ben Derusai or is fully cooked and improves through further reduction, would at first appear to follow Baal HaMaor. According to him, food that has reached ma'achal ben Derusai and continues to improve is forbidden, and permission applies only when it has reached ma'achal ben Derusai and further reduction is detrimental, or when it is fully cooked even though further reduction improves it. This is difficult, however, because the Mechaber then writes that the prohibition applies only where it has not reached ma'achal ben Derusai, even though he had already mentioned the case of a swept or ash-covered stove together with food that had reached ma'achal ben Derusai and continued to improve. Be'er HaGolah, note 10, cites Rashi and Ri. This requires some study. The practice follows the latter view, permitting even food that has reached ma'achal ben Derusai and continues to improve. In chapter 1, Shabbos 18b, the Gemara states that a pot containing raw food is permitted, fully cooked food is also permitted, while food that is partly cooked is forbidden. According to Rif and Rambam, 'fully cooked' means completely cooked and further reduction is detrimental, while 'partly cooked' means that it has reached ma'achal ben Derusai and is therefore forbidden. Bach explains that according to this view, a pot cooked to less than ma'achal ben Derusai is permitted because it is treated like raw food; fully raw food is certainly permitted, because by the following day it will cook without stoking. According to Rashi and Ri, however, 'fully cooked' means that it has reached ma'achal ben Derusai, and 'partly cooked' means less than ma'achal ben Derusai. On that reading, only completely raw food is permitted. If so, this dispute should have been listed by Tur among the differences between Rif and Rashi. Since it was not, it follows that even according to Rif and Rambam there is no additional leniency, and raw food is permitted only when it has not cooked at all, as Magen Avraham writes in se'if katan 1. See siman 318. According to our ruling, once food has reached ma'achal ben Derusai, further cooking remains prohibited even while it is still boiling. Once it is fully cooked, however, even if further reduction improves it, there is no prohibition of cooking after cooking. This is unlike dyeing ingredients, which the Rosh writes in the chapter Kirah always require further cooking; see Taz, siman 318, se'if katan 22, and Rosh, chapter Kirah, daf 40, siman 11. Nevertheless, the concern that one may stoke the coals still applies, and stoking also kindles the coals. See se'if 2. With God's help, the law of returning food on Shabbos will be explained there. According to the second view, food that has reached ma'achal ben Derusai may be left because one will no longer come to stoke the coals.
