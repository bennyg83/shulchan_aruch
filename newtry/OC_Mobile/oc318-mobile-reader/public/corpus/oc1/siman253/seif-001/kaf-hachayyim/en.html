1) [Seif 1] A stove constructed like a pot, etc. The meaning of a stove, a kupach, and an oven in Talmudic times is as follows. A stove and a kupach were made of earthenware like an earthenware pot, with their openings on top. They were not attached to the ground but were movable. A stove, however, had a long opening that could accommodate two pots resting directly upon its mouth, upon a cover over its cavity, or upon its rim. A kupach had room for only one pot upon its opening, and therefore retained more heat than a stove but less than an oven. An oven was also made of earthenware, but it was attached to the ground and its opening was likewise on top. It was narrow above and broad below, causing its heat to be retained within it more than in a stove or kupach, which were not broader below than above. This oven is called a baker's oven, because they would attach the bread to the oven walls. Ran writes that Rabbeinu Chananel explains that our ovens, whose openings are on the side, have precisely the law of a stove. Bach. Beis Yosef writes the same; Taz, paragraph 16; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 7. See below, paragraph 18.
