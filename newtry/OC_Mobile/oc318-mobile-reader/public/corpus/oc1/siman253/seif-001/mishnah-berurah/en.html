Since this siman has many branches, and so that the reader's eyes not become confused by it, I decided to copy from Machatzis HaShekel a brief introduction containing its principal laws, so that the reader may proceed through it readily. Know that there is a law of leaving food in place before Shabbos, and a law of returning it. Leaving food in place means that one places a cooked dish before Shabbos upon a stove, not by way of insulation, leaves it standing upon the stove, and removes it from the stove on Shabbos. Returning means that one placed it before Shabbos upon a stove, removed it from the stove on Shabbos, and wishes to return it a second time to the stove. This is the principal law of returning according to all opinions, although there is also another form of returning, where one removes it before Shabbos close to nightfall and returns it, as explained in se'if 2 in the gloss. Chananya and the Sages disagree. Chananya maintains that once the dish has been cooked to the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai, namely half-cooked, and some say one-third cooked, it may be left upon a stove even if the coals were not removed and were not covered with ashes. The Sages maintain that this is forbidden unless the coals were removed or covered, except where the dish is fully cooked and further reduction is detrimental to it. At the beginning of the chapter Kirah, the Mishnah teaches: A stove that was heated, and so forth, with olive refuse or wood, one may not place upon it until he removes the coals or places ashes upon them. The Gemara asks whether the Mishnah's statement, 'one may not place,' means that one may not leave food there unless the coals were removed or covered, in accordance with the Sages who disagree with Chananya; or whether it means that one may not return food there, while leaving it there is permitted even if the coals were not removed or covered, in accordance with Chananya. This question was not resolved. The Rif, Rambam, and those who follow their approach rule that the Mishnah refers to leaving food in place and follows the Sages who disagree with Chananya, so that even leaving food there requires a stove whose coals were removed or covered. Tosafot rule that the Mishnah refers to returning food, but leaving food there is permitted even upon a stove whose coals were not removed once the food has reached ma'achal ben Derusai, in accordance with Chananya. These are the two opinions brought in the Shulchan Aruch in this siman. The first opinion is that of the Rif and Rambam. The alternative opinion brought by the Mechaber at the end of se'if 1 is that of Rashi and Tosafot as stated above. This is what we have explained briefly.
