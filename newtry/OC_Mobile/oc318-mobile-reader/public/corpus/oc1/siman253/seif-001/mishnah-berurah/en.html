Because this siman has many commentaries, so the reader's eyes will not be confused, I copied from Machatzit HaShekel a brief introduction to its main laws so the reader may run through it. Know there is 'that was' and 'return.' 'That was' means one places a dish from erev Shabbat on a stove not as insulation and leaves it standing on the stove and on Shabbat takes from the stove. 'Return' means he left it from erev Shabbat on the stove, on Shabbat took it from the stove, and wishes to return it again on the stove [this is the essence of return for all; but there is another return — taking it erev Shabbat near dark and returning as explained seif 2 in the gloss]. Hananiah and the rabbis disagree: Hananiah holds if already cooked as food of ben drusai — half-cooked [some say one-third] — one may leave it on the stove even if not swept and stoked [coals covered with ash]; sages forbid unless swept and stoked unless fully cooked and shriveled and spoiled. In the first chapter of Kirah we learn: a stove they lit with straw and wood — one should not place until swept or ashes are placed; the Gemara asks whether 'one should not place' means 'one should not leave' unless swept and stoked per rabbis of Hananiah, or 'one should not return' but leaving was permitted even unswept per Hananiah — unresolved. Rif, Rambam, and their camp rule 'leave it' like rabbis of Hananiah — even to leave requires swept and stoked. Tosafos rule 'return it' but leaving is permitted even unswept once cooked as ben drusai like Hananiah. These are the two views in Shulchan Aruch here: view one is Rif, Rambam, and the view the Mechaber brings at end of seif 1 is Rashi and Tosafos — as explained briefly; now we explain Shulchan Aruch with God's help.
