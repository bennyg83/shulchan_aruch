The laws of a kirah and an oven, regarding placing a pot upon them before Shabbos. It contains 5 seifim: A kirah is made like a large pot, with room on its opening above for 2 pots. If it was heated with olive refuse or with wood, it is forbidden before Shabbos to place a cooked dish upon it to remain there, unless the dish is fully cooked and further reduction is detrimental to it, so that there is no concern that one may stir the coals, or unless the food is completely raw and has not cooked at all, because when it is raw one puts it out of mind until the next day and it can cook throughout the night without stirring the coals. But if it has cooked somewhat and is not fully cooked, or even if it is fully cooked but further reduction improves it, we are concerned that one may stir the coals, and it is forbidden to leave it there unless the kirah was garuf, meaning that all the coals were removed, or katum, meaning that the coals were covered with ashes to reduce their heat. If he placed a raw piece into the pot, it is permitted as though the entire dish were raw, because by doing so he puts it out of mind. Even when the kirah is neither garuf nor katum, it is permitted to place a pot adjacent to it on the outside. If it was heated with straw or stubble, one may leave a pot upon it even if it is neither garuf nor katum. {Rama: If 2 kirahs adjoin one another with an earthenware wall between them, one being garuf and katum and the other not garuf and katum, it is permitted to leave food on the garuf and katum one even though it receives additional heat from the one that is not garuf and katum. [Gemara, chapter Kirah]} An oven, even if heated with straw or stubble, is forbidden even for placing a pot adjacent to it, even if it is garuf and katum. {Rama: All this applies while it is yad soledes bo. [Hagahot Mordechai]} All the more so is it forbidden to leave a pot inside it or on top of it. A kupach, which has room for 1 pot, has the law of a kirah if heated with straw or stubble, and the law of an oven if heated with olive refuse or wood (and our ovens have the law of a kirah. [Ran, beginning of chapter Kirah; Kol Bo; and so is implied by Rashi]). If one forgot and left a dish there, if it was fully cooked it is permitted even if further reduction improves it. If it had begun cooking but was not fully cooked, it is forbidden until after Shabbos. If he deliberately left it there in violation of the law, it is forbidden in both cases. {Rama: Until after Shabbos for the amount of time required for the act to have been done. [Hagahot Ashiri; Rambam, chapter 3] If a non-Jew returned them on Shabbos, the law is like one who forgot and left them there. [Hagahot Ashiri] If a Jew returned them, the law is like one who deliberately left them there. [Hagahot Mordechai] And if further reduction is detrimental to it, it is permitted, because he derived no benefit from the prohibition. [Beit Yosef; and see above at the beginning of siman 227]} And some say that whenever it has cooked to the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai — meaning the name of a person who would eat his food when it was not fully cooked — or it is fully cooked and further reduction improves it, it may be left on a kirah. {Rama: Or even on top of an oven. [Maggid Mishneh, chapter 3; Hagahot Mordechai; Maimoniyot, chapter 3; beginning of chapter Kirah in Beit Yosef]} This applies even if it was heated with olive refuse or wood and even if it is neither garuf nor katum. Garuf and katum, and heating with straw or stubble, were mentioned only where it had begun to cook but had not reached ma'achal ben Derusai; and likewise regarding if one removed the pot from upon it and wishes to return it upon it on Shabbos. And if one forgot and left a dish that had begun cooking but had not reached ma'achal ben Derusai, it is forbidden, and needless to say if he deliberately left it there. {Rama: And the custom is to be lenient in accordance with the latter view.} All of this regarding leaving applies when the pot sits on an iron stand or on stones and does not touch the coals; but insulating upon coals is forbidden according to all opinions. {Rama: And some say that even if the pot stands directly upon the fire, as long as it is uncovered on top it is not called insulation and is permitted, and this is the custom; only they are careful to detach it somewhat from the fire before Shabbos so that a Jew will be able to remove it from there. And if he did not detach it from the fire and finds it upon the fire on Shabbos, it should be removed by a non-Jew. And if there is no non-Jew, a Jew may remove it from there, and he should be careful to take it gently and not move the coals; and then even if he moves them slightly, an unintended act is permitted. [Mordechai, beginning of chapter Kirah; Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 7]}
