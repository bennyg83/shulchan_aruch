And if a non-Jew returned them. An intentional act by a non-Jew on behalf of a Jew is equivalent to an inadvertent act by a Jew. Furthermore, the Gemara modifies and presses the Mishnah in order to establish it according to him. See Tosafot there, s.v. 'Actually,' and so forth. Moreover, Rabbi Shimon said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan, and Rava supported him, and the Gemara says, 'Both were taught in our Mishnah.' It then answers that he teaches us an inference from the Mishnah. Thus, the Mishnah is precisely consistent with his view. Rav Shmuel bar Yehudah likewise holds this view, except that he teaches the additional point that food whose continued reduction improves it is permitted. Rav Hai explained that this is the novelty: although there is reason to decree because even a slight stoking helps, and one may therefore pay attention and stoke the coals, it is nevertheless permitted. A proof is that Rav forbids food whose continued reduction improves it, yet at the end of the first chapter Rav permits it once it has reached ma'achal ben Derusai. Although Rav and Shmuel disagree with Rabbi Yochanan, the halachah follows Rabbi Yochanan against them, as stated at the beginning of Yom Tov and in chapter 4 of Eruvin. Furthermore, Mar Ukva of Mishan said to Rabbi Elazar, 'You who are near,' and so forth, 'but we,' and so forth. This shows that the halachah follows Rabbi Yochanan, except that those near him had to observe the stringency because of matters that are permitted but which others had treated as forbidden. In Syria they would leave food and eat it. Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak, a master of practical rulings, also acted accordingly, and an incident is authoritative, as stated at the end of chapter 17 of Shabbat. The first opinion maintains that since the Gemara concludes with Rav Nachman's statement, 'The general rule is,' and so forth, this shows that the halachah follows it, certainly where the food was not fully cooked. This is also the force of the Rashba's question, for Chananya states without qualification that anything cooked to the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai is permitted, which implies from that point onward. The discussion at the end of the first chapter is no proof, because there all agree, as the Ran writes there. The proof is as stated above: Rav forbids food whose continued reduction improves it, yet there he permits food cooked to ma'achal ben Derusai. Rava's support for Rav Sheshet is likewise no proof; see the Rosh. Nor is there proof from Rabbi Yochanan, since the halachah does not follow him against Rav and Shmuel, as the Rif writes in several places and as stated at the end of chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah.
