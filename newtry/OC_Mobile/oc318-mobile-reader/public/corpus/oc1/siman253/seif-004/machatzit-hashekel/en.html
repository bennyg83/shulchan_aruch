S.k. 4: A piece, etc. Vegetables, however, are ineffective. The Magen Avraham did not place this comment above, upon the Mechaber's words, 'or when it is raw,' because the Magen Avraham's intention is not to exclude the opinion of Rambam in his Commentary to the Mishnah, cited by Beis Yosef and by the Taz. Rambam maintains that even placing a raw vegetable into the pot is sufficient, although a raw vegetable can cook during the night. One might have said that perhaps he will stoke the coals, yet it is nevertheless permitted because he performed an act demonstrating that he has diverted his attention from the food, and we are not concerned that he will stoke. We, however, do not rule like Rambam in this respect. See Beis Yosef and the words of the Magen Avraham. The Magen Avraham therefore placed his comment here. This differs from the earlier ruling, where the Mechaber wrote, 'or when it is raw,' meaning that the entire pot is filled with raw food. In that case, Rambam also agrees that it must specifically be meat, for if the entire pot were filled with raw vegetables, there would certainly be concern that he might stoke the coals, since he performed no act demonstrating that he had diverted his attention from it.
