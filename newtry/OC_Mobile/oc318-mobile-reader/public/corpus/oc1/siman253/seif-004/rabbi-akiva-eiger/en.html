Magen Avraham, s.k. 5. 'It is forbidden to place it adjacent to a stove that was not swept.' Dagul Merevavah writes that this means it is forbidden to place it there initially on Shabbos, but if it was placed there before Shabbos, one may return it on Shabbos and place it adjacent to the stove, as is evident from the case of two adjoining stoves; see there. With due respect, this is incorrect. The Magen Avraham means that according to the opinion that does not rule like Chananya, which is the position of the Rif, placing food there before Shabbos is also forbidden. He proves this from the Gemara's question whether one may place food adjacent to the stove, which indicates that leaving it there is forbidden. The Ran explains that the Rif maintains that if this question concerned returning food on Shabbos, how could the Gemara resolve it from the baraisa concerning two adjoining stoves? That baraisa concerns leaving food there before Shabbos, and perhaps returning it is more stringent; see there. It follows that according to this opinion, returning food adjacent to the stove is forbidden. It is therefore clear that the Gaon's statement that permission is evident from the case of two adjoining stoves is mistaken, since that case concerns leaving food there before Shabbos. Regarding this the Magen Avraham writes, 'But according to the second opinion it is permitted,' meaning the second opinion, which rules like Chananya. According to that view, the Gemara's question whether one may place food adjacent to the stove concerns returning it, and the Gemara resolves the question from the case of two adjoining stoves. It is thus explicit that returning food there is also permitted. Nevertheless, this applies only by way of returning it. One may not initially place food adjacent to the stove on Shabbos, as is evident from the Rama at the end of this siman, who writes, 'and it has likewise been explained that the custom is to be lenient.' Thus, initially placing it adjacent is forbidden, while returning it depends upon these two opinions, as the Magen Avraham explains, contrary to the words of the Gaon of blessed memory.
