If, however, it was placed there earlier in the day, it is forbidden, because it will already have cooked somewhat before Shabbos, and one must remove it once night falls. Magen Avraham. Now, Rambam's Commentary to the Mishnah implies that anything not yet cooked to half its required cooking is considered raw. His Mishneh Torah, however, implies that once it has cooked at all, even less than one-third, it is forbidden, and it must be completely raw. This also appears from all the halachic authorities. See the Taz's objection to the Bach. His words can readily be rejected, but the practical law is nevertheless correctly stated as I have explained, and I have therefore written briefly. In any event, the Bach's inferences on this matter are not compelling to one who examines them.
