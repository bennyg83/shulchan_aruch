Se'if 2: As long as it is boiling hot. Although there is no cooking after cooking, as is evident from the fact that one may return it on Shabbat, once it has cooled there is cooking, as we say there on 34a: a decree lest he bring it to a boil. See the Rosh, chapter 3, siman 10.
And it is still in his hand. He rules the unresolved questions there stringently, as the Rosh wrote.
And he did not place it on the ground. This is the language of the Rambam, who rules the unresolved question leniently, for he follows the Yerushalmi in accordance with his general method. In the Yerushalmi there: if he placed it on the ground, it is forbidden to move it; if it was suspended from a peg, so long as it was still hot it is permitted. However, afterward it says there in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: this applies only where he did not remove his hand from it, but if he removed his hand from it, it is forbidden. This implies the view stated in the gloss.

This follows the first version there, stringently, as the Rosh wrote in chapter 4, siman 2, in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel, because the stringency of Shabbat is treated like a matter of Torah law. Its law is like Shabbat itself. This is not exact. Rather, it is forbidden when the stove was neither swept nor covered with ashes, but when it was swept it is permitted in every case. There is no distinction between whether he placed it on the ground or it remained in his hand, as the Rosh wrote there. Although in chapter 4 he did not write this, there he merely copied the language of Tosafot, as is his manner; his own view, however, is that it is permitted, as stated above. There is support for this view in the Yerushalmi there: if he removed it while it was still day, he may return it while it is still day; if he removed it after dark, he may return it after dark; if he removed it while it was still day and the day became sanctified while it was in his hand, then if he placed it on the ground it is forbidden, and so forth. In the other cases, however, it is permitted in every circumstance. According to the Ran, the phrase 'from daytime into darkness' is not exact.
And some are lenient. The Ran maintains that one cannot derive the law of Beit Hillel from Beit Shammai, because Beit Shammai follow their own reasoning that anything forbidden on Shabbat is forbidden even on Friday, as stated in the Mishnah there on 17b. This is the meaning of 'even on Shabbat': one might have thought that everyone agrees it is forbidden on Shabbat and that they disagree only regarding Friday, each following his own view, as stated above.
