And specifically when the food, etc. This refers back to the opening ruling, which states that it is permitted to return the pot even on Shabbos. That permission applies where the food is fully cooked and has not cooled, for no further cooking applies to it. If it is not yet cooked to that extent, however, further cooking does apply. In responsum 102, Rama permits opening the stove on Friday night and allowing a non-Jew to close it again, provided that the food has reached the degree of cooking of ma'achal ben Derusai.
Only when it was removed from the stove before the onset of Shabbos. The reason appears to be that the original insulation was then nullified, and returning it would be comparable to initially insulating food on Shabbos, as Rashi explains regarding the prohibition where one set it upon the ground. This does not apply when one removes it on Shabbos.
And it is proper to be stringent. For the Ran writes this leniency, whereas Tosafos, the Rosh, and those who follow them do not accept it.
If it cooled, etc. For renewed cooking then applies to it. Beis Yosef writes in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham that if it did not cool, even if its boiling subsided somewhat, it is permitted to return it. End quote. Wherever returning it is permitted here, one may even close the mouth of the stove with a board after returning it, because closing it with a board is not considered insulation, as Beis Yosef writes near the end of this siman. Regarding the practice of covering the mouth of the stove with garments, since the garments do not touch the pot, this is certainly not insulation and is permitted, etc. End quote. The same therefore applies to this board. All this applies where there are no burning coals in the stove. If burning coals are present, however, the Mishnah states explicitly that where the stove was neither swept nor covered with ashes, even Beis Hillel forbids returning food to it. Even with our stoves, which resemble a kira, returning food is forbidden in every circumstance. One may ask why returning food to a swept stove was forbidden where the pot had been placed upon the ground or in a similar case. The food was already fully cooked, and the Ran writes that returning it is forbidden even where further reduction is detrimental. There appears to be no concern, since no further cooking applies and there are no coals that one might stoke. I saw that the Ran answered this by writing that although all these matters are only rabbinic, since anything cooked to the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai is no longer subject to the Torah prohibition of cooking, the Sages were stringent in these matters because they readily lead to a Torah prohibition. End quote. According to the Rosh as well, the same reasoning applies after the food is fully cooked.
It is forbidden to place it among cushions, etc. Although cushions and blankets do not add heat, insulating with them on Shabbos is nevertheless forbidden, as stated in siman 257. Although returning a pot is permitted, as stated nearby, even to another stove, that applies where it is not placed in a manner of insulation. Here, where it is placed in a manner of insulation, it is forbidden. For this reason, one may place it among cushions and blankets if it remains uncovered on top.
Close to nightfall, etc. The Tur writes that this applies specifically after the pot has finished cooking completely and one leaves it standing upon the stove until evening to preserve its heat, etc. This is from the words of the Rosh. Beis Yosef explains the reason: as long as it has not cooked completely, we do not decree lest one come to return it after nightfall, because doing so would constitute cooking on Shabbos, and it is obvious that one will be careful about this even if returning it before Shabbos is permitted. End quote. One should not object that we find many decrees lest one forget and stoke the coals on Shabbos. There, the decree is lest he forget and stoke. Here, the only decree would be lest he derive permission from returning it close to nightfall, and that concern does not apply where returning it would certainly involve cooking. One might object that, as cited above from the Ran, returning food on Shabbos is forbidden as a safeguard against a Torah prohibition, and consequently a decree before Shabbos on account of Shabbos would be a decree upon a decree. It appears to me that since on Shabbos itself one could very readily come to a prohibition, as the Ran writes, it is treated like a certain prohibition, and the Sages therefore properly decreed on its account even before Shabbos.
And with an oven there is no distinction. This refers to an oven of Talmudic times, and the Shulchan Aruch already wrote this explicitly in this siman. Our ovens, however, which open from the side, are like a kira, as Beis Yosef writes regarding the Tur's ruling. I do not know for what purpose Rama wrote this here, since it may lead one mistakenly to interpret it as referring to our ovens.
