Boiling hot. The practice is to be lenient if the food has not cooled completely, as stated in siman 318, seif 4; see also the gloss at the end of that siman.

To return it. Beit Yosef maintains that it is permitted even if, when he removed it, he did not intend to return it.

On top of it. Our stoves are flat, so their interior and upper surface are considered one area, and returning the pot is permitted. See Shiltei Giborim. When there is fire upon the stove, see siman 318, seifim 14 and 15.

While it is still day. At that point the original insulation has ended, and replacing it is considered insulating anew on Shabbat. This does not apply when it is removed on Shabbat itself.

It cooled. This means that it cooled completely, so that reheating it would constitute a new act of cooking. See siman 318. Taz writes that wherever returning the pot is permitted, one may even close the mouth of the oven with a board after returning it, because closing it with a board is not considered insulation. This applies only where there are no glowing coals in the oven. If glowing coals remain, the Mishnah explicitly rules that when the oven was neither swept nor covered with ashes, even Beit Hillel forbids returning the pot. This is so even with our ovens, which resemble a stove, for returning is then forbidden in every manner.

And cushions. This is worse than returning it to an oven, because it is considered a new act of insulation. See Darkei Moshe. If the top is open, however, it is permitted, since this is not considered insulation, as Rama writes. See Taz.

On Shabbat. If the food is not fully cooked, it is forbidden to return it shortly before nightfall. If it is entirely raw, however, it is permitted when placed immediately before nightfall, as stated in seif 1. One should not remove a pot from a stove and place it upon an oven that was neither swept nor covered with ashes shortly before nightfall. See Magen Avraham and seif katan 26 regarding our ovens.

Nightfall. This applies even if the food cooled completely, for otherwise it would be permitted even on Shabbat. See siman 318.

A distinction. This refers to the type of oven used in the time of the Gemara. Shulchan Aruch has already ruled that our ovens have the status of a stove.
