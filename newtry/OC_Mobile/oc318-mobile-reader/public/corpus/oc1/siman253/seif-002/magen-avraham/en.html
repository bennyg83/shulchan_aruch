Boiling hot. Meaning that it is at the temperature of yad soledes bo. See what I wrote in siman 318, se'if 4, contrary to the Olas Shabbos. The custom is to be lenient so long as it has not cooled completely, as stated in siman 318, se'if 15; see the end of this siman in the gloss. This is likewise written in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham, contrary to the Bach. If, however, it has cooled, reheating it constitutes cooking; see siman 318, se'if 4.
And it is still in his hand. The Beit Yosef maintains that it is forbidden only when he set it upon the ground. If he set it upon a bed or bench, or transferred it from one hot-water urn to another, it is permitted. The Rama, however, maintains that all these cases are forbidden, since this is regarded as placing it there initially on Shabbos. See siman 257, se'if 5.
And he intends to return it. The Beit Yosef maintains that it is permitted even if he did not intend to return it, as the Rambam writes.
Upon it. Since the Beit Yosef ruled without qualification, it follows that he maintains that even if one suspends the pot within the airspace of the stove, it is permitted. It is not considered inside the stove unless it rests below, upon the floor of the stove. The Bach writes likewise; see se'if 3. The Shiltei Gibborim writes that with our flat-topped stoves, the inside and the top are considered one and the same, and it is permitted. If there is fire upon the stove, see siman 318, se'if 15.
To another stove. This is permitted even if the second stove has greater heat than the first. If, however, it was initially insulated and on Shabbos he wishes to place it upon a stove, or to transfer it from a stove into insulation, it is forbidden. Rambam, ibid.
When he removed it. He removed it while it was still daytime. Consequently, if he returns it on Shabbos, it is regarded as placing it there initially.
It is forbidden to place it. This is more stringent than returning it to an oven, since it is regarded as beginning an act of insulation. Darkei Moshe.
Close to Barechu. At the end of the chapter Bameh Madlikin, Rashi and the Ran write that one may attach bread to the wall of an oven close to Barechu, provided sufficient time remains in the day for its surface to form a crust before nightfall. The Maggid Mishneh likewise writes at the end of chapter 5 that we do not decree lest one stir the coals after Barechu, since doing so is not prohibited by Torah law. All the more so should one be lenient here, for this very prohibition exists only because of a decree: were returning permitted before Shabbos, one might come to return it on Shabbos, when it is forbidden lest he stir the coals. Why, then, should an additional decree be imposed close to Barechu?
Its law is the same as on Shabbos. If the food is not yet fully cooked, it is permitted to return it close to nightfall, for we do not decree lest one do so on Shabbos, since he certainly will not cook on Shabbos. Tur and Rosh. The Semag, Semak, and Hagahos Maimoniyos prohibit even this. Bach. If the food is entirely raw, however, it is permitted immediately before nightfall, as stated in se'if 1.
It is proper to be stringent. One should not remove a pot from the stove and place it upon an oven that has not been swept or covered with ashes close to nightfall. See se'if 3.
Even close to nightfall. This applies even if it cooled completely, for otherwise it would be permitted even on Shabbos, as stated in siman 318, se'if 15.
