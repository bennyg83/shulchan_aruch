It is regarded as an initial placement, which is forbidden even upon the opening of a stove whose coals were removed or covered with ashes. Even the Rama agrees that an initial placement on Shabbos is forbidden. At first glance, the Mechaber appears to contradict himself, since below in se'if 5 he rules in accordance with the Rashba, cited by Beit Yosef in the name of the Ran in the chapter Kirah, that one may place a pot there initially on Shabbos. Although the lower pot stands upon the fire, since it intervenes and the upper pot is placed upon it, this is not the normal manner of cooking. It is therefore permitted, and is even more lenient than a stove whose coals were removed or covered. It is comparable to placing food opposite a fire, which is permitted even on Shabbos when the food is fully cooked. See below, siman 318, se'if 15 and the gloss, for the details of this law. I found that Dagul Merevavah struggled with this question and suggested that perhaps the Mechaber relies upon the Ran's reasoning that, since panadish is not normally baked upon a stove, one may be lenient. See there. This is very strained, because the Mechaber does not accept the Ran's approach as normative law, as the Magen Avraham writes. The explanation of the Gra in se'if 5 likewise demonstrates that the Shulchan Aruch follows the Rashba's approach cited above. I later found that Peri Megadim resolves the question well. In se'if 5, where the upper pot is placed upon a pot of hot water or cooked food, it is not regarded at all as standing upon a stove. It is therefore permitted even to place it there initially on Shabbos. In our case, by contrast, the lower pot is empty and serves only to block some of the stove's heat. It merely transforms the stove into the equivalent of an ordinary stove whose coals were removed or covered, upon which it remains forbidden to place a cooked dish initially on Shabbos. In truth, if a pot containing cooked food had been standing there from before Shabbos, everyone would agree that one may place this pot upon it even initially on Shabbos.
