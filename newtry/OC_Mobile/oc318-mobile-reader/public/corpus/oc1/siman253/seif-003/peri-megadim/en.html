Since Tosafos wrote that raw food is permitted upon a kirah, and did not write 'even upon a kirah,' it follows that they interpret both the case of a raw pot and that of cooked but not fully cooked food as applying equally to a kirah and an oven; even upon a kirah, raw food is permitted. Furthermore, although Tosafos write on daf 38a that one may distinguish by saying that raw food is permitted in an oven but forbidden on a kirah, one could still say the reverse regarding food that has reached ma'achal ben Derusai: it is permitted on a kirah, in accordance with the plain meaning of the baraisa, 'Chananya says that anything which has reached ma'achal ben Derusai may be left upon a kirah that is neither swept nor covered with ashes,' but not in an oven. The Mechaber, when presenting the second view, omitted the law of an oven, while Rama states it explicitly. Even if one were to say that food which has reached ma'achal ben Derusai is permitted on an unswept kirah because one is unlikely to stoke merely so that it finish cooking after some time, an oven is different. Since its heat is intense, one may stoke so that it finish immediately. There is therefore no proof from a kirah to an oven, and Tosafos should not have stated without qualification that although raw food is permitted in an oven, that is because it will be ready only the next day. This proves the point. See Rambam. According to what I have written, the matter is resolved.
