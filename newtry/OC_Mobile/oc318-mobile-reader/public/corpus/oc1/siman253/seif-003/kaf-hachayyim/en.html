3) There: To leave it upon the stove. Tur writes, 'To leave it upon the stove for the evening.' Levush writes the same. This implies that if it is intended for the daytime, it is permitted in every case, because he has diverted his attention from it and we are not concerned that he might stoke the coals. Beis Yosef, however, is uncertain about this. Since the Shulchan Aruch does not include the words 'for the evening,' it appears that he rules there is no distinction between food intended for the evening and food intended for the daytime. Bach rules this way. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, similarly writes that as a matter of practical halachah it is logical to forbid it even when left for the next day. Since he could complete its cooking at night by stoking the coals, we are concerned that he might change his mind and eat it at night, and it is therefore considered needed for the evening; see there.
