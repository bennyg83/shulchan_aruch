Provided that it has not cooled completely. See Magen Avraham, who writes that even a food without liquid, for which there is no prohibition of cooking, is forbidden once it has cooled, because this is comparable to placing it initially upon a stove on Shabbos, which is forbidden. The Sages permitted only returning. His intention is that the ruling refers to panadish mentioned above, which is a food without liquid. Regarding such food we rule that there is no cooking after cooking even if it cooled completely, as explained above in se'if katan 86. He therefore answers that it remains forbidden because it is considered an initial placement upon the stove, thereby introducing a new rule. The explanation of the Gra, however, writes, 'provided,' as above. He means that the reason is the same as at the beginning of se'if 2, where the food must specifically remain boiling hot. As the Gra explains there, if it cooled, further cooking would occur. This is also implied by Darkei Moshe, who writes that regarding a cold item the custom follows Beit Yosef and is stringent. The Rama's words in this gloss derive from that ruling. It is well known that Beit Yosef's reason for stringency is that the food must remain boiling hot. As Beit Yosef himself initially asks, the requirement in se'if 2 that it remain boiling hot is explained there by Beit Yosef on the ground that otherwise further cooking applies. All this demonstrates that the correct explanation follows the Gra. As for the Magen Avraham's question that the ruling refers to panadish, which is a dry food, the answer is simple: the Rama included within the lenient opinion also a food containing liquid, and concerning that case he says, 'provided that it has not cooled completely.'
To warm the pot. Even a dry food that was previously cooked and then cooled completely, although it is no longer subject to a concern of cooking, as stated in siman 318, se'if 15, may nevertheless not be warmed in an oven, even through a non-Jew. Although our ovens have the status of a stove, returning food even upon a stove is forbidden when its coals were not removed, and it is forbidden even while the food is still hot. According to Magen Avraham in se'if katan 36, it is forbidden even upon a stove whose coals were removed when the food cooled completely, because this is considered an initial placement. Even after the fact, the food is forbidden in this case while it is still hot. When it has become cold, however, Peri Megadim permits it in se'if katan 37. See there. In se'if katan 38 he writes that, since we rule that a rabbinic prohibition may be performed for the sake of Shabbos, and regarding a dry food the prohibition of returning is only rabbinic, perhaps one should not rebuke a person who is lenient through a non-Jew. See below at the end of siman 318, where Sha'arei Teshuvah cites Maharit as permitting one initially to place a dry food without liquid into an oven through a non-Jew. Several authorities in this siman appear not to agree, as may be seen in Magen Avraham, se'if katan 36, Tosefet Shabbos, and others. Nevertheless, it appears that for the sake of Shabbos one may rely upon this opinion and be lenient, as stated above in the name of Peri Megadim. I also found in Birkei Yosef, in the name of one of the later authorities, an argument for leniency even regarding a food containing liquid, allowing it to be warmed through a non-Jew after it cooled, provided it is not placed directly upon the fire or the stove. He relies upon the authorities who maintain that there is no cooking after cooking even after the food cooled. It requires study whether this view may be relied upon, since this siman does not appear to support it. Yet where it is needed for Shabbos and no other solution is available, it is possible that one may rely upon it.

To place it adjacent, and so forth. According to Dagul Merevavah's explanation at the beginning of the siman of the Magen Avraham's words in se'if katan 5, the Rama must be referring to placing the pot adjacent to the heat initially on Shabbos. This is strained. First, why does the Rama say, 'and the same applies to placing it adjacent,' since an initial placement adjacent to the heat has no connection to placing a pot upon the ground? Second, this passage concerns returning food after one removes it from the oven on Shabbos morning, where it had been insulated, and then places it beside the winter oven while it is still hot. It is more correct to follow Rabbi Akiva Eiger's explanation there, that the Rama is discussing returning the food. He therefore interprets the Rama's words as we have explained them.
