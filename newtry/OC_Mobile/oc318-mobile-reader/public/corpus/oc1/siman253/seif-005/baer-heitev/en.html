The explanation is that since it is forbidden to instruct a non-Jew to heat it, it is likewise forbidden to tell a non-Jew to place a dish that has cooled upon a stove that has already been fired. Therefore, the practice is for non-Jews to place a dish that has cooled completely upon the stove before it is fired. Magen Avraham. The Taz disputes this leniency and writes that a conscientious person should not rely upon it; see there at length.
In such a case. For this is comparable to one person placing the pot and another placing the fire: the first is exempt, but the act is forbidden. This differs from instructing a non-Jew, which is permitted here, since the non-Jew's primary intention is to heat the house, which is permitted, as stated at the end of siman 276. From this, it appears to me that if a non-Jew placed water into a pot fixed in the stove and the water became hot, a Jew may benefit from it by washing dishes with it. It is possible that this is permitted even if the non-Jew placed the water there after the stove had been fired. A Jew, however, may not place the water there even before the stove is fired. Nevertheless, where the dishes are soiled with fat, it appears to me that one may not pour boiling water over them. I found written in the name of Maharam of Rothenburg that this is permitted, since his intention is only to clean them. It nevertheless appears to me that he should place the utensil into the water and not pour the water over it. Magen Avraham.

The stove. It is forbidden, however, to place it directly upon the stove, since the stove is neither swept nor covered with ashes. With our stoves, one may place it there even when the fire is inside the stove, because there is a layer of masonry separating it from the fire. Maharil likewise writes that if one placed a piece of wood or a board upon the stove, one may place a pot upon it. Keep this general rule in mind: all the permissions mentioned in this siman apply only to a hot dish. Regarding a cold dish or other cold items, see siman 318 for which cases are forbidden because of cooking according to the Torah. Magen Avraham.
