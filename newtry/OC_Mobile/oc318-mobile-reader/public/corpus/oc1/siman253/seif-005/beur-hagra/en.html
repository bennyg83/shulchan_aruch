(1) 318:15 — it is permitted to give, etc., because there is no usual cooking method, etc. And this is per what is written in siman 318 seif 15 in the gloss 'and some are lenient,' etc. — it is the view of Rashba, and as he wrote in his chiddushim that it is not forbidden opposite the fire nor in a kli rishon for cold that was not cooked; the decree of hafshah in place of cooking is lest one cook; but cold that was cooked, even in a kli rishon or opposite the fire, and even where yad soledes bo applies, is permitted — provided he does not place it on top of or directly on a stove. And know that we say whatever entered hot, etc. — and even though derivatives of fire are like fire and one who cooks in them is liable for a chatat, as written in perek 3 of Shabbat 'the roller is liable'; we also say oil — even though yad soledes bo applies to it, is permitted, for he holds oil has no concern of cooking; and salt, even in a kli rishon, is permitted because it does not cook in a kli rishon. From here, even something not cooked at all, if it is a hard thing such as meat, is permitted even in a kli rishon or opposite the fire, even where yad soledes bo applies. The general rule: wherever it will not come to cooking, and there is no concern of cooking, or it does not cook — it is permitted whether in a kli rishon or opposite the fire; even though they forbade returning on an un-swept stove and placing l'chatchila even on a swept one, even something hot and fully cooked — there the reason is because it appears like cooking, since the usual method of cooking is thus, and similarly on the fire; but on a kli rishon or opposite the fire, where non-Jews usually do not cook thus most of the time, it does not appear like cooking but like cooling, and it is forbidden only where it will come to cooking. From all these matters it is permitted to place on a pot, etc., because there is no usual cooking method thus, and it is only like placing beside the kettle or opposite the fire. The first reasoning in Shulchan Aruch there, that something with gravy is forbidden when congealed, is the view of Rosh, who establishes the mishnah 'whatever entered hot,' etc., for a dry matter; and he was compelled from what was written 'a decree lest it boil,' and per Rashi's explanation it appears there is no difficulty on Rashba — one can say 'lest it boil' on opposite the fire or a stove; and similarly what is written 'a cooked dish that was cooked,' etc., is also per Rosh as above and is not necessary.
