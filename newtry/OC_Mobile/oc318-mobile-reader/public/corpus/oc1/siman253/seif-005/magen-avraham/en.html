Only returning was permitted. To warm the pot. Beit Yosef writes in the name of Rashba that one may not tell a non-Jew to kindle a fire in order to warm the pot. Rashba himself holds that a Jew may warm the pot in the manner explained in siman 318, seif 15, because it is not placed directly upon the fire or stove. Rama writes without qualification that one may not tell a non-Jew to warm it, because according to our view, which holds that warming it is prohibited by Torah law, one may likewise not instruct a non-Jew to do so. If the non-Jew did so, the food is forbidden even after the fact, as stated in seif 1 concerning a non-Jew who returned food to the stove. Nevertheless, if the non-Jew did not warm it directly upon the fire or stove, there is room for leniency because major poskim permit even a Jew to do so. Darkei Moshe cites Or Zarua: I saw in the home of my teacher in Sharlion that the cholent would sometimes cool. Before the meal on Shabbos, the servants would kindle a fire near the pots so that they would become thoroughly warm. Some would move the pots and place them near the fire. They explained that this was permitted because the pots were presumably still somewhat warm. Even if they had cooled considerably, the food should not be prohibited, because the servants were also acting for their own benefit. Even if they intended to benefit the Jews, there is no concern, because the Jews presumably did not want this. Had the pots cooled completely, they would not have permitted them to be warmed at all. The servants cannot prohibit the food against the Jews’ will. Nevertheless, if one sees that the food has cooled completely, he must protest and prevent the non-Jew from warming it, as stated in siman 252, seif 2. Responsa Maharam writes that a ban was enacted requiring a Jewish supervisor at weddings to ensure that a non-Jew does not add water to a cauldron on Shabbos. At weddings servants commonly act in this manner, and no one refrains from eating the food. Rabbeinu Tam and other leading authorities likewise hold it permitted. Nevertheless, it is proper not to do so because it is conspicuous and degrades Shabbos. In any place lacking scholars, this entire practice should be prohibited.
