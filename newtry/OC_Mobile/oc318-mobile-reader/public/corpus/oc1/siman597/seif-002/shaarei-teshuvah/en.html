In Shulchan Aruch: there are places, etc. Shevus Yaakov, part 1, end of siman 30, wrote that a baal bris who is accustomed to fast every Rosh Hashanah is forbidden to eat, for this fast of Rosh Hashanah certainly requires acceptance and a vow, as implied by Shulchan Aruch, siman 597. The wording of the poskim, one who is accustomed to fast all his days, implies even where there is a mitzvah meal, just as he nullifies the joy of Yom Tov. Also, according to what is written that there is danger in the matter, that one who does not complete his year is in danger, and danger is stricter than prohibition, therefore he should not change his practice, so that he and his children will live long. Kenesses Yechezkel, siman 23, wrote regarding one who was seized in iron chains in mortal danger because of a libel, and vowed a vow with public force that when Hashem saved him he would fast all his life on the two days of Rosh Hashanah. He did not want to deal with releasing his vow, because he proved from Rosh Hashanah 18, which says, On the third of Tishrei the mention was nullified, etc., that according to the conclusion it is a mitzvah to fast on Rosh Hashanah, and he rejected the proofs of the Taz in siman 597; see there. He wrote that three other scholars released him, and I said: since it was released, it was released, since there are opinions that after the fact release works even for a vow made with public force; see there. See Shaagas Aryeh, siman 21, who brought proof from the above Talmud in Rosh Hashanah that it is forbidden to fast on Rosh Hashanah. See there, siman 102, that not only is it forbidden to fast on it, but its joy is a mitzvah like other Yamim Tovim, obligating Kiddush like other Yamim Tovim; it is forbidden to taste anything before Kiddush, Kiddush must be in the place of a meal, and all the laws of the Yom Tov meal apply to it.
