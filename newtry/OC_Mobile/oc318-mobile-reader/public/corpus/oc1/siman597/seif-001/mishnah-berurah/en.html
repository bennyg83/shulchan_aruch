They eat, drink, and rejoice. That is to say, although it is the Day of Judgment, nevertheless the mitzvah of and you shall rejoice on your chag applies to it as well, for it too is included in the term chag, as it is written, Blow the shofar at the new moon, at the covered time for the day of our chag, and it says in Nechemiah 8: Eat rich foods and drink sweet drinks, etc., for the day is holy to our Master, and do not be sad, for the joy of Hashem is your strength.
And one does not fast on Rosh Hashanah. Except for a fast for a dream, for on other Yamim Tovim as well one fasts for this. The Ateres Zekenim wrote that according to everyone it is permitted on Rosh Hashanah to fast and pray until midday, even though on Shabbos and other Yamim Tovim it is forbidden; and so it is stated above in siman 584, seif 1, in the gloss.
