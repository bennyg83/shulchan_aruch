3) Ibid. One does not fast on Rosh Hashanah. The Beit Yosef cited Terumas HaDeshen, siman 278, that there are several great authorities who hold that it is a mitzvah to fast on Rosh Hashanah, while there are also several great authorities who forbid fasting on Rosh Hashanah, as explained in the Tur and Beit Yosef, see there. The Shulchan Aruch therefore ruled here according to the view of those who forbid fasting on Rosh Hashanah, because they are the majority. The Taz, s.k. 1, wrote by way of resolution that it is impossible to say that it is a mitzvah to fast, for we find concerning Elisha that he ate in the home of the Shunammite woman, and that day was Rosh Hashanah, as stated in the Midrash, see there. Eliyah Rabbah, os 1, challenged the aforementioned words of the Taz. Birkei Yosef, os 1, resolved the matter, although Birkei Yosef wrote there that, in truth, the proof of the Taz is weak, for through his intention while eating, Elisha effected heavenly rectifications tenfold beyond those effected by other pious individuals through fasting. Nevertheless, Birkei Yosef cited there the words of Shaagas Aryeh, siman 101 and siman 102, who concluded that it is forbidden to fast and that all the laws of a Yom Tov meal apply on Rosh Hashanah, see there. Shaarei Teshuvah likewise cited the aforementioned words of Shaagas Aryeh, see there. The Levush also ruled in accordance with the Shulchan Aruch that one should not fast at all, neither on Rosh Hashanah nor on Shabbos Teshuvah. So too wrote the Bach, adding that this is the custom. So too ruled Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 1; Biur HaGra; Chayei Adam, klal 139, os 11.
