One who fasts. It is written in Teshuvos Maharam, end of siman 54: Maharam fasted and recited Kiddusha Rabba in his house, and he said the reason was because it is D'Oraisa. End quote. See siman 289, that we rule it is rabbinic, and what I wrote at the beginning of siman 271. Nevertheless it appears to me that on the night of Rosh Hashanah it is forbidden to fast, as on other Yamim Tovim, as written in siman 529; see there. For in the dream they showed him only the day and not the night.
A fast for a dream. If there is doubt whether it is among the dreams for which one fasts on Shabbos, one should not fast; Kenesses HaGedolah.
All his days. One who is not afraid need not fast, as above; so it is implied in Minhagim and Agur.
And he need not observe. Even if it falls on Shabbos; there.
For it is a mitzvah to fast. One must say that he holds as written at the beginning of siman 418 in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham, that nowadays, when Megillas Taanis has been nullified, there is no prohibition to fast on Rosh Hashanah. See Rosh Hashanah, folio 19, and the Bach here and there.
On Yom Kippur. Because they are days of judgment; there. That is, it is not appropriate to say Our fast. Kol Bo wrote: one who stipulated to fast on Rosh Hashanah need not fast forever. End quote. Nevertheless it appears to me that he requires release, as with other vows, and it is not comparable to one who vows on Shabbos or Yom Tov, since some say that one must fast on Rosh Hashanah. End quote, Beis Yosef. It appears to me that this speaks where he accepted it in the language of accepting a fast; see siman 570, and all the more so if he swore.
