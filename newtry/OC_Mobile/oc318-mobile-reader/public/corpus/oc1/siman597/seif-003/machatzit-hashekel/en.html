(s.k. 3) One who fasts, etc. And he recited Kiddusha Rabba in his house, meaning the daytime Kiddush; see above, siman 289, why it is called Kiddusha Rabba, etc., because it is D'Oraisa. That is to say, he certainly could discharge the members of his household if they did not know how, as written above in siman 273, seif 4; see there. Nevertheless, from where would it follow that he himself fulfilled his obligation? To this he wrote: because it is D'Oraisa. If so, through the Kiddush that he recited he fulfilled the Torah obligation of Kiddush, even though we rule that Kiddush is only valid in the place of a meal, and here he did not eat. Nevertheless, he holds that the rule that Kiddush must be in the place of a meal is only rabbinic. The proof is that the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 271 that according to the Torah one fulfilled the obligation by mentioning it in prayer; see there. This is unlike if the Kiddush were rabbinic, for then it would not help him to fulfill his own obligation, since rabbinically Kiddush is only valid in the place of a meal.
For we rule that it is rabbinic. For in siman 289 he wrote that even on Shabbos, where the nighttime Kiddush is D'Oraisa, nevertheless the daytime Kiddush is rabbinic. And in siman 271 he wrote that on Yom Tov even the nighttime Kiddush is rabbinic. Also on Shabbos, if he already prayed, he fulfilled the Torah obligation through mentioning it in prayer; see there.
Nevertheless it appears to me, etc. That in a dream they did not permit, etc. Nevertheless, in my humble opinion, according to what I wrote above in siman 288 in the name of some authorities, if one slept an afternoon sleep and had a bad dream, he should fast until midnight. The same applies here. If this occurred to him on the first day of Rosh Hashanah, for those who are accustomed to sleep, I do not know how to judge what he should do in later years.
