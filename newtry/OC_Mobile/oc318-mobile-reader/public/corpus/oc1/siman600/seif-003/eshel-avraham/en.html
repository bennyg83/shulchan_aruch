On the second day. The reason we do not blow shofar on Shabbos is because of Rabbah's decree. Therefore, if one transgressed, blew, and recited Shehecheyanu, do we say that he should not recite Shehecheyanu again on the second day, or perhaps, since he violated a rabbinic prohibition, his act is of no effect and he returns and recites the blessing? See Teshuvas Pnei Yehoshua, part 2, siman 14, who developed a long discussion whether in a rabbinic matter, if one acted, it is ineffective. See above, siman 339, and Choshen Mishpat, siman 208, and Hagahos Mordechai in Shevuos, brought by Maharam in the gloss to Yoreh Deah, siman 230, and Teshuvos Maharshdam, Yoreh Deah, siman 115. The above author concluded that in any case it does not leave the realm of doubt, and we rule that in doubtful blessings we are lenient. One who examines the words of Maharit, who wrote that in Torah matters it is ineffective, will see that this is where it is possible to repair the prohibition by saying that his act is ineffective. This is unlike where the act has already been done and the prohibition remains in place. The same law applies to lulav as well, and this is obvious.
