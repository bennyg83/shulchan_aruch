3) [Seif 2] At Kiddush on the second night, he should wear a new garment, etc. The Tur cites a dispute concerning this, regarding the blessing of Shehecheyanu at Kiddush on the second night and on the shofar on the second day. Some say that the blessing of time is recited only on the first day, because the two days possess one sanctity and are considered one extended day. Others say that there is no distinction between the Yom Tov of Rosh Hashanah and the other Yamim Tovim of the Diaspora, except regarding an egg and produce attached to the ground, which are forbidden from one day to the other; however, regarding the blessing of time, it is recited on the second day as on other Yamim Tovim. The Tur wrote that his father, the Rosh, of blessed memory, wrote that it is proper for a person to take a new fruit, place it before him, recite Shehecheyanu, and intend that the blessing also apply to the fruit, thereby satisfying the doubt. Maharam of Rothenburg followed this practice as well, see there. The Beit Yosef wrote: "The common practice is to seek a new fruit in order to recite the blessing of time over it at Kiddush. However, regarding the shofar, the blessing of time is not recited at all on the second day unless the first day fell on Shabbos," end quote. These are also his words here in the Shulchan Aruch. However, our master the Rama, of blessed memory, wrote in Darkei Moshe that our practice is to recite the blessing of time over the shofar on both days, while for Kiddush we seek a new fruit. Nevertheless, when they have no new fruit, they recite the blessing of time even without it. So too wrote the Agur, that this is the practice of meticulous people in Ashkenaz, see there. This is what he wrote in the gloss in seif 3: "Some say to recite it, etc., and such is the custom in these lands," meaning in the lands of Ashkenaz. However, in the Sephardic lands the blessing of time is not recited over the shofar on the second day, as the Beit Yosef wrote, as stated above.
