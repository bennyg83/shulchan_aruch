8) [Seif 4] A non-Jew is not included in Zimun. The Gemara asks that this is obvious and answers that the case concerns a convert in Eretz Yisrael who underwent circumcision but not immersion. Bach. Even if he intends to bless our God, he is not included in Zimun. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4.
9) There: Even a convert who underwent circumcision but not immersion, etc. As long as he has not immersed properly, he is considered not to have immersed, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 124, seif 2. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. If he immersed before circumcision, his status is uncertain, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 268, seif 1. Regarding a tumtum, it requires consideration whether he may be compared to one whose organ was severed, mentioned in Yoreh Deah there, or not. It is obvious that Birkas HaMazon is subject to a Torah-level doubt and must be treated stringently, and one of uncertain status cannot discharge one of definite status. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2. Some say that a Jewish apostate who repented requires immersion, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 267, seif 8, and siman 268; see there. Therefore, until he immerses he may be included specifically in a Zimun of three. With ten, where the Name is mentioned, one should be stringent.
10) There: A complete convert, however, is included in Zimun, etc. The Shulchan Aruch likewise rules above in siman 53, seif 19. See our words there, paragraph 73.
11) There: He may recite Birkas HaMazon, etc. This applies even to discharging others. So is implied by Beis Yosef, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4, writes the same.
