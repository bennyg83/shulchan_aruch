Women and slaves, etc.—this is Mishnah beginning of chapter 3 who ate; and in Tur it is written that R' Yehuda Kohen would rule halacha lema'aseh to combine a woman for zimun and would bring proof from Gemara chapter ma she-hayah—whether women are obligated in birkat hamazon d'oraisa or d'rabbanan—practical difference to discharge others—from which it is obvious to him they combine—for otherwise it should require whether they combine; and Rambam responded to his words they do not combine, etc.; and it is difficult how he explains the Mishnah that women and slaves they do not invite zimun on them; and in Mordechai it is written Rabbeinu Simcha did an act to combine a woman for zimun—only there he concludes it was for mention of the Name alone and not other zimun; but R' Yehuda's words in Tur do not imply to explain thus from arrangement of his words—and the dispute that Binyamin brings on this.) And it appears one should say the Mishnah they do not invite zimun on women—means that he give them to bless on the cup of zimun—similar to 'until where does one invite zimun'—Rashi explained it refers to the blesser in zimun; and if so in combination that a man bless with them—it is possible it is permitted; and even though the general rule is with them slaves and minors—and these even for combination are forbidden—nevertheless in this matter they are equal that they not bless in zimun. And this is proven in chapter ma she-hayah that there it does not refer to zimun but without zimun—that is a woman with her husband and her husband does not know birkat hamazon and she knows—for on this the sugya there refers afterward. And on this we are in doubt there—and Rabbeinu Yehuda was precise: why is he not in doubt whether she combines for zimun of three—from which it is obvious she combines for zimun of three—only that she will not bless; and Rambam holds zimun is stricter and it is obvious even for combination it is forbidden—except to discharge her husband in berachah is lighter since he does not know—we are in doubt specifically thus. And here he brought Rambam's view—and his language that even for combination they are forbidden. And further one may say for what Beit Yosef wrote after this in this siman in the name of Kol Bo regarding minor they combine for three—specifically one—for majority of adults is required—so too for women in the Mishnah—meaning if they are the majority one man should not invite zimun for them; but to combine two men and she is third—properly she combines—so is R' Yehuda Kohen's view.
