They combine for zimun—See in Mishna Berurah that it deals with a deaf-mute who speaks and does not hear. It seems simply that specifically for combination; but it is not proper l'chatchila that he be the one inviting zimun to discharge others in birkat hamazon—for we hold in siman 185 seif 2 that l'chatchila he must make heard to his ear; and if so, even though hearing is like answering, nevertheless they are not better than he himself—and berachot remain with them in speech alone without hearing to the ears. Afterward I found in Chiddushei Ra'ah who also wrote thus regarding that it is not worthwhile that he be shaliach tzibur l'chatchila also for this reason see there. And behold when the invitees discharge in birkat hamazon certainly so it is; but as today when they do not discharge in birkat hamazon at all and each blesses for himself and the inviter says only n'varech alone—it is possible to be lenient that he too be the inviter l'chatchila, and it is not comparable to prayer; for even in our time each blesses for himself—nevertheless what the shaliach tzibur repeats in prayer, even though he already prayed himself before, is because the enactment of the Sages did not move from its place; not so here, where the responders who say Baruch she'achalnu, etc.—behold they make heard to their ears; and what difference is it to us if one who says n'varech cannot make heard to his ear—and it requires study.
