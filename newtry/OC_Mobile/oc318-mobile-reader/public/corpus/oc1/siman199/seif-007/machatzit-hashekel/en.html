(s.k. 7) We establish him, etc.—meaning one need not, etc.—do not err in what Rama wrote we establish him as an adult who brought two hairs—meaning his law is like an adult who brought two hairs; and even if we know he did not bring two hairs he combines—on this Magen Avraham wrote; rather meaning in stam we establish him on a presumption that he brought two hairs—for granted for d'rabbanan we rely on presumption that most thirteen-year-olds bring two hairs.

Before thirteen it does not help—for they are mere presumption.

And in Yeshuot Yaakov, etc.—therefore we rely on presumption—for it is per his view a rabbinic matter—and it requires study, etc.—since they ruled the doubt stringently—meaning the doubt was for them on daf 20 folio 2 whether women are obligated in birkat hamazon from the Torah or d'rabbanan; and practical difference: if they discharge men—if from the Torah they discharge; and if d'rabbanan they do not discharge—for d'rabbanan does not come and discharge d'oraisa (for women are not in the category of arevut so much the Rosh); and he wrote above siman 186: since they are a doubt whether they discharge men. And if this regarding a minor whose obligation is not from the Torah—cannot discharge an adult except d'rabbanan—the same for a woman: even if not obligated from the Torah, what does not discharge a man is d'rabbanan; and since this question was not resolved—it should go to leniency that they discharge—for it is a doubt d'rabbanan.

And further in seif 2 there in siman 186 and so in the Gemara—for it asks from this that we taught a son blesses for his father—even though the son is minor and obligated only d'rabbanan, nevertheless he discharges his father who is obligated from the Torah. And it answers the father did not eat to satiety—for also the father is not obligated except d'rabbanan since he does not know, etc.—for they require that the son discharge him even if both know—the mitzvah to separate.

As written siman 193—two who ate—it is a mitzvah to separate; and even so if one of them does not know to bless—the knower blesses and discharges the non-knower—behold we push aside a rabbinic mitzvah they said mitzvah to separate so the non-knower not be idle from birkat hamazon. And for a minor arevut is not applicable. And as explained in Sotah 41 per the count that he calculated there regarding arevut—therefore minors were not in the category of arevut—see there.

In Milchamot part 3—on the above doubt whether women in birkat hamazon are obligated from the Torah.
