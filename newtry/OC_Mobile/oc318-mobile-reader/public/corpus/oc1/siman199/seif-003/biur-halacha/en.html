Am ha'aretz complete, etc.—See in Mishna Berurah what he wrote that all the more so if he is a complete wicked person transgressing sins publicly we do not invite zimun on him; and see in Peri Megadim who wrote there is no contradiction to this from seif 11, which explains there specifically when they excommunicated him for a sin, but otherwise we invite zimun on him—for there it is one sin and here he transgressed many sins; and in my humble opinion it appears there is no distinction in the matter, but for other holy matters such as Torah reading and kedusha and other holy matters requiring ten—certainly even if he transgressed many sins for appetite, as long as they did not excommunicate him he counts toward the number ten—for so is implied from the Mechaber's conclusion above siman 55 seif 11 see there [and also the proof they brought there from Achan that it is written in him Yisrael has sinned—even though he sinned he is Yisrael—also teaches thus, for there there was chillul Shabbat and several other transgressions]; and here for zimun it is reasonable that even if he transgressed one sin publicly b'mezid [such as he ate pig or nevelah and terefah which is a matter publicized among Israel as forbidden] we do not invite zimun on him; and the reason is that the matter of zimun is that his obligation comes because at first they joined together for eating and therefore likewise afterward they need to join to bless Heaven blessed be He on the eating they ate; and on this it says in the Mishnah that if he is am ha'aretz we do not invite zimun on him—and that is because he was negligent in not learning—therefore one does not join with him to invite zimun as the poskim wrote; and therefore Magen Avraham says if he is wicked and transgresses the Torah publicly certainly it is not proper to join with him for zimun [and seif 11 does not deal with such a case]; and by inference it is reasonable that even if they saw him once publicly who denied a sin likewise one does not invite zimun with him. And know that for zimun they were much stricter—for behold certainly even a complete am ha'aretz they combine for ten for kaddish and Barchu and the like; and for zimun it says in the Mishnah we do not invite zimun on him—or that for zimun it is much stricter and as we wrote the reason. And according to all this what is stated above in siman 55 that if they did not excommunicate him he combines for the number ten—that is only for other matters requiring ten; but for zimun with the Name certainly not—for he is no better than am ha'aretz in Talmudic times; and nevertheless it was possible to say further that what the Mishnah says am ha'aretz we do not invite zimun on him—that is specifically for three; but for adding a branch to ten they do make; and according to this the law of Magen Avraham regarding a wicked person who transgresses publicly is also specifically for three and not for ten. And according to this what is stated in Shulchan Aruch above in siman 55 that he counts for ten—is even for zimun with the Name—and it requires study.

We invite zimun on him in this time—See in Mishna Berurah s.k. 2; and all this is regarding whether we combine him for zimun on him; and the reason that because he was negligent in not learning—from the Gemara's law one does not join with him to combine him for zimun, as the Rosh wrote; and all the more so if he does not fulfill mitzvot of the Torah this reason applies; but he himself is certainly obligated in zimun like other Israelites [and there is practical difference whether three such were obligated to invite zimun, or if he sat in a company of fit men who were three besides him—certainly he is not permitted to separate from them and invite them] for one who ate garlic and his odor wafts, etc., and as Rambam wrote in a responsum that even Yerovam ben Nevat who worshipped idols nevertheless is punished also for light matters such as eruv tavshilin and the like; and so is proven in Tosafot Chagigah 22 s.v. keman in the Beit HaSafek see there; afterward I found so too in Shitah Mekubetzet Berachot see there.
