[3] [Levush] And even with her husband, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov wrote this is his language: he wrote in derashot of Maharash that he ate with his wife and called one to drink with him to invite zimun with him and told him he fulfills the final blessing on wine in the berachah of ha-Zan that he heard; and this zimun with his wife is difficult for me—for Rabbeinu Yonah wrote even with their husbands not, because their company is not fitting—until here his language. And I did not know what was difficult for him—for it seems to him like what Tur wrote in the name of R' Yehuda Kohen who ruled halacha lema'aseh to combine a woman for zimun and brought proof from that the Talmud requires [clarification whether] women in birkat hamazon are d'oraisa or d'rabbanan, and he said what practical difference—to discharge others' obligation—for it is obvious to him they combine; for if you do not say so, when it requires whether they can discharge, it should require whether they combine—until here. And incidentally let us say in it a matter—that Bach wrote on Tur's words mentioned above: you might say a great wonder—for we taught explicitly women, slaves, and minors they do not invite zimun on them; and it appears he did not rule except to combine for ten and not for three, and so we conclude—until here. And it escaped me the responsum of Maharam of Rothenburg siman 227 who wrote explicitly she can combine even for three; and so wrote in Agur siman 249; and aside from this you might say on him that if he admits it is forbidden to combine for three—if so still difficult—it should require whether they combine for three. And the main wonder that Bach wondered—one may say R' Yehuda Kohen holds with her husband it is permitted and through this he permitted; and I saw in Derishah who answered that it seems to him the Mishnah says thus: women, slaves, and minors in combinations together do not combine because of promiscuity of slaves with women, and slaves with minors because of homosexuality—until here his language. And I wonder—for if so what does the Talmud daf 47 ask from the Mishnah on R' Yosi who said a minor lying in a cradle—they invite zimun on him—for R' Yosi deals not with combination of women and slaves together but with free males. I still have wonder on Bach who interprets Maharam of Rothenburg's words that Tur wrote ha in the responsum mentioned above explicit to the contrary—see there, for I shortened since it does not affect our law—for I wrote above we practice not to combine a woman at all even for ten; and even though Bach ruled to combine a woman or slave for ten for mention of the Name—I never saw practiced thus; and so wrote Agur siman 244.
