[3] [Levush] Even with her husband, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov writes: “Derashos Maharash records that he ate with his wife and called another person to drink with them so they could recite zimun. He told that person that by hearing Bircas Hazan he had fulfilled the after-blessing on the wine. Regarding zimun with his wife, I find this difficult, since Rabbeinu Yonah writes that even with their husbands women may not join because their company is improper.” I do not know the difficulty. Maharash evidently follows the Tur’s citation of Rabbi Yehudah Kohen, who ruled practically that a woman may combine for zimun. He brought proof from the Gemara’s question whether women are obligated in Bircas HaMazon by Torah law or Rabbinically, and its practical distinction whether they can discharge others. This implies that it was obvious to the Gemara that they may combine; otherwise it should first have asked whether they combine. Bach expresses great astonishment at the Tur, since the Mishnah explicitly states that women, slaves, and minors are not included. He therefore explains that Rabbi Yehudah Kohen permitted a woman to combine only for ten, not for three, and rules accordingly. Bach overlooked Responsa Maharam of Rothenburg, siman 227, which explicitly permits her to combine even for three; Agur, siman 249, writes likewise. Moreover, if Bach agrees that combining for three is forbidden, the Gemara’s question remains: it should have asked whether women combine for three. The principal objection to Rabbi Yehudah Kohen may be answered by saying that he permitted a wife specifically with her husband. Derishah answers that the Mishnah means that women, slaves, and minors may not combine together: slaves with women because of impropriety, and slaves with minors because of concern for homosexual relations. This is difficult, for if so why does the Gemara on 47a challenge Rabbi Yosi, who says that an infant in a cradle may be included, from the Mishnah? Rabbi Yosi discusses an infant joining free adult men, not joining women and slaves. I also wonder at Bach’s explanation of the Maharam of Rothenburg cited by the Tur, since the responsum explicitly says the opposite. I have abbreviated because the matter has no practical consequence for us. As I wrote above, the custom is not to include a woman at all, even for ten. Although Bach rules that a woman or slave may join ten for mentioning Hashem’s Name, I have never seen this practiced. Agur, siman 240, writes likewise.
[4] [Levush] That Bircas HaMazon requires, etc. It appears that Levush thereby resolves Lechem Chamudos’s question, folio 56, that Rama’s rulings here and in siman 193 contradict one another, for there he writes that those hearing must understand. Levush apparently distinguishes: regarding the zimun formula they need not understand, but regarding Bircas HaMazon they must understand. Nachalas Tzvi explains Rama likewise. A reason is still needed. Perhaps we rely here on the authorities who hold that women are never obligated in zimun, but participate only voluntarily. Since there is no actual obligation, Rama follows Rashi and Ri that they fulfill even without understanding. Alternatively, Rama may follow Knesses HaGedolah, siman 62, and Olas Tamid, siman 193, citing Bircas Avraham: the requirement to understand applies specifically when one individual discharges another individual. Where one individual discharges many, such as zimun with three, where one discharges two, and certainly with ten and mention of Hashem’s Name, or a chazzan discharging the congregation in prayer, everyone agrees that they fulfill even without understanding. Accordingly, women fulfill here in zimun, while siman 193 discusses a case without zimun. Levush, however, implies that even when Bircas HaMazon is recited with zimun of three, those who do not understand do not fulfill it, for his statement refers to zimun and he writes, “as I already wrote,” etc. I also examined Bircas Avraham itself, folio 247, where he writes that women, since they do not combine for zimun, are considered like one individual being discharged by another. See what I wrote above in siman 193, seif katan 2, regarding practical conduct. I have therefore abbreviated.
[5] [Levush] It is from the Torah, etc. Olas Tamid holds that zimun is Rabbinic and the verse is merely an asmachta. An androgynos is not obligated, but may voluntarily form a zimun with others of the same status. This is also implied by Tosafos, Berachos 46a, s.v. ad, and Kiryas Sefer writes likewise. Regarding zimun of ten, however, where Hashem’s Name is mentioned, he writes that it is Torah-level, based on “and you shall bless Hashem your God.” This requires study. See below, siman 589 and siman 689. Levush omitted the law of slaves, while Tur and Shulchan Aruch state that women and slaves are not included. Levush must rely on the rule that every mitzvah incumbent upon a woman is also incumbent upon a slave. Olas Tamid writes that since the Mishnah says “slaves” rather than “free men,” it implies that one who is half-slave and half-free may be included. Since the court compels his master to free him, he is treated as though already free. He concludes that this requires study in practice. His meaning is that the Mishnah could otherwise have said that only a free man may be included. This is difficult, for in siman 589 regarding shofar and siman 689 regarding Megillah we rule that one who is half-slave and half-free cannot even discharge another of the same status. It is also difficult because above, siman 143, regarding the requirement of ten free men for Torah reading, Olas Tamid writes that complete slaves are excluded but one who is half-slave and half-free appears obligated. His rulings therefore contradict one another. Above he ruled without qualification, whereas here he leaves the matter requiring study, although it was explained above that the slave component certainly remains. Here, however, it is possible that he may be included because the concern is impropriety, and one who is half-free and whose master is compelled to free him presents less concern. Chagigah 4a also implies that when the Mishnah states “slaves” without qualification, it means complete slaves; otherwise it should have said “slaves who have not been freed.” Olas Tamid’s words may perhaps be understood this way. Examine carefully. See Rokeach, siman 333, and my work on Yoreh Deah, siman 1.
