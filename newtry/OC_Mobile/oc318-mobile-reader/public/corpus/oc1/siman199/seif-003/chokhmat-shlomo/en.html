There, seif 3: A non-Jew is not included in a zimun, and even a convert who underwent brit milah but has not immersed is not included. Note: See Magen Avraham, who writes that this applies whenever he did not immerse properly, as stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 124, seif 2. In my humble opinion, no proof can be brought from there. There the issue is a stringency concerning wine handled by one whose immersion was defective. Here, however, if the conversion is valid by Torah law and the defect in the immersion is only rabbinic, he is a convert by Torah law and is obligated in Birkat HaMazon, so why should he not join the zimun? He is no worse than the categories that the Sages allowed to join where the basic Jewish status exists. An example is immersion that was not performed before a beit din of 3. In Yoreh De'ah, siman 268, the Rif and Rambam are cited as regarding this defect as rabbinic in certain circumstances, and the children born afterward retain their status. The Gemara's wording also indicates that the decisive case is one who underwent brit milah but did not immerse at all. Had an improperly constituted immersion been equivalent to no immersion whatsoever, the Gemara could have stated the greater novelty. The same reasoning applies to the proof from Berachos. Complete absence of immersion prevents completion of conversion, whereas a defect in the manner of immersion that is only rabbinic does not necessarily prevent one from joining a zimun when his Jewish status is valid by Torah law. Therefore Magen Avraham's extension requires study.
