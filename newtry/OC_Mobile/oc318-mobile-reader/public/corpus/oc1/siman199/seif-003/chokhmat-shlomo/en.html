There, seif 3. A non-Jew is not included in zimun, and even a convert who circumcised but did not immerse is not included. Note: See Magen Avraham, who writes that this applies whenever he did not immerse properly, as in Yoreh Deah 124:2. In my humble opinion there is no proof. There the law is stringent, and therefore one who did not immerse properly renders wine forbidden as gentile wine. Here, however, since by Torah law he is obligated in zimun, even if his immersion was invalid only Rabbinically, he is a convert by Torah law and obligated in Bircas HaMazon, and may join. He is no worse than a Kusi, who may be included. An example is one who did not immerse before three judges. Yoreh Deah 268 states that according to Rif and Rambam such immersion is invalid, yet this invalidity is only Rabbinic, since they agree that his children are valid. Their position is also compelled by the Gemara. Rabbi Yehudah says that one who circumcised but did not immerse is as though he did not circumcise. Why did he not teach the greater novelty that even one who immersed, but not before three, is not a convert? Necessarily, even according to those authorities, immersion without three is deficient only Rabbinically. Rabbi Yehudah teaches that one who did not immerse at all is as though he did not circumcise and is not a convert by Torah law. One who immersed outside the presence of three, however, is a convert by Torah law, though not Rabbinically, and therefore may join zimun. There is further proof from Berachos. The Gemara asks why it was necessary to teach that a non-Jew may not be included and answers that it refers to a convert who circumcised but did not immerse, in accordance with Rabbi Chiya bar Abba’s statement that such a person is as though he did not circumcise. If improper immersion also disqualified him from zimun, the Gemara should have taught the greater novelty that even one who immersed, but improperly, is excluded. Since it specifies one who did not immerse at all, it follows that one who immersed, although not properly, may be included, contrary to Magen Avraham. Examine this carefully.
