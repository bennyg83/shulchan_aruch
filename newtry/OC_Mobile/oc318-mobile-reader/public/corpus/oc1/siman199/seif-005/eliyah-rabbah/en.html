[5] [Levush] It is from the Torah, etc. And the view of Olat Tamid is it is d'rabbanan and the verse is merely an asmachta; and an androgynous is not obligated but reshut to invite zimun with his kind; and so implies Tosafot Berachot daf 46 s.v. ad, etc.; and so wrote in Sefer Keriat Sefar; however zimun with ten who mention the Name he wrote is d'oraisa and berachat et Heaven Elokecha—and requires study; and see below siman 589 and siman 689. Levush omitted the law of slaves; and in Tur and Shulchan Aruch it wrote women and slaves they do not invite zimun on them; and one must say Levush relies on the rule that whatever a woman is commanded a slave is commanded. Olat Tamid wrote and it appears from what it teaches slaves and does not teach free men—it implies half-slave half-free they invite zimun on him—and that is the reason since they force his master to make him a free man he is as if already a free man—and requires study for practice—until here his language. And he wanted to say it should have taught they invite zimun only on a free man—and easy to understand. And it is difficult—for below siman 589 regarding shofar and siman 689 regarding Megillah we hold even for himself one does not discharge who is half-slave half-free. And further difficult—for above siman 143 it wrote on what Shulchan Aruch wrote they do not read Torah with fewer than ten free men—and this is his language—to exclude complete slaves and half-slave half-free—it appears he is obligated—until here his language; behold his words contradict. Also above he ruled like stam and here he left for practice in requires study—and above it was explained for law that certainly the aspect of slavery exists on him. And nevertheless here it is possible they invite zimun on him since the reason is promiscuity—and since he is half-free and they force his master there is not so much promiscuity. Also from Talmud Chagigah daf 4 it implies where it taught slaves stam it implies completely—for if not let it teach slaves who are not freed; and it is possible to align thus Olat Tamid's words—and examine well; and see Rokeach siman 343, and in Sifrei Yoreh Deah siman 1.
