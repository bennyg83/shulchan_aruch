(s.k. 5) Even though, etc.—and nevertheless they must understand birkat hamazon, etc.—Levush. He comes to settle the difficulty of Levush who challenged on what Rama wrote even though they do not understand—for Rabbenu Yonah wrote siman 193: when two eat and one does not know Hebrew to bless—the knower blesses and discharges the non-knower; and specifically when the non-knower understands lashon kodesh; and Rama did not distinguish from him. Therefore Levush answered what he wrote in siman 193—meaning he understands birkat hamazon. And what Rama wrote here even though they do not understand—meaning they do not understand berachat zimun; but in birkat hamazon they understand.

And Levush wrote the majority, etc.—and meaning he disagrees with Rama who wrote even though they do not understand. And this he brought from responsum of Rosh who wrote women fulfill through hearing birkat hamazon of men; and it is Rama's view—even though it is not women's way to understand lashon kodesh, nevertheless even if they do not understand they fulfill.

And therefore Levush wrote it is not so; rather Rosh's reason is stam women understand somewhat; but if they do not understand at all—in truth they do not fulfill; and as Magen Avraham wrote siman 193 s.k. 1. And in Sefer Avraham Razal he wrote Levush's reason who distinguishes between birkat hamazon and berachat zimun—for in birkat hamazon one need not understand, for we rely on poskim that women in zimun are reshut; therefore in combination one may rely on poskim that one need not understand; and see further there what he wrote.
