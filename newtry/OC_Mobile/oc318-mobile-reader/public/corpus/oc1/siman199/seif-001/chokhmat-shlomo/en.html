Seif 1 — The waiter who ate a kezayit—they invite zimun on him. In my humble opinion: behold in the Talmud they ask it is obvious and answer lest you say he did not fix [a place], etc. Behold in my composition Sefer HaChaim siman 486 I raised that even for prohibition food between the teeth does not combine and we require a kezayit outside the teeth; nevertheless for a mitzvah, even between the teeth combines from the power of achshaveh—for the mitzvah considers it [as eating] and he wants it to be eating—therefore even between the teeth combines [and there in Sefer HaChaim I did not write from the power of achshaveh but in my chiddushim to Mechaber I wrote thus and compared this to what Beit Yosef wrote in Shulchan Aruch Orach Chayim siman 31: if it is worth in one place it helps for me; if it is not worth in any place it does not help for me. So one may say similarly regarding kezayit: when it is a kezayit, then when lacking between the teeth, since before he ate it was a kezayit, achshaveh helps; but if it is less than a kezayit literally, achshaveh does not help; and for prohibition achshaveh is not applicable for he does not want to become obligated—and on the contrary one may say he ate knowing it was not a kezayit except between the teeth and he will be exempt, therefore achshaveh is not applicable]. And behold one should examine in birkat hamazon what its law is: shall we say since in birkat hamazon it is a mitzvah, it also combines between the teeth; or since there is no obligation to bless lest it be annulled and not blessed, the eating itself is reshut and what is between the teeth does not combine. And apparently it would be proven from here—for what does the Talmud ask it is obvious; shall we say it teaches us that even if he ate only with between the teeth it also combines, and it would be proven from this that it does not combine. However from this there is no proof, for it cannot answer thus—for if so it is difficult why specifically a waiter did it mention; behold this applies to every person; therefore it must answer lest you say he did not fix. But this may prove from what it asks afterward: and the waiter who ate less than a kezayit—it is obvious—and answers since, etc.; and what does it ask; shall we say for at first it said therefore it mentioned waiter, for one would think he did not fix and that is he has no intent to combine. And according to this it teaches that even though he did not fix he still combines; nevertheless one may say thus: this is the distinction—that in any case in him we require a kezayit outside the teeth; therefore it teaches the waiter who ate less than a kezayit—meaning with between the teeth it is a kezayit only if what between the teeth is not combined it is only less than a kezayit; and it teaches that in another person it combines even between the teeth for birkat hamazon—meaning since he fixed we testify that at the time of eating his intent was to combine and bless birkat hamazon and his intent was to be considered eating even with between the teeth; but in a waiter who did not fix, at the time of eating it did not enter his mind to combine—therefore he did not consider it eating even for the teeth; and therefore since he did not consider it at eating time to combine also between the teeth, again it is less than a kezayit and they do not invite zimun on him. And it teaches much: and what does it ask it is obvious; and why should I force and say since; and perforce it is proven that in birkat hamazon even in every person we require outside the teeth; and if so it is difficult why specifically waiter; therefore it answers since—and examine well, for it is correct.
