1) [Seif 1] An attendant who ate, etc. One might have thought that he has no fixed framework because he comes and goes, so the ruling teaches otherwise. Berachos 47b; Beis Yosef; Ateres Zekeinim, paragraph 1. Although he does not establish one location for the meal, since he must go here and there, nevertheless, because this is his usual manner, it constitutes his established framework. Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1. Petach HaDevir in siman 197, however, writes that this law does not apply to the type of attendant common among us. The reason an attendant combines even without establishing himself is that this is his manner of eating: an attendant used to wash his hands, recite HaMotzi, and stand to serve the diners. For an attendant nowadays, however, eating from the diners' table is not his manner of eating. Rather, he afterward reclines at his own table like everyone else, and people are not accustomed to give him anything from the table as they did in the Talmudic era. The Shelah, folio 62, writes similarly. His law therefore reverts to that of any other person, regarding whom Rosh writes that another person who eats like an attendant does not combine because he has no fixed framework; see there. Sedei Chemed, Asifas Dinim, section zayin, paragraph 8, cites this. Therefore, one should follow the local practice: where attendants follow the Talmudic-era practice, an attendant combines even without establishing himself; where the practice is as Petach HaDevir describes, he combines only if he establishes himself with them.
