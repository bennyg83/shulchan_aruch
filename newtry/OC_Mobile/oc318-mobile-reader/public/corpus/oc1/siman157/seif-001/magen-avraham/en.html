The fourth hour. Pesachim 12 implies that these are seasonal hours counted from dawn, since that passage is cited regarding chametz on Pesach, where we calculate in this manner, as stated in siman 443. In Taanis 25, regarding Achav, it appears that six hours are counted from the time he rose from bed; see Rashi there. We must say that Pesachim counts from dawn because most people rise at dawn. This also resolves the practice of prolonging sleep on Shabbos and Yom Tov, sometimes beyond six hours, because the six hours are counted from the time people rise from bed. See siman 288.
The sixth hour. Meaning, the beginning of the sixth hour. He should not delay beyond the sixth hour. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 5.
A stone into a flask. For example, a wine flask that is lacking is filled by throwing in a stone. This accomplishes nothing, and it would have been better to fill it with wine. Shelah, citing Shvilei Emunah. A person should relieve himself before his meal. See Zohar, Parashas Terumah, page 275, for the kabbalistic reason. Berachos 23 states that one who wishes to enter a meal should walk ten times four amos, or four times ten amos, and then relieve himself. A person should not cook food from one day for the next, nor retain it from one day to the next. Zohar, Beshalach, page 109. It is proper to have bran prepared in a utensil so that if one's hand touches milk, he can wash it with bran. Sefer Chassidim 58 and 371.
