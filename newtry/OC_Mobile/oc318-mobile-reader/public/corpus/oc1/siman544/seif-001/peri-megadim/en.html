And the same applies. On the Taz: Magen Avraham note 5 brought the words of Beis Yosef: any need of a mitzvah that is permitted on the moed, it is permitted to take wages for it; see siman 542. That is: a mitzvah for the need of the moed, whether layman's work or craftsman's work, and there are provisions for food together with a mitzvah, or communal needs needed on the moed, and the like: wages are permitted. And the proof is from Tosafos, Moed Katan 6a, s.v. "demozlei gaben," who challenged according to the view below on 13a that even a laborer who has nothing to eat is forbidden to take wages, and from this likewise for melacha permitted on the moed, wages would be forbidden even if he has nothing to eat; yet from the Mishnah it is evident that they take wages for permitted melacha. And they answered that a mitzvah is different; and from this Beis Yosef learned that wherever there is a mitzvah, even for an individual, if it is permitted on the moed according to the ways explained above, it is permitted to take wages even if he has what to eat. And what is written in the gloss in the name of the responsa of the Rashba, of blessed memory, and the Rivash (he brought them in Darkei Moshe; in Beis Yosef I found only the responsa of the Rashba, of blessed memory): that is communal needs on the moed that involve bodily benefit, a bathhouse and the like, and it is permitted even as craftsman's work. But building a synagogue that is needed on the moed, or another similar mitzvah that has no bodily benefit: craftsman's work is forbidden, but layman's work is permitted, and wages are permitted for layman's work. And if so it is settled satisfactorily; and the intent of the Taz, of blessed memory, requires further study; and see Eliyah Rabbah; and see what I wrote on the Taz note 2 and Magen Avraham regarding this, with Hashem's help.
