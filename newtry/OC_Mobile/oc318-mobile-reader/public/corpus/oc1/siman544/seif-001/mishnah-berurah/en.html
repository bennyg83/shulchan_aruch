(1) Communal needs are permitted etc. - Know that all communal needs are permitted even if they intended their melacha for the moed, and also in public and with great exertion. And the reason they permitted intending one's melacha [for the moed] is that all communal needs are completed only at a time when they are all idle and gather together, and if they do not do it now the matter will be nullified. [Therefore if they have a leader who has authority to do it alone, it is forbidden for him to intend his melacha for the moed.] And in this there are two distinctions: if it is craftsman's work, it is not permitted unless it is a need of the moed; and if it is layman's work, it is permitted even if it is only a need for after the moed.
(2) For example etc. - And even craftsman's work is permitted in this, since this matter is needed also on the moed.
(3) And to remove obstacles from them - In the Gemara it is stated that they are obligated in this, so that it not be like one who sheds blood if an obstacle occurs; see there.
(4) And to fix the mikvaos - And the same applies that l'chatchilah it is also permitted to make a mikveh on Chol HaMoed, similar to digging a pit that is explained in seif 2; and specifically when it will be finished on Chol HaMoed and it will be a need of the moed, for otherwise it is forbidden, because it is craftsman's work.
(5) Communal needs such as these etc. - And the same applies to fixing a bathhouse for communal need, for there is bodily distress if one does not bathe; and in this too, specifically if it will be finished on Chol HaMoed and it will be a need of the moed, if it is craftsman's work.
(6) For example, building a synagogue - He mentioned this for an additional novelty: that even building a synagogue, which is a mitzvah in order to pray in it on the moed with a minyan of ten, and even if they have no other place to pray with a minyan of ten, it is likewise forbidden, for this is not a need of the human body; and even if they already began to build it and on the moed they need only to complete it, it is forbidden because this requires craftsman's work. The poskim wrote that nowadays building a synagogue is something that would be lost, for we are concerned that if he waits until after the moed, the gentiles will prevent building it. And in the book Maamar Mordechai he wrote that it all depends on the place and the time, and a judge has only what his eyes see; see there.
(7) To do on the moed - He wrote in Shaarei Teshuvah: they customarily arrange communal needs on Chol HaMoed, to appoint gabbaim and community leaders. And it appears to me that if one needs to write regarding this matter, he should not write Ashuris script, but rather meshita or our script; for regarding Ashuris script, which is craftsman's work, communal needs were permitted only when it is for bodily need, as above.
(8) Needs of a mitzvah etc. - That is to say, that the distinction of a mitzvah does not avail to permit craftsman's work on that account even when he needs it on the moed; and layman's work is permitted for the need of the moed even when there is no mitzvah in it, as above in siman 541, seif 5.
