And the same applies regarding other needs of a mitzvah. Beis Yosef at the end of this siman brings the words of Tosafos on what the Gemara said: they go out [to clear] kilayim on the moed because then laborers are hired cheaply. And Tosafos challenged according to the view that wages for work, for one who has nothing to eat, are forbidden: how can they hire laborers? And they answered that perhaps one may say that these cases of a mitzvah they permitted. And Beis Yosef wrote on this: and even though we hold like the view that wages for work are permitted when he has nothing to eat, nevertheless one should learn that where it is for the need of a mitzvah, even though the laborer has what to eat, it is permitted, end quote. And this contradicts what Rama wrote here, that for other needs of the mitzvah it is forbidden, and that is from the words of the Rivash that Beis Yosef brings. And at first glance it appears to me to distinguish: specifically a positive commandment, even though it is incumbent upon a person to do it, nevertheless he can do it after the moed; in this [case] it is forbidden, and on Chol HaMoed he sits idle. But where there is a prohibition upon a person to sit idle, as in that case of kilayim, for one who maintains kilayim is lashed, it is certainly permitted to hire a laborer even if he has what to eat. But there is a difficulty against this from what is written in the following siman, that they do not proofread even one letter, and there there is a prohibition because of "do not cause an offense to dwell in your tent." And therefore it appears to my humble opinion that one should not learn from these words of Tosafos a permission for a mitzvah. And their difficulty it appears to my humble opinion to resolve: that going out on kilayim is among communal needs, for the Tanna counted it among other communal needs at the beginning of Moed Katan in the Mishnah, and in this it is permitted as is written in the following seif. Also Tosafos wrote their answer only by way of "perhaps"; but from the words of the Tur in the following siman it is evident that a mitzvah is permitted, and see there.
