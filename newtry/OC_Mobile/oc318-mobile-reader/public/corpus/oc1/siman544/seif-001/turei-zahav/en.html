And likewise for other mitzvah needs. Beit Yosef at the end of this siman brings the words of Tosafot on what the Gemara says: they go out for kilayim on the festival because then workers are hired cheaply, and Tosafot challenged the one who says worker's pay when he has nothing to eat is forbidden — how can they hire workers? And they answered: perhaps one could say that these of mitzvah they permitted. And Beit Yosef wrote on this: and even though we hold like the one who says worker's pay is permitted when he has nothing to eat, nevertheless one can learn that where it was for mitzvah need, even if the worker has what to eat, it is permitted — end of his words. And this contradicts what the Rama wrote here regarding other mitzvah needs that it is forbidden, and this is from the words of Ribash whom Beit Yosef brings. And at first glance it appears to me to distinguish that specifically on the moed, even though it is incumbent on the person to do it, nevertheless he can do it after the festival — in this it is forbidden, and on Chol Hamoed one sits idle. But where there is a prohibition on the person to sit idle, such as the case of kilayim where one who maintains kilayim is lashed — certainly it is permitted to hire a worker even if he has what to eat. However a question arises on this from what is written in the siman after that one does not proofread even one letter, and behold there is a prohibition because of "do not place an obstacle in your tent" — and therefore it appears to me that one should not learn from these words of Tosafot permission for mitzvah, and their question it appears to me to answer that going out for kilayim is public needs, for the tanna counted it among the other public needs at the beginning of Moed Katan in the mishnah, and through this it is permitted as written in the seif after. Also Tosafot did not write their reasoning except by way of "perhaps"; but from the words of the Tur in the siman after it is proven that mitzvah is permitted — see there.
