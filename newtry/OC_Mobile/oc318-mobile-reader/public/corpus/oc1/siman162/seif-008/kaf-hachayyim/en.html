8) The Shelah writes there on folio 81b in a gloss: 'I received a tradition that after one washes and raises his hands, before reciting the blessing, he should say the verse, Raise your hands in holiness and bless Hashem. This is not considered an interruption.' Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, and Siddur Beis Oved, paragraph 1, cite him. The words of the Arizal, of blessed memory, however, do not mention saying this verse. Chesed LaAlafim in siman 166, paragraph 1, similarly writes that some are concerned not to say 'Raise your hands in holiness' before the blessing over netilas yadayim, so as not to interrupt between washing and the blessing. Ruach Chaim there likewise writes that according to the mekubalim, one should say neither 'Raise your hands in holiness,' etc., nor the verse, 'I shall raise my palms to Your commandments,' etc., except before washing his hands. He writes the same in his sefer Kaf HaChaim, siman 22, paragraph 1. See above, siman 4, end of paragraph 16, and below, siman 166, paragraph 4.
