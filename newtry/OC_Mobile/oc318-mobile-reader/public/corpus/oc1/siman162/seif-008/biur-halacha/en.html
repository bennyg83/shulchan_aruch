The Mechaber copied this from Rabbeinu Shimshon's commentary to chapter 2, mishnah 2, explaining the Tosefta: if one dried his hand after the first water, his hands became pure and do not require second water. Rabbeinu Shimshon writes the same at the beginning of Yadayim. Beis Yosef likewise cites Rabbi Yitzchak Aboab in siman 165, as Magen Avraham cites here. Why, then, was second water required at all rather than simply drying the hands? Rabbeinu Shimshon explains in another version at the beginning of Yadayim that one might not be careful and could touch the impure water with which he initially dried his hands. The usual practice is therefore to pour a second time to purify the hands. Rav Ovadiah of Bartenura writes similarly, and this also appears from Rashba in Mishmeres HaBayis. Accordingly, although the practice of second water was instituted because of this concern and, strictly speaking, one who already dried his hand does not require second water, once the Sages instituted second water a person cannot initially exempt himself through drying. Otherwise, second water would never be required, since drying is necessary even after the second water, as the Gemara in Sotah states that everyone who washes his hands must dry them. Tur and Shulchan Aruch above in siman 158 similarly exempt only one who immerses his hands or washes with a revi'is; if drying always sufficed, second water would never be needed. The first version of Rabbeinu Shimshon at the beginning of Yadayim does imply that the second water is not a fixed enactment and depends on the person's choice. The other poskim do not imply this; they state the law of second water as an established element of washing. See Tosafos, Semag, Semak, Eshkol, Raavad, Sefer HaTerumah, Shibbolei HaLeket, Mordechai, the Rosh's rulings, Rabbeinu Yerucham, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch's wording in se'if 2. Tosafos, Sefer HaTerumah, Semag, and Mordechai explain the Mishnah in chapter 2 stating that one copious pouring is pure as referring specifically to a pouring as abundant as two. Rambam likewise writes plainly in his commentary and code that one washing requires first and second water, though he holds this was taught only for terumah rather than ordinary food, and we do not rule like him on that point. Only when one pours a revi'is at once do several Rishonim hold that second water is unnecessary; Raavad is stringent even then, as Beis Yosef explains, holding that second water is never eliminated even when much water is poured at once. It follows from all this that initially second water is specifically required. The Tosefta cited by Rabbeinu Shimshon at the beginning of Yadayim, which states that one dried his hand and then reconsidered and poured second water, would be understood by these poskim as a case in which he dried after the fact. Our version of the Tosefta does not say that he reconsidered; one could say that even after drying he requires second water, following Raavad cited in Toras HaBayis HaAruch. As a matter of law, if he already dried his hand, one may be lenient in accordance with Shulchan Aruch and the later authorities here. Initially, however, it appears that he specifically requires second water unless he pours a revi'is at once, in accordance with the plain wording of the Rishonim listed above. The Tosefta in chapter 2 says that if he rubbed his hand on his head it is pure. Rabbeinu Shimshon proves that this must refer to after the first water and that the hand is purified thereby. The poskim listed above must either interpret this as after the fact, or explain that it does not discuss drying at all, as Beis Yosef explains, but merely rubbing to remove perspiration. Its statement that the hand is pure means only that it did not become impure from his head as it would from an unwashed hand; second water is nevertheless required, as everywhere else, for after the first water the hand itself is pure but second water is needed to purify the water. This entire ruling of Shulchan Aruch concerns a case in which the required washing measure reached the hand and the vessel contained a revi'is; without a revi'is in the vessel there is no washing at all. He poured part of the revi'is over one hand; for one need not pour the full revi'is over one hand, since a revi'is suffices for two hands; and the hand became pure, but second water was required to purify the water. Shulchan Aruch rules that drying suffices for this. Eliyah Rabbah, citing Maadanei Yom Tov.
