One who washes his hands pours, etc. Since the poskim state this without qualification, it appears that he need not dry his hands between the washings. This is obvious according to Shulchan Aruch and Levush, who regard the preliminary pouring as part of the washing itself. Even according to what we write below (that it is merely preparation for the washing) it appears that the water is not rendered impure by this preliminary washing so as to require drying. This resembles the ruling at the end of siman 160, se'if 11, that touching the water before washing does not invalidate it. Lechem Chamudos writes similarly, as cited by Magen Avraham below in paragraph 10.
Some of the revi'is. This law originates in Tosafos, Chullin 106. Although the Mishnah in Yadayim mentions only two pourings for washing, Tosafos writes that one must first pour an additional time to remove dirt. Semag, the Rosh, and Tur also cite this. Removing an intervening substance is itself explicit in a Mishnah and baraisa in Yadayim; their novelty is that one should not think it valid to remove the obstruction with the water poured for the actual washing. A third, preliminary pouring is specifically required to remove it. Beis Yosef and Shulchan Aruch understand them to mean that this preliminary water comes from the same revi'is. This is very difficult at first glance, because the required revi'is is thereby diminished before the washing. Even according to the opinion cited above in siman 160, se'if 13, that one need not pour the entire revi'is over one person's hands, everyone agrees that a complete revi'is must initially be present in the vessel for the first person who washes. Levush already noticed this and answered that all the water serves the purification of the hands. This still does not fully resolve the difficulty, for removing the obstruction plainly appears to be only preparation for the washing. Tosafos itself does not compel Beis Yosef's interpretation. It states only that a preliminary pouring is required to remove the obstruction, teaching that it may not be removed during the actual washing; this can readily mean with other water rather than the revi'is designated for washing. This also appears to be Tosafos's intent. Tosafos itself writes that the Mishnah in Yadayim concerns clean hands; thus, the measure of a revi'is applies to clean hands, and what basis is there to reduce that measure when the hands are dirty? As a matter of law, even if we accept Beis Yosef's interpretation of Tosafos, it is a novel ruling not mentioned by the other Rishonim. Raavad explicitly implies that the two pourings may never contain less than a revi'is, writing that this is the measure Chazal determined as sufficient to wash the hand properly. That measure applies even to clean hands; if some is deducted to remove an obstruction, the washing will not be valid. Raavad's words are cited by Kol Bo. It is therefore proper initially to be stringent. If one must clean his hands and has only a revi'is of water, he may clean them with water invalid for washing or another cleansing substance.
