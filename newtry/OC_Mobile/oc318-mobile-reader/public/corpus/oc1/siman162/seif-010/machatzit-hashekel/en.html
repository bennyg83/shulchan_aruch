Seif katan 10, "upon the second." "And if he touched," etc., "he need not dry them," etc., because they had already become pure. Yam Shel Shlomo explains that the rule in seif 4 is different. There, when only first waters had been poured and the hand touched the other, unwashed hand, those waters then contained 2 forms of impurity: first, the ordinary impurity of first waters that results from washing the hand; second, the additional impurity that came from contact with the other impure hand. When he washes the first hand a second time, the second waters cannot purify 2 separate forms of impurity, for we find that second waters purify only 1 type of impurity, namely the impurity the water received from the hand that was washed. Here, however, where a revi'it was poured or the first hand had already been washed twice and its water had become pure, contact with the second hand introduces only 1 new form of impurity. Therefore another proper washing can purify it. This is the distinction underlying the Shulchan Aruch's rulings. If another person's impure hand touched the water, the rules explained above apply. Where drying is required, he must first dry and then wash again. I had difficulty with the wording cited in the previous seif katan concerning grasping the vessel, because when one pours a revi'it over the hand, the hand is already pure. If he then touches the vessel and later touches it again, the analysis depends upon whether actual impure water was transferred back to the hand. Similarly, when he holds the vessel by its handle with his second hand before that hand has been washed, this alone does not render the water impure, because the water does not become impure merely through the impure hand's contact with the outside of the vessel, as explained here.
