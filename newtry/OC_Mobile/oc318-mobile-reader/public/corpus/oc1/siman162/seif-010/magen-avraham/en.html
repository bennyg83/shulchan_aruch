And there is a bandage upon it. This is difficult, for a bandage interposes, as written in siman 161. One may answer that here it is speaking where he is not particular about it. Lechem Chamudos. The Bach answered that here it is speaking where there is a wound on his hand, as the Rosh wrote, and this is his language: Although it is forbidden to eat with a cloth, by decree lest he touch, here it is different, for there is no concern that he will touch the flesh beneath the bandage, since he does not remove it because of the pain of the wound. Until here. Earlier, however, it is speaking where he has a mere discomfort, and there is concern that he may remove the bandage and touch the food; therefore he must wash beneath the bandage, and there is an interposition. Until here is his language. This appears to be the primary explanation.
