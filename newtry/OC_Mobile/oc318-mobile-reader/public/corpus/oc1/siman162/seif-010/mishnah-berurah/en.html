(67) And a bandage - See siman 161 seif katan 4 in Mishna Berurah.
(68) It is enough for him, etc. - This refers to where he cannot remove it because of the pain of the wound, as we wrote above there in Mishna Berurah seif katan 5, see there. Therefore it is enough for him, and this is not washing by halves, since he cannot wash the other half; it is like one whose finger was cut off, who washes the rest of the hand.
(69) The rest of the hand - If he cannot wash his hands at all because of illness, he should wrap his hands in a cloth.
(70) So that he not touch the bandage - It should read: so that the water not touch the bandage, as Peri Megadim writes. That is, whenever some of the first waters spilled onto the bandage, the second waters cannot purify them, and afterward the water on the bandage returns onto the hand and renders it impure, as above in seif 8 regarding a pebble or splinter. This is why the Mechaber concludes, "so that they not return," etc.
(71) Or he should pour a revi'it, etc., for then they were not rendered impure - Meaning, even if the water later returns, there is no concern. The Acharonim wrote that this advice is better than the first method, for it is impossible to be careful to pour exactly adjacent to the place of the bandage while ensuring no water reaches the bandage. They further wrote that after washing, when he comes to eat, it is good to wrap the bandage with a clean white cloth to cover the filth of the bandage. The Acharonim wrote that if the bandage fell off during the meal, he must wash again, like the law of a cloth in the manner explained below in siman 163 where this is permitted; for if he removed the cloth, it is forbidden to eat so, and the same applies here.
(72) One washes mayim rishonim, etc. - That is, the water before the meal, excluding mayim acharonim, as below in siman 181 seif 2.
