The words of the geonim, meaning Rabbeinu Yonah and Rama, require examination, etc. The hands impart impurity only within the joint, meaning the area that requires washing according to each view, as explained at the end of siman 161. According to the Rosh, this extends to the point where the fingers join the palm. Water poured first within that area becomes impure through contact with the hands. If it then flows beyond the joint, it remains impure even if second water is poured there, because second water does not purify outside the required washing area, as Magen Avraham explains below.
Second water outside the joint is of no consequence. Such water is intrinsically pure, and outside the joint it does not contract impurity.
Therefore he must raise his hands. If he began raising them and the first water flowed beyond the joint before he poured the second water, that water remains impure, since it cannot be purified there. He may not later lower his hands, lest the impure first water return.
Even if he washes up to the arm. Even according to the first opinion at the end of siman 161, the view of the Rif, that the required washing extends to the arm, one must still be concerned that the water traveled beyond that point.
The same applies if he wishes to lower them. In that case the water certainly did not travel beyond the joint. The Rashba holds that even according to the view requiring washing only to the base of the fingers, the area from there to the arm can contract impurity and can be purified by second water. The concern is that the first pouring may extend slightly beyond the base of the fingers, while the second pouring may not reach equally far, leaving some of the first water impure.
Or if he poured the second water farther than the first. Although the second water purified the first water beneath it, the portion of the second water that extended beyond the first is itself considered first water at that location and becomes impure.
And it will return and render impure, etc. This means when it flows back down onto the fingers. The arm itself is not the place whose impurity is under discussion.
When immersing, etc. Beit Yosef writes that one must likewise raise the hands. Although according to the Rashba and the Aruch the area above the required washing can contract and lose impurity, the rule that a reviit or immersion water does not contract impurity from the hands applies only within the required area. The Shulchan Aruch later retracted this point and accepted that a reviit or immersion is effective even beyond the ordinary washing area, as the halachic authorities write in the name of the Tosefta.
This is in accordance with the Rashba. According to the Rosh, however, even if he washed up to the arm he must raise his hands, as Magen Avraham wrote above.
It must therefore be said that he retracted. Meaning, the Shulchan Aruch consistently follows the Rashba, and this explains what he wrote, that this applies when one does not wash the entire hand, etc. As for what he then wrote, "and likewise, if possible," etc., "a reviit," etc., and likewise one who immerses, etc., in this point the Shulchan Aruch retracted here from what he had written in the Beit Yosef. He now holds that even according to the Rashba and the Aruch, washing with a reviit or immersion is effective, because even outside the required washing area, a reviit and immersion water do not contract impurity.
For the poskim wrote this. They wrote in the name of the Tosefta that if one washed with a reviit or immersed, he need not raise his hands. It is possible that this gloss, "and the same applies if he lowers them," should appear after the "some say" written by the Rama regarding the Mechaber's statement that if one washed the entire hand he need not raise it. The Rama then writes that some say otherwise, meaning the Rosh, and according to him it is indeed effective if one keeps the hands lowered from the outset.
Seif katan 1. The fingertips, etc. He need wash only the fingers. Rashi follows his own approach in his commentary to Chullin, chapter Kol HaBasar, that one need not wash beyond them, and therefore one likewise need not raise the hands beyond them.
It is impossible to explain it this way, because nothing of this was written at the end of siman 161. This is the correct reading. Meaning, if so, one would have to say that the Rama follows Rashi, that one need wash only the fingers. This is the view of the Rosh and the second opinion cited at the end of siman 161. Since the Beit Yosef wrote there that it is proper to follow the practice of washing up to the arm and the Rama did not comment, it implies that he too does not rule like the Rosh.
In my view, his intent is, etc., and he should not dry them. If he raises his hands only enough that the water reaches the arm, the remaining water will be removed when he dries his hands. But if he raises them excessively and the water runs beneath his clothing, it may remain there impure because people do not ordinarily dry beneath their sleeves.
It is forbidden to lower them. Rashi explains why the Gemara requires raising the fingertips. If they were lowered while pouring, the water would run down the sides and fail to reach their tips. Once one raised them at the beginning, one may not lower them afterward because of the Rosh’s concern that impure first water may have traveled beyond the washing area and then return. Accordingly, one whose hands are held level should turn them from side to side while washing so that the water reaches the lower edge of the hand as well. See citations 17, 200.
