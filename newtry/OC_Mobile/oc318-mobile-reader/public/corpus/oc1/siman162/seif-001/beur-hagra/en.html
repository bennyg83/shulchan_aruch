(א) Seif 1 — one who washes, etc. Rashi's and Seder HaTefillah's method: there is no concern that water left beyond the joint and returned, unless only the first water went beyond the joint and not the last; and likewise within the joint this concern would apply, but within the joint one must in any case wash between first and last — see Rashi there. Beyond the joint, all agree as he holds — until the place netilah is required. Rosh wrote on this that due to his sharpness he did not examine the mishnah: the mishnah teaches hands become impure and purified until the joint — how? He took the first until the joint and the second beyond the joint and they returned to a pure hand; he took the first and second beyond the joint and they returned to an impure hand — end of his words. Meaning second water does not purify first water beyond the joint; this is what it teaches and purifies until the joint. Rashi's text cannot be grasped differently, as poskim wrote and as Rashi wrote there. Ra'ash, Rambam, and Shulchan Aruch hold like Rosh. Rashba in a responsum wrote the concern is not that water left the hand itself afterward beyond the joint, but the water one pours beyond the joint becomes impure there from the hands and returns to the joint; the mishnah holds like the baraita until splitting of fingers, and hands are impure until the joint of the palm. He wrote but for us who hold netilah until end of palm, this is unnecessary; therefore he omitted this law in Tur — his view is like Rif that netilah is until end of palm as I wrote; but per Rashi and Rosh there is no distinction and one must always raise. Arukh too holds like Rashba's explanation, but holds the forearm too is an impure place, therefore one must always raise. Mahari Avraham challenged Rashi and Rosh: if concern is water leaving and returning, lowering should also help; answer: we are concerned for Rashba's concern. He wrote per Rif and Rashba who require netilah until end of palm, Rashba's concern does not apply as above; Beit Yosef answered it is not so — rather one should not raise and then lower; and although per Arukh we always concern as above, nevertheless he is singular, for all poskim hold impurity is not beyond the palm. Mordechai and Semag in Tosafot's name: one who immerses hands need not raise; Semak wrote likewise when pouring revi'it in chapter 1 — their reason: they hold like Rosh that concern is water leaving and returning; but for immersion and revi'it there is no impure water at all, likewise per Rashi; but per Rashba and Arukh there is no distinction — see all this in Beit Yosef.
