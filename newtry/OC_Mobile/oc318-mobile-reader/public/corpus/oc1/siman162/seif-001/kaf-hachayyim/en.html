1) [Seif 1] One who washes must raise his hands, etc. A mnemonic for this is, 'He shall take them and lift them.' Tur. The Rav, of blessed memory, writes the same in Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Eikev. The poskim, the Tur, and Maran, of blessed memory, explain that the reason for raising the hands is so that the water will not pass beyond the joint and then return and render the hands impure, as explained in the Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch. Accordingly, the Shulchan Aruch writes that if one poured a revi'is all at once or immersed his hands, he need not raise them. The Rav, of blessed memory, however, gives a kabbalistic reason in Shaar HaMitzvos there why one must always raise the hands after washing. He further writes there that since our kavanah in netilas yadayim is to drive away the kelipos that cling to the hands, one raises them after washing so that the kelipos will not return and cling to them as before. One must therefore raise them directly opposite the head, or at least opposite the face; see there. According to this, there is no distinction. Even if one pours a revi'is all at once or immerses his hands, he must raise them.
2) We will now explain the order of washing, rubbing, and raising the hands according to the Arizal, of blessed memory. First, one should take the vessel in his right hand and give it to his left. With his left hand he should pour three consecutive times over his entire right hand, meaning the fingers together with the palm up to the wrist. He should then take the vessel in his right hand and pour three consecutive times over his entire left hand in the same manner. Afterward, he should rub both hands together three times. He should then raise his hands opposite his head and join his two arms in such a way that the elbow joints do not protrude backward, corresponding to the verse, 'All their back parts faced inward.' He must also open his palms like one who wishes to receive something in them. This alludes to receiving purity. Only the tips of his fingers should be slightly raised upward. He should recite the blessing over netilas yadayim, then lower his hands from opposite his head and dry them. Although Shaar HaMitzvos there, s.v. the matter of netilas yadayim, etc., states that one should pour twice consecutively over each hand and then rub once, nevertheless, from what it writes afterward, s.v. the matter of washing and eating, etc., that when washing the right hand one should have kavanah for Havayah of numerical value 72, and for the left hand Havayah of numerical value 63, etc., it follows that one should wash and have kavanah as in the morning netilas yadayim. This means the simple, filled, and fully expanded forms of Havayah of numerical value 72 for the right hand; likewise, Havayah of numerical value 63 for the left hand; and for the rubbing, Havayah of numerical value 45. That is, during the first pouring on the right hand one should have kavanah for the fully expanded Havayah of numerical value 72; during the second pouring, for the filled Havayah of numerical value 72; and during the third, for the simple Havayah. Although the simple Name corresponds to Kesser and the filled Name to Chochmah, etc., as is known, the third pouring uses the simple Name rather than the first because the order of netilas yadayim is that the final pouring is greatest of all, as written below in paragraph 13. One can also explain that this follows the order in which the mochin are drawn, first in Binah, then in Chochmah, and then in Kesser, proceeding upward from below, as is known to those familiar with the hidden wisdom. Examine this carefully. The same order applies to the left hand, but with Havayah of numerical value 63, as written in Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar HaBerachos, chapter 3, and in Olas Tamid, Shaar HaTefillah; see there. The Rashash, of blessed memory, likewise arranged the kavanah for netilas yadayim before eating in the same manner as the morning netilas yadayim. The morning washing must alternate, once on this hand and once on the other, because of Shibta, as written in Shaar HaKavanos, folio 1d, which we cited above in siman 4, paragraph 12; see there. Netilas yadayim before eating, however, need not alternate, since there is no concern of Shibta. Rather, one pours three consecutive times over each hand. Regarding rubbing, although Pri Etz Chaim and Olas Tamid imply that once suffices, the Rashash, of blessed memory, arranged that one rub three times both for the morning washing and for washing before eating. He set forth their kavanos: during the first rubbing, one should intend the fully expanded Havayah of numerical value 45; during the second, the filled Havayah of numerical value 45; and during the third, the simple Havayah; see there. R' Shemen Sasson, of blessed memory, volume 4, on Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Eikev, writes that the passage in Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Eikev, from s.v. the matter of netilas yadayim through s.v. the matter of washing and eating, is not from the Rav, of blessed memory, but from Maharam Alshich, of blessed memory. The Rashash, of blessed memory, likewise arranged kavanos only for what the Rav, of blessed memory, wrote; see there. See Ben Ish Chai, parashas Shemini, paragraph 4, and examine carefully. See also what we wrote above in siman 158, paragraph 5, and siman 4, paragraph 16. The following is the order of the kavanos for washing and rubbing, both in the morning and before a meal.
2) Right hand, first pouring: Yod: Yod, Vav, Dalet. Hei: Hei, Yod. Vav: Vav, Yod, Vav. Hei: Hei, Yod.
2) Second pouring: Yod, Hei, Vav, Hei, the filled Havayah of numerical value 72.
2) Third pouring: Havayah in its simple form.
2) Left hand, first pouring: Yod: Yod, Vav, Dalet. Hei: Hei, Yod. Vav: Vav, Alef, Vav. Hei: Hei, Yod.
2) Second pouring: Yod, Hei, Vav with Alef, Hei, the filled Havayah of numerical value 63.
2) Third pouring: Havayah in its simple form.
2) First rubbing: Yod: Yod, Vav, Dalet. Hei: Hei, Alef. Vav: Vav, Alef, Vav. Hei: Hei, Alef.
2) Second rubbing: Yod, Hei with Alef, Vav with Alef, Hei with Alef, the filled Havayah of numerical value 45.
2) Third rubbing: Havayah in its simple form.
3) If one must walk several amos from the place where he washed to the place where he will eat, he should raise his hands only slightly, so that the water will not return and render his hands impure. When he reaches the place where he will eat, he should raise his hands opposite his head as explained above and immediately recite the blessing. After the blessing, he should lower them from opposite his head, but they should remain slightly raised so that the water does not return and render the hands impure, and he should dry them. He should not raise them fully before the time of the blessing, as stated in the holy Zohar, parashas Naso, folio 145a: 'It is forbidden for a person to raise his fingers upright for no purpose, except in prayer and blessings,' etc.; see there. Whether walking constitutes an interruption is discussed below in siman 166, paragraphs 6 and 10.
4) There: One must raise his hands, etc. Some explain that the hands render liquids impure only up to the joint, while liquids that touch beyond the joint remain pure. Likewise, the second water purifies the first water only while it remains within the joint. If some of the first water within the joint passes beyond it and he then pours second water over it, that second water does not purify the water beyond the joint. If that water returns to the hand, it renders the hand impure. Therefore, one must raise his hands so that the first water that passed beyond the joint will not return and render them impure. This follows the view of the Rosh, the Tur, and those who agree with them, as explained in the Tur and Beit Yosef. The Rashba, however, writes in a responsum that anyone who washes through the upper joint, meaning the end of the palm where it joins the arm, and then applies second water through that same joint, need not raise his hands. What concern is there? He applies the second water wherever he applied the first. There is no concern for water that passes above that joint onto the forearm, because it has no impurity at all, as the Sages decreed impurity only upon the hands. The incident in the first chapter of Sotah concerns one who washed only through the division of the fingers and therefore had to raise them; see there. The Beit Yosef cites him and writes that this is also the view of the Agur in the name of R' Avraham HaKohen; see there. This is why the Shulchan Aruch writes here that one who washes through the connection of the hand need not raise his hands, following the Rashba and Agur. This is also why the Rama, of blessed memory, glosses upon the Shulchan Aruch, 'Some disagree.' He means the Rosh and Tur, who hold that even if one washed through the connection of the hand, he must raise it, since it is impossible to be exact. Some of the first water may pass beyond the joint, where the second water cannot purify it, and it might return and render the hands impure. Therefore, he must raise them. Examine this carefully. Magen Avraham writes the same at the beginning of the siman; see there. See below, paragraph 9. The Taz, paragraph 2, however, explains that the dissenting view cited by the Rama, of blessed memory, is that of the Aruch, cited by the Beit Yosef, who holds that water that passes beyond the hand renders the forearm impure; see there. See Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5, and below, paragraphs 9 and 10.
5) There, in the gloss: Meaning the tips of his fingers. Rashi, of blessed memory, writes this. The Beit Yosef comments that Rashi's reason appears to be that he holds that for non-sacred food one need wash only through the place where the fingers join the palm. Therefore, he need raise only the area that requires washing; see there. The Rama, of blessed memory, cites this here regarding the Shulchan Aruch's words because the Shulchan Aruch likewise discusses the view that one need not wash the entire hand through its connection with the arm, as written at the end of siman 161. According to the authorities who require washing through the connection with the arm, however, the Shulchan Aruch writes that one need not raise the hands. See Magen Avraham and Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1, and examine carefully. Magen Avraham writes there that the intent is that one should not raise the hands completely, for then water will pass onto the forearm beneath his clothing and will not be dried. Later, while eating, it may return and render his hands impure; see there. It appears that this is uncommon. Even if a little water passes onto the forearm beneath the clothing, it is absorbed by the garment. It is very uncommon for enough water to pass beneath the garment and then return. Therefore, one need not be concerned and should raise the entire hand completely, in accordance with the unqualified ruling of the Shulchan Aruch. Although our custom is to wash through the connection with the arm, as written above at the end of siman 161, and according to Maran, of blessed memory, one who washes through the connection with the arm need not raise his hands because water that passes beyond the joint is pure, as written in the preceding paragraph, nevertheless, according to the Arizal there is a kabbalistic reason to raise the hand opposite the head when reciting the blessing, as written above in paragraph 2; see there. Therefore, one should raise the entire hand completely and need not be concerned that water will pass beneath the garment and return. First, since our custom is to wash the entire hand, according to Maran, of blessed memory, the water that passes beyond it is pure. Second, even according to the Rosh, one can say that it is extremely uncommon for enough water to pass out and then return while eating. Examine this carefully.
6) There, in the gloss: The same applies if he lowers them, etc. The Bach questions these words of the Rama. He writes that although R' Beit Yosef explained the matter this way in his sefer and Mahari Chaviv agreed with him, the Beit Yosef did not rely upon this view in practice. In the Shulchan Aruch he wrote, 'One who washes must raise his hands upward,' etc. How, then, can the Rav rule in his gloss that it is proper to keep the hands lowered from beginning to end? In the Bach's humble opinion, Rashi's words indicate precisely that if one lowers his fingers, as explained in the following paragraph, he has not fulfilled his obligation and eats without netilas yadayim. This is the ruling we accept. Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot Beit Yosef, paragraph 7, and Olas Tamid, paragraph 2, cite him. Maharshal in Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 8 of Chullin, siman 55, likewise rules that one must specifically raise them; see there. The Shelah writes the same in Shaar HaOsiyos, folio 81a, and Recanati writes the same in parashas Eikev according to Kabbalah. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, cites them. We also wrote above in paragraph 1 that according to the Arizal, of blessed memory, one must specifically raise them; see there. See the following paragraph.
7) The Bach writes there that one must raise his hands at the beginning so that the fingertips will also be washed. There is no doubt that if he points his fingers downward, the water will reach only the length and sides of the fingers, but not the fingertips facing downward. Therefore, he must initially raise the fingertips so that the water will also flow over their ends, and he thereby fulfills the obligation of netilas yadayim; see there. The Shelah similarly writes there in a gloss: 'I have seen many people pour over the hand from the side, leaving the fingertips unwashed. It is as though they did not wash them. Rather, one must raise the fingertips, meaning that he should bend the hand so that the fingertips are above the palm. He should not raise the entire hand vertically, but should raise the fingers above the palm'; see there. It appears that even if a person holds his hands in this manner, the water will not reach the entire hand. The conclusion from the foregoing is therefore that one must pay attention while washing and turn his hands so that the water reaches the entire hand and every side. Examine this carefully.
8) The Shelah writes there on folio 81b in a gloss: 'I received a tradition that after one washes and raises his hands, before reciting the blessing, he should say the verse, Raise your hands in holiness and bless Hashem. This is not considered an interruption.' Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, and Siddur Beis Oved, paragraph 1, cite him. The words of the Arizal, of blessed memory, however, do not mention saying this verse. Chesed LaAlafim in siman 166, paragraph 1, similarly writes that some are concerned not to say 'Raise your hands in holiness' before the blessing over netilas yadayim, so as not to interrupt between washing and the blessing. Ruach Chaim there likewise writes that according to the mekubalim, one should say neither 'Raise your hands in holiness,' etc., nor the verse, 'I shall raise my palms to Your commandments,' etc., except before washing his hands. He writes the same in his sefer Kaf HaChaim, siman 22, paragraph 1. See above, siman 4, end of paragraph 16, and below, siman 166, paragraph 4.
9) There, in the gloss: Some disagree about this. See what we wrote about this above in paragraph 4, what Eliyah Rabbah writes in paragraph 4 that the dissenting view appears primary, and what we wrote in paragraph 1 in the name of the Arizal, of blessed memory, that raising the hands prevents the external forces from clinging to them; see there. Therefore, one should raise his hands even if he washed the entire hand through its connection with the arm. The practical manner of raising them is to take care that the water does not return from the forearm and render the hands impure from the time of washing until drying. This means after the second water, or, according to the Arizal, who requires three pourings, after the third water. Before then, even if the water returns, he will pour additional water over it to purify it. When reciting the blessing, he should raise his hands opposite his head, or at least opposite his face. Immediately after the blessing he should lower them from opposite his head, leaving them only slightly raised so that water does not return from the forearm to the hands, and then dry them, as written above in paragraphs 2 and 3; see there. After the fact, however, if he did not raise his hands, he need not wash a second time. The concern is only that the water might return. Furthermore, our custom is to wash through the connection with the arm, and according to Maran, of blessed memory, one then need not raise the hands. We also customarily pour three times, and some say that one who pours three times need not raise them. For all these reasons, it appears that after the fact, if he did not raise his hands, he need not wash again.
10) There: Likewise, if he poured a revi'is over both hands at once, etc. All the more so if he pours a revi'is over each hand at once. Taz, paragraph 3. The Beit Yosef, however, writes that according to the Aruch, who explains that one must raise his hands because water that passes beyond the hand renders the forearm impure, even one who immerses his hands must raise them. There remains a concern that water may pass beyond the joint and then return and render the hands impure. Maran, of blessed memory, did not take the Aruch's view into account here in the Shulchan Aruch because he had already written earlier in the Beit Yosef, regarding the Aruch's words, that the commentators do not agree; see there. For us, however, we already wrote above in paragraph 8 that according to the Arizal, of blessed memory, one must raise the hands in every circumstance, even if he poured a revi'is at once and even if he immersed them; see there.
