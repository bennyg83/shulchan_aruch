1) [Seif 1] One who washes must raise his hands, etc. A mnemonic for this is, 'He shall take them and lift them.' Tur. The Rav, of blessed memory, writes the same in Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Eikev. The poskim, the Tur, and Maran, of blessed memory, explain that the reason for raising the hands is so that the water will not pass beyond the joint and then return and render the hands impure, as explained in the Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch. Accordingly, the Shulchan Aruch writes that if one poured a revi'is all at once or immersed his hands, he need not raise them. The Rav, of blessed memory, however, gives a kabbalistic reason in Shaar HaMitzvos there why one must always raise the hands after washing. He further writes there that since our kavanah in netilas yadayim is to drive away the kelipos that cling to the hands, one raises them after washing so that the kelipos will not return and cling to them as before. One must therefore raise them directly opposite the head, or at least opposite the face; see there. According to this, there is no distinction. Even if one pours a revi'is all at once or immerses his hands, he must raise them.
