"Upon them." See Taz and Magen Avraham for the reason. "For all of this." If another person whose hands are impure touched them, see Magen Avraham. A vessel with a hole in its bottom in which a stopper is inserted, and the stopper is pushed upward so that the water flows out, may be used. Although the stopper is moist and he touches it with his hands, there is no concern. Lechem Chamudot. Magen Avraham writes that one should nevertheless be concerned that after washing the first hand, when he again pushes the stopper with that wet hand, some of the impure water adheres to the stopper. When he then pushes it with the second hand, that hand can become impure from the moisture. He should therefore pour a revi'it at once. Likewise, if he grasps the vessel with his wet hand, the handle becomes impure from the water. If he then grasps it again with the first hand, that hand becomes impure from it and must be dried, as stated in seif 4. Therefore he should pour a revi'it at once. It is also effective to pour first and second waters separately over each hand. See Magen Avraham.
