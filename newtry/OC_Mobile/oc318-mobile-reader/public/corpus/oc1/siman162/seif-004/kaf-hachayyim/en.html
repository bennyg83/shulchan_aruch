4) There: One must raise his hands, etc. Some explain that the hands render liquids impure only up to the joint, while liquids that touch beyond the joint remain pure. Likewise, the second water purifies the first water only while it remains within the joint. If some of the first water within the joint passes beyond it and he then pours second water over it, that second water does not purify the water beyond the joint. If that water returns to the hand, it renders the hand impure. Therefore, one must raise his hands so that the first water that passed beyond the joint will not return and render them impure. This follows the view of the Rosh, the Tur, and those who agree with them, as explained in the Tur and Beit Yosef. The Rashba, however, writes in a responsum that anyone who washes through the upper joint, meaning the end of the palm where it joins the arm, and then applies second water through that same joint, need not raise his hands. What concern is there? He applies the second water wherever he applied the first. There is no concern for water that passes above that joint onto the forearm, because it has no impurity at all, as the Sages decreed impurity only upon the hands. The incident in the first chapter of Sotah concerns one who washed only through the division of the fingers and therefore had to raise them; see there. The Beit Yosef cites him and writes that this is also the view of the Agur in the name of R' Avraham HaKohen; see there. This is why the Shulchan Aruch writes here that one who washes through the connection of the hand need not raise his hands, following the Rashba and Agur. This is also why the Rama, of blessed memory, glosses upon the Shulchan Aruch, 'Some disagree.' He means the Rosh and Tur, who hold that even if one washed through the connection of the hand, he must raise it, since it is impossible to be exact. Some of the first water may pass beyond the joint, where the second water cannot purify it, and it might return and render the hands impure. Therefore, he must raise them. Examine this carefully. Magen Avraham writes the same at the beginning of the siman; see there. See below, paragraph 9. The Taz, paragraph 2, however, explains that the dissenting view cited by the Rama, of blessed memory, is that of the Aruch, cited by the Beit Yosef, who holds that water that passes beyond the hand renders the forearm impure; see there. See Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5, and below, paragraphs 9 and 10.
