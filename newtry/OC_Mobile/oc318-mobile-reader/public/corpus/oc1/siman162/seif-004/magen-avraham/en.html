Its fellow became impure. The same applies to the water on one hand: it became impure from the second hand, as stated above, and see seif 7.
That he should pour for him. Meaning, when he washes both hands together, he may rub them, as stated in seif 5. And if you ask: let him pour over each hand twice and afterward rub them. One may answer that he must rub during the first pouring in order to wash away the sweat properly; see the Beit Yosef in the name of the Rashba. The Bach wrote in the name of the Maharshal that when one pours over one hand and raises that hand, and the water goes beyond the joint, then when he goes back and takes the vessel with that hand to pour over the second hand, he will need to lower the hand that he washed first, and the water will return and render the hands impure, as written in seif 1. And if he took the vessel with his wet hand, the handle of the vessel became impure from the water; and if he then grasped it again with his first hand, his hand became impure from it and he must dry it, as written in seif 8. Nevertheless, it is possible to pour the first and second waters over each hand separately, but the clearer explanation is as I wrote.
Over each one a reviis. This implies that if he poured a reviis over one hand and it touched the other, it became impure from the other. This is difficult, for in seif 1 I wrote that a reviis does not receive impurity. One must say that nevertheless the water on the hand does receive impurity at the time of washing, for in the place of washing and at the time of washing it easily becomes impure, as written at the end of siman 160, seif 11; see there. Alternatively, in any case there is not a reviis on the second hand, and the water on the second hand became impure. See what I wrote at the end of seif 7.
Over the second. If one touched, with an impure hand, one hand that had already received the second waters, he need not dry it; rather, he afterward puts water upon it, for since it had already become entirely pure, even though it became impure from the other hand, the second waters help. Yam Shel Shlomo, seif 33; see the end of seif 7. A vessel that has a hole in its bottom and a stopper inserted into it, and he presses it upward so that the water emerges onto his hand, is permitted. Even though the stopper is wet and he touched it with his hands, we have no concern, as written at the end of siman 160, seif 11. Lechem Chamudos. Nevertheless, there is reason to be concerned that after he washed the first hand, when he again presses the stopper with his wet hand, impure water adheres to the stopper, and when he again presses it with his second hand, it becomes impure from the wet water. Therefore, he should wash with a reviis at once.
