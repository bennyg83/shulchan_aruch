(32) "He poured water," etc. This refers to one who poured only the first waters, using part of a revi'it on 1 hand, and then rubbed it with the second hand, which had not been washed, because he thought that this rubbing would count as washing the second hand as well. The same law applies if the rubbing was done merely to remove sweat or dirt, or if there was simply incidental contact. (33) "It did not count," etc. It did not count for the second hand, and the first hand also became impure through contact with the other hand, as explained below. (34) "They became impure." The first waters are considered impure because they became impure through contact with the hand during washing, as explained above in seif 2. (35) "And when he rubbed it, it became impure," etc. This new impurity is more severe than a case in which the hands had not been washed at all. Water that became impure on the hand from this external contact will not be purified even if many kavim of water are poured over it, until the hand is first dried. So explains the Rosh. (36) "The other hand." Consequently the first hand itself also became impure, and it too must be dried, because its water received impurity through contact with the second, impure hand. Second waters purify only the impurity originating in the hand itself; they do not purify impurity that came upon the water from another source. Accordingly, if one poured first waters separately over both hands and then rubbed the hands together, the waters on the hands make each other impure and second waters do not suffice to purify them, as explained below in seif 6. (37) "Therefore one who pours," etc. Since the other hand became impure and thereby the first hand became impure as well, he must first dry both hands and then wash them again properly. Without drying, repeated pouring is ineffective. (38) "Therefore one who washes," etc. Since one must be careful that 1 hand not touch the other before the washing is complete, and this is a common source of error, it is preferable to use 1 of 3 methods: another person pours a revi'it over both hands at once; he does this himself in the manner explained below; or he pours a revi'it at once over each hand separately. In these ways the hands become completely pure, and afterward their touching each other causes no problem. On the contrary, it is a mitzvah initially to rub them together. (39) "Upon them." Meaning upon both hands at once. They are then considered like 1 hand and do not become impure by touching each other during the washing, as explained below in seif 5. (40) "And if he has no other person," etc. When another person is available, it is preferable that the other person pour, because when he washes by himself while holding the vessel with his fingertips, it is difficult to ensure that the water reaches the hands and the fingertips properly. (41) "Upon both hands at once." In this manner 1 revi'it is sufficient, and second waters are not needed. (42) "Or he should pour a revi'it on each one." Meaning that he should hold the vessel normally and pour a revi'it at once upon each hand separately. If he pours a revi'it only on 1 hand, that hand can become impure by touching the other. (43) "And afterward he rubs them." This applies to all the methods just mentioned. In each of them there is no longer any concern that rubbing will cause impurity. (44) "For the one who pours," etc. Meaning that through this method the purification of the hands is complete, and there is no longer any concern from their touching each other. (45) "If another person touched them." Even if he had already washed with second waters, or poured a revi'it at once so that his hands were completely purified, if another person whose hands were not washed touched them, he must dry his hands and wash them again.
