7) The Bach writes there that one must raise his hands at the beginning so that the fingertips will also be washed. There is no doubt that if he points his fingers downward, the water will reach only the length and sides of the fingers, but not the fingertips facing downward. Therefore, he must initially raise the fingertips so that the water will also flow over their ends, and he thereby fulfills the obligation of netilas yadayim; see there. The Shelah similarly writes there in a gloss: 'I have seen many people pour over the hand from the side, leaving the fingertips unwashed. It is as though they did not wash them. Rather, one must raise the fingertips, meaning that he should bend the hand so that the fingertips are above the palm. He should not raise the entire hand vertically, but should raise the fingers above the palm'; see there. It appears that even if a person holds his hands in this manner, the water will not reach the entire hand. The conclusion from the foregoing is therefore that one must pay attention while washing and turn his hands so that the water reaches the entire hand and every side. Examine this carefully.
