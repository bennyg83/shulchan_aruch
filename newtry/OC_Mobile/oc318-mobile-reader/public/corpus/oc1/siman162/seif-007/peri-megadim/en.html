If he washed one hand. See Taz, who cites Beis Yosef here. When both hands were washed separately with a reviis on each, there is no need to explain the ruling as involving residual water of purity. This is correct. Rema, Magen Avraham, and the commentators discuss how rubbing can be effective when the hands touch each other. The case must be one in which sufficient water remains upon them, or where the second hand is washed from the first hand’s residual water in the manner described by the Rishonim. If each hand received less than a reviis, the first water becomes impure and can render the other hand impure when they touch. If each hand received a full reviis at once, the water does not become impure and the hands may touch. When one hand was washed and then rubs the other before the second washing, he must be careful that the unwashed hand not render the washed hand impure. See siman 165, seif katan 2. The essential distinction is between water that remains first water and water that has already become impure through contact with an unwashed hand. The language of Shulchan Aruch and the Tur should be understood accordingly. See siman 12. See siman 140.
