If he washed one hand, etc. See the Taz, who cites the words of the Beit Yosef here; see there. What he wrote, that when each of the two hands was washed separately there is no need to explain the case as involving residual water of purification, is absolutely correct; see what I wrote regarding the Magen Avraham, seif katan 15 and 16. However, what he explained, that the case is where he poured the second waters and rubbed the hand against his head, and nevertheless the hand is impure when he subsequently touches those waters, I have not merited to understand. This is the opposite of what the Rosh writes in chapter 2, Mishnah 2 of Yadayim, and what the Bartenura writes in chapter 1, Mishnah 1. When the Rosh writes, "Nevertheless, perhaps rubbing is always required," he means drying, lest one say that the entire discussion of eating without touching concerns a case where there were no second waters. But he appears to understand that the Rambam was uncertain whether even where second waters were poured and the hand afterward touched them, it becomes impure. This requires study. See Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 18; at present this requires study. See siman 165, seif katan 2, on this matter, with God's help.
The author says: In light of the many particulars explained in this siman and the preceding simanim, an average person must reflect and say: perhaps I have never in my life washed my hands properly. In an ordinary pouring, in most cases the water does not reach every place on the entire portion of the hand that requires washing. If you would say that such a very small minority of the surface is not so important, that is mistaken. With immersion, if even a small part of the body remains outside the water, by Torah law the immersion is invalid. If you ask that a small amount to which one is particular invalidates only rabbinically, or that a majority to which one is not particular does not invalidate by Torah law, nevertheless in those cases we can still say that "all his flesh" is in the water, whereas if he extended one finger outside the water, by Torah law it is not an immersion. The same follows for washing the hands: wherever even one required place did not receive water, the washing is invalid. This is as the Bach writes here in seif 1 concerning the fingertips according to Rashi; see there. The water must also reach between the fingers everywhere. It would therefore appear that this is why the hands must be rubbed after washing, as the Rama writes in seif 3 and the Taz in seif katan 4, because there are places that the water did not reach and rubbing causes it to reach them. The first waters together with rubbing and drying suffice, as the Magen Avraham writes in seif katan 16; see also the Taz here. Concerning immersion in nine kav, I do not know: if the water does not reach every part of the body without leaving any place untouched, I do not know what benefit there is.
Also in siman 161, seif 4, some say that one must wash to the arm, while others say only to the place where the fingers join the palm; see Taz, seif katan 6. According to this, the thumb likewise requires washing through the end of its two joints. What the Beit Yosef writes, that it is only to one joint, is according to the opinion that for ordinary food two joints are required, whereas according to the Rosh three joints are required and the thumb has two. The most proper practice is to wash to the arm, using a reviis for each hand separately, in which case there is no issue of impure waters. Even so, care is still required, and the matter requires study.
Regarding what the Taz writes, that the water was absorbed into the cloth, see Berachos 50b concerning a cloth on the table.
