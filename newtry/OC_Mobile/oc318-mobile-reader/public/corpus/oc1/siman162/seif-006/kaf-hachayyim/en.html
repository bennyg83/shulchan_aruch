6) There, in the gloss: The same applies if he lowers them, etc. The Bach questions these words of the Rama. He writes that although R' Beit Yosef explained the matter this way in his sefer and Mahari Chaviv agreed with him, the Beit Yosef did not rely upon this view in practice. In the Shulchan Aruch he wrote, 'One who washes must raise his hands upward,' etc. How, then, can the Rav rule in his gloss that it is proper to keep the hands lowered from beginning to end? In the Bach's humble opinion, Rashi's words indicate precisely that if one lowers his fingers, as explained in the following paragraph, he has not fulfilled his obligation and eats without netilas yadayim. This is the ruling we accept. Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot Beit Yosef, paragraph 7, and Olas Tamid, paragraph 2, cite him. Maharshal in Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 8 of Chullin, siman 55, likewise rules that one must specifically raise them; see there. The Shelah writes the same in Shaar HaOsiyos, folio 81a, and Recanati writes the same in parashas Eikev according to Kabbalah. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, cites them. We also wrote above in paragraph 1 that according to the Arizal, of blessed memory, one must specifically raise them; see there. See the following paragraph.
