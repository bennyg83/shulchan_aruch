As it was, etc. Another pouring is required up to the joint, followed by the second pouring. Nachalat Tzvi. It is obvious that if one pours three times, the first pouring may be done in sections, because two additional pourings follow. The purpose of the first pouring is to remove dirt, and dirt can also be removed in sections.
[Levush] And it is tahor. When does this apply, etc. The Bach is stringent even where the first water remains wet enough to moisten something else. He concludes that he saw his teachers act stringently and not wash from a vessel commonly called a vessel with a handle and tap, because the first pour cannot reach the entire required area in one flow. The world is lenient and has authorities upon whom to rely. Nevertheless, with such a vessel one must be careful to keep manipulating the handle continuously during the entire flow of water. The Taz is surprised at those who are stringent, because where the flow never stops everyone should consider it one pouring. He was not precise, because the tap must be closed each time. Perhaps such a brief interruption is not considered a break. See above siman 159, seif katan 15. Magen Avraham infers from the Bach that one should not wash from a vessel with a narrow mouth. This is incorrect. The Bach agrees that if one can pour continuously without interruption, it is permitted. Some make a vessel with a hole in its bottom and a peg inserted through the hole, thick at its upper end. When one wishes to wash, he pushes the peg upward with his fingertips and the water flows over his hands. This is preferable to a tap that must be repeatedly opened and closed. Although the peg is ordinarily wet and touches his hands, this is not a concern, as stated in siman 160. So writes Lechem Chamudot. Magen Avraham adds that one should nevertheless be concerned that after washing the first hand, tamei water remains attached to the tap, and when he presses it with the second hand that hand becomes tamei from the moisture. Therefore he should pour a full reviit at once. It also seems to me that a solution is to receive the water on both hands simultaneously. The same should be done with a handled tap vessel. Note this well.
