3) If one must walk several amos from the place where he washed to the place where he will eat, he should raise his hands only slightly, so that the water will not return and render his hands impure. When he reaches the place where he will eat, he should raise his hands opposite his head as explained above and immediately recite the blessing. After the blessing, he should lower them from opposite his head, but they should remain slightly raised so that the water does not return and render the hands impure, and he should dry them. He should not raise them fully before the time of the blessing, as stated in the holy Zohar, parashas Naso, folio 145a: 'It is forbidden for a person to raise his fingers upright for no purpose, except in prayer and blessings,' etc.; see there. Whether walking constitutes an interruption is discussed below in siman 166, paragraphs 6 and 10.
