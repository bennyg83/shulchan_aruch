5) There, in the gloss: Meaning the tips of his fingers. Rashi, of blessed memory, writes this. The Beit Yosef comments that Rashi's reason appears to be that he holds that for non-sacred food one need wash only through the place where the fingers join the palm. Therefore, he need raise only the area that requires washing; see there. The Rama, of blessed memory, cites this here regarding the Shulchan Aruch's words because the Shulchan Aruch likewise discusses the view that one need not wash the entire hand through its connection with the arm, as written at the end of siman 161. According to the authorities who require washing through the connection with the arm, however, the Shulchan Aruch writes that one need not raise the hands. See Magen Avraham and Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1, and examine carefully. Magen Avraham writes there that the intent is that one should not raise the hands completely, for then water will pass onto the forearm beneath his clothing and will not be dried. Later, while eating, it may return and render his hands impure; see there. It appears that this is uncommon. Even if a little water passes onto the forearm beneath the clothing, it is absorbed by the garment. It is very uncommon for enough water to pass beneath the garment and then return. Therefore, one need not be concerned and should raise the entire hand completely, in accordance with the unqualified ruling of the Shulchan Aruch. Although our custom is to wash through the connection with the arm, as written above at the end of siman 161, and according to Maran, of blessed memory, one who washes through the connection with the arm need not raise his hands because water that passes beyond the joint is pure, as written in the preceding paragraph, nevertheless, according to the Arizal there is a kabbalistic reason to raise the hand opposite the head when reciting the blessing, as written above in paragraph 2; see there. Therefore, one should raise the entire hand completely and need not be concerned that water will pass beneath the garment and return. First, since our custom is to wash the entire hand, according to Maran, of blessed memory, the water that passes beyond it is pure. Second, even according to the Rosh, one can say that it is extremely uncommon for enough water to pass out and then return while eating. Examine this carefully.
