(א) Tofach, etc. — thus it is resolved, etc., in chapter 2 of Gittin 16b: Ilfa asked whether hands are pure in halves or not; the gemara explains the question — such as he took half the hand and afterward added and took the other half (this is the law mentioned here in Shulchan Aruch); the gemara challenges from R. Yanai who said hands are not pure in halves; it answers R. Yanai deals with when he took the second half the water on the first half was already dried so there is not a tofach on condition to rub; Ilfa's case is when water on the first half is still tofach on condition to rub. Magen Avraham found this difficult: initially he did not raise the distinction between tofach on condition to rub or not — before challenging from R. Yanai he should have challenged from mishnah Yadayim we do not add to first water — if he took half a hand he may not afterward add and take the other half, because hands are not pure in halves; also he could answer the mishnah deals where there is no tofach on condition to rub; or from the mishnah we do not learn — mishnah deals l'chatchila and Ilfa deals b'dieved if he already took in halves, as the wording hands are pure in halves implies; therefore he needed to resolve from R. Yanai who also used that wording implying even b'dieved it does not help. Since the external reasoning did not distinguish tofach on condition to rub, in any case hands are not pure — this too was the challenger's reasoning from R. Yanai, but Ilfa innovated this reasoning per the gemara's conclusion that perhaps with tofach on condition to rub hands are pure in halves; since it was not resolved and netilah is rabbinic, we are lenient in doubt; therefore one may not add to be lenient except where we find Ilfa doubted — b'dieved; but l'chatchila we do not find Ilfa doubted — the external reasoning remains that even with tofach on condition to rub it is forbidden.
