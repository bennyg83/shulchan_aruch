In Taz, seif katan 7, "after he rubbed," etc. His words show that he explains the Shulchan Aruch's statement, "although so long as he has not rubbed, they are purified through rubbing," as follows. The difficulty is why this contact should render the hand impure, since there is a means of purification through the second waters together with rubbing. Taz answers that because the new contact occurred after the rubbing, the situation is worse. This is also clear from his later words, where he questions Levush: what was Levush's difficulty, when R' Shimshon and the Shulchan Aruch explicitly teach that rubbing can worsen the situation? If the rubbing itself were what purified the water, why would rubbing a second time not help after the hand was touched again? The same type of impure water was previously on the hand and the hands were purified through rubbing. Even now, after renewed contact, one could seemingly rub again. This is the meaning of the phrase that he "returned and touched," and the matter is straightforward in light of Magen Avraham, seif katan 16. Taz, seif katan 16, cites Maharshal as requiring rubbing in every case, and one might wish to prove from there that the second waters do not purify until rubbing. This is not correct. Mishbetzot Zahav explains that Maharshal means that even after second waters, drying remains necessary because of the rule that one who eats without drying his hands is treated as though he ate impure bread. The second waters certainly purify the first waters. The hands nevertheless require proper drying. This is also the intent of R' Shimshon.
