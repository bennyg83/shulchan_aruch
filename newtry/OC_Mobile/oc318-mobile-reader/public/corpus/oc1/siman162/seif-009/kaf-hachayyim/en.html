9) There, in the gloss: Some disagree about this. See what we wrote about this above in paragraph 4, what Eliyah Rabbah writes in paragraph 4 that the dissenting view appears primary, and what we wrote in paragraph 1 in the name of the Arizal, of blessed memory, that raising the hands prevents the external forces from clinging to them; see there. Therefore, one should raise his hands even if he washed the entire hand through its connection with the arm. The practical manner of raising them is to take care that the water does not return from the forearm and render the hands impure from the time of washing until drying. This means after the second water, or, according to the Arizal, who requires three pourings, after the third water. Before then, even if the water returns, he will pour additional water over it to purify it. When reciting the blessing, he should raise his hands opposite his head, or at least opposite his face. Immediately after the blessing he should lower them from opposite his head, leaving them only slightly raised so that water does not return from the forearm to the hands, and then dry them, as written above in paragraphs 2 and 3; see there. After the fact, however, if he did not raise his hands, he need not wash a second time. The concern is only that the water might return. Furthermore, our custom is to wash through the connection with the arm, and according to Maran, of blessed memory, one then need not raise the hands. We also customarily pour three times, and some say that one who pours three times need not raise them. For all these reasons, it appears that after the fact, if he did not raise his hands, he need not wash again.
