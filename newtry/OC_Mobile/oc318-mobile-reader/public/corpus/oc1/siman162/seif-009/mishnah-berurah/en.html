(62) One must be careful, etc. If he was not careful and, after pouring the second waters, a pebble or splinter was found on his hand, his hands are impure. He must dry them and wash again with both the first and second waters as required. If, however, he removed the pebble after the first waters and then poured the second waters, or if the pebble or splinter was washed away by the second waters, the matter requires study, for perhaps this is effective. I found that Peri Megadim and the commentary of the Gaon R. Mordechai Banet on the Mordechai express doubt about this. See there.
(63) That there should not remain, etc. This applies even when they are loose and water can reach beneath them, because the disqualification here is not due to an intervening substance, but for the reason explained below.
(64) Or any other object. See Magen Avraham and the other Acharonim, who write that even something whose origin is from water, such as red gnats, or snow and hail that were not crushed, has the same status as a pebble or splinter. Peri Megadim, however, is uncertain whether snow and hail should disqualify the washing if they are found on one's hand afterward. See what we write about this in Biur Halachah. Know that according to our practice of washing each hand with a revi'is, none of this has practical consequence, as explained below.
(65) For water does not purify, etc. Meaning, the reason for the disqualification is that the water upon the pebble became impure through contact with his hands and then returns and renders the hands impure. The second waters that he pours do not help, because they purify only the water upon the hand, not the water upon the pebble.
(66) If he pours a revi'is, etc. As explained above in seif 8.
