And when the time arrives he should feel them and recite the blessing. Nevertheless he is called one who transgresses by doing so — and this applies specifically when he walks on foot or rides, for he cannot sleep while walking; but one who travels in a wagon may not leave them on, lest he sleep in them (Lechem Rav from Maharam of Padua). It seems this is an excessive stringency, for even if he sleeps a casual sleep while they are on his head it is permitted, as Mahari wrote, and the Rosh on the statement of the Sages: a person should not hold tefillin in his hand, etc. Therefore there is no concern in such a case at the hour when one sets out on a journey, for presumably at that time when one sets out on a journey, before daylight, it is impossible for him to sleep a substantial sleep. Moreover there are many clear proofs from the Talmud and poskim that one need not be stringent like Maharam of Padua's stringency; therefore one who is stringent may be stringent, and one who is lenient has not lost.
