Section 3 — "and he blesses." But in the hagahah of Semak he wrote: specifically per the one who says night is not time for tefillin; but according to us, who hold night is time for tefillin, he blesses at night — and so too the Tur cites from him; and according to his method, who holds he comes like the one who says night is not time for tefillin — and specifically because of concern lest they be lost, and there is no mitzvah in donning them; but according to us, who hold night is time for tefillin, only that rabbinically it is forbidden lest he sleep — in such a case, where he does not don for the sake of the mitzvah, he blesses; but from the words of the Rif and Rosh, who omitted this concern of lest they be lost, it follows they hold like the one who says night is time for tefillin; and nevertheless he wrote "and when the time arrives," etc. — meaning, even per the one who says night is time for tefillin; and this is what the Tur means. And the Rosh did not write thus; and even though regarding tzitzis he wrote that per the one who says the time of tzitzis applies, one may bless even at night, as was explained in the incident of Rav Yehuda — one may distinguish, since here it is not permitted except in such a case.

That there is no concern, etc. Hagahat Semak — and meaning: even though it is forbidden per the main text to leave them on, as written in seif 2, since we hold night is time for tefillin and the prohibition is only lest he sleep — therefore he wrote stated in the hagahah: and it appears that even without concern lest they be lost, it is permitted upon arising early, etc. — for there is no concern lest he sleep, etc. And so he omitted in the Shulchan Aruch; and even though in the Gemara it says "lest they be lost" — that is per the first Tanna, who holds night is not time for tefillin, as was explained regarding "until when," etc. From here is proof for Rashi's explanation that to guard them it is permitted even to leave them on — and even per the one who says night is not time for tefillin; and so is the plain sense of the Gemara, that R' Eliezer said "and if to guard them it is permitted" — and this refers to that which was written above: "anyone who leaves tefillin on," etc. — and there it deals specifically with one who dons, as written in seif 2; and all the more so according to what we hold, that night is time for tefillin — for even the Rambam concedes that to leave them on to guard them is permitted, for the baraita is arranged thus, as was written here. Therefore the Rambam omitted it — for he holds it is per the Sages, who hold night is time for tefillin; and he explained like the one who says night is not time for tefillin, even though he strained to say the first clause of the baraita is not the words of the first Tanna — even though we find likewise at the beginning of Berachos 3a. Nevertheless the words of the Shulchan Aruch require investigation — for here he explained unlike the Rambam, whom he omitted, and in seif 2 he explained like the Rambam; and the Rambam explained only according to his method — and in this too Rashi disagrees, as above.
