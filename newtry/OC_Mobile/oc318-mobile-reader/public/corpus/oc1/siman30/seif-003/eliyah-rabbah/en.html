[3] Lest he sleep, etc. Those traveling in a wagon may not put them on, lest they fall asleep. Levush. Lechem Chamudos, Taz, and Nachalas Tzvi agree. In Migdal David, however, I saw that he challenges Levush and permits it in practice even in a wagon, because we do not suspect that he will fall asleep during such a short period. Although his proof can be rejected, in pressing circumstances one may rely upon him. Magen Avraham cites Levush as saying that even one riding an animal is forbidden. This is surprising, and perhaps the word is a scribal error. Lechem Chamudos, page 95, writes that on Tisha B'Av, when tefillin are worn at Minchah, one should not remove them before Maariv. The public custom of removing them is because not everyone knows the law, and the Sages of the generation may not instruct otherwise, since this ruling is not taught publicly. Malbushei Yom Tov likewise writes below, at the end of siman 555, that one who knows the law should not remove them before Maariv. Shiurei Knesses HaGedolah writes that this was his own practice. Below, siman 559, however, I cited Maharil, who removed his tefillin on Tisha B'Av after Kedushah. This requires study, for Maharil certainly knew the pathways of halachah. Responsa Menachem Azaryah, siman 39, likewise implies that he should remove them during Maariv after the passage “And it shall be if you listen.” It appears to me that Maharil held that if he personally acted otherwise, everyone would observe him and imitate him, which would be equivalent to issuing a public ruling. Magen Avraham likewise implies this. There is proof from Menachos 35a: Rav Ashi told his students that he left them on to guard them, although he did not actually need them for safekeeping. Evidently, whenever no such explanation is available one should not act this way publicly. It is also possible that the permission applies specifically to an individual, but not to a congregation. Therefore, it appears proper to follow Maharil's practice. There is room to question whether one who mistakenly put them on before daybreak and recited the blessing must handle them and recite another blessing after daybreak. It appears that since according to Torah law night is a time for tefillin, one may rely upon the first blessing recited at night.
