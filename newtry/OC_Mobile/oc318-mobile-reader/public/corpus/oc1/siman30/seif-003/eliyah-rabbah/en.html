[3] Lest he sleep, etc. But those traveling in a wagon may not leave them on lest he sleep — Levush; and so agreed Lechem Chamudos, Taz, and Nachalat Tzvi. But in Migdal David I saw he challenged the Levush and is lenient in practice even in a wagon, for we are not concerned he will sleep in them in such a short time, see there. Even though his proof may be rejected, in a time of need one may rely on him. Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Levush that a rider too is forbidden — this is astonishing, and perhaps it is a scribal error. Lechem Chamudos wrote on page 95 regarding Tisha BeAv, when they don tefillin at Minchah prayer, one should not remove them before the evening prayer; and what the people are accustomed to do, removing them — is because not everyone has learned the law, and the sages of the generation have no authority to instruct, for one does not instruct thus — so far. And so wrote Levush Yom Tov below at the end of siman 555, that one who knows should not remove before Maariv; and Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah wrote this was his practice, see there. But below in siman 559 I brought in the name of Maharil that he removed tefillin on Tisha BeAv after the sanctification; if so, investigation is needed — certainly Maharil was clear on the paths of the law; and also in responsa Menachem Azariah siman 39 it implies that after the parashah "and it shall be if hearing" in Maariv one removes. It seems to my humble opinion he holds: when he does so, if so the whole world will see from him and act likewise, and it is as if he instructed them thus — and so Magen Avraham implies. Proof: in Menachot 35, Rav Ashi said to his students not to remove in order to guard, even though in truth he did not need to guard — but certainly wherever there is no room to answer that he acts in order to guard, he should not do so; and furthermore, perhaps specifically for an individual it is permitted but not in public; therefore it seems to me to act like Maharil's custom. One may be uncertain: if he erred and donned before day and blessed, whether he must feel them and bless again by day. It is reasonable: since from Torah law night is time for tefillin, we rely on the first blessing of the night.
