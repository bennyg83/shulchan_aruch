And when their time arrives. — See Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote; and nevertheless, if he advanced, etc. — this is from the innovations of R' Elazar Azikri, who wrote that one may rely on this on the view of Rabbeinu Peretz, see there; and it follows from this that without arising early and setting out on the road, one must return and bless if he blessed over the tefillin at night. And from the words of Birkei Yosef cited in Shaarei Teshuvah it seems that post factum in all cases one need not return and bless — and Birkei Yosef did not give a reason for this; perhaps post factum we rely on the view of Rashi and Rabbeinu Yona, who hold that what we say "the halacha is X but one does not instruct thus" applies even regarding leaving them on ab initio — but in Shulchan Aruch 72 he did not rule like them, and the practical law is uncertain. From all this understand that what some people practice in winter days, donning tefillin and blessing on them immediately when the morning star rises — they do not act properly.

Since he arose early and set out on the road. — See Mishnah Berurah; and see in Taz and Peri Megadim in Eshel Avraham note 3, who lean to say that even in a wagon it appears permitted; and in Erech HaChayim he agrees with the stringent view. It seems to me that if he fears lest they be lost and acts in order to guard them, certainly one should not be stringent even when sitting in a wagon — for in any case the view of Avnei Tzedek and the Gra is to be lenient in leaving them on for the sake of guarding.
