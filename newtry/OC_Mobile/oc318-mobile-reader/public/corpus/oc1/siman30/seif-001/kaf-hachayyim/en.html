1) [Seif 1] The time for putting them on in the morning is when he can recognize his friend, etc. The reason this was made dependent on recognizing his friend is that seeing is written in connection with tefillin, as it is stated: “And all the peoples of the earth shall see that the name of Hashem is called upon you,” etc. Beit Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah; Levush; Taz, se’if katan 1; Olat Tamid, ot 1; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1. This is only an asmachta. The principal reason is that, since we hold that nighttime is a time for tefillin, but the Sages prohibited putting them on at night lest one forget them and sleep in them, as stated in se’if 2; and the prohibition against sleeping in them is lest one pass gas while wearing them, as stated in Beit Yosef, siman 44, the Taz here, se’if katan 3, and Olat Tamid, ot 2; the Sages attached their enactment to the verse “And all the peoples of the earth shall see,” etc., and prohibited putting them on until the time when one can recognize a friend with whom he is familiar, etc. Before this time everything is considered nighttime, and the aforementioned concerns apply. Levush, se’if 2; Peri Megadim, Mishbetzot Zahav, ot 1; Shitilei Zeitim, ot 3. Even according to the Rambam, of blessed memory, who holds that nighttime is not a time for tefillin, as stated in chapter 4, law 10, of Hilkhot Tefillin, it may likewise be said that tefillin are not put on until one can recognize a friend with whom he is familiar, etc., because of the aforementioned concerns. So too, according to the Arizal’s words in Shaar HaKavanot, derush 5 concerning tefillin, and in Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar HaTefillin, chapter 7, where he wrote that nighttime is not a time for tefillin and that the spiritual aspect of tefillin exists only from the light of morning onward, it may likewise be said that tefillin are not to be put on until the time when one can recognize his friend, etc., because of the aforementioned concerns. It is not as some wished to say, that according to the Arizal it is initially permissible to put on tefillin from dawn onward. Heaven forbid that one should say that the Arizal was lenient against that which was treated stringently in the Talmud (an explicit passage in Berakhot 9b) and ruled accordingly by the halachic authorities: the Rif, the Rambam, the Rosh, and the later authorities. Rather, the Arizal meant that the beginning of the spiritual stage of the arrival of the mochin associated with tefillin is from the light of morning onward; nevertheless, the tefillin are not to be put on until the time established by the Sages in the Talmud and the halachic authorities, for one may not depart from their words. This is clear.
2) There: When he can recognize a friend with whom he is somewhat familiar, at a distance of four amot, and recognize him. This time is later than the time when one can distinguish between techelet and white, stated in siman 18, se’if 3. Peri Megadim, Mishbetzot Zahav, ot 1; see there. Chesed LaAlafim, ot 1, writes that it is better to delay than to be early. See what the Peri Megadim writes in siman 58, Eshel Avraham, ot 2; our words above in siman 18, ot 18; and below in siman 51, ot 10; examine carefully.
3) There: A friend with whom he is somewhat familiar, etc. That is, if he is very familiar with him, he will recognize him even at a distance; but if he is not familiar with him, he will not recognize him even when very close. Beit Yosef in the name of the Jerusalem Talmud; Tosafot, Berakhot 9b, s.v. Acherim Omrim; the Rosh; and Rabbeinu Yonah. The Taz, se’if katan 3, and the later authorities write likewise.
