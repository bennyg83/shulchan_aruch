Beit Yosef writes in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah that the law depends upon sight because the verse says, etc. Nevertheless, this is only an asmachta, because tefillin are not mentioned explicitly there. The principal reason is concern that he may sleep in them, and until this time it is considered night. Levush explains this in seifim 1 and 2; see there. Regarding one who departs on a journey and dawn has arrived, it requires study whether he should recite a blessing then. See seif 3. Rabbeinu Peretz, cited by Tur, maintains that one recites the blessing while traveling, while we rule that he does not. Nevertheless, at dawn one could argue that he should recite it. According to Levush, the principal reason is that this period is considered night with respect to tefillin and there is concern that he may sleep. Once it is day, there is no distinction, and he should not recite the blessing until the proper time. In siman 18, seif 2, concerning tzitzis, the time is when one can distinguish between techeiles and white. It appears that recognizing an acquaintance at a distance of four amos occurs later. See Tosafos on Berachos 9b, s.v. “Others say,” and Magen Avraham, seif katan 1.
