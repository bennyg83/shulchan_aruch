[2] In order to guard them and without a blessing, etc. Lechem Chamudos wrote on page 96 and Levush Yom Tov; and it seems to me their reason is: even though one instructs thus, nevertheless since rabbinically it is not time for tefillin, one does not bless — even though they permitted in such a case, for one who arises early to set out on the road they also permitted, and even so he does not bless until their time arrives. He should also have written according to this view that even ab initio one may don them not for guarding, but one does not instruct thus — so far. In my humble opinion, the Levush's intent is: even per the first view he brought, it is permitted to don ab initio, but one does not instruct thus. Evidence: he wrote "but the main reason," etc., "because the Sages forbade donning," etc., "and since one does not don," etc. — behold he speaks of donning; but according to this view, when he donned while it was still day, one does not instruct him not to remove them unless it is in order to guard them — for one instructs not to remove. But "there are those who say": if in order to guard, one instructs even to don; and if not in order to guard, one instructs not to remove, and to don is permitted but one does not instruct thus. Now it is resolved why he had to write according to "there are those who say" that even without guarding, if they were on him close to sunset, he need not remove them — for it is obvious: even per the first view he need not remove, but he explains that "there are those who say" instruct thus, as they instruct to don them in order to guard; examine this. All this follows the Levush; but the Shulchan Aruch ruled that not for guarding it is forbidden to don, and he need not remove them but one does not instruct thus; and in order to guard, one instructs not to remove, but to don is forbidden. So it seems to me to instruct — although Nachalat Tzvi and Olat Tamid challenged the Shulchan Aruch: since we hold night is not time for tefillin, it should be permitted to don them in order to guard; also Bach wondered similarly, see there. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty: Beit Yosef relied on the Rif, who also holds night is time for tefillin, and even so forbids donning even to guard. Nachalat Tzvi disagrees with Beit Yosef and wrote the Rif holds night is not time for tefillin — and he omitted the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham page 171, who wrote explicitly the Rif rules night is time for tefillin. Even so Nachalat Tzvi's words are strained to reconcile the Rif this way. Also what Nachalat Tzvi wondered from Hagahat Semak brought by Beit Yosef, permitting donning when arising early — implying all the more one should permit in his mind to guard lest they be lost, see there — in my humble opinion one may say: all the more so it is permitted when donning at arising early because they will not be lost — there are two reasons, one because of arising early and another lest they be lost; but at night, where there is only lest they be lost alone, it is forbidden to don. Even so there is no difficulty to say the Rif does not hold like Hagahat Semak in this.
