(3) At night. Regarding twilight, Magen Avraham implies that one may put them on initially. Peri Megadim is uncertain, unless he has not yet fulfilled the mitzvah of tefillin that day, in which case he should put them on during twilight.
(4) And he may sleep in them. We are concerned that he may pass wind while sleeping. According to Torah law, however, it is permitted to put them on at night, for we rule that nighttime is a time for tefillin.
(5) Before, etc. Meaning, the Sages prohibited only putting them on initially at night. Once they were already upon him from daytime, they did not require him to remove them.
(6) All night. Meaning, so long as he does not remove them. Once he removes them even once, he may not put them on again.
(7) It is permitted. Meaning, until the time he wishes to sleep. Then he must remove them, because fixed sleep while wearing tefillin is forbidden, as explained below in siman 44. It is possible that at night, which is a time normally designated for sleep, even temporary sleep is forbidden by strict law.
(8) And one does not rule this way publicly. Meaning, one should not issue this as practical instruction to others, lest they come to put them on initially at night. Therefore, he should act this way only privately, not publicly, for public conduct is itself like a ruling. Accordingly, one should not pray Maariv publicly while wearing tefillin on a communal fast.
(9) And they were found upon him. Specifically where they were already upon him do we rule leniently and instruct that for purposes of guarding them he need not remove them even all night, so long as he does not sleep. Putting them on initially for safekeeping, however, is forbidden. Some authorities are lenient. Olas Tamid and Gra.
