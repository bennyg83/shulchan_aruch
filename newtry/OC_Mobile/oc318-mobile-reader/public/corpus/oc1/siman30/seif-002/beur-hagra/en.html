Seif 2: It is forbidden, etc., lest, etc. Meaning, we rule that nighttime is a time for tefillin, in accordance with Rabbi Akiva, for the halachah follows Rabbi Akiva against an individual colleague. Rabbi Yaakov and the Sages likewise hold this view, as Tosafos write there, s.v. “Rabbi Yaakov.” Reish Lakish also rules this way according to the first version, as do Rabbi Chiya and Rabbah bar Rav Huna, and Rav Ashi, as stated there. See Rashi there. This differs from Rambam, who maintains that nighttime is not a time for tefillin, following Rabbi Elazar and Rabbi Yose, and explains Rav Ashi as holding that the prohibition applies only to putting them on. The Yerushalmi in the second chapter of Berachos likewise states: Rabbi Abahu said in the name of Rabbi Elazar that one who puts on tefillin at night violates a positive commandment. Some wished to say that this was stated only regarding putting them on, but if they were already upon him from daytime it is permitted. The same is true where he wears them in order to guard them: it is permitted to leave them upon himself, and one may rule this publicly, as stated there. This is so even according to the view that nighttime is not a time for tefillin, for that authority himself says only that one who puts them on violates a positive commandment. All the poskim, however, explain that Rav Ashi maintains that nighttime is a time for tefillin, yet it is nevertheless forbidden to put them on then, as stated above, and as the Sages agree. Tosafos there, s.v. “Rabbi Yaakov,” write the same. The statement in Eruvin 96a, “Rather, this one, etc., one who is awake at night, etc.,” means that he leaves them upon himself, in accordance with Rif's version of the incident of Rav Ashi: darkness came and he did not remove his tefillin. This is why Shulchan Aruch writes, “If he put them on, etc.,” following the Sages, whose view Rav Ashi accepts.
If he did not remove them, etc. Rambam explains the Gemara's statement, “and if it is to guard them, it is permitted,” as referring to removal, unlike Rashi. Although even without the purpose of guarding them one may leave them on, that ruling is not publicly taught. Here, however, it is taught publicly. This is why Shulchan Aruch writes, “and one may rule this way publicly.” The incident involving Rav Ashi likewise implies this, for he said that he needed them in order to guard them.
