(2) It is forbidden, lest, etc. Meaning: we hold that night is time for tefillin, like R' Akiva — for the halacha follows R' Akiva against his colleague; and so holds R' Yaakov and the Sages, as Tosafos there (s.v. R' Yaakov) wrote; and so explained the meaning according to the first version; and so holds R' Chananel and Rabbah on Rosh Hashanah; and so holds Rav Ashi, as was explained above. And he holds, etc., and Rashi there — and not like the Rambam, who holds that night is not time for tefillin, like R' Eliezer and R' Yehuda. And he also explained regarding Rav Ashi, who holds that the prohibition is only to don them; and so in Yerushalmi chapter 2 of Berachos: R' Avahu in the name of R' Eliezer — one who dons tefillin at night violates a positive commandment, etc. There are those who want to say: he said this only about one who dons them; but if they were on him from still daytime, it is permitted — and like for guarding them, which is permitted while they remain on him; and one instructs thus, as was explained above. And R' Eliezer, etc. — and even according to the one who says night is not time for tefillin, for he himself said that one violates a positive commandment; but all the poskim explain that Rav Ashi holds night is time for tefillin, and nevertheless it is forbidden to leave them on, as was explained. And the Sages agree, etc., as Tosafos there (s.v. R' Yaakov) wrote. And that which we say in Eruvin 96a — "rather this one who was awake at night," etc. — meaning: he leaves them on him; and like the Rif's version in the incident of Rav Ashi, that it grew dark and he did not remove his tefillin; and this is what "if he left them on," etc. means — and Rav Ashi holds like the Sages.

If he did not remove them, etc. The Rambam — and explained that which was written: "and if to guard them it is permitted" — refers to removal, not like Rashi; and even though in such a case, even when not for guarding, it is permitted there, one does not instruct thus; not so here. And this is what "and one instructs thus" means — and so is clear from the incident of Rav Ashi, who said it was to guard them.
