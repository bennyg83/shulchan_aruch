If he did not remove his tefillin from sunset — meaning: the completion of sunset, which is emergence of the stars; then is the time for removing tefillin by law. But during bein hashemashot, even if he had a place to guard them, he need not remove them — so is proven in Menachos 36b — even for one who holds night is not time for tefillin; and all the more so for us, who rule night is time for tefillin. And so wrote the Levush and Peri Megadim that one instructs thus ab initio.

And they were found on him in order to guard them — see Magen Avraham, who wrote that he must say to them thus, that he does so in order to guard them; and so is proven in the Gemara. And all the commentators [Peri Megadim, Machatzis HaShekel, and Erech HaChayim] were exhausted — for in the Gemara it seems the opposite in the incident of Rav Ashi, see there. And in my humble opinion it appears straightforward that he comes to add to the author's words: even if in truth his intent is not for guarding, only that he says to them thus, that he does so for guarding — so that they should not come to permit donning ab initio at night — that too suffices; and so is proven explicitly there in the Gemara in the incident of Rav Ashi.

It is permitted, and one instructs thus. — See Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote, that there are those who are lenient even to don them ab initio for the sake of guarding; and it seems straightforward to me that even though it is permitted to intend then in his thought that he wears them for the sake of the mitzvah, as is proven from the incident of R' Eliezer cited above — nevertheless he should not bless then over the tefillin. And evidence for our words: the main proof of Avnei Tzedek and the Gra is from what is stated in that baraita, "and he fears lest they be lost," etc.; and behold there too the baraita ruled that one should not bless until the time arrives — and as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch ruled like the Rosh, and not like Rabbeinu Peretz, as the Gra wrote.
