One instructs thus. And therefore in public one must remove them (Magen Avraham). The author of Shenei Luchot HaBerit wrote: my custom on a Taanis fast day after Minchah is that I stand in the congregation until Maariv so as not to remove tefillin and tallis until after the evening prayer, for since night is time for tefillin — only that ab initio one should not put them on, and since they are already on me from Minchah, it is better not to remove them until after the evening prayer. Therefore on Tisha BeAv, when one dons tefillin at Minchah prayer, one should not remove them before the evening prayer. And what the people are accustomed to do, removing them — that is because not everyone has learned the law, and the sages of the generation have no authority to instruct, for one does not instruct thus. And so too in Lechem Chamudos, Laws of Tefillin siman 179, and the author of Avnei Tzedek wrote that his words are correct, there; and so ruled in Hilchot Ketanot vol. 1 siman 298, there. But Maharil in the laws of Tisha BeAv wrote that he would remove his tefillin immediately after the sanctification for Minchah, and so appears to be the view of the Magen Avraham, who wrote that in public one must remove them. And so decided in responsa Shevut Yaakov vol. 2 siman 24, there.

And one instructs thus. And he must say that he does so in order to guard them; but ab initio one does not instruct to leave them on for the sake of guarding. Taz.
