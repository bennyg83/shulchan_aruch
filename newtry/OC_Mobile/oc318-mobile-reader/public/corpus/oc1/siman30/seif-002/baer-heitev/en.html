One does not rule this way publicly. Accordingly, when among the public he must remove them. Magen Avraham. Shiurei Knesses HaGedolah writes: “My practice on a public fast day is that after Minchah I remain with the congregation until Maariv and do not remove my tefillin and tallis until after Maariv. Since nighttime is a time for tefillin, except that initially one should not put them on then, and since they have already been upon me from Minchah, it is preferable not to remove them until after Maariv. Therefore, on Tisha B'Av, when tefillin are put on at Minchah, they should not be removed before Maariv. The public custom of removing them is because not everyone knows the law, and the Sages of the generation should not instruct otherwise, since this ruling is not taught publicly.” Lechem Chamudos, Hilchos Tefillin, letter 79, writes the same, and Olas Tamid writes that his words are correct. Halachos Ketanos, part 1, siman 298, likewise rules this way. Maharil, however, writes in Hilchos Tisha B'Av that he removed his tefillin immediately after Kedushah at Minchah. Magen Avraham also appears to hold that when among the public one must remove them. Responsa Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 24, rules this way as well.
And one does rule this way publicly. He must state that he is doing so in order to guard them. Initially, however, one should not instruct a person to put them on merely for safekeeping. Taz.
