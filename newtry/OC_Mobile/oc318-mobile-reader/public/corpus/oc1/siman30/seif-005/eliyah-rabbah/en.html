[5] He should not put them on, etc. This is derived from Piskei Mahari, siman 121, based upon the law of a mourner who hears of a death after Maariv and begins counting only from the following day. In Eliyahu Zuta I objected that mourning requires seven days. Therefore, if it is considered night for Maariv, it no longer counts as part of the day. Tefillin, however, are different, for we rule that night is a time for tefillin. The Sages prohibited putting them on initially only lest he sleep and pass wind. It is possible that the Sages did not enact this decree while it is still daytime, for there is no concern that he will sleep then, just as there is no concern when one rises early to depart. Responsa Maharil, siman 174, offers some support for this. It further appears that since he has not yet put on tefillin, this resembles Taz's ruling in Yoreh Deah, siman 402, seif 11, that one who hears a report of mourning on the thirtieth day after Maariv must observe mourning, because we do not rule leniently that it is already night and thereby classify it as a distant report. Furthermore, some poskim permit putting on tefillin even at night, though this view is not publicly taught. Although I ruled above against them, where he has not yet put on tefillin and it is still daytime, one may certainly rely upon them and put them on without a blessing, and this may be publicly taught in this case. If he himself has not yet prayed Maariv, even though the congregation already prayed, it appears permissible for him to put them on with a blessing. I discussed this at length with proofs in Eliyahu Zuta; see there.
