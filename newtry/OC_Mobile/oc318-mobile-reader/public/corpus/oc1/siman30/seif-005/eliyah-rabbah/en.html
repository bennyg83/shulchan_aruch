[5] He may not don, etc. And he also brought this ruling from Maharai siman 121, and learned thus from a mourner who heard of a death after the evening prayer — he begins counting from the next day. But in Eliyahu Zuta I challenged: there is no proof from mourning, which requires seven days; and if so, if it is night for the purpose of Maariv it does not count as a day — but regarding tefillin we hold night is time for tefillin and he need not remove them as above, only because he may sleep and pass wind in them the Sages decreed ab initio; perhaps the Sages did not decree at all while it is still day, for we are not concerned while it is still day that he will sleep, as we are not concerned when arising early to set out on the road. Some support and evidence for this seems to me from responsa Maharil siman 174, see there. Furthermore it seems to me: since he has not yet donned tefillin, it is somewhat comparable to what Taz wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 402 seif 11 regarding mourning on the thirtieth day after the evening prayer — one must mourn, for we do not say leniently that it is night and the report is distant; and also for some poskim it is permitted to don tefillin even at night, but one does not instruct thus — and even though I established above the halacha is not like them, nevertheless since he has not donned tefillin and it is still day, surely one may rely on them and don without a blessing, and one instructs thus in this. However, if he did not pray Maariv, even though the congregation prayed, it seems lenient to don with a blessing — and in Eliyahu Zuta I expanded on this with proofs, see there.
