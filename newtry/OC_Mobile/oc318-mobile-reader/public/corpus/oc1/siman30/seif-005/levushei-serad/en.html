(Taz note 6) Taz objects — he does not hold like what Magen Avraham wrote, that in public it is like instruction; therefore specifically on the road, one who encounters him will think he donned after sunset — not so in the beit midrash, where they saw them on him from the beginning; therefore regarding the beit midrash he had to write "and the day was sanctified upon him." For Magen Avraham, who holds in public it is instruction, the law applies in the beit midrash even on a weekday; if so, there is a difficulty for us: why regarding the beit midrash did he write "and the day was sanctified upon him"? See Bach, who truly explains this "and the day was sanctified" deals with a weekday — see there at length.
