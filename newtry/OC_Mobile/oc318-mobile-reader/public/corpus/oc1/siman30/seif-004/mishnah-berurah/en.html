(15) And the sun set, etc. This first clause concerns a weekday. He is afraid to remove them and carry them in his hand lest they fall from him on the road, so the Sages permitted him to place his hand over them and cover them. Although seif 2 states that once they were already upon him he need not remove them, this case is different because a person who encounters him may mistakenly conclude that one may put on tefillin at that time. The earlier case concerns one sitting in his home. “And the sun set” means the end of sunset, namely the emergence of the stars. During twilight he need not remove them, and this may be publicly ruled as practical halachah. According to strict law this is the ruling. In the name of the Arizal, however, it is written that he was careful to remove them after sunset.
(16) And the day became sanctified, etc. Meaning, twilight began. He cannot carry them in his hand to his home because of the sanctity of Shabbos, and he cannot leave them there because their batei midrash were in the fields, where they were not protected from thieves. The Sages therefore permitted him to carry them upon himself in the manner of clothing until reaching home. He must cover them so that people do not see him wearing tefillin on Shabbos.
