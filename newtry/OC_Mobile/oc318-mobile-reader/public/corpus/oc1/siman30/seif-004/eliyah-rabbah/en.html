[4] And the sun set, etc., on a weekday. Although in seif 2 we rule that he need not remove them even when he is not traveling, this case is different because of concern for observers. The earlier case concerns one in his own home. Taz and Magen Avraham explain Shulchan Aruch this way, and this supports Maharil's practice cited above. Olas Tamid writes that the case must concern Shabbos, for if it concerned a weekday, the preceding seif already permits one departing on a journey to put them on even initially, and certainly he would not need to remove them. This is inaccurate. In the preceding case he rises early and departs, so we assume he will not sleep because he has just awakened and day is approaching. Here, however, night is beginning, and we certainly suspect that he may fail to remove them and then sleep in them at night.
