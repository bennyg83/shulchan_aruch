[4] And the sun set, etc., on a weekday, etc. Even though above in seif 2 we hold he need not remove them even when not on the road — nevertheless here is different, for there is concern for onlookers; but above deals with when he is in his home — and so Taz and Magen Avraham explained the words of the Shulchan Aruch; and from this is evidence for the custom of Maharil that I wrote. Olat Tamid wrote it deals with Shabbat — for if it deals with a weekday, this was explained nearby, that one traveling on the road may leave them on even ab initio, all the more so he need not remove them — so far. He was not precise: nearby regarding one who arose early and sets out on the road we say presumably he will not sleep, since he arose and the day is imminent — and so in responsa Maharil siman 174; but here, dealing with the onset of night, certainly we are concerned he will not remove them and will sleep in them at night.
