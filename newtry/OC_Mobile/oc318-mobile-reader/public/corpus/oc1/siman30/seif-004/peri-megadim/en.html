Shulchan Aruch's ruling is Rambam's language in chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillin, halachos 11 and 12: for purposes of guarding them it is permitted, and this implies that one may rule so publicly. This must be so, for otherwise even without a need to guard them he may leave them on; see Beit Yosef. Tur omits this law. Beit Yosef gives two explanations. First, Rabbi Elazar in Menachos 36b, who permits leaving them on for safekeeping, maintains that nighttime is not a time for tefillin and one would ordinarily have to remove them. Only for safekeeping is it permitted. We, however, rule that nighttime is a time for tefillin. According to this, there is no special public ruling for safekeeping. Second, putting them on initially for safekeeping is permitted, and Tur relied upon his ruling concerning one who rises early to depart and fears they may be lost. According to this, one may put them on initially for safekeeping. The Mechaber, however, gives the reason in seif 3, “since he rose early, etc.” This implies that otherwise it is forbidden even for safekeeping, and even privately. The phrase “one does not rule this way publicly” is attached to the words “initially,” as Bach explains: one does not publicly instruct putting them on initially. According to the Mechaber, however, the meaning is that one does not publicly rule that they may remain on without removal.
I shall explain further. Leaving them upon oneself all night is permitted privately, in accordance with the Sages who disagree with Rabbi Yaakov in Menachos 36a. “Until the time of sleep” means until he wishes to sleep, at which point he must remove them, because fixed sleep in tefillin is forbidden. It is possible that at night, which is the normal time for sleep, even temporary sleep is forbidden by strict law. See siman 44 and the discussion in Berachos concerning one overcome by sleep. This is how I understood the matter from Mordechai. Tosafos there, s.v. “Rabbi Yaakov,” however, write that Rabbi Yaakov requires a greater safeguard than the Sages, implying that the Sages also require some safeguard. The Mechaber does not cite this, because he holds that all the poskim disagree and that the halachah follows the Sages: all night he need not remove them, as I wrote.
