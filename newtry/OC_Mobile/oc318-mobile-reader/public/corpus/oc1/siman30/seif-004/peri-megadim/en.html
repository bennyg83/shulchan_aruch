Taz: Shulchan Aruch ruled — these are the words of the Rambam in seif 4 of Hilchot Tefillin 11–12: for guarding, permitted; and it implies "and one instructs" — and it is necessary, for otherwise even without guarding it would be permitted (Avnei Yosef); but the Tur omitted this. Avnei Yosef gives two answers: (1) R' Eliezer in Menachot 36b — to guard, permitted; he holds night is not time for tefillin and one must remove, only for guarding permitted; but we hold night is time for tefillin — therefore one does not instruct thus for guarding. (2) For guarding, ab initio permitted to don; the Tur relied on what is written regarding arising early to travel, fearing lest they be lost (as the baraita there) — that is the reason it is permitted; accordingly for guarding it is permitted ab initio to don. But the author in seif 3 gave the reason "since he arose early," etc. — behold not so, forbidden even for guarding, even between himself and himself; and what "one does not instruct" means is attached to the word "ab initio" (as Bach explains: one does not instruct means to don ab initio; but for the author the explanation is: one does not instruct in public not to remove them).

Furthermore I will explain: to leave them on him all night is permitted between himself and himself, as the sages of R' Yaakov in Menachot 36a — until time of sleep, meaning until he wishes to sleep; then he must remove, for substantial sleep is forbidden in them; and possibly even casual sleep at night, time of sleep, is forbidden by law — see siman 44; and see Berachot regarding one overcome by sleep. So I learned the explanation from Mordechai in Berachot; but in Tosafot there s.v. R' Yaakov they wrote "Abaye." Erech HaChayim: "more than rabbinic" implies for the rabbis too there is distance — and the author did not bring this, holding all poskim disagree; and the halacha follows the sages: all night he need not remove, as I wrote.
