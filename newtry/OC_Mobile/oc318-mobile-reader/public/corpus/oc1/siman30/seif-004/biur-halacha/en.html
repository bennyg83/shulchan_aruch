If he was traveling on the road, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, seifim katanim 15 and 16. The Mechaber could likewise have stated a single case: if he was traveling and the day became sanctified, applying it also to Shabbos, as below in siman 266, seif 10. He instead used the Gemara's wording. One may say that the Gemara teaches a novelty: even though he is standing among a congregation, placing his hand over the tefillin is effective, and we do not concern ourselves that others will notice that he is wearing tefillin or that he is carrying them out of the beis midrash on Shabbos. Why did the Gemara and Mechaber not mention the case of one sitting in the beis midrash also regarding an ordinary weekday? Taz answers that on a weekday, while he remains in the beis midrash, he need not place his hand over them, because everyone there knows that he put them on before nightfall and there is no prohibition. Magen Avraham does not accept this distinction, as the later authorities write. According to Magen Avraham, it appears to me that the simple answer is that the Gemara teaches that even while traveling he must place his hand over them, lest people encounter him and suspect that he put on tefillin at night. This differs from seif 3, where one rises early to depart and may put them on, and the poskim do not mention that he must place his hand over them. There the time is close to dawn and very near the point at which one can recognize an acquaintance. If so, a new law emerges: one who rises several hours before dawn and puts them on would have to place his hand over them. This requires practical study. It appears likely that this is why Rambam omitted the baraisa concerning one who rises early to depart, an omission that puzzled the poskim. He understood that it conflicts with the baraisa in Beitzah 15a, which requires placing one's hand over them, and he ruled like the baraisa in Beitzah because Abaye, a later authority, follows it. Artzos HaChayim writes similarly that the two baraisos contradict one another; see there.
