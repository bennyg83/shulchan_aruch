If he was traveling on the road, etc. — See Mishnah Berurah in notes 15–16; and likewise the author could have formulated in one case: "if he was traveling on the road and the day was sanctified upon him" — also regarding Shabbat, as below in siman 266:10 — but he used the language of the Gemara. We may say the Gemara teaches a greater matter: even though he stands in the midst of the congregation, nevertheless placing his hand on them is effective, and we are not concerned lest they notice him carrying tefillin on his head; and he may also go out with them from the beit midrash to outside on Shabbat. That which the Gemara and the author did not cite the case of one sitting in the beit midrash also regarding a weekday — Taz answered: on a weekday, if he sits in the beit midrash, he need not place his hand on them [when he is in the beit midrash], for everyone there knows he donned them while it was still day and there is no prohibition; and Magen Avraham does not accept this distinction — so the latter authorities wrote. It seems to me to answer for him simply: the Gemara teaches a greater matter — that even when traveling on the road one must place his hand on them, lest people encounter him and suspect he left tefillin on at night; and this is unlike the case in seif 3 of one who wishes to set out on the road, who dons them and the poskim did not mention at all that he must place his hand on them — for there it is close to dawn and very close to the time of recognition; yet from this one might derive a new law, that if he arose several hours before morning light and donned them, he must place his hand on them — and the practical law is uncertain. What is close to me: therefore the Rambam omitted this baraita of one who arose early to set out on the road, which the poskim wondered about — because of this baraita in Beitzah 15a, which says one must place his hand on them, and it seemed to him they disagree on this; and he ruled like this baraita in Beitzah because Abbaye, who is later, holds like it. Similarly Erech HaChayim wrote that the baraitot contradict each other, see there.
