And the sun set upon him. It deals with a weekday; and even though nearby we say he need not remove them — here is different, lest one who encounters him err and say it is permitted to don tefillin then; but nearby deals with one sitting in his home — and so Shulchan Aruch wrote afterward: "and the day was sanctified upon him" — meaning the sanctity of Shabbat is upon him and he must remove them; but on a weekday he need not — since he is in the beit midrash and there is no concern of error.
