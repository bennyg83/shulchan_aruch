Magen Avraham, seif katan 5. “Siman 26.” Perhaps his intent is to resolve his difficulty there against Rosh: how could the delay of one's journey permit setting aside the requirement that so long as the tefillin are between your eyes, there should be two upon him? See our discussion there. He answers here that the case concerns one who departs before the time for tefillin. Therefore, the principle that so long as the tefillin are between your eyes there should be two is not so strict, since he could in any event have chosen not to put them on at all. This explanation works only according to the second reason cited by Taz, seif katan 5, that one leaving on a journey before the proper time may put them on for safekeeping. The primary purpose of wearing them is then protection, as though they were placed in a case, and the principle concerning two tefillin does not apply. According to the first reason, however, that because he is traveling there is no concern that he will sleep, the period before the proper time is treated like the proper time, and he fulfills the mitzvah of tefillin. The requirement that so long as the tefillin are between your eyes there should be two would then certainly apply. It appears that Magen Avraham accepts both reasons. When he says that one sitting in a wagon may not put them on, he refers to a case in which they do not require guarding. This explains the sequence of his words. After stating the law of one sitting in a wagon, he writes, “See siman 26,” intending to answer his difficulty against Rosh. His initial answer is that the case concerns one who is permitted to put them on only for safekeeping, and therefore the principle concerning two tefillin does not apply. One could object that he in fact fulfills the mitzvah of tefillin because of the first reason. Magen Avraham therefore first writes that when he is in a wagon, the first reason does not apply and he is permitted only because of safekeeping. The case in siman 26 can thus be established where he is traveling in a wagon. Examine this carefully.
