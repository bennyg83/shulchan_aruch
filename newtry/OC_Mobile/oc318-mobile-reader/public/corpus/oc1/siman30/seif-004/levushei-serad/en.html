(Magen Avraham note 5) Siman 26 — it is possible his intent is to settle what he challenged there on the Rosh: how because of delaying his journey do we permit this verse "they shall be between your eyes" — see there in our words; and he came to answer here that it deals with one who set out before his time, and therefore then there is not so much stringency of "between your eyes they shall be two," since in any case if he wished he would not don at all; however this applies only to the second reason brought in Taz note 5, that what was permitted for one setting out on the road to don before the time is because of guarding — and therefore the main donning is because of guarding and as placed in a box; then it may be said this "between your eyes" does not apply. Not so the first reason: since he sets out on the road there is no concern he will sleep — then it is before their time like their time and he fulfills the mitzvah of tefillin, and therefore certainly "between your eyes" applies. It seems to me Magen Avraham holds both reasons are true; and what he says regarding one sitting in a wagon is where guarding is not needed — with this his continuation may be explained: after writing the law of one sitting in a wagon he wrote "and see siman 26," and his intent is to answer the challenge on the Rosh in what he wrote first, that it deals with one who was not permitted to don except for guarding — therefore "between your eyes" does not apply; but on this it is difficult, for in truth he fulfills the mitzvah of tefillin properly because of the first reason; therefore he brought first regarding a wagon there is no first reason and it was permitted only for guarding — and the case in siman 26 may be illustrated in a wagon; examine this.
