(In Shulchan Aruch se'if 1) And even to proofread, etc. And see in Panim Meiros, part 1, siman 66, regarding one who found an extra [letter] in a Sefer Torah on Chol HaMoed, and they have another Sefer Torah; and he answered to permit, for erasing the letter, even though it is a full melacha, nevertheless since he is not doing craftsman's work it is permitted to correct on Chol HaMoed for communal needs even though the public does not need it right now; and his proof is from siman 544 regarding repairing communal pits and springs, see there. And see in the following s.k. that there is a distinction between erasure in the middle of a word and at the end of a word. And see in Mor UKetziyah that it is forbidden to print books even through a non-Jewish worker, but arranging the letters is permitted even through a Jew, and tying the arranged letters so they not separate is forbidden; and likewise it is forbidden to separate the letters from the forms after printing to return them to their places in the arranged boxes, see there. And see there that he wrote that wherever writing of Torah study and teaching is permitted, certainly all acts of preparing them are also permitted. And see further there regarding a Sefer Torah that was finished close to the festival and there was no time to correct it, and it is needed on the moed: he should make the poles through a non-Jew and the sewing of the sheets through a Jew, for otherwise it does not suffice; and mezuzos needed on the moed: he makes for it a housing that is layman's work and affixes them as well; and tefillin: he makes for them housings and straps and their knots and all their needs, for otherwise they are unfit for anything; and other books written or printed that lack binding should not be done through a Jewish craftsman, but he may do them through a non-Jew, see there. And see above in siman 537 what is written in the name of Birkei Yosef regarding printing books on Chol HaMoed. And see in Givas Shaul siman 57, where a bent kaf was found in a Sefer Torah that had split into two, and they took out another Sefer Torah, and he permitted correcting on the moed even though they have another Sefer Torah; see there that he wrote like the words of Panim Meiros, and afterward wrote that one can say that since there is a communal leader in the city who alone has authority to act, it is not [called] communal needs as Magen Avraham wrote; but nevertheless, since it is a mitzvah need to correct a Sefer that is not proofread, and specifically to proofread a complete letter, which is craftsman's work, but to fix a split kaf by drawing a little ink is not [craftsman's] work and is permitted, see there. And so it appears from what is written below from the responsa of the Radbaz.
