And even to proofread one letter. Because this is not a need of the moed, it is not called communal needs, since they have another Sefer to read from; and see what I wrote in the preceding siman.
And for communal need some forbid. This is the view of Terumas HaDeshen siman 85 regarding a community that seeks to send for a chazzan and needs to write on Chol HaMoed, that it is forbidden; and he brings proof from what Or Zarua wrote: they do not proofread even one letter in the Sefer HaAzarah, meaning that they take out in the Azarah to read from it, and even for communal need; thus even communal needs they do not write on the moed. And that which we permit writing women's gittin and emancipation of slaves mentioned in se'if 5, one must say that these are included in acts of beis din. And regarding the Sefer HaAzarah one can say that it was not needed to read on the moed, etc. And writing is craftsman's work, as is evident in the Rambam, who wrote that the Sages permitted writing greetings and accounts because a person is not particular about his writing and it is like layman's work; implying that the Sages forbade because it is craftsman's work, end quote. And Beis Yosef wrote on this that it is not smooth, for it certainly implies that writing is not craftsman's work except regarding a Sefer Torah, tefillin, and mezuzos; but writing a letter is certainly layman's work, as he wrote in the name of the Rambam, end quote. And if so it implies that according to Beis Yosef it is permitted even in the script of a Sefer Torah that we call square writing, and the permission is that they are not particular to write those letters properly, since they are only for letters. Therefore what Rama wrote on this, "and they customarily were lenient in our script which is not craftsman's work," is not clear to me; for he is certainly speaking of meshita script, which is our practice; and if so it does not refer to proofreading of books that the Shulchan Aruch cited, which are square writing, but rather to other writings he says they customarily were lenient; and that cannot be, for in se'if 5 he said they customarily were stringent regarding greeting. And if he means square writing, that they are lenient in our script which is not as perfected as writing of a Sefer Torah and tefillin, in this "they customarily were lenient" does not apply, for certainly they did not customarily permit at all correcting a Sefer Torah and tefillin on Chol HaMoed. And one must say that it refers to what Terumas HaDeshen mentioned regarding writing needed for the community to send for a chazzan and the like, which is not for the need of the moed; and in truth even in this there is no clear permission even in meshita script, for only thick writing was given at Sinai. Beis Yosef wrote on this: and this is not clear in my eyes; therefore Rama wrote from the side of custom to be lenient in this; and see se'if 5.
