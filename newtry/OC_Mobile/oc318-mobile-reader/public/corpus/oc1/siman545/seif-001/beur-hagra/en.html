Se'if 1. Some forbid etc. According to the Rambam's method, who wrote "and these they write" etc., because they are communal needs, as is written in the Yerushalmi and Tosefta: these are communal needs: they judge monetary cases, capital cases, etc., and letters of reshut; and likewise one writes his accounts because he is not particular about their writing and it is layman's work; for even where an individual needs [something], it is permitted, as above in the preceding siman. And according to this such writing would be permitted, for communal needs are permitted in layman's work even not for the need of the moed; but according to those who forbid greeting below in se'if 5, if so every writing is craftsman's work and is forbidden; and that is what [he meant by] "and they customarily" etc.; and see siman 5, for there he wrote that even in this they customarily were stringent; but since it is because of custom, one should not seize upon this. And the words of Terumas HaDeshen here require further study, for he brings proof from the Rambam to forbid; and see Beis Yosef who also seized upon him; and if so these two views are the words of Terumas HaDeshen and Beis Yosef; and see Taz and Magen Avraham. But from that the gloss wrote this law here, it implies that it refers to writing books, and that is the words of Kol Bo; and those who disagree are Beis Yosef: for Kol Bo wrote it is permitted to proofread and to write books for free etc.; and Beis Yosef wrote: and it appears that he means for communal need, except that they permitted not for the need of the moed only in a matter that is not craftsman's work; and that is what [he meant by] "and they customarily were lenient" etc.
