(ב) And for communal needs some forbid, etc. — see Mishna Berurah what we explained in the Rama's words, and it is per Taz, Magen Avraham, and other later authorities; and see Biur HaGra who also explains thus from the outset, but at the end wrote: from what is written in the Hagahah, this law here implies it refers to writing books, and these are Kol Bo's words; and those who disagree are Beit Yosef — Kol Bo wrote it seems permitted to edit and write books gratuitously lest after Yom Tov he lack time for this — end of his words; and Beit Yosef wrote: it appears to me he speaks of communal needs, and they permitted him from the law of communal needs, for even if intending to do them on the festival it is permitted as explained in siman 544, except there they did not permit except not for festival needs but in a matter that is not professional melakha — end of Beit Yosef's words, and so wrote, and they were lenient, etc. — end of Gra's words. And behold from the Rama's wording and likewise from Beit Yosef it implies Kol Bo is lenient even in professional melakha, and seemingly this is very astounding — why did he permit writing books, which is professional melakha, and in what case does the Rama lenient in our writing? Is not writing a Torah scroll professional melakha? Indeed I examined Kol Bo inside and saw he brought there initially Ra'avad's view on the mishnah that one does not write books — tefillin and mezuzot for others as a favor [i.e., without wages] are permitted; from this it is plainly proven Ra'avad's view is like Tur and Shiltei Gibborim regarding tefillin and mezuzot for others as a favor they ruled permitted, and this is R' Meir's view in the Gemara, and he holds R' Yosi comes to be lenient and does not argue on R' Meir in this; and the same for a Torah scroll — and this is also Kol Bo's view; but in this Kol Bo is more lenient than Tur, for Tur permits only for the festival itself, as his words prove, and for Kol Bo it would be permitted also for after the festival [and per him the baraita's wording fits better: as R' Meir said a person writes tefillin for himself — i.e., even for after the festival per many poskim's view, so too what he concludes 'and for others as a favor' is also in this manner] — and this is lest after the festival he lack time for this; and he holds that generally we do not permit communal needs when not for festival needs in professional melakha, but here for tefillin and likewise Torah scroll, which is a mitzvah matter, we are lenient even in professional melakha; and Beit Yosef disagrees on this; and what the Rama concludes 'and they were lenient in our writing' which is not professional melakha — meaning the world's custom is to be strict per Beit Yosef, and they are lenient only in our writing — meaning not writing a Torah scroll but writing other books or fixing them for communal needs, and in Shita Mekubetzet's writing.
