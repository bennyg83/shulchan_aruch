(א) For himself, and even if he does not work at the time, he will not be able to do so, nor is he able to write for another, and according to what he has written in a time when he is in order to have his or her expenses in order to be more enslaved in any way, he shall not be able to write any of his mitzvot, and he shall write, as a result, and that he shall be done

(ב) And if he has nothing to eat — it appears in this matter the same applies to other labors: if the worker has nothing to eat, he may do work for his livelihood, as Rambam wrote in chapter 7 of Hilchot Yom Tov halakha 24; and there Rosh and Tur concede to Rambam that specifically when he has nothing to eat — see above in siman 542 in Biur Halachah; and see there in Mishna Berurah that specifically discreetly. And know further that per those who hold above in siman 542 'he has nothing to eat' means he lacks enough for Yom Tov joy — i.e., meat and wine — automatically the same in our matter, even per Beit Yosef who copied Rambam's wording for law, meaning also he lacks enough for Yom Tov; and Rama who added 'or that he have' etc. means even if he has meat and wine but in limited measure, he may write in order to earn and have more comfort for Yom Tov joy [Noda B'Yehuda, Kuntres 106 letter 4]:

(ג) Or that his expenses be more comfortable, etc. — from the plain sense it implies it refers to the above; and if so, just as when he has nothing to eat at all we certainly permit for others even for after the festival [and especially since there the Mechaber copied all Rambam's wording, and he holds per Beit Yosef that on Chol HaMoed one does not lay tefillin] — so too what the Rama concludes is also in this manner [and thereby fits well what he concludes afterward 'and if he wrote them in order to lay them on the festival' etc.]; and so Magen Avraham s.k. 10 implies; but when we examine Tur from which Rama took his law, it is explicit there he deals with one who writes for others so they fulfill on Chol HaMoed itself the mitzvah of tefillin; and so Maamar Mordechai: 'or that he have' etc. is explained in Beit Yosef — this matter is subject to dispute: Rambam and Geonim did not permit here more than what was permitted in other labors, for there we permit only specifically when he has nothing to eat; but Rashi, Rosh, Tur, and Shiltei Gibborim hold that here it is more permitted, for even if he has what to eat, it is permitted so he have more comfort; and if so the Rama should have written in the language of 'some say'; but thus is the Mechaber's custom in many places to write his words not in dispute language [and so Magen Avraham]; however from Levush's words it implies Rama wrote thus per their custom that they lay tefillin on Chol HaMoed, per what the Mechaber explained. In Beit Yosef — reason for dispute between Rambam and Rosh: Rambam holds one does not lay tefillin on Chol HaMoed, as we rule above siman 31, and Rosh follows his reason that he holds there one lays tefillin on Chol HaMoed, and therefore we are more lenient in this labor — see there; and therefore one can say Rama does not argue on the Mechaber z"l — he follows what was ruled above siman 31, and Rama z"l wrote what he wrote per their custom, and this is proven from Rabbenu Bachya z"l end of letter 2 — end of his words; behold we have Levush, Bachya, and Maamar Mordechai — all hold one may not permit writing for another for comfort except on the festival for fulfilling the mitzvah of tefillin; and so is proven in Tur as well, and so I wrote my words inside:
