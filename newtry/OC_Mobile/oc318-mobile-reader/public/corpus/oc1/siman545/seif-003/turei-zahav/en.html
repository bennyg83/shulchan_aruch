And he spins on his thigh. Meaning by way of a change; but the Tur and Rambam wrote even without a change, and so Beis Yosef ruled for practical law; and it is astonishing what is written here "on his thigh," which implies that a change is required.
And it is forbidden only regarding writing. It appears that this too refers to what he wrote "in order to put them on during the moed," and this is the intent: if he writes for others for pay and he has what to eat, then writing is forbidden, but making them is permitted since it is in any case for the need of the moed. And one must say the reason is that making is not craftsman's work; but not for the need of the moed it is certainly forbidden even regarding making. And greater than this Beis Yosef wrote at the head of this siman in the name of the Rokeach, that it is forbidden to prepare booklets to write after Yom Tov; and it is astonishing to me that he ruled to be lenient regarding making, and that is from the words of Tosafos that Beis Yosef cites, who wrote "and perhaps there is no prohibition except regarding writing and spinning": behold they wrote only "perhaps," and not in a clear way, for one can say that the Tanna mentioned writing and the same law applies to making.
