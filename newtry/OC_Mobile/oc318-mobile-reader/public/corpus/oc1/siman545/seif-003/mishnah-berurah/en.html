For himself. Even if he intends to leave them for after the festival. If he intends to wear them during the festival, in places where tefillin are worn on Chol HaMoed, he may even write them for others, as stated below in the gloss.
Upon his thigh. This is not literal. It is permitted even with a spindle. It excludes only the use of a wheel, which is highly conspicuous.
For his garment. Even if he intends to attach them to his garment and wear it after the festival, as stated above.
And he sells to others, etc. This means that even if the purchasers intend to leave them for after the festival, it is permitted. It is no worse than other work, where an employer may hire a worker who has nothing to eat, as stated above in siman 542, seif 2.
Or so that he will have, etc. Meaning, even if he has enough to eat. Ordinarily he may not perform work, but because this is a mitzvah the Sages were lenient so that he would have more for the joy of Yom Tov. This leniency applies only when the purchaser buys them to fulfill the mitzvah of tefillin on Chol HaMoed itself, not when he buys them for after the festival. See Biur Halachah.
More generously for the joy of Yom Tov. If he does not need even this because he is wealthy, he may not write and accept payment. He may, however, write without payment for his fellow so that the latter can fulfill the mitzvah of tefillin on Chol HaMoed. According to all opinions, he may not do so for use after the festival.
If he wrote them in order to wear them during the festival. The beginning of the seif also concerns writing them for use during the festival. This clause explains the Mechaber’s words, “A person may write tefillin and mezuzos for himself,” which imply that there is no permission to write for others unless the writer has nothing to eat. The Rama clarifies that this limitation applies only when he writes for use after the festival. If he writes for another person to wear them during the festival itself, it is permitted even for payment, whether or not he has enough to eat. This is the meaning of the concluding phrase, “it is permitted in every manner.”
And only the writing is forbidden, etc. This refers to writing them for use after the festival, which is forbidden for payment unless the writer has nothing to eat. The Rama states that only the writing is forbidden, but making the other components is permitted, because it is connected with a mitzvah and the Sages did not decree against it.
