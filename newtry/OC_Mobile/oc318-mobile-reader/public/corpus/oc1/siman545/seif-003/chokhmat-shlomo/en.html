Se'if 3. A person writes tefillin and mezuzos for himself etc. Nota bene: this is the language of the Rambam, chapter 7 of Hilchos Yom Tov, halacha 13: and it is forbidden to write etc., but a person writes tefillin and mezuzos for himself and spins techeles for his garment. And Kesef Mishneh wrote there: it appears that his reason is: from where [do we know]? For we do not put on tefillin on Chol HaMoed, for it is included in Yom Tov; and if so there is no reason to permit writing tefillin more than other melacha etc. And I was asked by one scholar: if so, how would he explain regarding a mezuzah, for this certainly applies on Chol HaMoed? And I answered that this is not a need of the moed, for he can give the house as a gift to another and dwell in it as a borrower and be exempt from mezuzah. And likewise Magen Avraham asked in the name of his brother above in the laws of tzitzis, siman 13; and what he answered there does not apply here, for on Chol HaMoed it is permitted to give as a gift, and if so it is not a need of the moed. Granted regarding tefillin: even though he can borrow from others, nevertheless since he is obligated in tefillin, a person wants to fulfill with his own, for it is pleasing to a person to fulfill a mitzvah with his own money, and it is a need of the moed. And from this he proves that the Rambam holds it is forbidden on Chol HaMoed; but regarding a mezuzah, if he gives the house as a gift to another he is entirely exempt, therefore it is not considered a need of the moed. And further one can say more: now Magen Avraham wrote in siman 446, s.k. 2: and since he wants to destroy it but is forced by the Sages' enactment, he does not transgress on account of it. And in Shach, Choshen Mishpat siman 201, it is explained that we do not say that being forced is as if he did [the act]; and if so, granted that regarding the negative commandment he does not transgress for he is forced, nevertheless fulfillment of the mitzvah there is not; and if so, still [one could ask:] push aside the prohibition for this shevus prohibition, for in any case fulfillment of the mitzvah there is not. But it appears that being forced is as if he did not do [it] specifically regarding what he is obligated to do and from which he cannot in any way become exempt, as there, where he stipulated with her to do such and such and he is not exempt without an act; but regarding something from which there is no necessity that he be unable to become exempt, in this being forced is as if he did. Therefore regarding chametz: if he had no chametz he would be exempt, and there is no obligation to have chametz; and also if he has chametz he can destroy it through another, and it is not specifically a bodily obligation; therefore in this, the force makes it as if he actually did it, and it is like actual fulfillment of the positive commandment. Therefore it works well regarding mezuzah: there is no obligation that he have a house in which to make a mezuzah, and the mitzvah is also not [specifically] in his body, and agency works for it; therefore the force makes it as if he fulfilled the positive commandment; and therefore it is not a need of the moed, for on the contrary, if it were forbidden to write he would be permitted to dwell in it without a mezuzah, since he is forced and it is like actual fulfillment of the positive commandment, and if so he is exempt from this; and likewise regarding spinning techeles for his garment. But regarding tefillin, which is specifically an obligation and from which one cannot become exempt in any way, and it is also specifically a bodily mitzvah and agency does not help: if so, what of it that being forced is as if he did, nevertheless in his body it is not; and also in this we do not say that being forced is as if he did, since it is specifically an obligation. Therefore if there is an obligation on Chol HaMoed, this is by force of the moed; and understand well; and this is the main point according to my humble opinion. And a proof for our words is from what is written in the Talmud Berachos: if a person intended to do a mitzvah and was forced and did not do it, Scripture credits him as if he did it; and why in Pesachim do we say: if he erred or was forced and did not do the first and did the second, and is liable to kares if he did not do the second, for being forced is as if he did? Necessarily there is a distinction between a mitzvah in which one is specifically obligated and one from which one can become exempt; and "he intended to do a mitzvah" deals with a mitzvah regarding which one can be exempt without [doing] it. And know that even without this there is one opinion in the Yerushalmi that being forced is as if he did; if so one can say that even according to us they admit in this; and understand well.
