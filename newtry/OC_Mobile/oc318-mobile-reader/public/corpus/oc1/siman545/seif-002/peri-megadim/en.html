And for need. See Taz regarding what our Rabbi wrote that they customarily were lenient in meshita: it does not refer to proofreading a Sefer Torah, for we require squared Ashuris script; and regarding greeting our Rabbi wrote in se'if 5 that they customarily change even in meshita, so it necessarily refers to what he relies upon. And regarding communal need there are some who say and some who explain; and in meshita according to all opinions it is not craftsman's work and is permitted for communal need even though it is not for the need of the moed, as in siman 544 in Taz 2. And Taz wrote that this is not clear, for Beis Yosef wrote that even though only thick Ashuris script was given at Sinai, one can say meshita is forbidden; therefore Taz wrote that it is not from the letter of the law but they customarily were lenient. And see Magen Avraham letter 3: one can say this is not clear, that meshita is writing and one is liable for it where possible; and nevertheless according to all opinions it is not craftsman's work, and communal needs that are layman's work [even] after the moed are permitted. What is implied, that to hire a chazzan and rav and communal leader on Chol HaMoed it is permitted to write craftsman's work: ostensibly this is difficult from siman 544, that communal needs specifically [are] for bodily benefit, building a synagogue not; and perhaps a rav who issues rulings or a chazzan is like bodily needs, and this requires further study.
