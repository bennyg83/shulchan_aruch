Ibid. This is unclear to me. The Taz initially understood that the Rama’s statement, “the custom is to be lenient,” does not refer specifically to communal needs but also to the Mechaber’s ruling. He inferred this because Rambam permits writing greetings on the ground that it is unskilled work, while he explains that writing a Sefer Torah is forbidden because it is skilled work. Accordingly, since the Rama writes that this is not skilled work, it should be permitted even for an individual. The difficulty is that even unskilled work was permitted for an individual only when needed for the festival, as explained in the preceding siman. Rambam’s permission to write greetings must therefore concern a need of the festival, as Beit Yosef also writes. This seif, however, deals with something not needed for the festival, while the law of a festival need is stated only in seif 2. The Taz’s conclusion is therefore primary. He accordingly asks to which ruling the Rama refers, and concludes that the Rama does not refer to the Mechaber’s words but to the law of communal needs discussed by the Rama.
