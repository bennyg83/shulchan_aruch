(there, seif 8) One who was sent to ask him, etc. — in responsa Radbaz vol. 2 siman 257 he wrote that to answer a responsum is communal needs, and even if not for festival needs it is permitted to write, for writing does not require expert skill; so too editing a Torah scroll is different, for it requires a skilled scribe to write Assyrian script similar to the book's script and that letters not stick to each other (and this supports what Peri Megadim Eshel Avraham wrote, that erasing a letter is not professional melakha and is permitted; but possibly if it was in the middle of a word and requires dragging and writing to bring letters together that separated by erasing the ink from there and one must draw or thicken letters so they come close — it seems this too requires skill and is forbidden on Chol HaMoed; and it seems everything depends on the case whether this act requires skill or not; and if it requires skill, even if now a layman wishes to do it, it is forbidden since the act itself requires skilled hands; and if he fixes them as a layman it is forbidden because it is not honor to the Torah — so it appears to me); but plain writing for communal needs is permitted etc., and even when writing through a scribe it is permitted for the reason I wrote etc., although they did not permit a slight modification in sewing as we taught and the artisan presses — implying the modification should be recognizable in the labor itself; nevertheless writing labor is more common and people are more accustomed to it than sewing, and a slight modification suffices — see there; and in Be'er Heitev he wrote responsum of Kiryat Sefer — Mahari Halevi from Morocco, who was early and asked questions from Heaven and they answered him; he asked if permitted to write a verse explanation or halakhah on Chol HaMoed, and they answered him: O you who love the Name, gates of Zion distinguished in halakhah who innovate in halakhah and establish them on their foundations — those gates we love more than all dwellings of Jacob; and all who think thoughts and reasoning in instructive halakhah and strict poskim are beloved and pleasant before the King on high; and if for loss of silver labor was permitted on Chol HaMoed, how much more so, and how much more that one should fear loss of the precious jewels that are reasoning and thoughts of precious Torah that the writer may lest they be forgotten and lost; and whoever writes them receives reward for them — this they explained to him, and they placed it on his lips; and it seems a person's pleasant letter without a matter of loss is forbidden to write even by modification; and all letters of peace asking a friend's welfare or informing him of his welfare — behold it gladdens him, and for that joy they permitted to write authorization letters; and he explained they are letters of peace — end of his words:
