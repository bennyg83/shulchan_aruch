(11) There. {Rama: And for public need some forbid etc.} So too in Teshuvos HaRadach siman 85, and he wrote there that ordinary writing that the Sages forbade is considered ma'aseh uman etc., and it appears from his words that even writing with our script for public need is forbidden if it is not for festival need — see there. However Bach disagreed with him and wrote that it is obvious that they did not forbid writing on the festival except in Assyrian script with which they write sifrei Torah, for it is ma'aseh uman; but all writing from our script is not ma'aseh uman, and therefore they permitted to write even a greeting inquiry and his accounts, and all the more it is permitted to write for public need even if it is not for festival need and without alteration — only he wrote that they are accustomed to be precise to write with alteration, and the alteration should be that he who writes himself should not be beautiful in his letters as he is accustomed to write beautifully on weekdays; but if he wrote beautifully as his weekday custom and only altered to begin writing in the corner of the paper — this is not alteration — see there. And Beit David that Shnei Luchot HaBrit wrote in Magen Avraham or 3 — see there or 1 A"R or 5, and one who is lenient to write without alteration in a place where there is no custom did not lose.
