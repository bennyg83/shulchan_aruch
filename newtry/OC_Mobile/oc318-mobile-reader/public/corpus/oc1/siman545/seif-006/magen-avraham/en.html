A promissory note on the moed. Ostensibly it appears that if he gives him the money it is permitted to write, for one must be concerned lest the witnesses die, for it is no worse than gift documents, as is written in se'if 5.
And the borrower needs money. For the moed: so wrote the Tur; and in Rabbeinu Yeruham he wrote for the need of the moed; and in the Gemara folio 13 in Rashi's commentary it is implied even if he has what to eat, only that he wants to lend for profit, it is permitted. And this requires further study why Rama omitted the word "for the moed"; and perhaps he relied on the Rambam, who wrote that he needs money on the moed, implying even if he now needs money for other matters it is permitted, for for him it is something that would be lost. However, if he does not need it now but only after the moed, according to all opinions it is forbidden. And it appears to me that if after the moed he will not find money to borrow, it is permitted to write, for it is something that would be lost.
A sale document. That is, when he sold for the need of the moed, as is written in siman 539, se'if 2; but when he sold before the moed, it is forbidden to write on the moed even though there is concern that they will retract, as with sharecropping documents, which are forbidden; and so it is implied in the Levush. And it is not comparable to gift documents, for here even if they retract there is no loss, since he can buy or take [a lease] for himself elsewhere.
