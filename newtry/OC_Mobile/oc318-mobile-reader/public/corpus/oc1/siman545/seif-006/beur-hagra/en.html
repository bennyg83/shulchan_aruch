Se'if 6. And the borrower etc. Rambam and Tur: that not for need it is forbidden like writing books, but for need it is permitted like women's gittin etc., for those are communal needs, and likewise here. Rambam; and as Rivash wrote that every court that occurs regularly is communal needs from the Mishnah and Yerushalmi aforementioned; and see Beis Yosef and see Nimukei Yosef on the Mishnah there.
Or that there is no etc. There, folio 13a.
It is permitted to write etc. As with a promissory note that has need; and that which the Mishnah did not teach it among "these they write" is because they sell only for the need of the moed, and for the need of the moed it is obvious that it is permitted (Tosafos there s.v. "There is no" etc.); and their conclusion is that the Mishnah's explanation is because it is something that would be lost and not because of [moed] need, and therefore it was not taught; and see Tosafos in that same discussion, "and the same law" etc.
But there is no etc. Tosefta chapter 2: they do not write sharecropping and contracting documents on the moed, and R' Yehuda permits lest another precede him; and Sefer HaTerumah explained: where he accepted before the moed, therefore R' Yehuda permits, for before writing both can retract until he goes down into it; and the halacha is like the first Tanna. And that is to say that to accept on the moed would not have been permitted, for R' Yehuda does not disagree that they do not write a promissory note on the moed except specifically when needed for the moed, as above.
