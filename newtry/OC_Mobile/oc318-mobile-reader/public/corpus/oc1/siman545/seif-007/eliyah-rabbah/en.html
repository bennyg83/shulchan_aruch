[7] [Levush] With them on a spindle etc. But the Shulchan Aruch wrote "on his thigh," implying that a change is required. And in truth Taz was astonished: behold the Tur, Rambam, and Beis Yosef ruled that a change is not required; and Magen Avraham appears to answer that he came to teach that with a spinning-wheel it is forbidden to spin because the matter is very conspicuous, but that a change is never required; and this is forced, and also against the sugyos of the Talmud Moed Katan folio 9. And in my humble opinion, the view of the Shulchan Aruch in the gloss is that specifically techeles, which is a minor matter, but all the threads of tzitzis require a change. And even though he wrote techeles in the Shulchan Aruch, he relied on the understanding that it deals with all the threads of tzitzis, for there is no techeles nowadays. And it is possible that this is the intent of Rama who added "tzitzis." Nevertheless for practical law it appears to me that the main view is like the Levush, that even for all the threads of tzitzis a change is not required, and so wrote Rabbeinu Yeruham. Bach wrote: from that the Mishnah cited "techeles for his tzitzis," the Tur learned it is forbidden for others even for free, for it is not comparable to tefillin, which is a bodily obligation; until here. But from the arrangement of the Levush's words it is clear that it is permitted for others like tefillin; and it appears to me that he holds it is R' Yehuda who said "for his tzitzis" and spun for his tzitzis and for others with benefit, implying that even for tzitzis it is permitted for free. And it is possible that R' Yehuda and R' Yosi agree on this. Also the Tur: it is possible it deals specifically with pay being forbidden; and so I found explained in the Yerushalmi there.
