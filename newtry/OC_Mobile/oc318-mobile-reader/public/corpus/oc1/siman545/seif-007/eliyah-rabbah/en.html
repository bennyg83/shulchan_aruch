(z) [Levush] on the thigh with a stone etc. But the Shulchan Aruch wrote on the thigh, implying that alteration is required. And in truth the Taz was astonished — behold the Tur, Rambam, and Beit Yosef ruled that alteration is not required, and Magen Avraham appears to answer that he came to inform us that with a spindle it is forbidden to spin because it is a very conspicuous matter, but nevertheless alteration is never required — and this is forced, also against the sugyot of Moed Katan daf 9. And in my humble opinion — the view of the Shulchan Aruch in the gloss is that specifically techelet, which is a minor matter, but all the threads of tzitzit require alteration. And even though he wrote techelet in the Shulchan Aruch, he relied on the understanding that it deals with all the threads of tzitzit, for there is no techelet in our time. And it is possible that this is the intent of the Rama who added "tzitzit." Nevertheless for the law it appears to me the main view is like the Levush that even in all the threads of tzitzit alteration is not required, and so wrote Rabbenu Yerucham. Bach wrote: from that he cited in the mishnah "techelet for his tzitzit" the Tur learned it is forbidden for others even for free, for it is not comparable to tefillin which is an obligation of the body — until here. But from the arrangement of the words of the Levush it is clear that it is permitted for others like tefillin, and it appears to me he holds it is R' Yehudah who said for his tzitzit and spun for his tzitzit and for others with benefit — it implies that even for tzitzit it is permitted for free. And it is possible R' Yehudah and R' Yosi agree on this. Also the Tur — it is possible it deals specifically with pay being forbidden, and so I found explained in the Yerushalmi there.
