If he heard a novel Torah insight. The same applies that he may initially listen to it in order to write it down. There is no distinction between an insight heard from another person and one he developed himself, for it is common to forget an insight and be unable to reconstruct it later. Even without concern for forgetting, a person is obligated at every moment to toil in Torah and develop insights according to his ability. One cannot say that he should wait until after Yom Tov to write the insight, since at that later time he will have a new obligation to develop other insights. If he must then review what he studied during the festival and reconstruct his earlier insight, he will be prevented from developing new insights at that time. There is no greater potential loss than this. It is reported that the Bach wrote his works on Chol HaMoed. Later authorities. Nevertheless, he should write his insights in cursive script, and if he must write a square letter he should make it broken, as stated above.
It is permitted to write it, etc. Calculations of astronomical cycles may not be written, because writing them does not remove anything from his mind and he will not forget them. A book needed for study during the festival may be repaired and bound by the person himself, and all the more so by a non-Jew, since this is a potential loss. It is forbidden, however, to repair a notebook in order to write in it after the festival. The later authorities write that whenever writing is permitted, one may also prepare the pen and ink needed for it. Eliyah Rabbah was uncertain whether one may rule lines for permitted writing, while Leket HaKemach forbids it because ruling is only for beautifying the writing. For tefillin and mezuzos, however, where writing without ruled lines is forbidden, it is permitted.
