(There, se'if 8) One whom they sent to ask of him, etc. In Responsa of the Radbaz, part 2, siman 357, he wrote that to answer a responsum is a communal need, and even if it is not for the need of the moed it is permitted to write, for writing does not require so much craftsmanship; and correcting a Sefer Torah is different, for it requires a great expert to write Ashuris script resembling the script of the Sefer, and that letter not cling to letter (and this supports what Panim Meiros wrote, that to erase a letter is not craftsman's work and is permitted; but perhaps if it was in the middle of a word and one needs scraping and writing to bring closer letters that became spaced through scraping away the extra from there, and one needs to extend or thicken the letters so they draw closer, it appears that this too requires skill and is forbidden on Chol HaMoed; and it appears that everything is according to the matter, whether this act requires skill or not; and if it requires skill, even if now a layman wants to do it, it is forbidden, since the essence of the act requires skilled hands; and if he corrects them as a layman it is forbidden because that is not the honor of the Torah: so it appears to my humble opinion; but ordinary writing for communal need is permitted, etc.; and even when he writes through a scribe it is permitted for the reason I wrote, etc. And even though they did not permit a slight change in sewing, as we learned "and the craftsman clamps," implying that the change must be recognizable in the work itself, nevertheless the work of writing is more common and people are more accustomed to it than sewing, and a slight change suffices, see there. And in Birkei Yosef he wrote a manuscript responsum of Mahari Halevi of Morocco, who was an early authority and asked questions of Heaven and they answered him, and he asked whether it is permitted to write a commentary on a verse or halacha on Chol HaMoed; and they answered him: "Hashem loves the gates of Zion that are marked in halacha," those who innovate a novelty in halacha and establish them clearly; those gates are more beloved than all the dwellings of Yaakov; and all those who think thoughts and theories in the instructing Torah and in the serious poskim are beloved and pleasant before the Supernal King; and if for the loss of silver melacha was permitted on Chol HaMoed, all the more so and all the more so one must be concerned for the loss of precious pearls, which are the theories and thoughts of the precious Torah, that one must write them so they not be forgotten and lost; and whoever writes that receives reward for them. This they explained to him, and they put truth in his mouth. And it appears that a letter of a person's utensils that contains nothing that would be lost is forbidden to write even through a change; and all letters of peace in which one asks after his friend's welfare or informs him of his welfare rejoice him, and for the sake of that joy they permitted writing letters of reshut, and he explained that these are letters of peace, end quote.
