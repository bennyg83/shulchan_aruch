(s.k. 8) On his thigh: it appears to me that he came to teach etc. That is to say, since we hold in the Talmud that "on his thigh," meaning through a change, is not precise, but likewise with a stone or with a spindle it is permitted, and as Magen Avraham also wrote in siman 100, note 7: if so, why did our Rabbi copy "on his thigh," implying a change is required, and that is against the conclusion of the Talmud? Therefore Magen Avraham answered that he came to exclude through a spinning-wheel: for although a change is not required and it is permitted through a stone or spindle, nevertheless through a spinning-wheel it is forbidden because the matter is conspicuous. And one must say why he did not cite stone or spindle: for then one would not know that his intent was to exclude a spinning-wheel; rather what he cited is a spindle, and the same applies to a spinning-wheel, and he mentioned one of them.
