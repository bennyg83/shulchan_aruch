Pesikta. How much this one gives for his son and that one for his daughter.
Diyatiki. A gift of a person on his deathbed.
Prozbolin. So that the debt not be cancelled in Shemittah.
Letters of appraisal. That they appraised the borrower's assets for the lender.
And letters of support. That he undertook to support his wife's daughter.
Selections [of dayanim]. That they write: this one selects one for himself and that one selects one for himself; or documents of claims. And the reason all these are permitted is because they are communal needs to uphold justice; and even though each time it is for a particular person, nevertheless since it occurs regularly it is called communal needs (Beis Yosef; and see there). And if so, perhaps only layman's work is permitted, since it is not for the need of the moed, as is written in siman 544, se'if 2; but Tosafos and Beis Yosef wrote that it is something that would be lost, lest the giver or the beis din or the witnesses die; therefore even craftsman's work is permitted. One who fled with other people's money: it is permitted to write against him the judges' decrees and a power of attorney, and it is good to do so discreetly; but if it is certain that he will return to his city, it is forbidden (Maharashdam 27 and Maharshach 113); see what I wrote at the beginning of this [siman], and I do not know why this should be worse than judicial rulings.
A kesubah on the moed. For the reason that it is something that would be lost, lest a challenge arise regarding the agreement of their conditions (Kol Bo). And it deals with one who married on the eve of the festival; and if so, in our countries where they write all kesubos alike, it is forbidden to write; and see in Even HaEzer siman 66, se'if 12. But Tosafos of a kesubah are permitted.
And some forbid. For it is like a promissory note; see se'if 6. And there is no concern lest the witnesses die, for witnesses have not yet been designated; and if the husband dies, is it not known that all women have a kesubah?
That is not being lost. But regarding something that would be lost it is permitted without a change, as is written at the beginning of siman 537.
And they customarily were stringent to change even etc. So it must read, and so it is in Darchei Moshe. And the change is that they write diagonally; and even though it is not called a change except when the body of the letters is broken, nevertheless, since according to the letter of the law it is permitted to write letters of greeting without a change, as our Rabbi Beis Yosef wrote, except that they customarily change, this change suffices; but writings that are forbidden to write are forbidden even diagonally, end quote of Darchei Moshe. And Bach wrote that meshita is not called writing at all; and so wrote the Agudah; and so wrote Beis Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yeruham and Orchos Chaim and Rabbeinu Tam; and see what I wrote in siman 340, se'if 5. And perhaps for this reason they customarily were lenient with all writings, and they have [authorities] on whom to rely; and some are accustomed to make the top line crooked.
