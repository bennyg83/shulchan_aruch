And even regarding a matter of merchandise that is not being lost. Now it is known that even layman's work is permitted only for the need of the moed; and here, what need of the moed is there? And I found in Peri Megadim that he sensed this and wrote: perhaps it is considered somewhat a need of the moed, and this requires a bit of further study, end quote. And perhaps his intent is that otherwise he has distress and it detracts from the joy of the festival; and that is a great stretch, for if so we would permit every matter of commerce by this [reasoning]. And in truth I do not know from where Beis Yosef derived to be lenient regarding writing for merchandise. And I searched and found my strength in Sefer Shibolei HaLeket, siman 225, and this is his language: "And letters of reshut: some explain that this is greeting, for since he is not writing his needs at all but only a greeting alone, there is no exertion in that writing and it does not resemble melacha at all; therefore it is permitted, for it is only like one strolling about," etc. "And the Rav R' Avigdor Kohen Tzedek replied regarding the letter that they write on the moed to send from place to place: if it is about a commercial matter that is not being lost, or other dealings between a person and his fellow, there is no permission in what they do by way of a change in a circle, for we learned: they do not proofread even one letter in the Sefer HaAzarah; and here it is impossible that he not write one letter in its usual way," and so wrote the students of Riva, of blessed memory, etc. "But regarding letters of greetings, some are accustomed [to be] lenient and say that these are the letters of reshut that we learned: and these they write on the moed; however, if he scheduled his work for the moed when he could have written beforehand, he forbids [it] there in his Tosafos," end quote. Behold you have explicitly that even if we permit regarding greeting, in any case regarding letters for merchandise that is not being lost one certainly should be stringent, except for writing in meshita and through a change; and even in this he should reduce as much as he can.
And some forbid regarding greeting. See in Beis Yosef that even according to the stringent view it is permitted to write a letter of reshut, that is, documents of the government, their writs and commands. And in Shibolei HaLeket I saw in the name of Rav Nachshon Gaon and in the name of R' Yitzchak Gaon: letters of reshut are a writ that the Exilarch writes and gives him authority to teach Israel forbidden and permitted matters and to instruct them in words of Torah, end quote. And ostensibly this is like a writ of semicha that they give nowadays.
Even in our writing, which is meshita writing. See Mishnah Berurah. And although regarding Shabbos we hold that one who writes in any script and language of any nation is liable, and how is a Hebrew script inferior to them, one must say that even so, regarding Chol HaMoed they forbade only a script that is craftsman's work, and ordinary writing alone, aside from Ashuris script, is called layman's work. And in Sefer Mor UKetziyah he elaborates on this matter, and his view is that one should be stringent even in meshita, at least in a scribe's handwriting, and as is the practice for the community to have a fine special clerk for their writings, which is craftsman's work; therefore it is proper that he not write a fine writing in any script and language, whether meshita or in the script of the nations, for all is writing for every nation in its script and language, end quote. And although the world customarily was lenient in this and one should not protest against them, for they have [authorities] on whom to rely, nevertheless it is good to be concerned for his words except for a great need; and especially regarding merchandise that is not being lost, it is certainly proper to reduce writing about this on Chol HaMoed as much as one can.
