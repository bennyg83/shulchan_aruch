It is permitted to write documents of kiddushin, documents recording pledged marriage contributions, gittin, receipts, deathbed dispositions, gifts, prozbul documents, appraisal documents, and support documents. A document of pledged contributions records that a person undertakes to give his son or daughter a specified amount. A deathbed disposition is a document stating that this shall stand and take effect. A prozbul is an enactment for the lender so that he will not lose his money, thereby enabling the borrower to find someone willing to lend to him. Appraisal documents record that beit din appraised a borrower's property and gave it to the creditor. A support document records that beit din sold land to provide sustenance for a wife and daughters and recorded the act of beit din, or alternatively records one who undertook to support his wife's daughter. It is also permitted to write documents of chalitzah and mi'un, arbitration documents, and judicial rulings. {Rama: It is permitted to write a ketubah during the Moed [Kol Bo], although some prohibit it [Semak].} A letter of greeting that a person sends to his fellow may be written, even concerning merchandise that is not in danger of loss; some prohibit writing greetings. [Tur in the name of Halakhot Gedolot] The custom is to be stringent even regarding our ordinary cursive writing.
