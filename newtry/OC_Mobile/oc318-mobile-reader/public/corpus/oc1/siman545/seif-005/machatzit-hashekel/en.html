(s.k. 5) It is etc. It appears to me that if etc.: that is to say, what is something that would be lost here? Behold one can wait with his proofreading until after the moed. Granted regarding a Sefer Torah, as long as it has not been proofread one cannot read from it; which is not so regarding other books, for one can read from them even though the error is still not corrected. Therefore Taz explained that the error is that through his analysis he sensed there is an error here; and if so it is properly something that would be lost, for perhaps if he waits until after the moed he will forget it and will not notice that error; see there. And Magen Avraham explained: for example, that one cannot learn from it if it has not been proofread.
And for this reason it is permitted to write books: that is to say, what Rama permitted, and the same law applies to other books to learn from, also deals specifically with such a case where it was something that would be lost, that if he does not write them it will not be possible for him to learn.
