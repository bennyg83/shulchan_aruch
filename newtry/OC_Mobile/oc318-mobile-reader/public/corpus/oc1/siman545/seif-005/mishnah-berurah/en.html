It is permitted to write, etc. One reason is that any matter that arises frequently is considered a communal need, even though each occurrence is private. Another possibility is that it is considered a festival need because such documents are needed during the festival. Others explain that it is a potential loss, lest the witnesses die or travel overseas, or lest the donor or the beis din die.
Pesikta. This is comparable to the writing of engagement conditions in our time. Later authorities.
Deathbed directives and gifts. A deathbed directive is the will of a dangerously ill person, while gifts are gifts made by a healthy person.
So that his money will not be lost. Through the Sabbatical year.
And declarations of refusal. These are documents recording miun. They apply to a minor orphan girl whom her brothers or mother married off with her consent. She may leave the marriage through a simple declaration that she does not want him, and a document of refusal is written.
Documents of selection. These record that one litigant chooses a particular judge and the other chooses another. The same applies to statements of claim. If one who holds another person’s money has fled, the judges’ decrees and an authorization may be written against him. If it is certain that he will return to the city, however, it is forbidden.
And judicial decisions. The rulings issued by the judges.
It is permitted to write, etc. The case is one in which he married shortly before the festival, since marriages are not performed during the festival. Although one may not seclude himself with his wife without a kesubah, it must have been a pressing circumstance, in which case he could rely upon witnesses to the acquisition. Machatzis HaShekel.
A kesubah during the festival. It is permitted because of potential loss, lest a dispute arise concerning the agreed conditions. Accordingly, in our countries, where all kesubos are written in a standard form, all opinions forbid writing one. An additional sum that the husband voluntarily adds may be written. Magen Avraham. Peri Megadim writes that one may be lenient in our time if it is written in cursive script.
Some forbid. Their reason is that it resembles a promissory note. The halachah follows the first opinion. As stated above, in our countries one should be lenient only in cursive script.
And letters of greeting. The reason is that a person is not highly exacting about writing them neatly, so they are considered unskilled work. They are also considered somewhat needed for the festival, since he inquires after his friend’s welfare. Otherwise even unskilled work would be forbidden, as stated above in siman 541.
That is not being lost. All the more so, if he may suffer a loss by not writing, it is certainly permitted. According to the Mechaber, all this may be written without a deviation. Nevertheless, one may be lenient only when he did not deliberately schedule the work for the festival.
Some forbid. Their reason is that all writing is skilled work.
Regarding greetings. All the more so regarding business matters.
The custom is to be stringent. The correct reading is: The custom is to be stringent and to make a deviation even in such writing. The deviation is to write all the lines diagonally. Although seif 7 states that anything forbidden to write remains forbidden even with a deviation, unless the letters themselves are broken in the middle, here the law permits letters of greeting without any deviation according to Beit Yosef. Since the deviation is only a custom, this change is sufficient.
Because it is cursive writing. Many later authorities write that in our countries the custom is to be lenient with cursive script and with our ordinary writing, since it is not skilled work. They have authorities upon whom to rely. One who wishes to be stringent should be stringent only for himself. The custom is to make a slight change by writing the upper line crooked. See Biur Halachah. Know that regarding business involving a potential loss, all opinions permit writing even in skilled script, and no deviation is required.
