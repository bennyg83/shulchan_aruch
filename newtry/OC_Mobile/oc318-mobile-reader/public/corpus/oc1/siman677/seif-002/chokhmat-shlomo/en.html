Note: I was uncertain regarding a minor who became an adult during the days of Chanukah, and a convert who converted during the eight days of Chanukah, whether he is obligated in the remaining lamps or not. And if you say that he is obligated, then if he converted at night after feet have ceased from the marketplace, for we hold that after the fact immersion at night is effective, see Yoreh Deah, siman 268, or in the case of a minor who became an adult then according to those who require exact hours in the years, is he obligated in the lamps of that night or not? Now Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos in siman 686 that a convert who converted before sunrise is exempt from the nighttime Megillah reading, because its main mitzvah is by day. It appears that what he wrote, that its main mitzvah is by day, is not needed to exempt him from the nighttime reading, for regarding that, why mention that its main mitzvah is by day? Even if its main mitzvah were by night, or they were equal, he would also be exempt from the nighttime reading in this case, since before sunrise it is day, and the nighttime reading applies then only by way of compensation. Since he was exempt at the time of the obligation, he remains exempt afterward as well, as the authorities wrote regarding an onen and Havdalah. Why then did he need the reason that its main mitzvah is by day? Rather, the intent is for the inference: if its main mitzvah were not by day, they would be exempt by day, since the entire time was not in a state of obligation. But since its main mitzvah is by day, the whole daytime obligation still remains in a state of obligation. His intent is therefore as follows: a convert who converted before sunrise is exempt from the nighttime reading, which implies that from the daytime reading he is obligated, because its main mitzvah is by day. If so, it implies that if its main mitzvah were not by day, he would be exempt even from that of the day. Accordingly, regarding Chanukah, where all the days are equal, it would seem that he is entirely exempt, since the whole time was not in a state of obligation, and at the beginning of the obligation he was exempt. However, it seems that this is not comparable. There, regarding Purim, the miracle occurred before the fourteenth, and the fourteenth was already a day of rest; it is only a remembrance of the miracle of yesterday. Therefore, if its main mitzvah were not by day, and the whole day were not in a state of obligation, they would be exempt from the day. But since we hold that it diminishes the time but does not diminish the miracle, as the Talmud explains, because the miracle exists on every day, here too, in this case, the miracle of each day is a new miracle, and he is obligated to bless over what is to come. Now with this one can understand the dispute of the amoraim concerning the reason of Beis Shammai and Beis Hillel. One opinion holds that the reason of Beis Shammai is corresponding to the days that are coming in, and the reason of Beis Hillel is corresponding to the days that have gone out. Another opinion holds that the reason of Beis Shammai is corresponding to the bulls of the Festival, and the reason of Beis Hillel is because we ascend in holiness and do not descend. At first glance, what practical legal difference is there in this? But according to the above, one may say that there is a practical difference regarding a convert who converted, or a minor who became an adult, in the middle of the days of Chanukah. According to the reason that Beis Shammai corresponds to the days that are coming in, the convert too is obligated to kindle according to the number of Chanukah days that are coming in, like everyone else. But according to Beis Hillel, where it corresponds to those that have gone out, he was not under obligation during the days that have gone out, and therefore the concept of beautification is not applicable to him, and he kindles only one lamp. Or perhaps, since it is not possible to kindle corresponding to those that have gone out, Beis Hillel agrees that he kindles corresponding to those coming in. Specifically where it is possible to kindle corresponding to those gone out does he kindle that way; but where it is not possible, Beis Hillel agrees that he kindles corresponding to those coming in. With this, there will be a distinction between the reasons. According to the other reason, that Beis Shammai corresponds to the bulls of the Festival, this is understandable if he began while obligated, for then he resembles the bulls of the Festival, since he kindled the entire number of the obligation. But if he began in the middle and did not kindle the whole obligation at all, he does not resemble the bulls of the Festival, and then Beis Shammai would agree that there is no beautification for him at all. Beis Hillel holds that we ascend in holiness and do not descend, and therefore he too kindles corresponding to those that have gone out, even though he was not then under obligation, in order to be ascending in holiness. There will be a major practical difference between the reasons. According to the reason of corresponding to those that have gone out or those coming in, then for Beis Shammai a convert who converted and a minor who became an adult kindle like everyone else, corresponding to those coming in. For Beis Hillel, where it corresponds to those gone out, this is not applicable to a convert; according to this he holds that there is no beautification for him at all, or that he kindles corresponding to those coming in. But according to the other reason, corresponding to the bulls of the Festival and ascending in holiness, the matter is reversed: according to Beis Shammai, a convert or minor who became obligated in the middle has no beautification at all, since he does not resemble the bulls of the Festival; but according to Beis Hillel, even in this case he kindles like everyone else, in order to ascend in holiness. This seems to me in my humble opinion. With this it is well understood that Rif and Rosh quoted the dispute about the reasons of Beis Shammai and Beis Hillel. At first glance, there is no practical legal difference. According to the above, there is indeed a practical legal difference. However, since it is so that a convert who converted in the middle and a minor who became an adult in the middle are obligated, one must examine if he converted, or became an adult according to those who require exact hours in the years, after feet have ceased from the marketplace, whether he is obligated that night or not. At first glance it would seem that since the main time is only until feet cease from the marketplace, and afterward it is by way of compensation, if at the time of his main obligation he was exempt, he is then obligated to kindle afterward? Rather, it would be like the case there of Megillah, where he is exempt from the nighttime reading, and like an onen regarding Havdalah. But according to this, we are left with one difficulty: in chapter 2 of Shabbos, the Talmud asks, if it went out he is not required to tend to it, but did the master not say that its mitzvah is until feet cease from the marketplace? It answers: this means that if he did not yet kindle, he kindles; alternatively, it refers to the measure. Why did it not answer that the practical difference is a convert who converted after the time when feet ceased, etc., who is exempt from that night's lighting because it is only compensation? But any other person who did not kindle does kindle afterward as compensation. Likewise, for the measure too we do not need it, and the only practical difference is that afterward it is only compensation, and a convert who converted or a minor who became adult then is exempt from that night, according to those who require exact hours. This requires examination.
