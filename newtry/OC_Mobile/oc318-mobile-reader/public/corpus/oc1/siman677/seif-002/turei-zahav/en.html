And some say that in this time, etc., he should kindle in the place where he eats. In Responsa Rashba, siman 541, he wrote: one who eats at the homeowner's table, even if he sleeps in a second house by himself, need not kindle, but must participate with the homeowner like a lodger who has no house in another place. Until here. From this Rema learned that we do not follow the place of sleeping, for if so this person would have an obligation to kindle in the place where he sleeps. It appears that Rema too is speaking only of one who always has a room designated for eating and another room for sleeping, but the main one is the eating room, as in the case of Rashba that I mentioned. This is not so for one who has a dwelling in the city and one time goes outside his home and dines at his friend's. It is obvious that he should not leave his home and kindle in the house where he eats one time at his friend's; rather, he must go to his home and kindle there. If the desire to eat seized him and he does not want to move from there now, he should command the members of his household to kindle for him in his home. The house where he is dining has the law only of a lodger for whom they kindle at home, and he does not kindle in the place where he is. Even according to what I wrote nearby, that a lodger may kindle also in the place where he is, that is where his sleeping will now also be there. This is not so in this case, where he will eat here for an hour or two and return to his place. There is no logic to say that he should kindle there and not in his home, for this is as though he were standing at the time of kindling in the street of the city, where no kindling belongs to him there. Although I have seen some people who, when eating a meal at someone else's home, send for their lamps and light there, relying on this gloss, this is an error in their hands, and they do not understand this matter. This is the language of Maharshal: a groom who eats in his father-in-law's house, if he has the means, even if he sleeps in his father-in-law's house, should not participate but should kindle for himself because of mehadrin, for he is a man and his household. If he sleeps in another house, it is obvious that he is obligated to kindle in the place where he sleeps. Until here. This is against the view of Rashba that I cited. In my humble opinion, the words of Rashba are primary in this. There is proof from Eruvei Chatzeiros, siman 370, section 5: one who eats in one place and sleeps in another, the place of eating is primary, etc. Although the Tur wrote in the name of Rosh that he must kindle in the place where he sleeps, this does not contradict it, for there he is speaking according to the law of the Talmud and the early authorities, where one must be careful because of suspicion, as the Tur wrote here, because of the neighbors, etc. This is not so for us, who kindle indoors and where recognition is only for the household members, as I wrote above at the end of siman 671. This is explained in Rashba, siman 541. We are not concerned about the place of sleeping, for the household members know that his fixed dwelling for eating is elsewhere, and there he kindles. This seems clear to me.
