For he is obligated to see. This contradicts siman 676, section 3, where he holds that when they kindle in his home he need not bless upon seeing. The law here is from Mordechai, while there the Mechaber ruled plainly like the other authorities who disagree with him. Therefore, as a matter of law, one should not practice this way, to kindle with a blessing, unless he says that he does not want to fulfill with his wife's lighting, as Rema writes afterward. See below.
If he wants to be stringent, etc. Meaning: although by the strict law he is exempt from kindling if they kindle for him in his home, nevertheless, if he wants to be stringent upon himself and kindle himself, he may, provided that he has in mind before the time of kindling that he does not want to fulfill through his wife's lighting. Automatically there is no blessing in vain here, for since he does not want to fulfill through her lighting, the obligation of kindling applies to him.
And so is the practice. So too many later authorities ruled plainly. Some authorities hold that after the Sages exempted him through his wife's lighting, he does not have the power to say, I do not want to fulfill through my wife's lighting; according to them he should kindle without a blessing. Now, although certainly one should not protest against those accustomed to bless, since many later authorities agreed to bless, nevertheless it is better to be careful to hear the blessings from another and answer amen, intending to fulfill through them, and then kindle his lamps. Or he should try to kindle his lamps and bless over them some time before his wife kindles at his home, meaning that she kindles after nightfall according to the practice of the world, and he kindles before nightfall.
