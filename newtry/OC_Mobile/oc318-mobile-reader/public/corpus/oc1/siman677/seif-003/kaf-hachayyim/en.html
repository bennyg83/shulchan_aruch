3) Ibid. He must give a perutah, etc. It appears that this applies specifically to a lodger who pays his expenses to the homeowner. He is then obligated to participate, and if he has a private room, he must kindle in his room. However, if a lodger is entirely dependent upon the homeowner, eats of his bread, and drinks of his cup, he need not do this at all. Since the homeowner provides all his needs, he will certainly transfer to him ownership of a portion of the money for the lights as well. There is no suspicion in this case, since it is known that he relies upon the homeowner's table. Gan HaMelech, siman 41; Yad Aharon, ibid.; Kisei Eliyahu, letter 1; Shulchan Gavohah, letter 2, in the supplement. Meaning, if he eats with the homeowner and is charged for everything he eats and drinks, he must give a perutah. However, if he eats there for a fixed sum per day or per week, including everything he needs, he need not give a perutah. It appears that the same applies if the homeowner provides all his needs without charge, and he need not give a perutah. Nevertheless, it appears preferable for him to say explicitly to the homeowner, "Transfer to me ownership of a portion of the oil in your Chanukah lights," and the homeowner should say, "I hereby transfer ownership to you, etc.," whether he eats there for payment or without charge. However, regarding what he wrote, that even if he has a private room he need not kindle, see below, letter 9. It appears that the same ruling applies here.
