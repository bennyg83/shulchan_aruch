A lodger, etc. The same applies to yeshivah students who study away from home: they must at least participate. This is specifically with a student who eats independently. But if he depends on the homeowner's table, he is included among the members of the household. The same applies to his servants, since they regularly depend on his table: by the strict law they need not kindle unless they want to be among those who beautify the mitzvah.
For whom they do not kindle in his home. For by the strict law, when his wife kindles the Chanukah lamp in his home, he fulfills his obligation through her kindling, even if he is in a place far from his home, and he no longer needs even to participate with coins. See Turei Zahav in the name of Maharshal, that this is specifically when he knows clearly that his wife kindles for him. But if he does not know, he must kindle and also bless. For this reason, one who did not know that they kindled for him and came home that night and found that they had kindled for him must kindle, because since he did not know at first, presumably his intent was to kindle himself and not fulfill through his wife's lighting, and automatically the ordinance of the Sages took effect upon him. See Magen Avraham, whose view on this is that he should kindle without a blessing.
To give a perutah. This means that the lodger need not give half of all the lamp expenses; a perutah is enough, because through this he transfers to him a share in the oil. Some later authorities wrote that the homeowner must add a little oil for him, beyond the measure that he must provide for half an hour for himself. The same applies if the homeowner transfers ownership to him of a share in the oil as a gift; that is permitted, for it is like participating with a perutah. Nevertheless, if the lodger has a lamp, it is more proper for him to kindle himself.
To participate with him. This means that even if he eats at the homeowner's table, we do not say that he is like a member of his household and that by the strict law one lamp is enough for all of them. Since he is a guest and does not depend on his table regularly, he must participate with him. The lodger must hear the blessings from the homeowner. Later authorities.
If he has, etc. The reason is suspicion, for they may suspect him of not kindling the Chanukah lamp; even if he knows that they kindle for him in his home, this does not help.
An entrance open, etc. Even if it is open in the direction where the homeowner's entrance is open, and the homeowner kindled the Chanukah lamp, nevertheless we are concerned that when people know that a guest is lodging here, they will say that he did not kindle the Chanukah lamp.
For himself. This implies that when there is no open entrance, even if he lodges in a room by himself, he may participate with coins together with the homeowner, or, if he is married, rely on his home. Some authorities hold that all this was in the time of the Gemara, when they kindled at the entrances of the houses and courtyards. But nowadays, when we kindle indoors in the house, all guests, even one who is married and for whom they certainly kindle in his home, must nevertheless kindle by themselves in every case, for who in the lodging place knows whether he has a wife at home who kindles for him? They may come to suspect him. Participation with coins also does not help, because since today's custom is that each person kindles in the house on his own, the household members may suspect him. Magen Avraham concludes that one should be stringent like their words only when he at least has a separate room. But if he does not have a separate room, he may rely on the members of his household who kindled for him at home or on coins. See above at the end of paragraph 3, where we wrote that when he has a lamp it is more proper for him to kindle himself. See above in siman 676, where we brought several authorities who hold that when he relies on the members of his household, although he thereby fulfills the obligation of the Chanukah lamp, nevertheless when he later sees a Chanukah lamp some say that he must recite Who performed miracles. Therefore it is more proper to kindle himself, even one lamp, and not rely on this leniency. See below in section 3, where we will write about this.
He must kindle, etc. Since in this case the kindling is only because of suspicion, some say that he should not bless if he knows that they kindle for him in his home. But if they do not kindle for him in his home, he must bless, because since he kindles himself, he certainly is not participating with them by coins, and the obligation of kindling applies to him by the strict law.
Even though that house, etc. Its reason is that since he has a house designated for sleeping, and people see him entering and leaving it, there is suspicion if he does not kindle there, for people do not know that he eats elsewhere. Tur.
Who eats at his father's. Even if he eats there regularly, since the reason is suspicion.
And some say that in this time, etc. See the later authorities, who wrote that the first view also agrees in our time, since when they kindle indoors there is no suspicion from passersby, only from the household members, and they know that he kindles in the place where he eats or participates with the homeowner there.
In the place where he eats. For the place where one eats is primary. This is specifically regarding the aforementioned lodger, whose home is not with him, or regarding a son who depends on his father's table. But one who dines at his friend's house incidentally and has a house in that city must go to his house to kindle the Chanukah lamp there. If he does not want to move from the place where he is dining, he should instruct his wife to kindle for him in his home. Nevertheless, it is a greater mitzvah for him to do it than through his agent. See Biur Halacha.
