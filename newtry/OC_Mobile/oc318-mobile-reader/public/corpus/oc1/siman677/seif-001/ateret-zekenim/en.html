Open for himself. Even a blind unmarried person, for whom they do not kindle in his home, and who is in a separate room by himself, is obligated in Chanukah lamps, as I wrote above in siman 671, section 8; see there.
Where he eats. See the words of Olas Chodesh, paragraph 7. He concludes that since it is known to everyone that they kindle in the winter room, in a place where they do not eat, and it is known that each person has, close by, immediately near his place of eating, a place for sleeping, it is properly called kindling in the place where he sleeps. But one who eats there incidentally one time, or even eats there regularly but his sleeping place is far from his eating place, kindles in the place where he sleeps. See further Shevus Yaakov, siman 73. According to the distinction I wrote above, the words of both of them can be upheld, namely the words of Rema in the gloss and also the words of my father-in-law's father, the aforementioned Rabbi Yaakov, as it seems to me.
