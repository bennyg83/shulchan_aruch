Responsa Maharshal wrote that yeshivah students also must participate. Perhaps this is specifically when he eats independently, but if he depends on the homeowner's table, he is included among the members of his household, and by the strict law he does not need to kindle unless he wants to be among those who beautify the mitzvah. So it also seems in Beis Yosef in the name of Mahari; see there. Maharshal wrote: one who did not know that they kindled for him in his home, and came home that night and found that they had kindled for him, must kindle, because the ordinance of the Sages had already taken effect upon him. Until here. See the end of siman 672. His words are not compelling; therefore one should not bless. In the oil. The homeowner must add a little oil for him. Bach, Agudah. Rashba, siman 542, wrote that the same applies if the homeowner transfers ownership to him of a share in the oil as a gift; it is permitted.
An open entrance. Even if it opens in the direction where the homeowner's entrance opens. Knesses HaGedolah.
For himself. Nowadays, when we kindle indoors, even guests kindle. Laws of Mahariv, siman 31, and Maharil, siman 156. This is specifically when he eats in a room by himself.
To kindle at his entrance. Since the reason is suspicion, he need not bless, as written at the end of siman 675. Knesses HaGedolah. One may distinguish; see section 3 in the gloss.
For a son who eats. It is obvious that even if he eats regularly at his father's, since the reason is suspicion, as the Tur wrote. Knesses HaGedolah elaborated needlessly.
And some say that in this time, etc. Even Rosh agrees to this. Bach, Darkei Moshe.
In the place where he eats. This means on a fixed basis. But one who eats in his friend's house incidentally must kindle in his own home; Shevus Yaakov, siman 73. Or his wife kindles for him; siman 675, section 3. Nevertheless, it is a greater mitzvah for him than through his agent. Until here are the words of Bach. See siman 409, section 8.
