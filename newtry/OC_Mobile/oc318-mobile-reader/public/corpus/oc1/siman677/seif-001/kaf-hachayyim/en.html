1) [Seif 1] A lodger for whom they are not kindling in his home, etc. At first glance, it would appear that the expression "they kindle for him" can be explained in two ways. According to the Rambam, of blessed memory, who holds that each member of the household kindles one light, "they kindle for him" means that they kindle one light on his behalf in his home, as though he were standing there. According to the other poskim, who hold that one light suffices for a man and his household, it means that they kindle within his home, that is, his wife kindles one light and he fulfills his obligation through her kindling. However, the words of the gaon, author of Terumas HaDeshen, siman 101, and those of the other later authorities, of blessed memory, do not imply this, and the matter is not conclusive. Maamar Mordechai, letter 1. See above, siman 671, letter 6.
2) Ibid. He must give a perutah, etc. The wording precisely says "a perutah," to teach that the lodger need not pay half of the entire expense of the lights. It suffices for him to give a perutah. Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur; Mishna Berurah, letter 3. When giving the perutah, he must say, "I hereby give you this perutah so that you will transfer to me ownership of a portion of the oil and wicks in your Chanukah lights that you will kindle tonight." Alternatively, he may say, "in all that you will kindle during the 8 nights of Chanukah." The homeowner says, "I hereby transfer to you ownership of a portion of the oil, through this perutah, in my Chanukah lights that I kindle during all 8 nights of Chanukah." Even without a perutah, the homeowner may transfer ownership to him if he wishes, as written below, letter 5, and he should say, "I hereby transfer ownership to you, etc." The lodger should stand beside him during the blessing and intend to fulfill his obligation through the homeowner's blessing, and the homeowner should likewise intend to discharge his obligation, as is known.
3) Ibid. He must give a perutah, etc. It appears that this applies specifically to a lodger who pays his expenses to the homeowner. He is then obligated to participate, and if he has a private room, he must kindle in his room. However, if a lodger is entirely dependent upon the homeowner, eats of his bread, and drinks of his cup, he need not do this at all. Since the homeowner provides all his needs, he will certainly transfer to him ownership of a portion of the money for the lights as well. There is no suspicion in this case, since it is known that he relies upon the homeowner's table. Gan HaMelech, siman 41; Yad Aharon, ibid.; Kisei Eliyahu, letter 1; Shulchan Gavohah, letter 2, in the supplement. Meaning, if he eats with the homeowner and is charged for everything he eats and drinks, he must give a perutah. However, if he eats there for a fixed sum per day or per week, including everything he needs, he need not give a perutah. It appears that the same applies if the homeowner provides all his needs without charge, and he need not give a perutah. Nevertheless, it appears preferable for him to say explicitly to the homeowner, "Transfer to me ownership of a portion of the oil in your Chanukah lights," and the homeowner should say, "I hereby transfer ownership to you, etc.," whether he eats there for payment or without charge. However, regarding what he wrote, that even if he has a private room he need not kindle, see below, letter 9. It appears that the same ruling applies here.
4) The Agudah wrote: I heard that one who participates with another in a Chanukah light must add what his fellow gives him, end quote. His reason is that if it is not evident that he added oil for his fellow, there is a suspicion that he did not participate. According to this, even if he did not add all that his fellow gave him, but only some of it, that also suffices, since his participation is evident from what was added beyond his normal amount. Bach; Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahos HaTur, letter 2; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. However, the words of Eliyah Rabbah, letter 2, imply that the homeowner must place oil sufficient to burn for half an hour on behalf of the lodger, as Eshel Avraham, letter 1, wrote. Pri Chadash wrote that he need not add to the light the value of what the lodger gave him, and that what the Bach wrote, that he must add a small amount, is not persuasive; see there. However, Derech HaChaim, letter 2, ruled in accordance with the Bach, that the homeowner must add a small amount of oil on behalf of the guest beyond the measure required to burn for half an hour. So too ruled Mishna Berurah, letter 3, and he wrote that this is unlike Eliyah Rabbah. This also appears to be the implication of the poskim who wrote that a perutah suffices, implying that he need add only a little beyond the required measure for the guest, merely as a recognizable indication.
