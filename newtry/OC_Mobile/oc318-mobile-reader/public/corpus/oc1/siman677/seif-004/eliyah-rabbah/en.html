Levush: Permission is in his hand, etc. And so is the practice; Rema. Maharshal, siman 85, ruled like Beis Yosef that he should not bless unless it is not known that she kindled for him. From this I find difficulty with Shaar Ephraim, siman 42, who wrote that Beis Yosef follows his own view that all members of the household kindle one lamp, but according to our custom, everyone agrees that he must bless, for he is included among those who beautify the mitzvah. Until here. But Maharshal ruled according to our custom, and so did Mateh Moshe and Shnei Luchos HaBris, and even so they ruled like Beis Yosef. Furthermore, in Responsa Maharil, siman 156, and in Maharil, he wrote about those who practice that each member of the household kindles, etc., that there is no blessing in vain, since he does not want to fulfill his obligation with that of his wife. Until here. Let him instead say that he is among those who beautify the mitzvah, like the other members of his household. I also wrote in siman 671, paragraph 2, that a man and his wife are not included in the category of mehadrin, for they are considered like one body. As a matter of law, Shnei Luchos HaBris ruled that when it is known that they kindle in his home, he should stand near the homeowner when he blesses and answer amen. If the homeowner already kindled, he should bless without the Name and kingship; and so ruled Mateh Moshe. But I say that one should not protest our custom, as Mahariv and Maharil, whom I cited, testified.
