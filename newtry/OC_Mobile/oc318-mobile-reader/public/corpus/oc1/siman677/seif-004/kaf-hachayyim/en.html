4) The Agudah wrote: I heard that one who participates with another in a Chanukah light must add what his fellow gives him, end quote. His reason is that if it is not evident that he added oil for his fellow, there is a suspicion that he did not participate. According to this, even if he did not add all that his fellow gave him, but only some of it, that also suffices, since his participation is evident from what was added beyond his normal amount. Bach; Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahos HaTur, letter 2; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. However, the words of Eliyah Rabbah, letter 2, imply that the homeowner must place oil sufficient to burn for half an hour on behalf of the lodger, as Eshel Avraham, letter 1, wrote. Pri Chadash wrote that he need not add to the light the value of what the lodger gave him, and that what the Bach wrote, that he must add a small amount, is not persuasive; see there. However, Derech HaChaim, letter 2, ruled in accordance with the Bach, that the homeowner must add a small amount of oil on behalf of the guest beyond the measure required to burn for half an hour. So too ruled Mishna Berurah, letter 3, and he wrote that this is unlike Eliyah Rabbah. This also appears to be the implication of the poskim who wrote that a perutah suffices, implying that he need add only a little beyond the required measure for the guest, merely as a recognizable indication.
