(Subparagraph 3) And if, etc. But one who sins intentionally, etc. So too the Shitah Mekubetzes on Beitzah, beginning of chapter ha-Meivi, wrote in the name of the Ritva, as follows: specifically in a matter where we know they act inadvertently, etc.; but in a matter where we know they know it is forbidden and act intentionally, this reason does not apply.
And if we do not protest against them, we are caught on account of them, for all Israel are responsible for one another, end quote.
Semak, siman 112, ruled like the opinion that it is until rebuke. Meaning unlike the Rema, who wrote at the end of the gloss, until he strikes him or curses him. In Eruvin 16b they disagree how far the mitzvah of rebuke extends: Rav says until striking; Shmuel says until cursing; Rabbi Yochanan says until rebuke. The Semak ruled like Rabbi Yochanan. Regarding the Rema, who wrote until striking or cursing, the Pri Chadash asked: since he ruled that cursing suffices, like Shmuel, all the more so striking should suffice; why did he need to write until he strikes him? He answered that it was obvious to the Rema not to rule like Rabbi Yochanan for the reason the Pri Chadash wrote; see there. Rather, the Rema was uncertain whether we rule like Rav or like Shmuel. Therefore the Rema copied the words of Rav and Shmuel: striking like Rav, and cursing like Shmuel; see there. In my humble opinion, one can say that the Pri Chadash wrote that the clearest ruling is like Shmuel, because it is the middle matter and middle reasoning between Rav and Rabbi Yochanan. So too one can say, in my humble opinion, that the Rema ruled, and therefore cursing suffices. What he first wrote, until he strikes him, is because the Talmud asks against the opinion that says until rebuke: we found that Yehonasan rebuked Shaul regarding David until striking, as it is written, "Shaul cast the spear to strike him." It answers: because of Yehonasan's love for David, he risked himself until striking. The same must be said according to the opinion that says until cursing, that Yehonasan risked himself until striking because of his love for David. So too Tosafos wrote there in one answer. Therefore the Rema wrote until he strikes him, meaning that one who wants to be stringent on himself is permitted to go until striking, for the love of the Holy One, blessed be He, when he rebukes for His honor, is no less than the love of an ordinary person, as we find with Yehonasan. But beyond this we do not find anyone who is stringent. The Rema concluded, "or curses him," meaning that until cursing, at least, he is obligated by law. However, the Rambam, chapter 6 of the Laws of Character Traits, ruled like Rav, until striking. See the Shelah at the beginning of the book, who brought the dispute of the authorities about this and his conclusion.
But if it is another person, etc. One should not rebuke him, etc. Similar to this the Rema wrote in Choshen Mishpat at the beginning of siman 12; see there in the Acharonim. So too in Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 334 he wrote in the name of Mahari Weil, in these words: although one is obligated to protest against transgressors, and whoever has the ability to protest and does not protest is caught in that sin, nevertheless a person is not obligated to spend his money for this. Therefore the custom was to be lenient and not protest against transgressors where there is concern that they will attack our bodies and our property, end quote. See there in the Taz, what he wrote in the name of the Derishah regarding excommunication nowadays. But in a place where there is no concern of danger, on the contrary, one should be more stringent in our generations, because of our sins. As written in Shitah Mekubetzes on Beitzah, beginning of chapter ha-Meivi, in these words: the Ritva testified in the name of a great rabbi from the Ashkenazim, who testified in the name of his French teachers, including Ri and Maharam of Rothenburg, that these words, to distinguish between Torah and rabbinic prohibitions, were said only for their generations. But in this generation, when people are lenient in many matters, it is proper to make a fence for the Torah, and even in rabbinic matters we protest and penalize them so that they transgress neither inadvertently nor intentionally. And so it is in a Yerushalmi midrash, and the matter appears correct, end quote.
