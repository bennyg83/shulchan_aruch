(12) He may return and eat, etc. - Some wrote that the custom is that after one has eaten, it is forbidden to eat, because by stopping he is considered as though he accepted verbally. Therefore it is proper that before Birkas ha-Mazon he explicitly say that he does not yet accept the fast upon himself, and at least he should think this in his heart.
(13) As long as, etc. - And if he accepted it upon himself, he is also forbidden in washing and anointing, and especially in labor.
(14) That he did not accept the fast upon himself - Acceptance is effective only from plag ha-minchah onward. But if he accepted the fast upon himself before plag ha-minchah, his acceptance is nothing. However, if he accepted upon himself simply not to eat anymore, this is effective even on the other days of the year, if he accepted upon himself not to eat from midday onward, as written in siman 562, se'if 11.
(15) See above, etc. - See there in the Mishnah Berurah, subparagraph 2, what I wrote in the name of the Bach and the Gra, and the other matters there; they also apply to our subject.
