1) [Seif 1] One eats and ceases eating before twilight, etc. For we derive from Scripture, Yoma 81a, that one must add from the weekday to the sacred, both regarding the prohibition against eating and regarding the performance of melacha. Tur. The Beit Yosef wrote that this appears to be the view of the Rif and the Rosh. However, from Rambam's words in Hilchos Shevisas Asor, chapter 1, it appears that he holds that the addition is required by Torah law only regarding affliction, but not regarding performing melacha, neither on Yom Kippur nor on Shabbos, see there. The Shulchan Aruch's words imply that he rules according to Rambam. The reason he did not cite the view of the Rif and the Rosh, even as an alternative opinion, may be that he relied upon what he wrote above in siman 261, seif 2, where he cited the view of the Rif and the Rosh as an alternative opinion, see there. However, see our words there, os 16, where we wrote in the name of the Radbaz that even according to Rambam, there is an obligation to add to Shabbos, see there. Nehar Shalom also discussed this matter at great length in this siman and concluded in accordance with the Radbaz that Rambam accepts the requirement of addition in all of them, namely, Shabbos, Yom Tov, and Yom Kippur, see there. The Levush also ruled in accordance with the Tur. Shulchan Gavoah, os 1, likewise wrote: As a matter of practical halacha, we follow the Rif and the Rosh, requiring an addition from the weekday to the sacred regarding the prohibition of melacha just as regarding the prohibition of eating, and this is the custom. End quote. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 1, ruled likewise. Accordingly, since there is a dispute regarding this, and some say that even Rambam holds that one must add also regarding the performance of melacha, one must be stringent.
2) One must add from the weekday to the sacred. However, there is no punishment of kares for violating this addition, as we say there in the Gemara: One is punished with kares for violating the essence of the day, but one is not punished with kares for violating the added period of affliction, see there. So too wrote Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman; Eliyah Rabbah, os 2; Pri Chadash; Eshel Avraham at the beginning of the siman; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 1; Mishna Berurah, os 1.
3) Ibid.: One must add from the weekday to the sacred. The same applies when Yom Kippur departs: one must add from the weekday to the sacred, as it is written, "From evening to evening." Gemara, ibid.; Levush; Pri Chadash; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ibid.; Mishna Berurah, ibid.
4) Ibid.: This addition has no fixed measure, but it must begin before twilight, etc. Twilight itself is of uncertain status, whether it is day or night. If one performs melacha during the two twilight periods, at the beginning and conclusion of the day, unintentionally, he is liable to bring a chatas. This period is not called an addition, because even without the Scriptural inclusion one would have to refrain due to the doubt, and there is also a possible liability of kares. Rather, before twilight, which begins the time required to walk 1,500 amos before nightfall, one must add from the weekday to the sacred. The measure of this addition was not clarified. Therefore, it cannot mean an addition of an infinitesimal amount; one must refrain a short while before twilight. Beit Yosef in the name of several poskim; Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 2.
