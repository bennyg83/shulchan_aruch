Before - See Taz and Beis Yosef, and not like the Rambam, who wrote addition only regarding eating, for the same applies to labor: addition is Torah law on Shabbos, Yom Tov, and Yom Kippur; see siman 261. Regarding what he wrote about the Levush, see the gloss there; the Levush answered it. In Eliyah Rabbah: women are obligated in the addition to Yom Kippur from the Torah, as in se'if 2. Although women are exempt from time-bound positive commandments that have no punishment, one can say this is because the essence of Yom Kippur itself applies to women as well. All the more so, women are obligated in the addition to Shabbos, and women are obligated in all positive commandments of Shabbos, even in the three meals; see siman 291. One should be uncertain: during the whole twilight period they did not decree shevus where there is great pressure and much need, as in siman 261 and other places. Regarding afflictions where the prohibition is rabbinic, as in siman 612, if during twilight there is pressure and great need, some healing and the like, whether it is permitted, or whether they decreed regarding eating in such a case.
