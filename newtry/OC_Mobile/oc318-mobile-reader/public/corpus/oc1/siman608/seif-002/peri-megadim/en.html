And if - See Taz, Tosafos Bava Basra 60b, comment beginning "better," and see there. This is with something not explicit in the Torah, so it is. The same applies even with something explicit, that he rebukes them publicly only once, namely when he knows with certainty that they will not listen. But when it is doubtful, one rebukes publicly; perhaps they will listen, and there will be no desecration of the Name when it is done publicly. One must say this applies when rebuking the public, but with an individual it is forbidden to shame him publicly. On the contrary, the first and second time he should speak privately. See the Rambam, Laws of Character Traits, and Magen Avraham letter 1; with the help of Heaven it will be explained there further.
