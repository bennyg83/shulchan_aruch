The addition of affliction is from the Torah, but there is no kareis for it. One does not protest. This is specifically when he knows with certainty that they will not accept from him, as the Rosh wrote in Beitzah, chapter 4, and in Responsa, siman 3, klal 6.
Explicit in the Torah. Even though it is a doubt, such as twilight, one protests (Yam shel Shlomo, Beitzah chapter 4).
And if he knows. This is specifically when it is clear to him, and specifically when the sinner is inadvertent. But if the sinner acts intentionally, although one who does not rebuke him is not punished like him, nevertheless he violates the positive commandment of "you shall surely rebuke," and is obligated to rebuke him until the sinner rebukes him. From then onward it is forbidden to rebuke him, as it is said, "Do not rebuke a scoffer." With a private sin he should rebuke him privately, and with a public sin he should rebuke him immediately so that the name of Heaven not be desecrated. Semak, siman 112, ruled like the opinion that it is until rebuke. In Sefer Chasidim, siman 413, he wrote: this is specifically "a man to his brother," one with whom he is familiar, but if it is another person who, if he rebukes him, will hate him and take revenge on him, one should not rebuke him, until here. A person should always dwell in the place of his teacher when he accepts his rebuke; if not, he should not dwell there, for it is better that they be inadvertent sinners (Berachos 8).
