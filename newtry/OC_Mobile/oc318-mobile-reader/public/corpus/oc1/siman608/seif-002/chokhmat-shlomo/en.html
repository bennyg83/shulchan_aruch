Se'if 2 In the gloss: "The same applies to every prohibited matter: we say it is better that they be inadvertent sinners and not intentional sinners, etc." Note: Regarding the law of better that they be inadvertent sinners and not intentional sinners, see my work on Yoreh De'ah, mahadura chadashah, siman 51, which belongs to Yoreh De'ah siman 117, where I wrote that this applies specifically if it is not public. But if it is public, where there is concern that others will learn from him, one is obligated to protest even regarding rabbinic law; see there and examine. (There in the gloss) "And specifically when it is not explicit in the Torah, although it is from the Torah; but if it is explicit in the Torah, one protests against them." Note: This is very difficult for me from the Talmud in Yevamos 49 regarding the prophet Yeshayah, where they judged him and killed him. It says there that Yeshayah said: if I tell him, I will make him intentional. This is difficult: murder, killing a person for nothing, is explicit in the Torah, and whenever the prohibition is explicit in the Torah we do not say better that they be inadvertent sinners and not intentional sinners. If so, why did Yeshayah say: if I tell him, I will make him intentional? One could answer that although the prohibition of murder is explicit in the Torah, the opposite, to execute a false prophet, is also explicit in the Torah and is a mitzvah. What made it forbidden to kill him here was that Yeshayah could have answered truthfully for himself. Since his answers are not explicit in the Torah, the prohibition is not called explicit in the Torah. But besides the fact that this is forced, what shall we do with what Tosafos wrote there in Yevamos in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, that Yeshayah wanted to answer him that also regarding Moshe it is written, "and they saw the God of Israel"? If so, even his answer is explicit in the Torah, so why did he say, "I will make him intentional"? Perhaps this is why Tosafos brought the words of Rabbenu Chananel there, which otherwise would be unnecessary, for one could say that he wanted to answer him as explicit in the Talmud here, that here it was with a clear speculum, etc. Rather, their intent is to teach like the words of Rabbenu Chananel, that he had the ability to answer him with verses explicit in the Torah and did not answer him. From this it is proved that even in such a case we also say better that they be inadvertent sinners and not intentional sinners. Therefore it at least appears that Tosafos in the name of Rabbenu Chananel disagree with the view of the authorities brought by the Magen Avraham, and they hold that even with something explicit in the Torah we say better that they be inadvertent sinners, etc. This requires study at present; examine carefully.
