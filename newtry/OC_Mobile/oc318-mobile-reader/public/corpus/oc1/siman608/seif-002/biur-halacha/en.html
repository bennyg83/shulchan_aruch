Until dark - Meaning until twilight. But at twilight one is obligated to protest against them, for it is like something explicit in the Torah, since it is a possible kareis violation (Magen Avraham in the name of Yam shel Shlomo). See Machatzis ha-Shekel, that this is according to the view that a Torah doubt is stringent from the Torah, and see Korban Nesanel for what he wrote about the Magen Avraham's words.
And specifically when it is not explicit in the Torah - See Machatzis ha-Shekel in the name of Responsa Me'il Tzedakah, siman 19, that when people have taken on a bad custom to be lenient publicly, it is considered known to us that they will not accept, and one need not protest unless it is explicit in the Torah.
But if it is explicit in the Torah, etc. This is from the words of the Ittur, copied by the Rosh and Ran. So too the Rashba and Meiri wrote in their novellae on Beitzah 30. Know that at first glance one can ask against this from the sugya in Shabbos 55, where they violated something explicit in the Torah, as it is written, "those who sigh and groan over all the abominations, etc.," and even so it implies in the Gemara that if it were clear they would not listen, they would not have been punished. One can answer that this is only regarding punishment, but regarding the positive commandment of rebuke, one is obligated in every case. This indeed is the view of R. Eliezer of Metz brought in the Semag and Semak, and the Magen Avraham copied them. The Semag himself, however, implies there that he holds that where it is clear to him that they will not accept, he is not obligated to rebuke him. So too it seems at first glance from the words of Tosafos there in Shabbos 55, comment beginning "although, etc." But one could reject this, that the matter of the Exilarch was not something explicit in the Torah, and therefore if it had been clear to Rabbi Simon that they would not listen to him, he would not have been obligated to rebuke them. However, from the Gra it is evident that Tosafos' view is to be lenient with the public, like the view of the Semag. Know that it is reasonable that what the Rema ruled, that with something explicit in the Torah one must protest, is specifically when it is occasional. But those who entirely throw off the yoke, such as one who desecrates Shabbos publicly or eats neveilos to anger, have already left the category of "your fellow," and one is not obligated to rebuke him. So it is stated in Tanna Devei Eliyahu Rabbah, chapter 18: "You shall surely rebuke your fellow" - your fellow who loves you and is with you in Torah and mitzvos, you are obligated to rebuke him; but a wicked person who hates you, you are not obligated to rebuke him. The Gra copied this briefly in Aderes Eliyahu; see there. Regarding one who eats neveilos for appetite, or one who desecrates Shabbos not publicly, the matter requires study.
One protests against them - See Birkei Yosef, who leans toward saying a new point: we say that with something explicit in the Torah one must protest even when he knows they will not accept only when our hand is strong over the transgressors to protest against them with force. But when we do not have the power to separate them, one is not obligated to rebuke them since he knows they will not accept. However, from the words of the Semak brought in the Magen Avraham, it implies that in this case, even though he is not caught in their sin, he nevertheless has an obligation from the positive commandment of rebuke.
He is obligated to rebuke him - In Sefer Chasidim, siman 413, he wrote: specifically a man to his brother, one with whom he is familiar; but if it is another person who, if he rebukes him, will hate him and take revenge on him, one should not rebuke him (Magen Avraham in his name). A person should always dwell in the place of his teacher when he accepts his rebuke; if not, he should not dwell there. It is better that they be inadvertent sinners (Berachos 8).
Until he strikes him - I saw in Sefer ha-Chinuch, mitzvah 239, as follows: What the Sages said, that the obligation of the mitzvah is until striking, means that the rebuker is obligated to increase his rebukes to the sinner until the sinner is close to striking the rebuker; see there further.
