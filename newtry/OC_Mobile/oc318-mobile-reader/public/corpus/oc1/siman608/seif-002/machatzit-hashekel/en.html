(Subparagraph 2) Explicit, etc. Even though it is a doubt, such as twilight, one protests. The Yam shel Shlomo there wrote the reason, in these words: since a Torah doubt is stringent, it is like certainty, end quote. According to this, in my humble opinion, this is specifically according to the authorities who hold that a Torah doubt is stringent from the Torah. But according to the authorities who hold that from the Torah we go leniently and only rabbinically go stringently, and this is an old dispute; see Pri Chadash, Yoreh De'ah siman 110, who wrote at length about this. Since it is only rabbinic, one need not protest. See Responsa Me'il Tzedakah, siman 19, that with something where they have adopted a bad custom publicly, it is considered known to us that they will not accept, and one need not protest unless it is explicit in the Torah. And his uncertainty is as written in the Yam shel Shlomo above. He inferred this from Rashi's language in Beitzah, beginning of chapter ha-Meivi.
