One does not protest. This is specifically when he knows with certainty that they will not accept from him; Rosh.
Explicit. Even though it is a doubt, such as twilight, one protests. Yam shel Shlomo, Beitzah, chapter 4.
Knows. This is specifically when it is clear to him, and specifically when the sinner is inadvertent. But when the sinner acts intentionally, he is obligated to rebuke him until the sinner rebukes him. From then onward it is forbidden to rebuke him, as it is said, "Do not rebuke a scoffer." With a private sin, he should rebuke him privately; with a public sin, he should rebuke him immediately so that the name of Heaven not be desecrated; Magen Avraham. In Sefer Chasidim, siman 413, he wrote that this is specifically "a man to his brother," one with whom he is familiar. But if it is another person who, if he rebukes him, will hate him and take revenge on him, one should not rebuke him; see there.
