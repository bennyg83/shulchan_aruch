[4] [Levush] And from what is written, you shall rest, etc. In Yoma 81: "And you shall afflict yourselves on the ninth of the month"; from here that one adds, etc. I have only Yom Kippur; from where do I know Yom Tov? The verse says "you shall rest." I have only Yom Tov; from where do I know Shabbosos? The verse says "your rests," until here. This is the language of the Maharshal in his glosses to the Semag, positive commandment 32: it is difficult, for if Yom Tov is forbidden, all the more so Shabbos. One can answer that since it says, "six days labor shall be done," I might have thought we require the doing of labor like the Holy One, blessed be He, who first rested on the seventh without addition, until here. The copyist said: the reason the Rav did not answer that we would have established the verse as referring to Shabbos if only "you shall rest" were written is because if so, the tanna should have learned Shabbos first, and should have said: I have only Yom Kippur; from where do I know Shabbos? Until here is his language. The copyist's answer is forced. Moreover, I saw that in the Rif and Raavan, chapter Yom ha-Kippurim, Sefer Yere'im siman 105, the text indeed reads this way: I have only Yom Kippur; from where do I know Shabbos? The verse says "you shall rest." From where do I know Yom Tov, etc. So too it is in Rosh Hashanah 9. Therefore it seems to me that one cannot answer that we would establish the verse as referring to Shabbos, because the difficulty would still remain: Shabbos, whose prohibition is stoning, can be learned by kal vachomer from Yom Kippur, which is lighter. Rather, it must refer to Yom Tov. And one should not ask why the Maharshal asked that Shabbos is a kal vachomer from Yom Tov, and did not ask that it is a kal vachomer from Yom Kippur, because he asked an even stronger question: even from Yom Tov it is a kal vachomer.
