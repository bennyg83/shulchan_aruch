(Subparagraph 4) That he did not accept, etc. Before plag ha-minchah, etc.; however, if he accepted explicitly, etc. In siman 553 the Tur brought the Rif's view in the name of a Gaon, that acceptance in the heart is not effective. This is also the view of most authorities, except for the Raavad, who holds it is effective. But if he accepted verbally, the Rif wrote that even with other public fasts, acceptance while it is still day is effective. The Ramban disagreed with him, that with other public fasts even if they accepted verbally while it is still day, it is not acceptance. Specifically on Yom Kippur, where one must add, as written at the beginning of this siman, his acceptance is effective because it is included in the addition. This is not so with other public fasts, and even Tisha B'Av: although twilight is forbidden, it has no addition, as written in siman 553, subparagraph 3, and acceptance before twilight is not effective. According to this, also on the eve of Yom Kippur acceptance is effective only from plag ha-minchah and onward, for the Rema wrote in siman 261, se'if 2, that one can make an addition to Shabbos, and the same applies to Yom Kippur, only from plag ha-minchah and onward. However, according to what the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 262, subparagraph 10, that some calculate from the beginning of sunset and it is like two hours before night, the same applies here: acceptance is effective within two hours before night. The Beis Yosef wrote that we adopt the Rif and most authorities, that acceptance in the heart is not effective, unlike the Raavad, as he wrote here also in the Shulchan Aruch. The Magen Avraham, however, was inclined to be stringent like the Raavad, that acceptance in the heart is effective. But before plag ha-minchah, since according to the Ramban even verbal acceptance is ineffective, although we do not rule like him, nevertheless with acceptance in the heart, since in any case the Raavad is an individual opinion, one can rely on the Ramban. But if he accepted verbally, which is effective according to the Rif even with other public fasts that have no addition, the same applies on the eve of Yom Kippur before plag ha-minchah: even though the time of addition has not yet arrived, we adopt the Rif, unlike the Ramban. And although the Magen Avraham wrote above in siman 553, subparagraph 2, in the name of the Bach, that even on Tisha B'Av, which has no addition, acceptance in the heart is nevertheless effective, and therefore the Magen Avraham concluded there that it is good to stipulate at the meal before the fast, see there, according to this acceptance should be effective even before plag ha-minchah although the time of addition has not yet arrived. One must say that above in siman 553 he wrote this only as a stringency, that at the outset it is good to stipulate, etc. So too the Magen Avraham concluded here in the name of Maharshal, Maharil, and Bach.
See siman 562, se'if 11. His intent is to reject the words of the Beis Yosef in siman 553, who strengthened the Ramban's reasoning, that with a public fast that has no addition, and consequently the same would apply on the eve of Yom Kippur before plag ha-minchah, acceptance is not effective even verbally. This is the Beis Yosef's language: but with any fast that has no addition, when one accepts upon himself to fast at the end of this day, since he ate and drank at the beginning of the day, it is not called a fast. Granted, on the eve of Yom Kippur, which has addition, from the time of acceptance it is included in addition. It is not even a fast of hours, according to the view of the Rosh that Rabbenu brought in siman 562, end quote of the Beis Yosef. To this the Magen Avraham wrote: see siman 562, se'if 11, where we rule that although in such a case, if he ate at the beginning of the day and accepted upon himself to fast at the end of the day, it is not called a fast for reciting Aneinu, nevertheless he must fulfill his vow, for it is called a vow. If so, the same applies here: it is acceptance and a vow.
