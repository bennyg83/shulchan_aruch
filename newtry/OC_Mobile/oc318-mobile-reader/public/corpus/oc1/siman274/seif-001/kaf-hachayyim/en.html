1) [Seif 1] He breaks bread over two loaves, etc. This is because the verse states, 'They gathered a double portion of bread.' Shabbos 117b; Tur. We already explained above in siman 262, paragraph 2, how to arrange the bread upon the table, how many loaves to place there, and the procedure for breaking bread according to the Arizal. Take the law from there and do not depart from it.
2) There: He should break the lower loaf. Bach writes: I am astonished how people could transgress the Talmudic rule that one may not pass over a mitzvah. Therefore, the view of the great authorities appears primary: one always recites the blessing over the upper loaf. One may not transgress a Talmudic rule because a practice is said to follow the Kabbalah; see there. We already wrote above that according to the Kabbalah of our teacher the Arizal as well, one must break the upper loaf, without distinction between the daytime meal and the nighttime meal. We possess only the tradition of the Arizal, who received it from Eliyahu HaNavi. Therefore, even one who has neither twelve nor four loaves, but only two, must break the upper loaf. See above, siman 262, paragraph 4, and note this well.
3) There: And he breaks bread over two loaves, etc. Avudraham writes that women are also obligated to break bread over two loaves. Ran and Mordechai in the chapter Kol Kisvei write the same in the name of Rabbeinu Tam. Beis Yosef, siman 291; Knesses HaGedolah; Olas Tamid, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2.
4) There: Whole loaves. Regarding whether bread partly burned to the point that the burned portion is unfit to eat is considered whole, see our words above in siman 167, paragraph 8. Regarding whether bread from which the required amount of challah was removed is considered whole, see our words above in siman 168, paragraph 10, where a dispute is recorded. It appears that if one has no other loaf, it is certainly more proper to use it as lechem mishneh than to recite the blessing over only one loaf. If a person has two halves of a loaf and no whole loaf, he should join them with a piece of wood that is not muktzeh, or with another item, in a manner that the break is not visible. The joined loaf then has the law of a whole one, as stated above in siman 168, seif 2, and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
