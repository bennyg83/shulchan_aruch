(1) Two loaves. This commemorates the manna, as it is written, "They gathered a double portion of bread." On Yom Tov as well, one must break bread over two loaves. Women are also obligated in lechem mishneh, because they too participated in the miracle of the manna. Whether one fulfills lechem mishneh with pas haba'ah b'kisnin is discussed above in siman 168, seif 7, in the Mishnah Berurah and Biur Halachah.
(2) Whole. Regarding a cake that was somewhat burned but from which nothing has yet been cut, some say that one fulfills the requirement of lechem mishneh with it. See Shaarei Teshuvah. If one does not have a whole loaf, this does not prevent him from proceeding, and he may recite Kiddush even over a kezayis of bread. It is proper for the person breaking the bread to intend, with his blessing of Hamotzi, to discharge all those seated with him, and he should tell them to intend to fulfill their obligation through his blessing, so that they all fulfill lechem mishneh. When discharging others, he should say, "With the permission of my teachers," even if he is the head of the household or the greatest person present, for this is a manner of humility.
(3) In his hand. At the time he recites Hamotzi.
(4) And he breaks, etc. The requirement of lechem mishneh was stated only regarding gathering the loaves. Maharshal and Shelah customarily cut both loaves, in accordance with the Rashba's explanation, and the Gra agreed. One who is concerned about the expense may prepare three large challos and three small ones, and at each meal cut one large and one small loaf. The general custom follows the Shulchan Aruch. Peri Megadim writes that if one has no Jewish bread on Shabbos and has only bread baked by a non-Jew, he may eat from it. See there further.
(5) The lower one. Bach expressed surprise, because one may not pass over a mitzvah. Taz corrected this by placing the lower loaf closer to himself than the upper loaf, so that he encounters the lower one first. Alternatively, one may take the upper loaf at the time of the blessing of Hamotzi, place it underneath, and cut it. See above, siman 167, seif 1, in the gloss, that one should not cut into the loaf until after the blessing so that the loaves remain whole; see there. Those who are meticulous customarily mark the loaf with the knife before the blessing.
