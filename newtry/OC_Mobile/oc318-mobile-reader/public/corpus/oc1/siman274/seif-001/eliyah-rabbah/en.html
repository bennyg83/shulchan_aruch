Two loaves, etc. If he cannot obtain two loaves, one whole loaf is sufficient. Mechilta states that on Erev Shabbos the manna descended for them in two omers. From the omer they made two loaves, one for Friday morning and the second for Friday night, the third for Shabbos morning, and the fourth for the third meal. Kol Bo, end of siman 31. In my humble opinion, this is not a proof. It may be that on Friday night and Shabbos morning they held two loaves and broke one, and only regarding the third meal, when only one loaf remained, is there a proof, as Tur writes in siman 291. Nevertheless, according to the opinion that all three meals are equal, there is a proof. Shelah, page 13, writes in the name of Kanfei Yonah that one should prepare twenty six loaves, corresponding to the numerical value of the Divine Name. On Friday night he arranges twelve upon his table, at Shacharis eight, at Minchah four, and on Motzaei Shabbos two. At Maariv, Shacharis, and Minchah he holds two in his hands, and on Motzaei Shabbos one. See there for what he says each time and for the mystical principles and intentions involved. A person should therefore not practice this unless he is thoroughly proficient in Kabbalah. There is also no allusion to this in the Talmud, in the chapter Kol Kisvei, and the passage in Mechilta cited above does not imply it. Agudah at the end of the chapter Arvei Pesachim writes that the practice on Yom Tov is also to use two loaves. This is difficult, because we say in VeAtah Kidashta, “You blessed it above all days and sanctified it above all seasons,” and the explanation is that the manna descended on Yom Tov itself. Perhaps the blessing means that the taste of cooked food was blessed. Thus far his words. Rabbi David Avudraham writes that the blessing and sanctification of Shabbos refer to those who observe Shabbos, for they are blessed and sanctified. Ibn Ezra explains blessing as an added good, whereby renewed strength and intellect arise in the body. I explained that the blessing is that one suffers no loss despite not performing labor on Shabbos.
