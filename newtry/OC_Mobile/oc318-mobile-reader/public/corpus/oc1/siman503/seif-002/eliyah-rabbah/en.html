[2] [Levush] It is better prepared, etc. Specifically in cooking, but not in roasting (Maharil). And it appears specifically meat, and not other dishes, as with water at the end of this siman. And as for the Semak at which Beis Yosef wondered, it appears to me that he is dealing with other dishes; and Beis Yosef and all the great authorities strained over his words; and for what I explained, his words fit their plain sense, somewhat aligning also the words of Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah that have no explanation for one who examines them. Also what he wrote, that Maharil likewise distinguished thus, is surprising; and I abbreviated. And in the responsum of R' Yom Tov Tzahalon he elaborates to wonder at the Rivash and the Ran in chapter 2 of Beitzah, who wrote the reason that it is better prepared: for without that it is permitted anyway by reason of one effort, as Rashi explained [Beitzah] folio 17; and he answered that he found in Shiltei Gibborim that for the reason that it is better prepared, even if he said explicitly for the need of tomorrow [it is permitted]; until here his words briefly. And one may wonder at him that he did not see in the Tur, from which Shiltei Gibborim copied. And further, that in truth it is explained in the Rokeach, whom Beis Yaakov cited, that even for the reason that it is better prepared it is [forbidden] explicitly; and so explained Maharshal and Bach and the later authorities, that it is forbidden; but after the fact it appears that according to everyone it is permitted. And regarding the main of his difficulty, he should rather have answered that therefore they wrote the reason that it is better prepared: even after he has placed the pot on the fire, where there is another effort, it is permitted to add meat, as Levush wrote at the end of this siman. And it is obvious that also Rashi, who explained the reason of effort, that is for the first tanna; but he agrees that R' Shimon ben Elazar holds the reason that it is better prepared. And what the Taz elaborated on this is already explained in Yam shel Shlomo and Bach. Further he left in the responsum of R' Yom Tov Tzahalon there a great wonder at the Ran there, who asked from Menachos regarding a sick person for whom they estimated two figs, and there are two on a branch and three on one branch: we bring two, we do not bring three, for increasing in measure is forbidden; what is the question, for this case is different because of better preparation; until here his words. And I did not reach the end of his intent, for R' Shimon ben Elazar only comes to add upon the first tanna that even so, where it is better prepared, it is also permitted; but he agrees with the first tanna that where there is one effort, increasing is permitted, as with the baker at the end of this siman, even though it is not better prepared; if so, the Ran asked well.
