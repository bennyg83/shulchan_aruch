(s.k. 2) But, etc. Even, etc., because the dish, etc. Know that on folio 17a it was taught: A woman fills a pot with meat even though she needs only one piece; a baker fills casks of water, etc.; but regarding baking he bakes only what he needs. R' Shimon ben Elazar says: A woman fills an oven with bread even though she needs only one loaf, because the bread is baked well when the oven is full. Rava said: The law is like R' Shimon ben Elazar. And Rashi explained the reason for the first tanna who permits a woman to fill a pot, etc.: because one effort suffices. And likewise with a baker regarding filling; end quote. And Beis Yosef understood from Rashi's view that with a pot filling the pot is not considered an improvement; for specifically regarding an oven, where it is baked well, it is considered an improvement; and therefore he needed the reason of one effort. And the Rashba, and likewise the Tur, wrote the reason also for filling the pot as improvement of the dish. And Beis Yosef wrote that there is a practical difference between the two reasons: according to Rashi, if he already set the pot on the fire, it is forbidden to add, for there is extra effort.
But according to Rashba, also in such a case it is permitted. And Beis Yosef ruled like the Rashba and Tur; and therefore Magen Avraham wrote, even after she has placed, etc. But the later authorities disagreed with Beis Yosef and hold that there is no dispute between Rashi and Rashba. For he only needed to give the reason of one effort for the first tanna, who does not permit because what one needs is improved by the addition; for it is forbidden to bake more than what one needs. But for us, who hold like R' Shimon ben Elazar who permits with bread because of improvement, Rashi agrees that also with a pot one can say because of improvement, and even if the pot is already beside the fire it is permitted to add, even though it is extra effort, because of the additions. And Eliyah Rabbah wrote in the name of Maharil: specifically in cooking, but not in roasting; until here.
That is to say, a roast in a pot where there is no gravy.
And the Tur wrote that it is permitted to say explicitly, etc. And this is his wording: And it appears that it is permitted. Since the reason of the permission is that the additions cause taste in the dish, therefore even if she stated explicitly, it is also permitted; end quote of the Tur.
And so it seems in the Bach; and see siman 512, se'if 1. That is to say, this is the Bach's proof; and with this he gave a reason for the view of those who forbid saying that one is doing for the need of the second day of Yom Tov. For the Bach brought in the name of Mordechai HaAroch, who asked: that which we forbid inviting a gentile on Yom Tov as a decree lest he increase for him, as explained in siman 512, se'if 1:
And what of it if he increases for him. Behold we hold, "A woman fills a pot with meat," etc. And one can say that there, if his stomach would hold it all, it is fit for him; therefore it is permitted to bake and cook at one time more than his need. But to bake and cook expressly for the need of a gentile is forbidden, etc. And the Bach wrote on this: And according to this it appears to me that even if he needs one piece and cooks much for the need of the night and puts it on the table to eat a little of it, he should not say to his household that they should eat of this dish only a little, for the main permission is because if his stomach would hold it all, etc.; rather if it remains, it remains; until here; and he should say nothing. And perhaps this is the reasoning of the "some say," etc.; end quote of the Bach. However, the poskim wrote another answer to the Mordechai's difficulty: that we are concerned lest he increase for him in another pot; see there in the Ran, chapter 2.
And in any case one must be careful, etc. That is to say, even though they are lenient in this, that they say explicitly that it is for the need of tomorrow and rely on the Tur, nevertheless in any case one must do so [eat a little].
And Beis Yosef wrote, etc. Only from the dish, etc.: for also the dish and the gravy are improved by the additions.
And the Bach wrote that specifically, etc. It is a matter of its own, and does not refer to what is above.
That specifically when he needs, etc. And he inferred so from the wording of the beraita that teaches, "A woman fills a pot with meat even though she needs only one piece." It is implied that in any case she truly needs one piece. And likewise he brought proof from the wordings of the poskim.
And he strained to resolve the words of Tosafos, folio 11b, s.v. "He circumvents and salts bone by bone," etc. And the words of Rabbeinu Yeruham are impossible for him to resolve, etc.: for he wrote that they eat only a little of the liver.
Or a small amount. It is implied that he does not need the chicken at all, and he eats only by way of circumvention.
That it is permitted to circumvent. And that which they forbid circumvention is when he does not, etc.; so the text should read.
See siman 527, se'if 21: there the law of circumvention is explained regarding one who did not set eruvei tavshilin. And Rashi explained: circumvention [is] that he does not need it, only to say perhaps guests will happen to come to him, etc.
To wrap the cheeses, etc. It is implied that in a pot, etc.: for this is the wording of the questioner to the Rashba: to wrap the cheeses in dough for the need of the night of the second day of Yom Tov, provided that he give from one of them to a child; end quote. And from this it is implied that he does not need it at all, and giving to the child is only so that it not be forbidden for him. And even so, were it not that one is not improved by the preparation of [the other], the Rashba would not have permitted it. And if so, in a pot that improves, it is permitted.
See there; and in the responsum of the Rashba, siman 681. Possibly Magen Avraham's intent in this is that first he brought the words of the Rashba as Beis Yosef cited them, and inferred from them that circumvention is permitted where it improves. And regarding this Magen Avraham hinted to see the responsum of the Rashba itself in siman 681. And from his words it appears at first glance to the contrary, that circumvention is forbidden. For they said to the Rashba that one permitted slaughtering after minchah for the need of tomorrow and eating from it today a small amount; and he also ruled to permit wrapping the cheeses. And he wrote that he erred in one law, for this is circumvention and is forbidden; and he rejected the teacher's proof from Rav Ada who salts bone by bone on folio 11. And regarding what he permitted to wrap, etc.: he erred even according to the teacher's view who permitted in slaughter, since one cannot [get] an olive-bulk of meat without slaughter; but with dough it is not permitted by giving one to a child except where it improves, etc. Behold he forbids circumvention in slaughter; and what he wrote regarding dough, since it does not improve, he wrote only according to the teacher's error. And so wrote Eliyah Rabbah, that Magen Avraham did not examine the body of the responsum but only what Beis Yosef brought. And the wording appears that he did examine there, for he wrote, "and see in siman 681." And regarding that he forbade circumvention in slaughter, the reason is that the slaughter was after minchah and after the meal; certainly circumvention is forbidden, as the Shulchan Aruch also wrote.
One can say that specifically, etc. Because each loaf needs effort, etc. And according to this, that which the Rashba needed to give a reason regarding wrapping the cheeses because it does not improve: it should have been enough that each one needs effort; therefore one must say like Rabbeinu Yoel who permits such a case even where each one needs effort. Nevertheless even according to Magen Avraham who permits circumvention where it improves, that is specifically where there is no extra effort. And if so, in any case Magen Avraham agrees that also with a pot that improves, if it was already placed beside the fire, or to pour from a small pot to a large one and add, it is forbidden by way of circumvention, since there is effort.
And what Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote, that this is called circumvention. That is even if in truth he needs one loaf [for] two and three: nevertheless it is called circumvention, for he can take that bread that he needs entirely from one oven. And so wrote the Taz according to those who forbid circumvention: even if he needs a little, it is forbidden to cook two pots of one kind and eat a little from each, for he can eat everything from one pot and does not need two pots.

That one should not forbid, etc. Even though the Radbaz forbids, nevertheless the world has great authorities on whom to hang.
In our time they have not practiced thus; so what have we to add them a leniency against the Radbaz. And the Taz holds like those who forbid when he says explicitly that it is for the need of the second day of Yom Tov; therefore one must be careful to cook before Yom Tov what is needed for the night of the second day of Yom Tov. Even though nonspecifically, if he does not say that he is doing for the need of the second day of Yom Tov, it is permitted by the strict law, nevertheless it is better to cook before Yom Tov.
