And a dish for which the greater quantity does not add improvement is forbidden with two efforts; the same applies to a roast beside the fire with two efforts: it is forbidden; see Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Maharil. And it appears that "small piece" can mean he wants good fat gravy, like a cooked dish with meat, which is permitted with two efforts when he needs only the dish; see Bach and see Taz. And specifically a roast beside the fire on a spit and the like; he knows that with one effort it is permitted with water; see the Ran, the reason that on Shabbos increasing in measure is forbidden, and on Yom Tov the Torah permitted so that one not be exacting, and it is permitted; alternatively, whatever one increases in measure with one effort. And one can say that therefore it is permitted by reason of "since" (ho'il), and by rabbinic law it is forbidden, and with one effort they permitted it because it is not noticeable. And in our novellae we wrote about this. And the law of "since, if guests come": it is implied even where it is prevented, as for example the governor commanded that they not come from elsewhere at all, and the like; nevertheless "since" is so called in such a case; only where one cooks near evening, there is no "since." See Beitzah 17a: at first glance the opening clause is superfluous, "One does not bake," only it should have said, "She fills a pot with meat and a baker with water, but she does not bake," etc. At first glance one can say there it even includes also a roast on coals and the like; and the first tanna would agree with R' Shimon ben Elazar that improvement permits with meat, only regarding bread he disagrees; and therefore he said "One does not bake," all kinds of baking of bread and meat on coals with two efforts; but a pot of meat with two efforts is permitted for the improvement that they are fat. Or [the wording] "One does not bake": a woman, to exclude from R' Shimon ben Elazar; and a baker does not bake, and we are not concerned that he refrain from the joy of Yom Tov; see Yam shel Shlomo 16. And Rashi wrote: "One does not bake": bread, with every roast if possible; and R' Shimon ben Elazar for the first tanna permits [what] not [in] chapter 3; and the first tanna teaches us both small and large: with a baker we are not concerned that he refrain; and one should not elaborate; see Pesachim 62a, and understand.
