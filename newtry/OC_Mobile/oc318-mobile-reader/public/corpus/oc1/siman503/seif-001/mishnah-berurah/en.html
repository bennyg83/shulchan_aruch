It is forbidden to bake, etc. - And even something that is not a melacha but mere effort, such as washing bowls and bringing wine from one Yom Tov to another, is likewise forbidden, as was explained at the end of siman 667; and see there what I wrote on this.
Even if it is Shabbos - For Yom Tov does not prepare for Shabbos, as the Gemara derives from a verse; and all the more so from Yom Tov to a weekday is forbidden; and see siman 527.
Or Yom Tov - That is to say, the second day of Yom Tov of the diaspora, which is a doubtful weekday, and it turns out that one is preparing from Yom Tov to a weekday.
And even on the two days of Rosh Hashanah - For to be stringent we say they are one sanctity, and not to be lenient.
But she fills, etc. - And even after she has placed the pot on the fire she is permitted to add meat, because the dish is better prepared when there is much meat there; and even if it is in a small pot, it is permitted to put it into a large pot and add upon it so that it be fatter. And all this applies specifically when cooking meat and fish in a pot; but with other dishes she is not allowed to add for the night once the pot is already standing on the fire, for that does not add improvement to the dish thereby. And likewise when roasting meat on a spit one is not allowed to add for the night once the spit is already standing on the fire; but initially one may add on the spit as much as one wishes, since it is one effort; and likewise with a cooked dish of that sort, and as in se'if 2.
Even though one does not need, etc. - That is, even if her intent in adding the meat is for the night which is a weekday, nevertheless it is permitted, since in any case she needs one piece to eat on Yom Tov. And many later authorities agreed that nevertheless she should be careful not to say with her mouth that she is cooking for the need of the night; and nevertheless after the fact it is not forbidden thereby.
But only a piece, etc. - And the same applies if she needs only the dish or the gravy alone: it is likewise permitted, for all of that is improved by adding the meat. And if she does not need the daytime meal at all, and her main cooking is only for the need of the night, and she eats a little of it so that cooking not be forbidden for her: there are opinions among the poskim; some forbid, for it is in the category of circumvention; and some permit, since in any case she eats a little of it, and it is also before eating, as below. And the world has practiced to be lenient like this view, cooking in the morning for the need of the night and tasting a little of them; and one should not protest against them, for they have authorities on whom to rely. (And specifically from the first day of Yom Tov to the second day of Yom Tov; but from Yom Tov to a weekday one should be careful about this.) And one who is stringent like the first view, not to cook for the need of the night in a pot of its own, but rather in that same pot in which he cooks for the need of the morning meal he increases for the need of the night: a blessing should come upon him; and see below in s.k. 10.
One - And specifically in this, where there is also benefit for the need of the day through the greater quantity; but it is forbidden to make knishes or kreplach and the like more than what one needs for that same day, for one does not improve the other, and one must toil with each one separately.
And all the more so one may slaughter, etc. - He said "all the more so" because it is impossible to slaughter for one olive-bulk alone unless one slaughters the entire animal. And specifically if there is still time until the time of eating to flay it, salt it, and cook it, or at least to roast it; but if there will not be enough time until after the time of eating, it is forbidden, as below.
And likewise she may cook many pots, etc. - That is to say, even if his intent is for the need of the night, since he wants to eat from each one at the morning meal. And it refers to each pot being cooked as a kind of its own; but one kind in two pots one should not permit, since the taste of the two pots is the same, so whatever he tastes from both of them he could taste from one, and it turns out that the second pot is being cooked only for the need of the night alone.
And to eat a little from each one - From the nonspecific wording it is implied that even if he does not need to eat, and only tastes a little from each pot so that he have permission to cook: and that is like the lenient view above in s.k. 7. And according to the forbidding view there, there is permission only for one whose true intent is to eat different delicacies at the morning meal and who intends to increase in each kind so that it remain for the night, but not when he tastes only by way of circumvention. And we have already written that the world has practiced like the lenient view.
For it is circumvention - And in this, according to everyone it is forbidden even if she afterward eats a little, for it is circumvention recognizable to all, since she has already eaten her meal.
However, if she transgressed and cooked, etc. - Some of the later authorities explain that it refers to the beginning of the se'if, namely if he intentionally transgressed and cooked from one Yom Tov for the other, and it does not refer at all to what is adjacent to it, which deals with the law of circumvention; for there it is possible that even after the fact it is forbidden, for circumvention is more stringent than intentional [transgression], as below in siman 527, se'if 24. But several later authorities wrote that it refers to what is adjacent to it, for this circumvention is light enough to permit after the fact, since in any case he ate a little of it while it was still day.
