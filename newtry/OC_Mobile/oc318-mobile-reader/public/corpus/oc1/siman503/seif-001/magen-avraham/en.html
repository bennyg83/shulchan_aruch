It is forbidden to prepare from one Yom Tov for the other, even something that is not a melacha; see the end of siman 667 what I wrote, and see siman 416; and all the more so nowadays, when we are expert in the fixing of the month, the second day of Yom Tov relative to the first is a weekday, for which it is forbidden. Of Rosh Hashanah. For to be stringent we say they are one sanctity, and not to be lenient. And it is difficult: for in siman 416, se'if 2, he wrote of one who mixes [eruv] for the two days of Yom Tov of the diaspora, and Maggid Mishneh and the Rashba and the Rambam wrote: but on Rosh Hashanah, even if it was eaten on the first [day], he fulfills [his obligation] on the second, for they are one sanctity even for leniency; see there. And one can say that in truth the first is primary, and if so it is forbidden to cook on it for the second day, for one is preparing from Yom Tov to a weekday; but there, whatever you say: if we say the second day is a weekday, he does not need an eruv.
But she fills, etc. Even after she has placed the pot on the fire she is permitted to add meat, because the dish is better prepared when there is much there (Tur, Beis Yosef, Yam shel Shlomo). And it is implied that even if it is in a small pot, it is permitted to put it into a large pot and add upon it so that it be fatter; and see this se'if. And the Tur wrote that it is permitted to say explicitly, "For the second day of Yom Tov I want this leftover"; and in Yam shel Shlomo he ruled like the Rokeach that it is forbidden; and so it seems in the Bach (and see siman 512, se'if 1). And the world practices a leniency, cooking in the morning for the need of the night; and in any case one must be careful to eat a little of it. And Beis Yosef wrote that even if one eats only from the dish that is with the meat, or from the gravy, it is permitted. And the Bach wrote that specifically when one needs one piece, then it is permitted to fill the pot; but if the main cooking is for the need of the night, and in the morning one eats a little of it so that it not be forbidden for him, that is circumvention, unlike many who err in this prohibition, etc. And he strained to resolve the words of Tosafos and Hagahos Asheri and Rabbeinu Yeruham, who wrote explicitly that they circumvent; see there. And the words of Rabbeinu Yeruham are impossible to resolve, for this is his wording: People are accustomed to slaughter the chicken and cook it for the need of a weekday, and to eat from the liver or a small amount, and they relied on that [case] of Rav Ada, etc. (see the end of siman 500); and Rabbeinu Shmuel wrote: specifically before eating; end quote. And so wrote Yam shel Shlomo explicitly that it is permitted to circumvent; and that which is forbidden with circumvention is when he does not eat at all from that pot, only saying perhaps guests will happen to come to him (see siman 527, se'if 21). And so it seems in Beis Yosef in the name of the Rashba, that it is forbidden to wrap cheeses in dough even though he eats one of them, because one is not improved by the preparation of the other; see there. It is implied that in a pot where it improves, it is permitted; see there. And in the responsum of the Rashba, siman 681. And the Mordechai wrote regarding one who needed bread for a bris milah, and R' Yoel permitted baking three ovens because they need one loaf from each oven for two and three Jews; and Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote that this is called circumvention and is forbidden. And one can say that specifically regarding bread he was stringent, because each loaf needs effort and preparation on its own (see siman 507, se'if 2 in Beis Yosef); but regarding a pot, which is one effort, it is permitted. Afterwards I saw that Kenesses HaGedolah wrote in the name of the Radbaz: specifically when he ate in the manner of eating, and not to circumvent; see there. And nevertheless it appears to me that since the custom is to be lenient, cooking in the morning and tasting something of it, one should not forbid it for them; but to prepare from Yom Tov to a weekday in such a manner is forbidden, even though Rabbeinu Yeruham wrote that they practice to be lenient, as I wrote above; in our time they have not practiced to be lenient in this.
