Se'if 1. Or to cook. From what it says there, "In truth they said," etc.; and the same applies to all of them.
Or to slaughter. From what it says [there], "So it is [regarding] one: an animal that is dying," etc., while it is still day.
For the need of tomorrow. There, according to the reading of the Rif and Rosh, and at the beginning of chapter 2, "so that they say," etc.
Even if it is Shabbos. Like the reading of the Rif and Rosh there, and at the beginning of chapter 2 there; and at the beginning of [tractate] Yom Tov: Yom Tov does not prepare for Shabbos.
Or Yom Tov. Like our reading in the Gemara.
And even on the two, etc. For according to the Torah it is only one day; for we say that regarding a corpse, even on the two days of Yom Tov of Rosh Hashanah the Sages treated them as a weekday, etc.; and we do not say they are one sanctity except for stringency, similar to an egg, because sometimes they would practice that day as sacred and the next day as sacred if [witnesses] came from minchah and onward, and that is from doubt; but in most years, when [witnesses] come before minchah, only the first day was sacred; and all the more so from the enactment of Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai and onward, that the first day is always sacred. And see Rashi at the beginning of [tractate] Yom Tov, folio 5b, s.v. "Is it not," etc.; and so wrote the Raavad in the Hassagot, chapter 8 of Hilchos Yom Tov. But the view of the Rambam there is that even for leniency they are one sanctity; and Maggid Mishneh wrote that so is the view of the Rashba and Rabbeinu Peretz.
And all the more so one may, etc. As it says there, "So it is [regarding] one: unless there is time," etc., as above; and see Rashi in Pesachim 46b, s.v. "Because of loss of his money," etc.; and likewise in the Gemara there; see there.
And likewise she may, etc. They learned from what it says [Beitzah] 11b, "He circumvents and salts," etc.; and see Tosafos there, s.v. "He circumvents," etc. But the Ran already wrote there and in chapter 2 that specifically there they permitted circumvention because the loss comes to him on account of the joy of Yom Tov, and as it says there, they permitted their end because of their beginning; but in chapter 2 they said, "provided that he not circumvent." And the Rosh wrote there another reason; see there.
And specifically, etc. So wrote Tosafos there, and Rashi in chapter 3 of Pesachim 46b, s.v. "One who bakes." And one must say so, for if not, why say "since, if guests come to him"; let him say "since, if he wishes, he may eat a little or give a little to a child." And likewise one who did not set eruvei tavshilin could cook a large pot and fill an oven with bread and feed a little today to a child. And even regarding an animal half of which belongs to a Jew it would be forbidden if it were not that one cannot [get] an olive-bulk of meat, etc. And even though we hold "since" (ho'il), that is regarding lashes, but there is a prohibition, as it says in Pesachim there: according to you, how do we bake from Yom Tov for Shabbos, etc. And if it were so, according to him the difficulty remains: why need eruvei tavshilin? And even though they said "he circumvents and salts," etc., [that is] before eating, because of the joy of Yom Tov; for if not, people would come to refrain, etc.
For it is circumvention. The wording of the Gemara: He said to him, "Is there circumvention greater than this?"
However, etc. There, [folio] 17: They asked them: If he transgressed and baked, etc.; and it was not resolved; and a doubt of rabbinic law is treated leniently, for according to the Torah we hold that we say "since" (ho'il), like Rabbah. And further, Rav Ashi and Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak rejected their resolution; that implies that they hold it is permitted.
