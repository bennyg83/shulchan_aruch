And even the two days of Rosh Hashanah. Even though they are like one long day, that is only to be stringent, but not to be lenient.
But a woman fills, etc. In the Gemara, the Rabbis taught: One does not bake from one Yom Tov for the other. In truth they said: A woman fills the entire pot with meat even though she needs only one piece. Rashi explained: because one effort suffices; and likewise with a baker regarding filling water; but regarding baking she bakes only what she needs, because each loaf needs baking of its own. R' Shimon ben Elazar says: A woman fills the entire oven with bread, because the bread is baked well when the oven is full; for if one sticks many loaves to the walls of the oven, the hollow space is reduced and the heat does not spread so much, and it is baked well. Rabbah said: The law is like R' Shimon ben Elazar. And the Tur wrote the law of baking bread regarding filling the pot with meat, even though one needs only one piece, because the dish is better prepared when there is much in the pot. And this is the Tur's intent for two reasons: the first, that in siman 507 he brought that specifically in their ovens the greater quantity of bread was an improvement, because they would stick [bread] to the walls of the oven, which is not so in our ovens; therefore the Tur wrote this law regarding a cooked dish with meat. The second reason: it teaches us that even with meat there is a benefit from the greater quantity. And it is obvious that also R' Shimon ben Elazar, with whom we hold, likewise holds the reason of the first tanna, that it is permitted whenever there is only one effort; for therefore it is permitted for a baker with water, where there is not the reason that there is benefit in the greater quantity, but rather because of one effort, like the first tanna; only that R' Shimon ben Elazar adds to permit even with two efforts, such as baking bread, because of the benefit of the greater quantity. And so the Tur holds as well. And if so, what he wrote of permission with a cooked dish with meat: its law is likewise even with two efforts; that is, if the pot is already set beside the fire, she may do a new act to put another piece there because of the benefit of the greater quantity, like bread according to R' Shimon ben Elazar. And therefore I wondered at Beis Yosef, who made a dispute between Rashi, who gave the reason of one effort, and the Tur, who explained because of the benefit of the greater quantity; and on this basis he made a practical difference between the two reasons, and from this he made a ruling of law as to with whom we hold; see there. And with apology to his honor, that is not the way; rather both are law, also according to Rashi. And Rashi mentioned his reason only for the first tanna, who does not hold the second reason; but the Tur writes his reason according to R' Shimon ben Elazar, with whom we hold, who holds both. And the proof is that he permitted a baker to heat water, for there in truth it is permitted only with one effort; and this is obvious in my humble opinion; and Beis Yosef, on account of his sharpness, did not examine this properly.
But only one piece. Meaning she may even add several times, as I wrote nearby, because of the benefit of the greater quantity, even meat upon meat. And Beis Yosef wrote that it is not permitted to add meat upon meat by reason of even a slight improvement, and he follows his view that we mentioned; but it has already been explained that it is not so, and it is permitted. And the Tur rules that even if she says explicitly that she is adding for weekday eating, it is permitted, since in any case there is benefit on Yom Tov from the greater quantity; and he wrote that there are those who forbid this; and Maharshal wrote that one should be stringent not to say so explicitly, but only in a nonspecific way.
And all the more so one may slaughter, etc. Its place is after what the Shulchan Aruch wrote, "But a woman fills, etc.," for all the more so here, since it is impossible to slaughter for one olive-bulk alone unless one slaughters the entire animal.
And specifically before eating. The Tur wrote so in the name of the Baal HaIttur, and this is his wording: Specifically before eating she may increase in cooking; but after eating they may not cook and say, "I will eat an olive-bulk of it," and there is no circumvention greater than this; end quote. And it is difficult for me: for here it is implied that circumvention is forbidden, and Tosafos wrote explicitly in Pesachim, folio 37, regarding thick bread, that Beis Hillel permit; Tosafos wrote the reason of Beis Hillel as we say, "A woman circumvents and bakes a full oven of loaves," etc.; that implies that circumvention is permitted in this. And one can say that when there is one side that she has benefit from the greater quantity of loaves, namely that she truly needs one loaf, she is permitted also to do circumvention and increase; and that is before eating; which is not so after eating, where there is only circumvention alone, and because of the circumvention she wants to eat an olive-bulk of it: that is forbidden. And from the end of the Tur's words, that he hung the prohibition on the circumvention of eating an olive-bulk, which itself is without need since he has already eaten: we learn from this that what he wrote at first, that before eating one may increase in cooking, that is even with several pots, since each one is fit to eat from truly, even though he knows for certain that he will not eat all of them; it suffices if he eats even an olive-bulk of it at the time of eating, for such is the way of eating to taste from some pots even a small amount, and that is the main of the eating. And in Tosafos, chapter 2, folio 17, they wrote: "provided that he not circumvent"; Rashi explained: with two pots, that he say, "I need to invite guests"; but with one pot it is permitted to circumvent and increase, etc. And if you say, what differs from that [case] of Rav Ada, who salts bone by bone: one can say that there there is only processing of foods, and because of the joy of Yom Tov, etc.; but here we are dealing with cooking, which is more stringent, and he could also have made a remedy by means of eruvei tavshilin; end quote. This does not contradict what I wrote in explaining the Tur, that before eating even many pots are permitted: for there each pot was a different kind of dish, and it is possible he will taste from all of them; and if so there is here one side together with circumvention, exactly like one pot. But Tosafos are dealing with one kind in two pots, and one pot would suffice for him: in that case there is only circumvention alone, even though it is before eating; and in that, tasting an olive-bulk from each one certainly does not avail, since in truth he does not need that tasting at all. And from this it emerges for us that a woman may cook many kinds of dishes on the first day of Yom Tov even though she knows for certain that they will leave over one kind, so long as they taste from it even an olive-bulk, since she cooks everything before eating. And in this it appears that it is forbidden to say explicitly that one kind will be for the second day of Yom Tov; and even the Tur who permits, as I wrote, that is in one pot to add because of the benefit of the greater quantity; but here, where the permission is only because it is before eating and he will taste from it an olive-bulk, if he says explicitly that it is for the need of the second day of Yom Tov, the tasting from it does not avail him. And in this it appears to me that such was the intent of the Rashba in the responsum that Beis Yosef brought regarding one who permitted handling on the first day of Yom Tov dough with cheese for the need of the night of the second day of Yom Tov, on the ground that he would give a child to taste from one of them, and the Rashba said of him that he erred, etc.: namely in this way, that they explicitly made food for the second day of Yom Tov, and in that tasting from it does not avail. And likewise what Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Semak: but to bake and cook from one Yom Tov for the other is forbidden even when he eats some of it; and Beis Yosef wondered at this, why it should be forbidden, and established it as after eating; and this explanation is surprising, for afterward he wrote there: However, specifically before the morning eating, etc. But according to me it is fine: when he says explicitly that this will be for the night of the second day of Yom Tov, tasting from it does not avail him, since it was done in prohibition; which is not so if he said nothing, even though he concludes in his mind to leave it for the second night: there is no prohibition in the matter, since it is before eating and it is relevant to his eating and he will taste some of it. And so wrote Tosafos in the first chapter of Beitzah, folio 12, in the name of Rabbeinu Shmuel, that people are accustomed to circumvent and eat a little of the chicken and eat the liver: that is specifically before eating, but after eating, not; until here. And Maharshal cited them in chapter 2. And it appears that even tasting by a child suffices in this, as I wrote in siman 512: if they are not particular whether they give bread to a child, etc.; and see what I wrote, what is difficult about this. And I have not seen anyone among all the poskim that Beis Yosef brings who disagrees with what I wrote. And nevertheless it appears as the practical law that since in any case there is a prohibition if one says explicitly that it will be for the second night, therefore one must be careful to cook before Yom Tov what is needed for the second night. And after the fact Maharshal wrote, and this is his wording: Nevertheless it appears that if he circumvented by eating an olive-bulk even after eating, it is permitted after the fact, for circumvention by eating an olive-bulk does not have the status of circumvention so as to forbid him more than an intentional [transgression]; and so wrote the Rivash in a responsum; end quote. And so it is nearby here, where the Shulchan Aruch wrote, "However, if he transgressed and cooked, it is permitted," and that is with circumvention by an olive-bulk. And see what I wrote in siman 526 at the end of the siman. And my teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, went too far to be stringent even after the fact where he needs only one piece; and what appears clear to me I have written. He wrote the Mordechai in chapter 2 of Beitzah that Rabbeinu Yoel permitted baking on Yom Tov for the need of a bris milah that fell on the next day, and it was their festival day so that they could not bake if not on Yom Tov, and one oven was not enough, and he permitted baking several times because they need bread for every oven; and Hagahos Maimoniyos disagrees with this, holding that one who circumvents is forbidden; and so ruled Beis Yosef.
