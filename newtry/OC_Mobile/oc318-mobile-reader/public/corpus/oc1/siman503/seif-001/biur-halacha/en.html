On Yom Tov for the need of tomorrow - And even during twilight of the first day it is likewise forbidden, for perhaps it is still day (Peri Megadim). And it is obvious that it is forbidden to begin any melacha of food preparation for the soul, even while the day is still long, if one estimates that the matter will continue and one will not be able to benefit from it until after sunset.
And even on the two days of Rosh Hashanah - See Mishnah Berurah, and see Magen Avraham, who wrote that the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch accords even according to the Rambam and those who join him (brought above in siman 416), who hold that the two days of Rosh Hashanah are one sanctity even for leniency; see there his reason. And the view of the Gra in his commentary is that according to the Rambam and those who join him this law would change; see there. And at first glance, the first day of Rosh Hashanah is certainly sacred according to the Torah, so how could it be permitted to cook on it for the need of the second day of Yom Tov? And it is possible that this view holds that even from Yom Tov to a weekday is likewise [permitted in principle], since there is no Torah prohibition in it by reason of "since" (ho'il); and therefore on Rosh Hashanah, where they are one sanctity for both, it is permitted from one Yom Tov to the other, for they [the Sages] said [it] and they [the Sages] said [it]. And according to this, even according to this view it would be permitted only if it would still be fit to benefit from it on that same day (for regarding this, "since, if guests come to him, it is fit for him, etc." applies), excluding a case where it will not finish until sunset, for regarding that there is a Torah-level doubt; even according to this view it is forbidden.
But she fills, etc. - And that is even if her intent in filling it is so that the leftover remain for the second day of Yom Tov or for a weekday; and the reason is that it is one effort. But to add and put meat into it when it is already set beside the fire is forbidden. And what the later authorities agreed, that it is also permitted to add afterward even though it is a separate effort: that is permitted only when she intends that thereby the dish she is cooking for the need of Yom Tov be improved; but for the need of a weekday it is forbidden. All this is clear from Magen Avraham in siman 507, s.k. 13, and so wrote Eliyah Rabbah there.
However, if she transgressed and cooked, etc. - See Magen Avraham, who wrote that [it is permitted] even if he has other cooked dishes, and he cited for this Beis Yosef, Rashba, and Yam shel Shlomo. And in truth this ruling is not mentioned in any of them regarding circumvention, only regarding intentional [transgression]; and Magen Avraham holds that the same applies to this circumvention, which is lighter than other circumventions because he ate from it while it was still day. And see below at the end of siman 527 in Biur Halacha, s.v. "If," that even regarding intentional [transgression] it is not so simple to permit when he has other cooked dishes.
