1) [Se'if 1] It is forbidden to bake or cook, etc. It is forbidden to prepare on one day of Yom Tov for the next even something that does not involve a melachah. This is certainly so nowadays, when we are expert in determining the new month, so that the second day of Yom Tov is a weekday relative to the first and preparation is forbidden. Magen Avraham, at the beginning of the siman. Nevertheless, regarding something that entails no melachah, it appears that having a non-Jew perform it is permitted. Eshel Avraham, at the beginning of the siman; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 13, Likutei Rima, subsection 3.
2) There. It is forbidden to bake or cook, etc. Likewise, one should not wash dishes on one day for the next. Derashos Mahari Weil, Hilchos Yom Tov; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1. It is also forbidden to bring wine on one day of Yom Tov for the next. Minhagim, Hilchos Pesach; Magen Avraham, at the end of siman 667; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 3; Chayei Adam, rule 99, subsection 1, and rule 153, subsection 6; Mikraei Kodesh there, subsection 5. Chayei Adam writes there, however, that in pressing circumstances, when wine will not be readily available at night, one may bring wine, and likewise water, during the preceding day of Yom Tov for the next. This is not considered preparation. He must bring it while much of the day remains, so that the purpose is not evident, as he might still need it for that day. He also should not bring it in the weekday manner but should alter the manner of carrying. Nevertheless, we have no proof that this may be done for a weekday, because it is possible that permission was granted only for the sake of a mitzvah. See there. Mikraei Kodesh there, Likutei Rima, subsection 5, and Pischei Teshuvah cite this. See further below, at the end of siman 667, in the gloss, and our words there, with Heaven's help.
