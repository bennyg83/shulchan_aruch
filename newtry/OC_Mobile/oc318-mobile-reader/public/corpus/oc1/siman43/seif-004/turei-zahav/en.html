Or in his garment in his hand. This is puzzling, for in the first version he wrote that even if he grasped them in his garment we are concerned lest he wipe sparks. And Levush wrote on this that it concerns a permanent privy, but in temporary it is truly forbidden because of sparks; and I am puzzled that he did not look in the Beit Yosef, who concluded on this that there is no concern for sparks because they are in his lap, etc. And it seems to me that this "in his garment in his hand" explains regarding the interruption, and thus he says: if they are in his lap and he interrupts between his lap and his nakedness with a belt, or he interrupts in his hand — the tefillin that are in his garment, meaning the tefillin are under his garment and he holds them outside on his garment so they do not fall down — then he will not take his hand from there to wipe sparks; as above, one must press and interpret thus.
