Or in his garment, in his hand. This is difficult, because in se'if 1 he writes that even if he grasps them in his garment, we are concerned that he may wipe away droplets. Levush writes that the present case concerns a permanent privy, but that in a temporary one it is indeed forbidden because of droplets. I am surprised that he did not examine Beis Yosef, who concludes that when they are in his bosom there is no concern about droplets, etc. It appears to me that "in his garment, in his hand" refers to the separation. That is, if they are in his bosom and a belt separates his bosom from his nakedness, or his hand creates the separation while the tefillin are in his garment; meaning that the tefillin are beneath his garment and he holds them from outside the garment so that they do not fall; he will not remove his hand to wipe away droplets. This appears to be the necessary, though strained, interpretation.
