Se'if katan 4: 'He removes them, etc.; this discusses a permanent privy.' Since the first clause discusses both a temporary and a permanent privy, it is reasonable to say that the subsequent words 'he removes them' refer to the permanent one. Had the first clause not discussed a permanent privy at all, there would be no basis for saying that the later words refer to one. This is also implied by the formulation 'he removes them.' If it referred to a temporary privy, then either way: if the tefillin are upon his head, he may urinate while wearing them. It must therefore be discussing tefillin already in his hand, in which case 'he removes them' is inapplicable; it should say 'he sets them aside,' since he has already removed them and holds them in his hand. Rather, it refers to a permanent privy and discusses tefillin upon his head, so the wording 'he removes them' is appropriate.
