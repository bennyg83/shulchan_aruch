“Or in his garment, in his hand.” This means specifically in his right hand, because there is no concern that he might wipe with it, since one may not wipe with the right hand. Above, in se'if 1, however, it says, “even in his garment, in his hand.” One might answer that here it refers to a permanent lavatory, as Rashi explains, but that is untenable: if so, it should have stated explicitly that the case concerns a permanent lavatory. Moreover, in a permanent lavatory it permitted holding them even in his hand without a garment. Lechem Chamudot writes that the Rabbi was not precise, because what I wrote in se'if 1 follows the Tur's view, whereas se'if 4 follows the view of Sefer HaTerumah; the two views disagree with one another, as stated above.
