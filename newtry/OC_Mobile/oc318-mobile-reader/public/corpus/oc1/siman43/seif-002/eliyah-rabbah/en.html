[2] Holds them in his hand, etc.—it implies that even without his garment it is permitted; and although in section 4 we require specifically [them] in his garment—that is for relieving oneself; but here it deals with urination, whose prohibition is only a decree, so we are not stringent when they are in his hand. But it is difficult: Shulchan Aruch and the Levush in siman 4 wrote that it is permitted both to urinate and to defecate, etc.—apparently for urination too we require specifically his garment. And one cannot say that what he wrote “both to urinate” refers to a temporary beit hakisei while standing, for it is explained in this section that while standing, even in his hand and garment, it is forbidden; and it is possible to say that incidentally he mentioned urination, but their main point is defecation alone. And now all the difficulties in Lechem Chamudot, 25a, and Nachalat Tzvi are resolved—see there, for I did not wish to elaborate; and see note 5. Nevertheless, for practical halacha it needs study, for I saw that Rambam, chapter 4, and the Rif wrote explicitly that even for urination we require his garment and hand; and so ruled the Taz; and according to this, we require specifically his right hand; and even in a pocket Magen Avraham concludes it is forbidden unless he entered a temporary beit hakisei to urinate—for then it is permitted in a pocket.
