In a permanent beit hakisei—meaning that there is in it, etc.—it appears that even if now there is no excrement in it at all, since it is a place where people habitually defecate, we decree lest he relieve himself; and the author did not use this wording only to teach us that if there is excrement in this place, we are also concerned lest he relieve himself in this place—even if until now this place was not designated for defecation. With this, what the author concluded at the end, “without a pit,” is understood: he teaches that if it is in a pit it is considered as if it does not exist, and it is permitted if that place was not designated for defecation until now. See Magen Avraham, note 6, what he wondered about the author; and according to this, everything is resolved.
