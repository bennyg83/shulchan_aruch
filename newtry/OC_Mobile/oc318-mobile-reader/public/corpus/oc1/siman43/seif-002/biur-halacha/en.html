A permanent bathroom means one in which there is, etc. It appears at first glance that even if there is presently no excrement there at all, since it is a place where people regularly relieve themselves, we decree lest he relieve himself. The author used this wording only to teach us that if excrement is present in this place, we are concerned that he too may relieve himself there, even if until now the place was not designated for that purpose. This also explains why the author concludes with the words 'without a pit': If the excrement is in a pit, it is considered as though it were absent, and it is permitted if the place was not previously designated for relieving oneself. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 6, who questions the author; according to this explanation, everything is resolved.
