Without a pit. A privy in a pit is considered closed, as in siman 83:4; Beis Yosef writes likewise in Rashi's name. At first glance this implies that one may urinate there while wearing head-tefillin, since it refers back to the preceding law. This requires examination. Rashi comments on the Gemara's earlier ruling that one removes them four amos away: this applies only without a pit; with a pit it resembles a temporary privy, where the Gemara says one removes them and immediately relieves himself. Rashi holds the removal is required because of entering the privy, so where there is a pit entry is permitted; even Krias Shema may be recited within it. But Rosh and Beis Yosef hold that removal is not because of entry, but because he intends to relieve himself; even at a temporary privy, one who intends to defecate must remove them four amos away. Chiddushei Rashba asks how one may remove them and immediately defecate at a temporary privy, when 'your camp shall be holy' is required. Certainly the same applies when he intends to defecate at a privy in a pit, and one also may not urinate there wearing head-tefillin, since we still decree lest he defecate. According to my explanation above; that 'rather, he removes them four amos away' concerns urinating at a permanent privy; the matter is resolved: it applies specifically without a pit, but with a pit he removes them and urinates immediately without distancing four amos. Beis Yosef, however, evidently erred here and thought Rashi explained this concerning the Gemara's inquiry; see there.
