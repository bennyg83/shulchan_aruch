"Without a pit" — but in a pit it is considered sealed, as stated in siman 83 seif 4; and so too the Beit Yosef in the name of Rashi. And it implies at first glance that he holds it is permitted to urinate in it while wearing tefillin on his head, for it refers to the preceding matter. And it requires investigation: for Rashi in the Gemara refers to the preceding matter from it (see Gemara ad loc.), which says "he removes them at a distance of four amot," and on this Rashi explained: specifically when it is without a pit; but when it is in a pit it is like a temporary bathroom, regarding which the Gemara says he removes them and defecates immediately. For Rashi holds the reason one must remove them is because of entering; if so, when it is in a pit there is no prohibition of entering, for even Keriat Shema may be recited inside it. But the Rosh and Beit Yosef, who hold one must remove them not because of entering but because he wished to relieve himself — even in a temporary bathroom, when he wishes to defecate he must remove them at a distance of four amot. And so it is written in the Rashba's gloss, and these are his words: "And it is difficult: how is it permitted in a temporary bathroom to remove them and defecate immediately — do we not require 'and your camp shall be holy'? And there is no basis for kal vechomer when he wishes to defecate in a bathroom that is in a pit. And it is also forbidden to urinate in it while wearing tefillin on his head, for nevertheless there is room to decree lest he defecate in them." And according to what was stated above, that "rather he removes them at a distance of four amot" refers when one wishes to urinate in a permanent bathroom — it comes out well that on this he said: specifically when it is without a pit; but when it is in a pit he removes them and urinates immediately and need not distance four amot, as above. But in Beit Yosef it is proved that he erred in this and thought Rashi explained so regarding the question; see there.
