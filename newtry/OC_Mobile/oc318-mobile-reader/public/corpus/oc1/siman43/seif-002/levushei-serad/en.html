Magen Avraham, seif katan 2. The meaning is, "and therefore." Rama's principal intent is to resolve a difficulty concerning the Mechaber. Since with respect to this matter there is no difference between a permanent and a temporary privy, why does the Mechaber state the permissive circumstance "while seated" specifically in the permanent case? Rama's words, "since he does not defecate there," are likewise difficult, for even in the permanent case under discussion he does not intend to defecate. Nevertheless, in a permanent privy the ordinary practice is to urinate while seated because it is permanent, while here the ordinary practice is to urinate while standing because it is temporary. Rama should therefore have written, "since it is temporary." Magen Avraham answers that Rama incidentally alludes to the distinction between a permanent and temporary privy when the tefillin are upon his head. In a permanent privy it is forbidden even to urinate, because ordinarily a person also defecates there. The word "seated" in Rama is as though he wrote "and seated," while in the temporary case Rama's word "since" is as though he wrote "and since"; meaning that it is not ordinarily used for defecation.
