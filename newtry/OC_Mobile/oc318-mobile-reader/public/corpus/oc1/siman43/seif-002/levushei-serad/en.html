Magen Avraham, note 2: “And therefore”—the main point of the Rama’s words is to resolve what the author found difficult: since for this matter there is no distinction between permanent and temporary, why in permanent did the author write the permitted side as “seated,” and so the Rama wrote “since he does not defecate for major needs”—it is difficult, for even in permanent here it deals with not defecating for major needs, and nevertheless the custom is to urinate seated because it is permanent, and here the custom is standing because it is temporary; and if so, he should have written “since it is temporary.” Therefore Magen Avraham resolved that incidentally through custom the Rama hinted (34) the distinction between permanent and temporary when they are on his head: in permanent it is forbidden even to urinate, because generally he relieves himself; and the word “seated” in the Rama is as if he wrote “and seated”; and regarding temporary, where the Rama wrote “since,” etc., it is as if he wrote “and since”—meaning it is not customary to defecate.
