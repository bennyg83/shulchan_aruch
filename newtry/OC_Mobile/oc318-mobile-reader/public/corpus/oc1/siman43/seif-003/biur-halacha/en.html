A temporary bathroom, etc.; initially a bathroom. See Mishnah Berurah in the name of Peri Megadim. If it is a place that smells because urine is always present there, then at first glance this place has the law of a permanent bathroom. It is no better than a foul-smelling receptacle for urine, whose law is that of excrement by Torah law, as Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah write; nor is it better than foul water or water in which flax is soaked, etc., discussed in siman 86, whose law is that of excrement; see there. It is possible, however, that for this purpose we do not distinguish, and in every case it is permitted to urinate there while wearing tefillin. We do not fear that he may defecate there, since it is designated only for urination. Nor can one prohibit it because of the urine itself on the ground, on the theory that because the place is designated for it the urine has the law of excrement. That cannot be so, for urine in the stream likewise has the law of excrement, yet in a temporary bathroom it is permitted to urinate while the tefillin are on his head. Even in a permanent bathroom this would be permitted were it not for the concern that he might defecate while wearing them. It is therefore possible that here we do not have that concern, and it is permitted.
