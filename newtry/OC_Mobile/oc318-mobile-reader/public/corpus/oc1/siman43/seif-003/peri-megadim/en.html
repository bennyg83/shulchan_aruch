Rather he removes them — the Taz: Behold, the Tur explains (and here too) that it refers to the first clause in a permanent privy: he removes at a distance of four amot, and even only to urinate — since he must remove, he removes at a distance of four amot (but it is permitted to enter in a section when he does not wish to urinate, as the author in seif 5, unlike Rav Hai Gaon). And what he wrote "and gives them to his fellow" — apparently the latter clause, when it is not permanent, and he need not [remove at] four amot, for he need not remove them at all and it is permitted when they are on his head; only that if he wishes to remove, he must give them to his fellow, for he himself is forbidden in his hand and garment too (but to tie with a belt and the like is permitted; see Magen Avraham, letter 5, and Taz 4). This is strained, and one may say as Magen Avraham wrote, letter 4: in permanent, standing, he removes at a distance of four amot and gives them to his fellow by necessity. And what he wrote on the Beit Yosef is questionable — for apparently the Beit Yosef understood that the Tur does not distinguish even when not permanent: he removes at a distance of four amot to urinate; and on this Maharam of Pano challenged well: he removes when not permanent at a distance of four amot, for he holds the reason is lest he pass wind and he must remove — therefore he removes at a distance of four amot; unlike the Tur, who holds the reason is lest he relieve himself, and when not permanent he need not remove at all to urinate — if so, how can he say he removes at a distance of four amot? But it is questionable why he suspected the luminary of Israel (Maran the Beit Yosef) in this; only the Beit Yosef understood in the Tur that he does not distinguish when not permanent, and one who now wishes to make it permanent also removes at a distance of four amot — but did not the Gemara say Rav Acha: there is no [distinction] except in permanent, etc.? And on this he says that Maharam of Pano also omitted Rav Acha's distinction, and even when not permanent and one now wishes to make it permanent he removes at a distance of four amot; and a reason is needed why, and investigation and inquiry on this. And behold, one may give seasoning as to why they omitted this of Rav Acha: for Rav Acha holds that in a permanent privy it is forbidden to enter with tefillin (and like the view of Rav Hai Gaon in seif 5 in the gloss), and therefore they distinguish between permanent from the outset and one who now makes it permanent — unlike us, who rule as the question was left (Berachot 23a): what is the law to enter a permanent [privy] to urinate? — from which it appears they hold it is permitted to enter a permanent [privy] with tefillin (unlike the conduct of R. Chanina the modest, as explained there). If so, why does he remove in permanent at a distance of four amot? Rather, since he removes them, the Sages then forbade him to remove and relieve himself immediately (or to urinate in them). There is no difference between permanent from the outset or permanent now: all who must remove must distance four amot, and even to urinate; likewise one needs distance of four amot. And I will explain further in Magen Avraham; and see there in Taz, letter 5, on this — and here one should not elaborate.
