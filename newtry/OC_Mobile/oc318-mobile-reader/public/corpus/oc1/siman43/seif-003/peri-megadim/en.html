The Tur (and the Mechaber here) explains this as referring back to the first case: at a permanent privy he removes them four cubits away, even if he intends only to urinate. Since he must remove them, he removes them four cubits away. He may, however, enter a permanent privy when he does not intend to urinate, as the Mechaber rules in seif 5, unlike Rav Hai. The words 'and gives them to another person' appear at first glance to refer to the latter clause, involving a nonpermanent place, where four cubits are unnecessary. Yet there he need not remove them at all and may keep them upon his head. If he nevertheless wishes to remove them, he must give them to another person, because even holding them in his hand and garment is forbidden. He may, however, tie them to his belt or the like; see Magen Avraham, ot 5, and Taz, ot 4. This explanation is strained. One can instead explain as Magen Avraham, ot 4, does: it concerns urinating while standing in a permanent privy, in which case he must remove them four cubits away and necessarily give them to another person. Taz's comment upon Beit Yosef requires study. Apparently he understood Beit Yosef as saying that the Tur makes no distinction and requires one who urinates even at a nonpermanent place to remove the tefillin four cubits away. Taz therefore objected: according to Rambam it is reasonable to remove them four cubits away at a nonpermanent place, because he holds that the reason is lest the person pass gas and therefore requires their removal; but according to the Tur, who holds that the reason is lest he defecate, he need not remove them at all to urinate at a nonpermanent place. How, then, could the Tur say that he removes them four cubits away? Yet it requires study why Taz suspected the luminary of Israel, Maran Beit Yosef, of such an understanding. Rather, Beit Yosef understood that the Tur does not distinguish even when the place was not previously permanent but the person now intends to use it as a permanent place: he must remove them four cubits away. Yet Rav Acha says in the Gemara, 'They taught this only regarding a permanent place, etc.' Beit Yosef therefore says that Rambam likewise omits Rav Acha's distinction, and even when the place was not previously permanent but one now intends to use it as such, he removes them four cubits away. The reason requires explanation; see Bach and Derishah. One can explain why they omitted Rav Acha's statement. Rav Acha holds that one may not enter a permanent privy while wearing tefillin, following Rav Hai's approach as in the gloss to seif 5. He therefore distinguishes between a place that was originally permanent and one the person now intends to use as permanent. We, however, follow the unresolved question in Berachos 23a whether one may enter a permanent privy to urinate, which shows that the authorities there hold that one may enter a permanent privy wearing tefillin, unlike R' Hai's reading; see Beit Yosef. Why, then, must he remove them four cubits away at a permanent place? The answer is that once he removes them, the Sages forbade him to remove them and immediately defecate; or urinate while holding them. There is consequently no distinction between a place that was permanent originally and one he now makes permanent. Whenever he must remove them, he must move four cubits away; even merely to urinate in such a case requires a distance of four cubits. With God's help, I will explain this further in Magen Avraham.
