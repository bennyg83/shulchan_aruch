[3] Removes them, etc., and gives them to his fellow, etc.—it needs study, for the Tur and Shulchan Aruch mention this only in a temporary beit hakisei when urinating while standing and he has tefillin in his hand and does not wish to place them on his head—then he gives them to his fellow; but in a permanent beit hakisei one need not give them to his fellow; and so Beit Yosef wrote explicitly. The reason is explained in the Talmud: so that he watch them; and Rashi explained—to guard them from harm, or that he must guard the tefillin himself. And although the Taz and Magen Avraham explained that removing them at four cubits’ distance and giving them to one’s fellow applies in a permanent beit hakisei, but in a temporary one one removes and relieves oneself immediately—that is when urinating while standing in a permanent [privy]; but when relieving oneself seated, not. And it is possible that for this reason he omitted the word “permanent” that he wrote in Shulchan Aruch and wrote “beit hakisei” without qualification, and it refers to temporary; nevertheless he should not have been vague. In my humble opinion, since when relieving oneself he necessarily urinates seated, if so it is possible to hold them in his hand. Also at the end of section 1 they wrote explicitly about a permanent beit hakisei; and it is strained to say that since there it deals only with urination, which is a short time, the reasons mentioned above do not apply. And that the Levush omitted “permanent” seems to me that he holds the Tur and Shulchan Aruch did not mean specifically “permanent,” since when one wants to relieve oneself, what difference is there between permanent and non-permanent—they are equal in all laws; and so wrote the Derishah. But in Olat Tamid he wrote that Shulchan Aruch specifically used “permanent”—in temporary one removes and turns away immediately; and he wondered on this, for in section 1 Shulchan Aruch wrote about temporary, and Beit Yosef wrote explicitly that even in temporary one removes at four cubits’ distance; and even according to what the Taz and Magen Avraham strained—that in temporary beit hakisei one removes and urinates immediately—it seems they agree regarding defecation.
