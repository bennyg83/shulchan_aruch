"He shall not bring them in at all" — so too Ri, and it implies even in a pouch he shall not bring them in. And he concluded: if he entered to defecate — and if he entered not to relieve himself, Rav Hai forbids and Tosafot permit, end quote. And it implies Tosafot permit even in a bathroom in the house. However, it implies from his wording: specifically when they are in his hand — see there regarding temporary bathroom; but on his head it is forbidden. If so, there is no question from a bathhouse, for a bathhouse is like a bathroom in the house that is in the city; see seif 5. And if he wishes to urinate in a temporary bathroom, if they are in their pouch it is permitted even per the Rambam, for the reason is lest he pass wind in them, and when they are in his pouch we are not concerned about passing wind, as Beit Yosef wrote in siman 45 in the name of the Ittur. And it implies in the Gemara that all these leniencies are only regarding tefillin that a person wears all day and walks with them in the field, and it is impossible to trouble him each time to return them home; and also impossible to leave them in holes and cracks because of the incident when a harlot took them and said so-and-so gave them to me as her fee. But other books are forbidden to bring in even in a field bathroom, for he can leave them in his house. And so in the Gemara Rabbi Yehudah gave a book of aggadah to his students. However, if they are in a pouch it is permitted to bring them in, as Yoreh Deah siman 282 seif 8 wrote; and this is clear from the Rambam's words in chapter 10 of Hilchot Sefer Torah, who wrote: "All sacred writings are forbidden to discard. Amulets that contain matters of sacred writings may not be brought into a bathroom unless covered in hide. A person should not hold a Torah scroll on his arm and enter a bathroom with it, even if wrapped in a cloth," etc. — implying sacred writings are permitted in a cloth like an amulet.
