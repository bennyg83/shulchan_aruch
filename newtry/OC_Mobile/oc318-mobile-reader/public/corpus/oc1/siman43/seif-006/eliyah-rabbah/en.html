[6] “One should not enter,” etc.—he wrote in the responsum of Nachalat Tzvi: tefillin and writings that contain divine names require a pouch within a pouch if one must go to a beit hakisei, and they used to buy leather vessels; and in Yoreh Deah, siman 282, section 14, an amulet covered with leather is permitted; and he wrote in Shiltei Gibborim, chapter “With what does a woman go out,” that amulets made for children, covered and engraved with letters of a name, need study—up to here. And it is somewhat difficult: what is his uncertainty? For from the reason of a name on the outside it is forbidden with tefillin in the Talmud; and one must say that since it is engraved and not written, we are uncertain; and see siman 40, note 2.
