[1] With tefillin on his head, etc.—it implies that with arm tefillin it is permitted to enter, since they are covered with leather; but in Nachalat Tzvi he wrote this wording. In my humble opinion it seems one should forbid even arm tefillin because of the yod; for if not, it is difficult why they were careful in section 5 that the strap not protrude—are not the dalet and yod in the straps complete letters? It is further difficult: the Tur in siman 32 wrote that one should make a small knot in the form of a yod to complete the letters of the Name, and Beit Yosef wrote on this that the anonymous view follows Rashi, but the main [view] is that it is forbidden—up to here; and so wrote Magen Avraham. And one may wonder on him: in Rashi himself it seems in Berachot 23a that it is permitted to enter with arm tefillin; and so wrote Maadanei Melech, 24a and 98a explicitly, that Rashi holds it is permitted. Rather, one must say that even for Rashi, who holds they are letters of the Name Shaddai, nevertheless they are not equal in sanctity, since there is no actual writing like the shin but only by knotting; and so it is explained in Maadanei Melech, 93a. And what he found difficult from that they were careful that the strap not protrude—one may say that certainly they have sanctity, but nevertheless they do not have so much sanctity that one should trouble to remove them; and so it seems in Maadanei Melech, 98a. And in the responsum of Beit Yaakov, siman 170, he wrote a reason to distinguish: head [tefillin] are exposed but arm are covered, and therefore they must not come out from under his hand—for if they come out, they are exposed. But one may wonder: the Rosh wrote in Hilchot Tefillin that Rabbenu Yitzchak of Orleans was uncertain whether one must remove arm tefillin—up to here; and from uncertainty we have to be stringent; and so I found in Sefer Adam veChavah that one should forbid out of uncertainty. Maadanei Melech also wondered on the Tur, who is certain about what his father the Rosh was uncertain about; and it seems to me, in my humble opinion, in resolving this, that the Tur holds Rabbenu Yitzchak of Orleans was not uncertain except when entering a permanent beit hakisei to relieve himself; but when entering to urinate, where the prohibition is only a rabbinic decree lest he relieve himself in them, we are not stringent regarding arm tefillin; and even if we say Rabbenu Yitzchak was uncertain in all cases, nevertheless we do not rule stringently on uncertainty here, since there is only a decree. And this seems to me Rashi’s view, who explained in the Talmud there on “what is the law—may a person enter with tefillin into a permanent beit hakisei to urinate water?”—Rashi explained: he speaks when they are on his head—up to here; and he did not explain on the Talmud stated earlier, “one who enters a beit hakisei removes tefillin,” except specifically here, where it is only a decree, that they are lenient regarding arm tefillin. And now it is also resolved why he warned that the strap not protrude from under his hand—for there it deals with relieving oneself in a permanent beit hakisei; understand this. The words of Rabbenu Yerucham cited above should also be explained thus; but for urination he agrees it is permitted with arm tefillin—so it seems to me, and so it seems to me for practical halacha; and see below, siman 45.
