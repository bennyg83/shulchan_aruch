Upon his head. For the same reason, the prohibition applies equally to tefillin upon his arm. The text mentions those upon his head in order to exclude a case in which he holds the tefillin in his hand. Rabbeinu Yerucham; Magen Avraham. See Nachalas Tzvi. Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 170, writes that the prohibition applies specifically to head tefillin because they are exposed, but arm tefillin are covered and are therefore permitted; they are no worse than tefillin held within one's garment and hand. See there. Yad Aharon disagrees and concludes as practical law that one may enter a privy neither with arm tefillin nor with head tefillin. He concurs with Magen Avraham and Nachalas Tzvi. Bnei Chiya writes likewise. See Tosafos, Menachos 35b, beginning with the words "these are the tefillin upon the head," etc., where Rabbi Yitzchak of Orleans remains uncertain whether one must remove the arm tefillin when entering a privy; see there. See Eliyah Rabbah for an extensive discussion of this matter.
In his hand. This means within his garment and held by his hand opposite his heart, as stated in seif 5. Taz.
While seated. Therefore, when the tefillin are upon his head, he may not even urinate, as a decree lest he defecate.
To defecate. Therefore, in a temporary privy it is permitted, because we do not decree lest he defecate while wearing them.
Four amos. Tur writes likewise. His statement refers to the first case, namely a permanent privy, where it is forbidden to urinate while the tefillin are upon his head; rather, he removes them at a distance of four amos. We consequently learn that in a temporary privy, when the tefillin are in his hand, there is no solution other than giving them to another person. Nevertheless, he need not remove them from his hand at a distance of four amos. Taz; Magen Avraham. Responsa Mateh Yosef, volume 2, siman 5, understood Tur's words, "rather, he removes them at a distance of four amos," as also referring to a temporary privy, and therefore raised numerous difficulties; see there. He did not examine the matter precisely. See Bach.
