On his head. Likewise, with those on his arm it is also forbidden, from the Tur; and that which he specified "on his head" comes to exclude if he holds them in his hand — Rabbeinu Yeruham, Magen Avraham; and see Nekudat HaKesef. And in the responsum of Beit Yaakov, siman 170, he wrote that specifically on his head [it is forbidden], because they are exposed; but those of the hand, which are covered, are permitted, for it is no worse than holding them in his garment and in his hand — see there. And the author Yad Aharon disagrees with him and ruled as halachah that whether of the hand or of the head it is forbidden to enter the privy; and he agreed with Magen Avraham and Nekudat HaKesef; and so too in Sefer Bnei Chayya. And see Tosafot on Menachos 35b, s.v. "these are tefillin on the head," etc., that R. Yitzchak of Orleans was uncertain whether one must remove the hand tefillin when entering the privy — see there. (And see in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah an extended analysis of this.)

In his hand. He meant: in his garment and in his hand, opposite his heart, as written in seif 5. Taz.

While seated. Therefore, when they are on his head, it is forbidden even to urinate — a decree lest he defecate.

For adults. Therefore, in a temporary privy it is permitted, for we do not decree lest he defecate while wearing them.

Four cubits. And so too the Tur; and it refers to the opening passage — namely, in a permanent privy it is forbidden to urinate when they are on his head, but he removes them at a distance of four cubits; and from this we infer that in a temporary privy, when they are in his hand, there is no remedy except to give them to his companion; but nevertheless he need not remove them from his hand at a distance of four cubits — Taz, Magen Avraham. And in the responsum of Mateh Yosef, part 2, siman 5, he understood that what the Tur wrote, "but he removes them at a distance of four cubits," refers also to a temporary privy; and therefore he raised many difficulties — see there — and was not precise. And see Bach.
