Seif 1 — "forbidden." A resolved question.

"And if," etc. There, 2: "Now, in a permanent privy," etc. — meaning, etc.; and see Taz; and so too in the Tur explicitly, that we require [them] in his garment and in his hand; and so in the Gemara.

"And that is," etc. As written above — meaning, in a permanent privy there is no [concern for sparks], etc.; and R. Yitzchak and the Rosh challenged: are there not adults without children? And as written, Rashi, s.v. "but this case," etc.; and one can explain that adults generally defecate while seated, as written in the last chapter of Berachos, and in the first chapter of Tamid, and in Sifrei on Parashat Tetzaveh: "and it shall be on your going out" — and not while standing; and then there are no sparks, as Magen Avraham wrote, and in the end of chapter 2 of Niddah; and this is what he wrote, "and likewise," etc.

"And in a temporary privy," etc. Tosafot there, s.v. "we are concerned," because the question was not raised except regarding a permanent privy.

"But if," etc. There, 2.

"Even if," etc. Rif and Rosh; and so in the Gemara there: "Now, in a permanent privy," etc.

And from the words of the Rambam, etc. As the Tur wrote in the name of Yirei Maimon that there is no resolution in this question regarding a permanent [privy]; and as he wrote, lest one pass wind—and Rav Hai Gaon reads “tzanua,” meaning its end will become permanent. The Tur, the Rosh, and Rabbenu Yerucham elsewhere: in a temporary beit hakisei it is certainly permitted; and regarding this they posed the question—do we decree lest he defecate? For it was difficult to him that the baraita teaches “one who enters the beit hakisei,” etc., implying that because of entering alone one must remove [tefillin]; but the Rosh disagrees—not because of entering, but because he wants to relieve himself, etc.
