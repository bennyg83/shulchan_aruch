Seif 1: It is forbidden. This was an unresolved question that was subsequently resolved.
And if, etc. The Gemara there states: “Now, in a permanent privy, etc.” This is its meaning. See Taz. Rabbeinu Yonah likewise states explicitly that both the garment and the hand are required, and this is also implied by the Gemara.
And this means, etc. As the Gemara explains, the case is a permanent privy where there is no concern, etc. Rabbeinu Yonah and Rosh ask that it is impossible to relieve oneself of solid waste without also urinating, as Rashi explains there. They answer that one relieves himself of solid waste while seated, as stated in the final chapter of Berachos, in the first chapter of Tamid, and in Sifrei, parashas Ki Seitzei: “When you sit,” and not while standing. In that posture there is no concern for droplets, as Magen Avraham explains. See also the beginning of chapter 2 of Niddah. This is the meaning of “and the same applies, etc.”
But in a temporary privy, etc. Tosafos there, in the passage beginning “we are concerned,” derive this from the fact that the question was asked only regarding a permanent privy.
But if, etc. This is stated there on 23b.
Even if, etc. This is the view of Rif and Rosh, and so it appears from the Gemara’s statement, “Now, in a permanent privy, etc.”
From Rambam’s words, etc. Rabbeinu Yonah cites some who do not include the word “permanent” in the Gemara’s question. This corresponds to the concern that he may pass wind. Rav Hai Gaon reads “a secluded privy,” meaning one destined ultimately to become permanent. Rabbeinu Yonah and Rosh explain that an ordinary temporary privy is certainly permitted, and the question concerns this particular case, whether we decree lest he actually relieve himself there. This is because the baraisa states that one entering a privy must remove them, implying that entry alone requires removal. Rosh disagrees and holds that the reason is not the entry itself, but his intention to relieve himself. See seif 5.
