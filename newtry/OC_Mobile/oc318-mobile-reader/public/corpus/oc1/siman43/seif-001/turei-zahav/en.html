And if he holds them. From his wording it appears that in his hand alone suffices, and I did not understand his view — why is he not concerned also in this lest he relieve himself? And we already rule that there is no permission to relieve oneself except when they are wrapped in the right hand and in his garment, as stated in seif 5; and also in the Beit Yosef he wrote, and these are his words: and regarding what we say that tefillin in his hand are permitted in a permanent privy — the Rosh wrote, and you might ask: how is it possible without sparks, etc. This too is puzzling, for in the chapter "One whose dead [relative] is before him" and in Hilchot Ketanot the Rosh mentioned specifically in his garment and hand, and these are his words: and although it is permitted in a permanent privy, as we said above, he holds them in his garment, in his hand, and enters, etc. And on this he wrote there what the Beit Yosef copied in his name: Again I have seen in the Darkhei Moshe, and these are his words: R. Yehuda wrote in chapter 40 that if the tefillin are in his hand it is permitted to enter with them into a permanent privy, and we are not concerned lest he relieve himself with them while they are still in his hand, etc. This too is puzzling, for the Beit Yosef wrote in explanation: since he holds them in his garment and hand, etc. — meaning, even if he relieves himself with them there is no prohibition in this; but if they are in his hand alone — although we are not concerned lest he wipe the sparks on his feet, because he urinates seated — nevertheless we are concerned lest he relieve himself with them. And likewise there is a gloss on the words of R. Yehuda printed according to Maharam of Tiktin, and these are his words: and we do not care even if he certainly relieves himself, since they are gathered in his garment and held in his hand, as explained in the Tur HaAshiri and explicitly in the Sefer HaTerumah, etc. Therefore one must interpret what he wrote here "he holds them in his hand" — meaning in his garment and hand against his heart; and if that is the intent, behold it is a stumbling block before investigators, for regarding the law we have only in his garment and hand against his heart.

Rather in a permanent privy. Explanation: in what they said to permit urinating in a permanent privy, they meant it as if they said seated; but otherwise — to urinate standing — it is surely forbidden even in permanent, lest he wipe the sparks.

Rather he removes them, etc. Likewise the Tur; and it refers to the first clause — namely in a permanent privy it is forbidden to urinate when they are on his head, but he removes them, etc. And from this we infer in a temporary privy that when they are in his hand there is no remedy except to give them to his fellow; but nevertheless he need not remove them from his hand at a distance of four amot, as stated explicitly in the Gemara that the Mishnah brings in this seif, and in the Beit Yosef it is written on the Tur's words here that he did not distinguish between permanent and not permanent; and likewise the Rambam, etc. And their words are puzzling — for it is different for the Rambam, who is stringent when not permanent like permanent, as the Shulchan Aruch wrote nearby; but for the Tur, who permits urinating in temporary when they are on his head — why should he remove at a distance of four amot?
