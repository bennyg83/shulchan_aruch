If he holds them. The wording implies that holding them in his hand alone suffices. I do not understand this view, for why are we not concerned that he may defecate? We have already established that defecation is permitted only when the tefillin are wrapped in his garment and held in his right hand, as stated in se'if 5. Beis Yosef likewise writes: Regarding the statement that tefillin in his hand are permitted in a permanent privy, the Rosh asks how this is possible without droplets, etc. This too is difficult, because in the chapters Mi Shemeiso and HaKomeitz the Rosh specifically mentions both his garment and his hand: "Although it is permitted in a permanent privy, as we stated above, he holds them in his garment and hand and enters," etc. It is upon this that he writes what Beis Yosef cites in his name. I subsequently saw that Darkei Moshe writes: Rabbeinu Yonah, in the chapter Mi Shemeiso, writes that if the tefillin are in his hand he may enter a permanent privy with them, and we are not concerned that he may defecate while they are in his hand. This too is difficult, for Beis Yosef explicitly writes that he holds them in his garment and hand. He means that even if he defecates while holding them, there is no prohibition. If they are merely in his hand, although we are not concerned that he will wipe droplets from his legs because he urinates while seated, we are nevertheless concerned that he may defecate while holding them. There is also a gloss to Rabbeinu Yonah printed according to Rabbi Meir of Tiktin: "It does not matter even if he will certainly defecate, since they are enclosed in his garment and held in his hand. This is implied by Tosafos HaRosh and stated explicitly in Sefer HaTerumah." We must therefore say that "he holds them in his hand" here means in his garment and hand, opposite his heart. If that is the intent, however, the wording presents a stumbling block to readers. As a matter of law, they must be in his garment, in his hand, and opposite his heart.
Except in a permanent privy. The permission to urinate in a permanent privy means, in effect, while seated. Urinating while standing is certainly forbidden even in a permanent privy, lest he wipe away droplets.
Rather, he removes them, etc. Tur writes this, referring back to the opening case of a permanent privy, where urinating while they are on his head is forbidden; rather, he removes them, etc. We consequently understand that in a temporary privy, where it is forbidden when they are in his hand, the only remedy is to give them to another person. Nevertheless, he need not remove them from his hand at a distance of four amos, as is explicit in the Gemara cited in this se'if. Beis Yosef writes regarding Tur's words that he made no distinction between a permanent and temporary privy, and that Rambam writes likewise. This is difficult: Rambam is different, because he is as stringent in a temporary privy as in a permanent one, as Shulchan Aruch writes immediately below. According to Tur, however, who permits urinating in a temporary privy while the tefillin are on his head, why should he remove them four amos away? Rather, the explanation is as I wrote.
