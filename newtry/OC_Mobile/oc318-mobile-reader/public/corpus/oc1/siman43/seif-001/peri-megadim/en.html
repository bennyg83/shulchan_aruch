And if. See Taz. The master, of blessed memory, took issue with the Mechaber. Here in seif 1, the words 'he holds them in his hand' imply without a garment, whereas in seif 5 he rules that they must be in his garment and his hand, in accordance with R' Akiva (Berachos 23a). One must therefore distinguish: when defecating, a garment is also required; but when urinating in a permanent privy, we do not decree lest he defecate, because even if he does defecate the degradation is not so great. They decreed specifically when the tefillin are on his head, because that is a great degradation. This is also the view of Darkei Moshe, who cites Rabbeinu Yonah that in a permanent privy, when the tefillin are in his hand, we are not concerned that he will defecate, and cites this to show that no garment is required. In truth, however, one who examines Rabbeinu Yonah sees explicitly that a garment is required lest he defecate. Maharam of Tiktin likewise writes this in his gloss there. Accordingly, the Rama's statement in the gloss that there is no distinction at all between a permanent and a temporary place, and that it is merely the ordinary manner for one to be seated and the other standing, follows his own approach in Darkei Moshe; this is unlike what appears from Rabbeinu Yonah. The Rosh in Berachos explicitly writes that in a permanent privy one may urinate while holding the tefillin in his hand and garment. This is unlike Beit Yosef's statement that regarding the permission to hold them 'in his hand,' the Rosh writes that this is incorrect because the halacha follows R' Akiva. Even because of the concern that he may defecate, both his garment and his hand are required. There is consequently a distinction between a permanent and temporary place: in a temporary place, when seated (or on loose earth or a slope) his hand alone suffices; in a permanent place, his garment is also required lest he defecate while holding them. This is the substance of the master's intent. See Nechalat Tzvi, seif 5, who writes likewise and also cites Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilchot Tefillin, halacha 17, that to urinate in a permanent privy he also needs his garment; see there. Know that even according to the Rama's approach in the gloss and Darkei Moshe, under which there is no distinction between a temporary and permanent place as stated, there is nevertheless a distinction. In a permanent place, when holding them in his hand, he must specifically use his right hand, as in seif 5, because of wiping. In a temporary place, when seated, there are no droplets and no concern that he will defecate, and he may therefore hold them even in his left hand. If you argue that they did not enact such a remote decree solely because of wiping, just as they did not decree against holding them merely in his hand according to the Rama's approach in Darkei Moshe, this is mistaken. Wiping with the hand that holds the tefillin is certainly a greater degradation than defecating while they are on his head. See Rabbeinu Yonah: it is obvious that droplets reaching the tefillin, and certainly wiping, constitute a great degradation. Bach, of blessed memory, guarded against this difficulty. He writes that one remembers everything held in his hand, and therefore we are not concerned that he will forget, defecate, and wipe with his left hand. If he defecates, he will remember and transfer them to his right hand. When they are upon his head, however, he may forget that he is wearing them; see there. Taz's words 'and so Beit Yosef writes' are a scribal error and should read 'Rabbeinu Yonah.' What he cites in the name of Darkei Moshe does not appear alongside the Tur editions except in the Fürth printing; see there. This is what appears correct to me here.
