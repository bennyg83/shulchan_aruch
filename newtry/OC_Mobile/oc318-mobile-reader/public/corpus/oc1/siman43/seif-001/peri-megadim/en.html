And if — the Taz: Behold, the master (z"l) disputed with our author the master in what he wrote in seif 1 — here "he holds them in his hand" implies without a garment, and in seif 5 he ruled with his garment and hand like Rabbi Akiva (Berachot 23a). And necessarily one must distinguish: for defecation one also needs a garment; but for urinating in a permanent privy — because of "lest he relieve himself" — if he does relieve himself, it is not so disgraceful; and only when on his head did they decree, for the disgrace is great. And likewise is the view of the Maggid Mishneh, who brought the words of Rabbeinu Yonah that in a permanent privy, when in his hand, we are not concerned lest he relieve himself while they are in his hand — and he brought this that one needs his garment. But in truth one who examines Rabbeinu Yonah's commentary explicitly finds there that his garment is required lest he relieve himself; likewise Maharam of Tiktin in the gloss there. Therefore what Rama wrote in the gloss that there is no distinction between permanent and temporary at all — only the usual manner, that one is seated and one standing — is according to her view in the Maggid Mishneh; not as appears from Rabbeinu Yonah and the Rosh in Berachot, who wrote explicitly that in a permanent privy, in his hand and garment, it is permitted to urinate (unlike Beit Yosef, who wrote: and regarding what we say "in his hand it is permitted," the Rosh wrote that it is not so, for the halachah follows Rabbi Akiva — and also for "lest he relieve himself" one needs his garment and hand). If so, there is a difference between permanent and temporary: for in temporary, seated (or sloped ground and incline), in his hand alone is permitted; and in permanent, his garment too is required lest he relieve himself with them. This is the content of the master's intent; see Netziv, siman 5, likewise. And he also brought the words of the Rambam (z"l) in chapter 4 of Hilchot Tefillin 17: to urinate in a permanent privy one also needs his garment; see there. And know that even according to Rama's view in the gloss and the Maggid Mishneh — and therefore there is no difference between temporary and permanent, as we say — nevertheless there is a difference: for in permanent, in his hand, specifically the right hand is required, as in seif 5 where he holds them specifically in the right hand because of wiping; but in temporary, seated, there are no sparks and also no "lest he relieve himself," and it is permitted even in the left hand, etc. And you might call it a distant decree merely because of wiping alone — they did not decree, just as they did not decree on the hand alone (according to Rama's view in the Maggid Mishneh): here one wipes with the hand in which the tefillin are, which is surely more disgraceful than when they are on his head (see Rabbeinu Yonah — sparks reach the tefillin; it is obvious; in wiping the disgrace is great). Nevertheless the Bach (z"l) was careful about this and wrote: everything in his hand he remembers and is mindful of, and we are not concerned lest he forget and relieve himself and wipe with the left — for if he relieves himself he will remember and take them in the right — unlike on his head, where he forgets whether he has them on his head; see there. (What the Taz wrote, and likewise Beit Yosef, letter 9 — and it should read Rabbeinu Yonah. What he wrote in the name of the Maggid Mishneh is not in the Tur except in the Fürth edition; see there.) This is what appears correct to me here.
