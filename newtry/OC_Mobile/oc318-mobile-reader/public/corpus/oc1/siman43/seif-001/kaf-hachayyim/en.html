1) [Seif 1] It is forbidden to enter a permanent privy to urinate while the tefillin are upon his head, etc. For the same reason, it is likewise forbidden when they are upon his arm. Rabbeinu Yerucham and the halachic authorities write accordingly. The text mentions those upon his head in order to exclude a case in which he holds them in his hand. Magen Avraham, seif katan 1. Nachalas Tzvi writes likewise, as cited by Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur. Yad Aharon agrees and expresses surprise at Beis Yaakov, siman 170, who permits arm tefillin because they are covered; see there. This also appears from Solet Belulah in Mekor Chochmah, paragraph 1. Bnei Chiya writes likewise, as cited by Be'er Heitev, seif katan 1; Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1. This also appears from Chayei Adam, rule 14, paragraph 26, and Shitilei Zeitim, paragraph 2, writes likewise. In particular, according to the Arizal in Shaar HaKavanos, the fifth discourse on tefillin, even the tefillin housings allude to supernal lights and Names; see there. One should therefore be stringent, contrary to Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1, and Erekh HaShulchan, paragraph 1. See Ateres Zekeinim.
2) There: If he holds them in his hand, etc. This means that they are within his garment and held by his hand opposite his heart, as stated in seif 5. Beis Yosef writes this in the name of Sefer HaTerumah, and Taz, seif katan 1, writes likewise. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Ateres Zekeinim, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3; Shitilei Zeitim, paragraph 2.
3) There, in the gloss: This applies specifically when he urinates while seated, etc. This appears from the words of Rosh and the other halachic authorities cited by Maran in Beis Yosef. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1. Levush writes likewise, as does Maharikash in Erekh Lechem. Pri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 2, and Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3; Shitilei Zeitim, paragraph 4; Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 3.
4) There, in the gloss: The same applies if he urinates while seated, etc. The correct text is: "The same applies if he urinates while standing upon a high place," meaning on a slope, "or upon loose soil, where there is no," etc. See above, siman 3, seif 13. Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 4.
5) There, in the gloss: In a permanent privy, a person ordinarily relieves himself while seated. This means that when the tefillin are upon his head, he is therefore forbidden even to urinate, as a decree lest he defecate. Magen Avraham, seif katan 2. It is forbidden even if he urinates while standing, and the same prohibition applies when the tefillin are in his hand. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2.
6) There, in the gloss: In a temporary privy, a person ordinarily urinates while standing, etc. Therefore, in a temporary privy it is permitted, because we do not decree lest he defecate while wearing them. Magen Avraham, seif katan 3; Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, paragraph 3.
7) There: In a temporary privy one may urinate while the tefillin are upon his head. This applies whether he urinates while standing or seated. We do not decree lest he defecate while wearing them, since the place is not permanent, nor do we fear that he will rub away droplets while touching them, since they are upon his head. Levush; Shitilei Zeitim, paragraph 6. With arm tefillin, however, it is forbidden to urinate while standing because of droplets. We are concerned that he may rub them away with his left hand among the wrapped straps. Rubbing away droplets may require even the right hand, and all the more so the left, and is forbidden. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, paragraph 3.
8) There: If he holds them in his hand, it is forbidden to urinate while standing, etc. This refers both to a temporary and to a permanent privy, as stated in the gloss. Magen Avraham, seif katan 4; Shitilei Zeitim, paragraph 7.
9) There: Rather, he removes them at a distance of four amos, etc. This refers to a permanent privy, where it is forbidden to urinate while the tefillin are upon his head. In a temporary privy, however, one may urinate while they are upon his head. If they are in his hand, even in a temporary privy he must give them to another person, but he need not do so at a distance of four amos. Taz, seif katan 3; Magen Avraham, seif katan 4; Shitilei Zeitim, paragraph 9. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 2, however, writes that if they are in his hand, even in a temporary privy he must give them to another person at a distance of four amos; see there. See Pri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 3, and our comments below, paragraph 18.
