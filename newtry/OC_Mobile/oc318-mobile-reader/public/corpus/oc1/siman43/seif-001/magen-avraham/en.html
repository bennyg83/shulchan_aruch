"Tefillin on his head" — the same applies on his arm; it is forbidden for the same reason. So too Ri and the poskim. And that which he specified "on his head" comes to exclude when he holds them in his hand.

"Presumably he relieves himself" — meaning, and therefore when they are on his head it is forbidden even to urinate — a decree lest he relieve himself.

"Since he does not" etc. — therefore in a temporary bathroom it is permitted, for we do not decree lest he defecate in them.

"He removes them at a distance of four amot" — at first glance it implies that even when one wishes to urinate in a temporary bathroom one must remove them at a distance of four amot. And it requires investigation: for even when they are on his head it is permitted to urinate, and in his hand the prohibition is only because of rubbing — so why should he remove them at a distance of four amot? And also in Ri and in the Rambam it implies he removes them and urinates immediately. Therefore it seems that "it is forbidden to urinate in them while standing" etc. deals with both temporary and permanent, as stated in the gloss; and "rather he removes them at a distance of four amot" deals with permanent. And see seif 5. And this is clear from what is taught "he removes them" — if so, necessarily it deals with permanent, for in temporary it is permitted to urinate when they are on his head, as above.

"Whether in a temporary bathroom" — for they decreed lest he pass wind in them, as is found in the Gemara; and so too in Shulchan Gavoah, and this is clear.
