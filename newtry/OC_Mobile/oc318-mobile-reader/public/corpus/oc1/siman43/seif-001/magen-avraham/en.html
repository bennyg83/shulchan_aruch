He presumably relieves himself. Therefore, when the tefillin are on his head, even urinating is forbidden as a decree lest he defecate while wearing them.
Since it is not, etc. Therefore, it is permitted at a temporary privy, where we do not decree lest he defecate while wearing them.
He removes them four amos away. At first glance this implies that even when he wishes to urinate at a temporary privy, he must remove them four amos away. This requires examination, since he may urinate there even with them on his head, and when holding them the only concern is wiping droplets; why then remove them four amos away? Rabbeinu Yerucham and the Remezim likewise imply that he removes them and urinates immediately. It therefore appears that the phrase 'it is forbidden to urinate while wearing them standing, etc.' applies both to temporary and permanent privies, as the gloss says, whereas 'rather, he removes them four amos away' concerns a permanent privy. See what I write in se'if 5. This is also implied by the instruction to remove them, since at a temporary privy he may urinate while wearing them on his head, as explained.
