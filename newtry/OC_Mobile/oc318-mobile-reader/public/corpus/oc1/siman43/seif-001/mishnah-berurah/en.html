(1) To urinate. Magen Avraham 9 writes that the Mechaber states this as the greater novelty: even entering to urinate, although it is a vital human need, is forbidden; certainly one may not enter unnecessarily.
(2) With the tefillin on his head. For the same reason it is also forbidden when they are on his arm. The reference to his head excludes where he holds them in his hand.
(3) In his hand. Meaning wrapped in his garment and held in his right hand opposite his heart, as written in se'if 5.
(4) It is obvious that it is forbidden. When holding them in his hand, lest he wipe away droplets. The same prohibition applies when they are on his head, because we fear he may sit and relieve himself while wearing them [Pri Megadim].
(5) Loose earth. Or a sloping place.
(6) Is also permitted, and there is no distinction, etc. Nevertheless, there is a minor distinction. In a permanent privy, where we fear he may relieve himself, the tefillin must be wrapped in his garment and held in his hand, as in paragraph 3. At a temporary place, holding them in his hand alone is permitted, since there is no concern about droplets.
(7) And in a temporary privy, etc. We do not decree there lest he relieve himself while wearing them; he need only take care not to pass wind while wearing them.
(8) Because he must, etc. We fear he might use the hand holding the tefillin to wipe away droplets that fall upon his legs.
(9) He removes them at a distance, etc. Magen Avraham explains that the Mechaber's opening prohibition against urinating while wearing them applies both at a temporary and permanent privy, as stated in the gloss. But the statement that he removes them four amos away concerns a permanent privy; at a temporary one he removes them and urinates immediately. Taz agrees.
(10) To another person. He himself may not hold them then. Although the case is a permanent privy and se'if 5 permits holding them wrapped in his garment and in his hand, here he urinates standing and we are concerned about droplets.
(11) When they are on his head. The same applies when they are on his arm, because this view decrees lest he pass wind while wearing them. If they are in their pouch and he holds the pouch, everyone permits urinating, because passing wind is not forbidden while they are enclosed, as explained in siman 44.
(12) According to his words. Where necessary, such as when removing them would cause him to miss communal prayer, he may rely on the first opinion, but must be extremely careful not to pass wind. So writes Chayei Adam. Nevertheless, the practice is to remove the head-tefillin. It is also proper to remove the arm-tefillin straps from the palm, lest he wipe droplets with them.
