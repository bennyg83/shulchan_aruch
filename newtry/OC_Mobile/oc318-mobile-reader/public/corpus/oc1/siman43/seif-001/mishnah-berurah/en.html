(1) To urinate — the Magen Avraham in siman katan 9 wrote that that which the Mechaber wrote "to urinate" was specified for emphasis: even to urinate, although it is a person's life, entry is forbidden — and all the more so not for a need.

(2) Tefillin on his head — the same applies on his arm; it is forbidden for the same reason. And that which he specified "on his head" comes to exclude when he holds them in his hand.

(3) In his hand — meaning in his garment and in his right hand opposite his heart, as stated in seif 5.

(4) It is obvious that it is forbidden — when holding them in his hand, lest he wipe the splashing droplets; and the same when they are on his head — it is forbidden, for we are concerned lest he sit and relieve himself in them [Peri Megadim].

(5) Soft earth — or in a sloped place.

(6) Also permitted, and there is no distinction, etc. — nevertheless there is a slight distinction: in a permanent privy, since there is concern lest he defecate, they must be in his garment and in his hand, as stated in s"k 3; but in a temporary one, even in his hand alone is also permitted in this regard, since there is no concern about sparks.

(7) And in a temporary privy, etc. — for we did not decree about it lest he relieve himself with them; only that he take care not to pass wind on them.

(8) Because it is necessary, etc. — and we are concerned lest he rub the sparks that fall on his feet with the hand holding the tefillin.

(9) He removes them at a distance, etc. — see Magen Avraham, who wrote that what the author wrote at the beginning — that it is forbidden to urinate in them while standing — applies both in temporary and permanent privies, as stated in the gloss; and what he wrote, "rather he removes them at a distance of four amot," applies in a permanent privy, for in a temporary privy he removes and urinates immediately; likewise the Taz.

(10) To his fellow — for he himself is forbidden to hold them; so even though it concerns a permanent privy, and as explained below in seif 5 that he holds them in his garment and hand — since it concerns urinating while standing, we are concerned about sparks.

(11) When they are on his head — and the same applies to his arm, for he holds that we decree lest he pass wind on them; but when they are in their pouch and he holds the pouch in his hand, it is permitted to urinate in them, for when they are in the pouch there is no prohibition of passing wind, as will be explained in siman 44.

(12) According to his words — and in time of need, such as when public prayer would be cancelled thereby, one may rely on the first view, but he must take great care lest he come to pass wind; likewise the Chayei Adam. Nevertheless, the custom is to remove the head tefillin. Also the arm tefillin should properly have the straps removed from the palm so he does not come to rub sparks with them.
