He removes them. See Taz. The Tur writes in the Mechaber's language, specifically referring to a permanent place, in accordance with Rav Acha. Rif and Rosh do not have the word 'permanent' in either baraita. Nevertheless, they hold that Beit Hillel and R' Akiva disagree with Beit Shammai and maintain that a distance of four cubits is unnecessary; R' Meyasha's ruling follows Beit Hillel and R' Akiva. If so, four cubits are unnecessary even at a permanent place. The Tur, however, was concerned for the other anonymous baraita, and so forth. Support can be given to Taz's words. Rif's text reads: 'The Sages taught' (this is the other baraita) 'He removes them four cubits away and enters; these are the words of Beit Shammai, etc.' Rif omits the instruction to place them in a window adjacent to the public domain. In the Gemara one can say that they disagree over whether he should place them in a window in the public domain and not bring them inside: Beit Hillel says that he brings them in and does not leave them in the public domain. According to Rif, however, it apparently follows necessarily that the dispute between Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel is whether he must remove them four cubits away. If one objects that since Rif and Rosh do not read 'permanent' in the other baraita, how do we know that the preceding baraita is not authoritative and that the response 'Who is its author? Beit Shammai' is decisive? Perhaps Beit Hillel and R' Akiva discuss a permanent place, in accordance with Rav Acha above, whereas the other baraita concerns a nonpermanent place, where removing them four cubits away is unnecessary, also in accordance with Rav Acha. One can answer that otherwise, what would be Beit Shammai's reason in a nonpermanent place? It is certainly logical that no four-cubit distance is necessary for removing them, and no one would disagree with this. There are three difficulties with the Mechaber. First, why specify a permanent place? The same applies to a nonpermanent place that he now intends to use as permanent. Second, why specify defecating? Even to urinate he must remove them four cubits away, as stated in seif 1, which refers to a permanent place, as Magen Avraham and Taz, se'if katan 3, write. Third, he writes that one rolls them in their straps. This is Rashi's explanation there, s.v. 'he rolls them,' and follows Beit Hillel, as Rashi writes s.v. 'and holds them in his right hand'; not in his garment. According to us, however, who rule like R' Akiva, one can say that he rolls them in a garment. See Nechalat Tzvi, seif 4. Moreover, regarding the support I suggested for Taz from Rif; that Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel disagree only over whether removal must take place four cubits away; which would imply that even Beit Shammai permits bringing them into the privy in his hand: how can the Gemara then say, 'It must be that the one who said this follows Beit Shammai'? It is explicit that according to Beit Shammai it is necessarily forbidden to bring them into the privy. If so, how do we know that they disagree about two matters? Perhaps Beit Hillel and R' Akiva also hold that they must be removed four cubits away. This requires further study.
