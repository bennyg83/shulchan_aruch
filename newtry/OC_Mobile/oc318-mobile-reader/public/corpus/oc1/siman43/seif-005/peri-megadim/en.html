He removes them — the Taz: the Tur wrote in the author's wording, specifically permanent, and like Rav Acha, Rif, and Rosh, who do not read "permanent" in two baraitot — nevertheless they hold that Beit Hillel and Rabbi Akiva disagree with Beit Shammai and hold that four amot are not required, and Rabbi Meyasha: the halachah is like Beit Hillel and Rabbi Akiva — if so, four amot are not required even in permanent; and the Tur was concerned for the other baraita stated anonymously, etc. And behold, there is seasoning for the words of the Taz: for the Rif reads the baraita (it is the other tannaitic source): he removes at a distance of four amot and enters — these are the words of Beit Shammai, etc. — and omitted that which places them in a window adjoining the public domain; and in the Gemara one may say they disagree whether when placing them in a window in the public domain and not bringing them in — Beit Hillel said: he brings them in and does not place them in the public domain — unlike the Rif, who apparently holds the dispute between Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel is whether he removes at a distance of four amot. And if it is difficult: since the Rif and Rosh do not read "permanent" (in the other baraita), who will say the baraita above is not halachah? And one may answer you: it is Beit Shammai — perhaps Beit Hillel and Rabbi Akiva speak of permanent, and like Rav Acha above; and the other baraita is when not permanent — therefore one need not remove at a distance of four amot; and Rav Acha one may say holds that if so, what is Beit Shammai's reason in not permanent? Surely the reasoning is that four amot are required to remove, and no one will disagree on this. And on the author it is difficult in three ways. First: why specify permanent — even temporary, and one who now makes it permanent, removes? Second: why specify his needs — even to urinate he also removes at a distance of four amot, as stated in seif 1, and it refers to permanent, as Magen Avraham and Taz wrote, letter 3. Third: in what he wrote "he rolls them in their straps" — this is Rashi's explanation there on "he rolls them," and he holds like Beit Hillel, as he wrote on "and holds them in his right hand" — not in his garment; but for us, who rule like Rabbi Akiva, one may say he rolls them in a garment; see Netziv, siman 4. Also what he wrote that there is seasoning for the words of the Taz from the Rif — that Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel disagree only whether one must remove at a distance of four amot, which implies that for Beit Shammai too it is permitted to bring them in his hand — if so, what is this that he said "one may answer you: it is Beit Shammai"? Behold explicitly for Beit Shammai it is forbidden by necessity to bring them into a privy — if so, from where does he know they disagree in two matters? And one might say Beit Hillel and Rabbi Akiva also hold one removes at a distance of four amot — it is questionable.
