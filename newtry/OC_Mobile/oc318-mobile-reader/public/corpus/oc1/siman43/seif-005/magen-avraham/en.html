"And some say: even" etc. — it implies that per the Rosh it is permitted to enter wearing tefillin into a bathroom without relieving himself. And there is a difficulty: is it preferable to a bathhouse, where one removes them, as stated in siman 45 seif 2? On the contrary, a bathroom is stricter, for in a new bathhouse one may recite Keriat Shema, but in a new bathroom one may not recite Keriat Shema. This difficulty I understood from the Shulchan Aruch manuscript of the Gaon, author of the Shakh, who wrote, and see siman 45 seif 2. Therefore it seems that certainly for all it is forbidden to enter a bathroom wearing tefillin not to urinate; and they did not ask except whether entry to urinate is permitted, since it is a person's life — and it is simple to him that it is forbidden, lest he defecate in them. And that which the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in seif 1 stated "it is forbidden to enter" etc. "to urinate" — they specified for emphasis: even to urinate entry is forbidden, and all the more so not for a need. Know that one must remove them at a distance of four amot; and per Rashi the reason is because of entering, as stated in seif 1. But in a bathhouse it is forbidden to enter wearing tefillin, for when bathing he in any case removes his garments; therefore the Tur did not bring Rav Hai's view, for it is an obvious matter. And see seifim 7 and 8, and investigate.

"In his right hand" — but not in his left, because he needs to wipe with it, as stated in siman 3 seif 10.

"The strap" — for in the strap too there is holiness, since God's Name is fixed in them. Beit Yosef wrote: and upon entering a permanent bathroom our master did not write that one gives them to a friend; and it appears his reason, etc., that it is a mitzvah to guard them oneself, end quote. But in Shulchan Aruch it deals with one who wishes to urinate while standing in a permanent bathroom; if so, it is impossible to bring them in his hand because he must rub, and on his head it is forbidden lest he defecate in them; therefore he must give them to a friend. And here it deals with one who relieves himself while seated, who may hold them in his hand to give them to a friend, as stated above. And in a temporary bathroom it is permitted to urinate when they are on his head. The Magen Avraham wrote in responsum siman 59: it is permitted to enter wearing tefillin into filthy alleys, even if there is an explicitly filthy place, and it is good to cover them with a hat; and if he wishes to remove them and return and don them, there is doubt, end quote. And this is clear in Berakhot 24: Rabbi Abahu would go and recite Keriat Shema; when he reached filthy alleys he would be silent — and surely he did not recite Keriat Shema without tefillin; if so, he should have had to remove tefillin. Rather, necessarily it is permitted to enter wearing them in filthy alleys. However, one may say: in any case, when he knows he will pass through filthy alleys, he should not leave them in his house but in the synagogue. And greater than this: on Shabbat 127 — Rabbi Yehoshua did not wish to enter wearing tefillin into a gentile's house; he said, let words of holiness not enter a place of impurity. And in Beit Eliyahu he wrote that one should leave them in his house and cover them in filthy alleys.
