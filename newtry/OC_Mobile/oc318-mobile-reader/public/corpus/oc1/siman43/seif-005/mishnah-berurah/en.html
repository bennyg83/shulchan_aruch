(18) To a permanent privy. 'Permanent' is stated because of the latter clause, teaching that he may bring the tefillin with him even there for safekeeping. In truth, even at a completely nonpermanent place he must remove them four amos from where he intends to defecate, as above at the end of paragraph 13. Similarly, 'to relieve himself' is stated for the novelty of the latter clause; in truth, even to urinate he must remove them four amos away, as above in se'if 1.
(19) And it is proper to be stringent. Magen Avraham concludes that by law one may not enter a privy wearing tefillin, for it is no better than a bathhouse, below in siman 45:2. Pri Megadim and Maamar Mordechai write that Rosh's words do not imply this, nor do Rav Hai's. Eshkol, in the name of the Gaon, does imply Magen Avraham's resolution; see there.
(20) In his right hand. Not his left, which he needs for wiping, as in se'if 3. There is no concern here that he might wipe droplets with the hand holding the tefillin, since the case is a permanent privy, where one presumably relieves himself seated. Responsa Rema MiPano 59 permits entering filthy alleyways wearing tefillin even where an openly filthy spot is present, though it is good to cover them with one's hat; one may instead remove and later replace them. Magen Avraham inclines to say that one who knows he will pass through filthy alleyways should put them on at the synagogue rather than at home, or put them on at home and cover them in the alleyways. Machatzis HaShekel is uncertain whether the straps must also be covered, especially the three windings on the finger. Responsa Radbaz IV:36 likewise implies that by law tefillin must be covered in filthy places; if one cannot cover them, it is preferable to put them on at the synagogue. The dalet and yud formed by the straps need not be covered, since they are formed by tying rather than writing.
