(18) To a permanent privy — he specified permanent because of the latter clause, to inform us that even in a permanent one he brings the tefillin with him for safekeeping; but otherwise — that even when it is not permanent at all — he must remove them at a distance of four amot from the place where he wishes to relieve himself, as above at the end of s"k 13. And likewise what he specified for defecation is also for emphasis in the latter clause; but in truth the same applies to urinating — he must remove them at a distance of four amot, as above in seif 1.

(19) And it is good to be stringent — Magen Avraham concludes that by law it is forbidden to enter a latrine with tefillin, for it is no less than a bathhouse, below siman 45 seif 2; see Peri Megadim and Maamar Mordechai, who wrote that from the words of the Rosh it does not appear so, likewise from Rav Hai Gaon; see there. And in Sefer HaEshkol in the name of the Gaon it appears as the Magen Avraham's answer; see there.

(20) In his right hand — but not in his left, because he must wipe with it, as stated in s"k 3; and here there is no concern about sparks that he will rub with the hand holding the tefillin, since it concerns a permanent privy where he presumably relieves himself seated. The Ram"a wrote in siman 59 that it is permitted to enter with tefillin into filthy alleys even where there is explicitly a filthy place, and it is good to cover them with a hat; and if he wishes to remove them and return to don them, that is fine. The Magen Avraham leans to say that when he knows he will pass through filthy alleys, he should not leave them in his house but in the synagogue, or leave them in his house and cover them in the filthy alleys. Machatzit HaShekel was unsure that perhaps one must also cover the straps, and especially the three windings on the finger. In responsa of the Radbaz, part 4 siman 36, it also appears that he holds that by law one must cover the tefillin in unclean places; and whoever cannot cover them, it is better to leave them in the synagogue. It appears there that the dalet-lamed and yod on the straps need not be covered, as they are through tying and not through writing; see there.
