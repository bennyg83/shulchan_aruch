He removes them, etc. The Gemara in the chapter Mi Shemeiso states: Rabbi Acha said in the name of Rav Sheshes that this was taught only regarding a permanent privy, but in a temporary privy he removes them and immediately relieves himself; when he puts them back on, he moves four amos away, because he has made the place into a permanent privy. Beis Yosef writes that since Tur, Rif, and the Rosh do not cite this, it follows that the halachah does not follow it, and he requires explanation why. I am surprised by this. There is plainly no question on Tur, for Tur (and Shulchan Aruch after him) explicitly discusses a permanent privy. We consequently know that in a temporary privy there is no need to remove them four amos away. The Gemara had to make the distinction only because the baraisa there states without qualification that one entering a privy removes them four amos away. Although Rif and the Rosh do not qualify that baraisa as permanent and nevertheless omit Rav Sheshes's statement, this too presents no difficulty. In their view, even at a permanent privy one need not remove them four amos away. They cite as the final halachah Beis Hillel's view that he holds them in his hand and enters, and Rabbi Akiva's view that he holds them in his garment and hand and enters. Rabbi Meyasha, son of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, concludes that the halachah is to roll them like a scroll and hold them in his right hand, etc. It follows that one need not remove them four amos away; otherwise he should have ruled that one removes them four amos away and rolls them, thereby deciding against Beis Hillel and like Rabbi Akiva on that point. Clearly, his ruling does not address bringing them in at all, for one need not move four amos away before entering. Tur alone takes account of the stringent view that initially requires a distance of four amos, following the other anonymous baraisa there, and he explicitly explains that it applies only to a permanent privy.
He holds them in his right hand. Beis Yosef, citing Nimukei Yosef and Mordechai, explains that one cleans himself with the left hand after relieving himself, as stated above in siman 3. He therefore may not hold the tefillin in his left hand. One might ask how it is possible to be in a privy without droplets, since defecation cannot occur without urination. The answer is that the case concerns a permanent privy, where he relieves himself while seated and the urine is fully discharged from the body, so there are no droplets. Rosh writes this. We learn that urinating while standing is more stringent than defecating.
So that the strap not be, etc. The straps possess sanctity, for the tefillin are tied with them, and their knot is fashioned in the form of a dalet, one of the letters of the Name Shaddai. The shin is formed in the walls of the outer compartment, and the yud is formed at the end of the arm-tefillin strap. Rashi.
