He removes them, etc. In the Gemara, chapter 40: on this Rav Acha said in the name of Rav Sheshet: there is no [distinction] except in a permanent privy; but in a temporary privy he removes and relieves himself immediately, and when he places them he distances four amot because he made it a permanent privy. And as the Beit Yosef — since he did not bring this in the Tur, Rif, and Rosh — it appears we do not rule thus; and it is questionable why, etc. And I am puzzled at his words — for regarding the Tur it is simple according to our reasoning, for it is written explicitly in the Tur and afterward in the Shulchan Aruch here that it concerns a permanent privy; from this we know that in temporary he need not remove at a distance of four amot, and the Gemara did not need to divide on this except for the baraita taught there anonymously: one who enters a privy removes at a distance of four amot — and although the Rif and Rosh did not mention permanent in the baraita, nevertheless they did not bring this of Rav Sheshet; also according to them, even in permanent one need not remove at a distance of four amot, for they brought in conclusion for halachah the words of Beit Hillel, who said: he holds them in his hand and enters; and Rabbi Akiva, who says: he holds them in his garment and hand and enters — and as Rabbi Meyasha son of R. Yehudah concluded: the halachah is: he rolls them like a scroll and holds them in his right hand, etc. — from which it appears one need not remove at a distance of four amot; for otherwise he should have said: the halachah is: he removes at a distance of four amot and rolls them, etc., to decide not like Beit Hillel and Rabbi Akiva on this. Rather it is clear he did not come to disagree on entering at all, that one need not distance four amot before entering; only the Tur was concerned to be stringent also regarding distancing four amot first, like the other anonymous baraita there — and he explains explicitly that it refers specifically to permanent.

And he holds them in his right hand. The reason in the Beit Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah and Ma'or: with the left he wipes himself after relieving himself, as above siman 3; therefore it is forbidden to hold the tefillin in the left. And you might ask: how is it possible in a privy without sparks? And is it not impossible for adults without children? One may say: since it concerns a permanent privy, he relieves himself seated and urine falls away from the body, and there are no sparks — likewise the Rosh. We learn that urinating while standing is stricter than defecating for adults.

That a strap should not, etc. That there is holiness in them, for with them he ties them; and in the knot it appears in his making like a kind of dalet — see there: one of the letters of Shadai; and the shin is made in the walls of the outer compartment, and the yod is made at the head of the strap of the arm tefillin — likewise Rashi.
