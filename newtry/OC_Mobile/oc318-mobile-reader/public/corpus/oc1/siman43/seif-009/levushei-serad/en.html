(ibid.) And it is also forbidden to urinate in it while wearing tefillin on one's head at all — these are the words of the Magen Avraham, who raises two difficulties. First: Rashi does not distinguish regarding a pit, but rather according to his view that removing tefillin four amot away is because of entering and not because of relieving oneself — unlike the Rosh and Beit Yosef, for whom four amot is because he intends to defecate, and this applies even in a temporary bathroom; so all the more so in a pit. And now he raises a second difficulty: that which Beit Yosef understood in Rashi's words — that it is permitted to urinate in head tefillin when they are in a pit — is not so at all, even according to Rashi; for in the end, since it is a permanent bathroom, there is concern lest he defecate in them, and this is the main reason for the prohibition in a permanent bathroom, end of quote.
