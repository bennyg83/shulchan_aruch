1) Se'if 1: When reciting blessings, he should intend the meaning of the words. The same applies to all his blessings and prayers. Otherwise a person resembles a chirping bird that does not understand its own sounds. Levush. So too write Beit Oved, Hilchos Netilas Yadayim, section 9; Shesilei Zeisim, section 1; and Yafeh LaLev, part 1, section 1. Sefer Chassidim, siman 46, states: When one washes his hands, blesses over fruit, or recites the blessings over mitzvos familiar to everyone, he should direct his heart to bless his Creator, Who has shown him wondrous kindness by giving him fruit or bread to enjoy and commanding him regarding the mitzvos. He should not act like one who does something by habit and utters words without thought. Hashem became angry with His people over this and sent Yeshayahu to say: 'Since this people approaches with its mouth and honors Me with its lips, but has distanced its heart from Me, and its fear of Me is a commandment of men learned by rote.' The Holy One, blessed is He, said to Yeshayahu: See the conduct of My children; it is merely external. They maintain it as one mechanically preserves ancestral custom. They enter My house and recite the fixed prayers before Me according to their ancestors' custom, but not wholeheartedly. They clean their hands and recite the blessing over washing; break bread and recite HaMotzi; drink and bless; all because the words are fluent in their mouths. Even while blessing, they do not intend to bless Me. Therefore His anger was kindled, and He swore by His great Name to destroy the wisdom of the sages who know Him yet bless Him habitually without intent, as the following verse says: 'Therefore, behold, I will continue, etc., and the wisdom of its wise men shall be lost, etc.' The Sages therefore warned about this and said: Perform deeds for the sake of the One Who made them. There was an incident involving a man who died and, after twelve months, appeared in a dream to a relative. He said: Every day I am judged because I was not careful to recite HaMotzi, blessings over fruit, and Birkas HaMazon with heartfelt intent. They asked him: Is the judgment of the wicked in Gehinnom not limited to twelve months? He answered: I am not subjected to punishments as severe as during the first twelve months. Chayei Adam, klal 5, section 26, cites this, as do Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 6:1, and Yafeh LaLev there. Kitzur Shulchan Aruch writes that one should accustom himself to recite blessings aloud, because the voice awakens concentration.
