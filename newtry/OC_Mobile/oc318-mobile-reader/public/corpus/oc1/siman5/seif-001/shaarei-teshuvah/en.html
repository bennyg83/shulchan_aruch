With yud-hei. See Baer Heitev. Machazik Berachah cites Luach Eresh of R' Yaakov Emden, who writes that the pronunciation of the Name is always identical, whether it is written as the Name Havayah or as Adonai: It is read with a chataf patach. This is unlike Magen Avraham's citation from Rabbeinu Bachya and his citation from Responsa Maharam Lublin that it should be pronounced so the patach sound is not perceptible; see siman 83. Eliyah Rabbah cites Or Chadash that Maharam retracted. Machazik Berachah similarly cites a small work called Responsa Geonim, printed in Amsterdam in 5467, containing a reply sent to Maharam and a responsum of Rema MiPano. When Maharam saw Rema MiPano's responsum, he retracted, and Shelah agreed. See Responsa Noda B'Yehuda, siman 2, who writes that there is no doubt that Adonai should be accented on the final syllable rather than the first. Machazik Berachah likewise cites this from Luach Eresh.
And He will be. See Baer Heitev. Magen Avraham writes concerning the word 'He was' that its vav takes the place of a yud, and refers to Tosafos Yom Tov in Sukkah. Tosafos Yom Tov there discusses 'and He will be.' Baer Heitev therefore appears to have emended Magen Avraham to read 'and He will be'; Eliyah Rabbah does likewise, and I have seen this correction entered in copies of Magen Avraham. In my humble opinion no emendation is needed. Tosafos Yom Tov writes there that 'He was' is produced by removing the yud that marks the future. This means that when the yud at the beginning of 'He will be' is removed, the word 'He was' remains, with the vav taking the place of the yud. Magen Avraham comments on the siddur's adoption of the Shulchan Aruch's wording, 'He was,' explaining that it results from the vav replacing the yud. The same principle consequently applies to 'and He will be.'
