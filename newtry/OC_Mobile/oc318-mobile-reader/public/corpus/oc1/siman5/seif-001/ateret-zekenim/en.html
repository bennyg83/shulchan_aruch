When reciting the blessings, he should intend the meaning of the words. When mentioning the Name Adonai, he should intend both its pronunciation and its written form. The Name is pronounced with a kamatz beneath the nun, not a patach. With the kamatz vowel points, its numerical value equals that of 'Hashem is One,' comprising three Names. This is not so with a patach. He should extend the kamatz beneath the nun slightly, allowing him to think the word 'One,' which the kamatz signifies.
And when mentioning, etc., he should intend that He possesses all powers. This explains the apparent plural implication in the word Elokim. If one mistakenly recited a blessing in vain, he should afterward say, 'Blessed is the Name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever.'
The alef of Adonai is read with a chataf patach. Some say this applies when the Name is read as it is written, such as 'Adonai, open my lips' or 'Adonai forever,' and similar phrases: A patach is read beneath the alef. God forbid that one read a patach beneath the nun; 'sin crouches at the entrance'; for he would substitute the mundane for the sacred.
