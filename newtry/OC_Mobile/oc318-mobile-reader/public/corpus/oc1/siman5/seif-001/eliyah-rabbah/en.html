[1] One should intend in blessings, etc. Darkei Moshe wrote that one must be careful not to skip any word as when counting coins, and likewise not to swallow them but must pronounce them from the mouth — until here. Orchos Chayim wrote it is not precise to say "word" — the same applies to one letter, whether from written letters or letters drawn from the vowel motions of gedolot — until here. Tzidah LaDerech p. 10 wrote: although Chazal said mitzvos do not require kavanah, that was for practical mitzvos like sukkah and the like; but prayer's essence is in the heart. And because man's heart rushes after vanity, they arranged for us to say it with the mouth and keep it in the heart — do you not see they ordained at the end "May the words of my mouth... and the meditation of my heart..."? If his heart is in [other] thoughts and he is like a chirping bird, would not a man be ashamed to speak thus to his Maker? This is what Assaf said: "My soul longed, even pined, for the courtyards of Heaven" — and juxtaposed to it "Even the bird found a home" — and the language of one has no relation to the other except to hint that if one lacks kavanah he is like a chirping bird. Ve'Hai Amudim wrote: I established a correction in the holy community of Frankfurt that they would find collections upon collections teaching the prayers of the whole year and piyyutim and selichos at least with the meaning of the words.
