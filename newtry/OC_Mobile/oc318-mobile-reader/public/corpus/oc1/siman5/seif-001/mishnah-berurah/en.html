(1) Meaning of the words — as Chazal said [Berachos 47a]: one should not cast the blessing from his mouth but should intend at the time of utterance and bless calmly. And thus wrote Sefer Chasidim siman 46: when he washes his hands or blesses on fruits or mitzvos habitual in every person's mouth, his heart should intend toward the name of his Creator who showed him wondrous kindness and gave him fruits or bread to enjoy them and commanded him regarding mitzvos — and he should not act like one who does something by custom and utters words from his mouth without the heart's understanding. For this Heaven was angry with His people and sent us by Isaiah saying: "Because this people drew near with its mouth and honored Me with its lips but its heart was far from Me" — and see there what he elaborated on the magnitude of punishment for this.

(2) Its reading in mastery (adnut) — for the awesome Name one may not read as written, as Chazal said: one who pronounces the Name with its letters has no share in the World to Come; rather one must read it as if Adonai were written there — and also with Adonai's pointing, namely the alef with chataf patach but not patach alone or sheva alone; the dalet with holam; the nun with kamatz; and one should emphasize the yud so it be clearly visible — only in kavanah one should intend the Tetragrammaton. And the reason for the accent is milra (on the final syllable).

(3) "Who was," etc. — and in Biur HaGra he wrote that in depth of law one need not intend except the meaning of its reading, for everywhere we follow the reading even though the writing has great secrets; only in Kerias Shema must one also intend "who was," etc. — see there. And where written alef-dalet, for everyone one need intend only that He is Master of all. If one recounts Heaven's favors done for him and from the start mentions the Name and wishes to finish his words of what Heaven did for him, it is forbidden to interrupt with other words lest he not finish his words and it turn out he mentioned the Name in vain. But if he wishes to curse someone and began with the Name, it is a mitzvah to interrupt him so he not finish his words and not sin [Sefer Chasidim, brought in Rabbi Akiva Eiger's chiddushim].
