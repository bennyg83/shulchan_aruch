(1) The meaning of the words. As our Sages said in Berachos 47a, one should not cast the blessing from his mouth, but should concentrate while reciting it and pronounce the blessing calmly. Sefer Chassidim, siman 46, writes: When one washes his hands, recites a blessing over fruit, or recites a blessing over mitzvos that are familiar to everyone, he should direct his heart toward his Creator, Who has shown him wondrous kindness by giving him fruit or bread from which to benefit and by commanding him concerning the mitzvos. He should not act like a person who does something merely from habit and utters words without the reflection of the heart. Because of this, Hashem's anger was kindled against His people, and He sent us the message through Yeshayahu: ‘Because this people approaches with its mouth and honors Me with its lips, while its heart is distant from Me.’ See there further, where he writes at length concerning the severity of the punishment for this.
(2) Its reading is as Adnus. The honored and awesome Name may not be pronounced as it is written. As our Sages said, one who pronounces the Name according to its letters has no share in the World to Come. Rather, it must be read as though the Name Adnus were written, and with the vowelization of Adnus: the aleph with a chataf patach, not with a plain patach or a sheva alone; the dalet with a cholam; and the nun with a kamatz. He should articulate the yud distinctly so that it is clearly heard. In his intention, however, he should have in mind the Name Havayah. The accent is on the final syllable.
(3) Who was, etc. In his commentary, the Gra writes that according to the strict depth of the law, one need intend only the meaning of the Name as it is read, because in all places the reading is determinative, even though the written form contains profound mysteries. Only in Krias Shema must one also intend that He was, is, and will be; see there. Where the Name is written as Adnus, all agree that one need intend only that He is Master of all. If someone is recounting the kindnesses Hashem performed for him, first mentions the Divine Name, and intends to complete his account of what Hashem did for him, it is forbidden to interrupt him with other matters. Otherwise, because of the interruption, he may fail to complete his statement and will have mentioned Hashem's Name in vain. If, however, he wishes to curse someone and begins with the Divine Name, it is a mitzvah to interrupt him so that he does not complete his words and sin. This is from Sefer Chassidim and is cited in the glosses of Rabbi Akiva Eiger.
