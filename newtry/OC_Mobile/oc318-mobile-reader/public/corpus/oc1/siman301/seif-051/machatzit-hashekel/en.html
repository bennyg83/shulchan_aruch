Regarding the hat discussed on Shabbos 138: Rashi's version concerns a felt hat with a brim one tefach wide. The Gemara first assumes the prohibition is due to making a tent; its conclusion distinguishes whether the hat is firmly fastened, because if it is not fastened the wind may blow it off and the wearer may carry it. Rabbeinu Tam omits the word 'rather,' so the conclusion still concerns a tent: A stiff brim is a tent, while a soft brim that bends downward is not. Beis Yosef rules in accordance with both interpretations: Rabbeinu Tam's in se'if 40 and Rashi's in se'if 41. Se'if 40 does not explicitly require stiffness because it assumes an ordinary hat is stiff. Magen Avraham cites Rambam, who requires it to be especially stiff, unlike ordinary hats.
See siman 315:8: A sloping tent is prohibited only if it has a flat roof one tefach wide, or if it reaches a width of one tefach within three tefachim of its top. Although there such a tent is initially forbidden, the present case resembles the canopy discussed in se'if 11, which may be made initially. See Magen Avraham's distinction there.
There is a further difficulty: The Gemara could have answered the challenge, 'But it was stated that a siani is forbidden,' by distinguishing the cases. The forbidden case necessarily concerns one who wears a tallis over his head, creating walls, while the permitted case of siani concerns one who does not wear a tallis.
The citation to Rosh at the end of Eruvin is abbreviated; the relevant discussion is actually in Beitzah, chapter 5, explaining Eruvin 102 concerning rolled mats. Leaving one tefach spread allows the remainder to be unrolled because it adds to an existing tent even though there are no walls. That construction is important and its principal purpose is to form a tent. An ordinary temporary construction such as the present one is not deemed a tent without walls. This explains the practice of prominent people who refrain from drawing a tallis over their heads. It cannot be explained merely by combining Rabbeinu Tam's view with the absence of walls, as Magen Avraham understood, for Magen Avraham himself objects to that explanation. Perhaps they follow Maharil's reading of Rashi: The Gemara's initial assumption was that it constitutes a tent even without walls, as in Rosh; its conclusion that the hat is fastened teaches that an ordinary temporary tent without walls is not prohibited, while the important construction in Eruvin remains an exception. Alternatively, even according to Rabbeinu Tam they refrain not simply because walls are absent, for Rosh shows that a structure of this kind may be a tent even without walls. Rabbeinu Tam's solution depends on the brim sloping downward. When one draws a tallis over a hat that was initially sloped, the tallis may inevitably flatten it; this is an unavoidable consequence, and therefore they refrain.
