155) For they decreed not to put them on. He is also afraid to carry them by hand in increments of less than four cubits, as below, because discovery would endanger him.
156) That they are tefillin. Without straps they may be amulets, since people sometimes make amulets resembling tefillin; he would then have no duty to guard them or wait until night. Magen Avraham, siman 11, writes that such amulets are uncommon nowadays, so even strapless objects must at least be guarded and brought after nightfall.
157) And they are tied. The head-tefillin must have their proper knot and the arm-tefillin a properly fitted knot, not oversized, so he can wear them. If untied, he may not make the knots on Shabbos and cannot bring them in by wearing one pair at a time; he must wait until nightfall.
158) One pair at a time. He wears one arm-tefillin and one head-tefillin to a guarded place, removes them, and repeats. Although Shabbos is not a time for tefillin, wearing them in the weekday manner is ornamental rather than carrying; there is no violation of adding to a mitzvah because he has no mitzvah intent. Magen Avraham forbids a woman to rescue them this way because she does not ordinarily wear tefillin, but other later authorities disagree. He similarly makes a woman liable for wearing a mitzvah-tallis that women do not ordinarily wear; other authorities dispute this too. See Shaar HaMelech, Hilchos Shabbos 19, who cites a manuscript showing that Raavad and Rashba disagree.
159) That there will not be enough time. Since before nightfall he cannot bring all of them, he should not begin wearing them in; he should remain to guard them until night and then bring them.
