Se'if 42: One who finds, etc. This is the language of the Tur.
In disgrace. Because of thieves, as written at the end of the first chapter of Yom Tov, folio 15a, that most, etc.
In a place. Because of dogs there.
If there are. This follows Rashi's second explanation, folio 97b.
If there are, etc.; and they are, etc. This follows Rava there, who holds that a person does trouble himself, for he understands that 'and now' is only a rejection. Therefore, 'tied' is not because of the trouble involved, for if so he would not have said 'and now,' etc.; rather, the concern of trouble is sufficiently addressed by the straps alone, and Rava too agrees to this. What he says 'and now' does not mean that it proves against Rava, but that there is no proof for Rava, since in any case we require them to be tied. But Rava himself does not hold so. The practical difference is that if they have no straps at all, one need not even wait until nightfall, unlike where they are not tied; therefore the Tur wrote both. I will explain the view of the Rif and the Rosh, and the Rambam in chapter 2 of Hilchos Tzitzis, halachah 7, will be resolved. There the Rambam ruled that one who finds techeles in the marketplace, even if the threads are spun and cut, it is invalid. The Raavad objected that this contradicts the conclusion of Eruvin 97a, 'and now,' etc., where all agree that a person does not trouble himself, etc.; if so the Gemara's law is the reverse, that they are valid. The sages of Lunel asked the Rambam this question, and he answered in his responsum: certainly the truth is as you say, and I erred, etc.; and they corrected the text; see there. But the truth shows its way: his original words are clear and valid, and he did not err at all. In his responsum the Rambam overlooked the Rif's method in chapter 10 of Eruvin there, on the mishnah of one who finds tefillin. The Rif wrote: why not with new ones? Perhaps they are amulets, etc.; but with old ones, since they are tied, a person does not trouble himself, etc. And the father of Rav Shmuel bar Yitzchak taught: what are new ones, etc.? Those with straps and tied are old, etc.; those without straps and not tied are new. So too wrote the Rosh there. At first glance these two reasons contradict one another, for the reason of amulets is because a person does trouble himself, while the statement of the father of Rav Shmuel bar Yitzchak necessarily holds that a person does not trouble himself, as stated in the Gemara, 'and now,' etc. Rather, their method in this sugya is as follows: the mishnah is necessarily not like Rabbi Meir, who said both new and old, etc.; nor is it like Rabbi Yehudah, for it is not reasonable to establish the mishnah like Rabbi Yehudah and not according to halachah, since Rabbi Yehudah follows his own view that a bow is a full knot, as Abaye said; Abaye's answer is more reasonable than Rav Chisda's answer there, as Tosafos objected there, that it is not reasonable to establish it like Rabbi Yehudah but not for his reason. Rather, the mishnah is not like Rabbi Meir or Rabbi Yehudah, as above. The mishnah holds that a person does trouble himself, because of the concern of an amulet, and it also accepts what the father of Rav Shmuel bar Yitzchak taught, 'what are new,' etc., but not for his reason of tying; rather, because of trouble. Anything not tied gives us reason to suspect that perhaps it is an amulet, for a person does trouble himself, etc. A compelling proof that the mishnah holds that a person does trouble himself is that otherwise, how could it teach that with new ones he is exempt? They are tefillin. If it held that a person does not trouble himself, etc., it should have said: but with new ones, or if he found them, etc., he waits until nightfall, etc. Rather, the mishnah necessarily holds that a person does trouble himself. That which Rava said, 'whether a person troubles himself or does not trouble himself is a dispute of Tannaim,' is according to the baraisa of Ahavah the son of Rabbi Zeira and Rabbi Elazar, who established it as a Tannaic dispute of Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehudah. The conclusion that everyone holds, etc., is also according to that baraisa, and Rabbi Elazar established them according to everyone, namely Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehudah, who both hold that a person does not trouble himself. But the mishnah necessarily holds that a person does trouble himself. According to this, the words of the Rif and Rosh are clear: they brought the reason of amulet regarding the mishnah, and also 'what are,' etc., to teach what counts as new. The Rambam's method is exactly like the Rif, as is his usual way, and the Rosh also follows their method, as above. It follows that according to the Rif, the Rosh, and the Rambam, even if they have straps we suspect they are an amulet until they are tied, as above. The practical difference is for danger from bandits, whether one may carry them less than four amos at a time or guard them. According to the Raavad, even without straps we do not suspect this, as above. The Baal HaMaor also objected to the Rif as above; see Milchamos Hashem, who reconciled the Rif in another way.
He carries it. This follows the first Tanna there.
Or he gives them, etc. He holds that Rabbi Yehudah and the Sages of the mishnah are a third view, permitting even giving them to another person, even for a mundane need, since this was not included among the eighteen decrees. They disagree with the first Tanna only because for a mundane need they did not permit carrying less than four amos at a time. One cannot say that they hold like Rabbi Shimon, for if so why did the Gemara need to say for Rabbi Shimon, 'this is preferable'? That implies it is permitted only because it is impossible otherwise, which is preferable to carrying less than four amos at a time; but according to Rabbi Shimon, even for a mundane need it is permitted.
