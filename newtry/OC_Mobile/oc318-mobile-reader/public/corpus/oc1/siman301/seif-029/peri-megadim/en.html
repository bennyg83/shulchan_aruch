The Mechaber’s statement in seif 43 that one wraps himself in a hide refers to carrying a Sefer Torah. He then covers it with clothing. Because it would otherwise be left in disgrace, the Sages permitted this. These are Rambam’s words in chapter 19, halachah 25, of Hilchos Shabbos. The carrying is performed in an unusual manner. See there. At present this requires some study; see Tosafos Shabbos 62a, s.v. kol.
The statement in seif 44 concerning a fire should be compared with siman 334, seif katan 9. There is a view there that one may do so only once. See Taz, seif katan 7, and Magen Avraham, seif katan 14. Regarding clothing, some permit this only into a jointly enclosed courtyard, while others permit it even into a courtyard that is not enclosed by an eruv. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained there. The statement in seif 45 concerning one whose garments became soaked in water should be compared with Bach. Even if they became soaked before he put them on, it may be permitted to wear them, and the Sages did not decree lest he wring them out. This differs from Rama’s gloss in seif 46, because there he has no need for them. Rama’s statement in seif 46, “provided he is particular about their water,” means that he does not want water to remain in them. Cloths that are constantly kept in water are not subject to this concern and may be moved.
From this one may derive from the Mechaber’s statement in seif 46 that it is forbidden to dry them near a fire, because of cooking or whitening. As Magen Avraham writes in seif katan 57, a cloth soaked in water may not be spread over a stove in a place where the heat reaches yad soledes bo in order to dry it. Likewise, it may not be spread on a table on Shabbos in a place that reaches yad soledes bo, because this constitutes cooking through a derivative of fire and whitening. The same applies to an infant’s cloths soiled with urine. They may not be dried by a stove in a place that reaches yad soledes bo because of whitening. If a cloth became soaked with beverages such as beer or mead, the matter depends upon the dispute in siman 318 concerning whether cooking applies after prior cooking to a liquid. It is possible that whitening applies even to colored beverages. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained in the laws of cooking. Know that the term garments includes every material, even wool.
The statement in seif 49 that one may wash his hands in a river on Shabbos should be compared with siman 326 concerning the laws of washing, drying near a fire, and washing the entire body in cold water. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained there.
Whether it is permitted to carry those canopies made from linen with a long wooden shaft, which open and spread like a tent, and whether they constitute a temporary shelter, will, with Hashem’s help, be explained in siman 315. If they were already opened before Shabbos, whether one may carry them for protection from the sun or rain so that the rain not fall upon his body requires consideration. Although seifim 13 and 14, and Magen Avraham, seifim katanim 22 and 23, permit certain matters because of discomfort, that is specifically where the item is worn as clothing, even though it is not a complete garment. Here, however, he carries it in his hand, which is not a manner of wearing at all but ordinary carrying by hand. It therefore appears forbidden in a courtyard not enclosed by an eruv. With Hashem’s help, more will be explained in siman 315. It is permitted to open a belt in a public domain and gird oneself with it by way of clothing. See siman 303, seif 16. The laws of laundering will be explained in the laws of cooking, and the laws of wringing in their place.
