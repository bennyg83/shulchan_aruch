110) Meaning that after, etc. Certainly, if he removes it entirely, folds it, and places it on his shoulder, he is liable.
111) He is liable to a sin-offering. This is not a manner of wearing but of carrying a burden.
112) Below his shoulders. This is permitted even if it does not cover most of his body, though some authorities require it to cover most of his body.
113) On this basis it is permitted, etc. He may shorten it somewhat at the bottom, since he remains wrapped below his shoulders. Although he lifts the lower edge, it is difficult to wrap his entire body as in the synagogue while walking in the public domain, even with a cloak over it.
114) And bring it to the synagogue. The gloss of the next se'if belongs here according to Be'er HaGolah and Olas Shabbos; under Gra's explanation below, it remains in its printed place.
