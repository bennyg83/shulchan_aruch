He should not go out. See Taz, who elaborated. There is a textual error, and it should read that the conclusion of the Rosh is that by a man, whether the needle is pierced or not pierced, he is exempt, for carrying it in his garment is not the normal manner of carrying, whereas in his hand he is liable, as in siman 252, seif 6. By a woman, with a pierced needle she is liable, as the Tosafot Yom Tov wrote, for it is the way of a craftswoman in her craft to sew and repair garments, and it is the normal way of carrying to stick a pierced needle in her garment, so she is liable. An unpierced needle, which she uses for opening her cloak and her scarves, is explained in Shiltei Giborim, seif 9, not to be a burden. But it is not her way to put a pierced needle in her scarves, etc. It appears at first glance that with a pierced needle, even if she puts it in her scarves, she is liable to bring a chattas, and with Heaven’s help this will be explained in Shiltei Giborim. He challenged the Beit Yosef, for it appears from his words that when a man takes out in his hand an unpierced needle he is exempt, and this is not so. Rather, the reason that when it is stuck in his garment he is exempt is that one who examines the Beit Yosef can say that this was his intention, namely, when it is stuck in his garment, and whether pierced or unpierced, by a man he is exempt. He explains three disputes: the first anonymous opinion is that of the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 19 of Hilchos Shabbos, halacha 3, in the name of the Geonim; the second opinion is the Rif and Rambam. The Mechaber omitted entirely the opinion of the Rosh and Tur, that by a man in both cases he is exempt, and the Rama also added nothing, which is somewhat difficult. It is also difficult why he wrote the first view anonymously and then ‘there are those who say’ for the Rif and Rambam, since his way is to rule like the Rif and Rambam as two of the three pillars, as he wrote in his introduction, and in Choshen Mishpat he wrote so. In Beer HaGolah, letters 90 and 100, he wrote that the first opinion is Rif and Rambam and the second is Rashi, but he did not note what was said in the name of the Geonim. This follows the version in the Beit Yosef, and requires study.
