Or before one who is greater. For if he goes to his student whom his rebbi needs, his law in this is like one greater than him. And even though at the end of chapter Elu Metzivot, regarding the lost object of one's rebbi, he is called 'his rebbi' only if most of his wisdom came from him, like R' Yehudah - and so we hold - that is not comparable here. For there, regarding precedence over his father for a lost object, it must specifically be one from whom most of his wisdom came; which is not so here, where we require only that he be greater than him. And regarding returning a lost object, being greater than him does not suffice - rather he must specifically have learned from him, as is in the Mishnah there that they brought him to the status of 'master of the house,' and that is because against the stature of his father so much is needed. But here it is similar to what is written there: Torah scholars in Bavel tear [garments] for one another, and Rashi explained that they sit in the beit midrash and raise difficulties and resolve them, and all learn from one another, and each is like a student to a teacher; whereas regarding a lost object in place of a father, [it is] only until he is his primary rebbi. And if so, the same applies with this student and his rebbi: since his rebbi also needs to learn from him - whether because he heard from other rabbis, or because of his sharpness and acuity - here they are like student and rebbi. And furthermore, in any case this is no worse than watching fruit, which is permitted in siman 613, as above.
