Or the face of one greater than he — for if he goes to his student who needs him, his teacher — the law is as one greater than he in this; and even though in the chapter "These are found" regarding returning a lost object of one's teacher, "teacher" is called only one whose wisdom is mostly greater than his, as R' Yehuda — for so is the halacha here: there, regarding precedence to one's father for a lost object, it must specifically be one whose wisdom is mostly greater; which is not so here, where we require only one greater than he. And in returning a lost object, one does not say "greater than he" but specifically "learned from him," as in the Mishnah there that they brought to the attention of the High Priest — and this is because of the honor of one's father one needs so much; but here it resembles what is said there: Torah scholars in Babylonia tear keriah, and Rashi explained they sit in the study hall and ask and answer, and all learn from one another, and it is like student to teacher; and regarding a lost object in place of a father, until his teacher is clear — if so, here too with this student whose teacher needs him, his teacher also learns from him, whether from having heard from other rabbis or from his sharpness — it is here like student and teacher. And furthermore, in any case this is not less than guarding produce, as he equated in siman 613, as above.
