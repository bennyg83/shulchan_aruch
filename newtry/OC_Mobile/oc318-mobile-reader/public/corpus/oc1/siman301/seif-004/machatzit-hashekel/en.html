Se'if katan 4: Likewise, to see; meaning, although it is forbidden, etc. Tur's wording is now sound: He divided the laws because each contains a different permission. First he permits running when the running itself is pleasurable; afterward he permits looking at images when their sight is pleasurable. In the latter clause Tur does not discuss permission to run in order to see them. Bach explains that one may run, etc. This differs from Taz, who permits only when the running itself is pleasurable. Here the running is merely instrumental to the pleasurable sight; during the running itself he has no pleasure, so Taz forbids it. Nevertheless, even according to Magen Avraham's interpretation, it appears that he agrees with Bach's practical ruling that one may run in order to see the sight despite deriving no pleasure from the running, as se'if katan 5 proves.
