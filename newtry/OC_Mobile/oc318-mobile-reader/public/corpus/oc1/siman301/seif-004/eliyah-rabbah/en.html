[7] [Levush] "But not in order to exercise," etc. This means for medicinal purposes, as explained below in siman 332 and in Baal Halachot Gedolot at siman 328, seif 42, unlike Taz. Levush implies that even if one merely walks and does not run, if his intention is exercise it is forbidden. Magen Avraham, however, permits any case in which he is not running. Levush's words appear supported by the Tosefta, chapter 7, which says that an animal that ate too much fruit may be walked about. This implies that doing so for a person is forbidden, and Rama also appears to imply this. [8] "And it is forbidden to pass through," etc. This is so even when it is impossible to go around. Regarding the reason stated, that he may come to squeeze out the garment, one should not object that soaking the garment is itself laundering and should already be forbidden. Tosafos in Shabbos 111 answer that the rule that soaking is laundering applies only where there is dirt on the garment. One may also answer that this rule does not apply where the dirt is not visible. I found, however, that Sefer Yere'im and Piskei Rekanati answer that the discussion concerns leather garments. This requires study; see siman 302, seif 9. [9] "He may pass through," etc. This applies even if it is possible to take another route. See siman 333, seif 8. According to Rashi, there is no need for the additional restriction suggested by some authorities. It appears that the explanation is as Tosafos write there, that for the sake of a mitzvah the Sages did not impose the decree concerning squeezing. Sefer HaTerumah and Semak write likewise. See also Rashi at the beginning of Yoma.
