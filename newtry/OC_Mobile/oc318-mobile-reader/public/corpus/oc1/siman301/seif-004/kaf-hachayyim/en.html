18) [Se'if 4] Such as to greet his teacher. One is obligated to greet him on a festival. On Shabbos and Rosh Chodesh there is no obligation, but there is a mitzvah. A woman is equivalent to a man in this respect, as learned from the Shunammite woman. Magen Avraham 7. See siman 554:12, where Magen Avraham writes that there is a mitzvah even on a weekday; see there. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, siman 94, however, which writes that nowadays, if one does not go to study from his teacher during the festival but only to greet him, there is no obligation, although it is proper to do so; see there.
19) There: Such as to greet his teacher. The same applies to greeting his father. Gemara. Whether a teacher may go to a student is left unresolved, and we rule stringently that it is forbidden. Beis Yosef.
20) There: Or someone greater, etc. If he goes to a student whom his teacher needs, that student has the status of one greater than he for this purpose. Taz 2.
21) There: He may cross it. This applies when the garment is not dirty. If it is soiled with mud or the like, according to Rosh's first answer, Yoma 77, crossing is forbidden because soaking constitutes laundering; according to his second answer it is permitted. Pischei Olam 8. See Ateres Zekeinim 3 and paragraph 17 above.
22) There: So that he will remember, etc. Rashi explains that he should not extend his hand from beneath the hem of his robe in order to lift the hem onto his arm, because it then does not appear worn as clothing but carried upon his shoulder. The master said: One who goes out on Shabbos with a tallis folded over his shoulder is liable. Beis Yosef.
23) There. See below, siman 613:5 and the gloss to se'if 8. Rama writes there that wherever crossing water is permitted, one may cross even if a route around it on dry land is available, because minimizing the distance walked is preferable. Taz and Magen Avraham there, however, write that one should be stringent and not cross through water if a dry route is possible, even though it lengthens the walk; see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav 13 rules likewise.
24) There: It is forbidden to cross while wearing his sandal, etc. Rashi explains the distinction between a shoe and a sandal: A sandal cannot be tightened and tied securely to the foot like a shoe. Beis Yosef.
