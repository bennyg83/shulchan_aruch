Se'if 4: For the sake of a mitzvah. Specifically so, since there the Gemara asks what the law is regarding a teacher going to his student, and it resolves the question stringently.
Provided, etc., so that, etc. The Rosh and the Ran there asked from the above, se'if 2, where it says that it is forbidden to cross, etc. They answered that since they permitted it only in the manner in which he is dressed, as it says below, 'provided that he not,' etc., the matter is recognizable and is not like ordinary carrying. The Ran brought proof from chapter 2 of Yom Tov, folio 18a: Rav Yosef said it is a decree lest he wring it out; they challenged him from the law of a niddah, etc., who immerses while wearing her garments. The answer is: there it is different, for since it was permitted to her only by means of wearing it, she remembers. It is clear from their words that the prohibition is not because of a burden, as Rashi explained, but because of recognizability. This is what is meant by 'provided,' etc. It does not mean changing from the usual manner of wearing them; on the contrary, he must go in the manner of wearing them. Rather, it is a change from the usual manner of crossing through water, for people are not accustomed to go through water in the manner of wearing them. Therefore, according to the Rosh, the reason that he should not take them out, etc., is because of the required change. For if the concern were burden, what does the answer 'since' accomplish, when there too it is needed in that manner? Rather, now that we say it is because of a change, unlike his ordinary crossing through water in garments, there, where the garments do not reach the water, as it says there that sometimes they are lifted, etc., it indicates that when this happens only occasionally, there is no change. The Tur learned from the Rosh's wording, 'since they permitted,' etc., that because of the water itself they did not permit it, unlike Rashi's explanation; otherwise he should have said, 'since it is forbidden except,' etc. The wording 'they permitted' implies that it is a special dispensation, as in Yom Tov there, 'since it was permitted to her,' etc., unlike the Beis Yosef in siman 613. They hold that it is not considered a burden, as will be explained in se'if 31; see there.
