10) To greet his rebbe. The same applies to greeting his father. Gemara. See Magen Avraham, who writes that a man and a woman are equally obligated in the mitzvah of greeting their rebbe; it appears that a woman may do so only with her husband's permission.
11) Or someone greater than he. This excludes a rebbe going to his student, which is forbidden. If the rebbe needs the student in certain matters, whether because of the student's sharp analysis or because he possesses teachings from other great authorities, Taz permits the rebbe to cross a river, since the student is at least greater than he in some respects. Tosefes Shabbos forbids it. If the rebbe is uncertain about a matter and goes to ask a student who knows it, Taz's permissive ruling is reasonable, for this is no less than any other mitzvah purpose.
12) And in se'if 8 in the gloss. There it is explained that even when another route is available by which he can go around, it is preferable to pass through the water rather than lengthen the journey, since he is traveling for a mitzvah. The later authorities there agree, however, that when he can go around, it is better to do so than to pass through the water.
