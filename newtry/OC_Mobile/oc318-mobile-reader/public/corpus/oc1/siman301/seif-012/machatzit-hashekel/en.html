Se'if katan 12: Because, etc. One might derive it simply from the fact that Shabbos is not the time for tefillin. Shabbos 61a comments on the Mishnah, 'One may not go out wearing tefillin.' Rav Safra says: Do not think this follows only the view that Shabbos is not the time for tefillin; even according to the view that Shabbos is the time, one may not go out, lest he come to carry them four amos in the public domain when he must remove them to enter the bathroom. Tosafos Yom Tov therefore asks that, for us who rule Shabbos is not the time for tefillin, the reason that he must remove them was unnecessary. Magen Avraham answers that although it is unnecessary regarding tefillin, the reason is true and was stated for its consequence regarding an expert amulet. Such an amulet is an ornament and may ordinarily be worn outside, but if Divine Names are written in it, it may be worn only when covered with leather, because he may need to remove it before entering the bathroom.
According to what is written at the end of siman 308:4, there is no difficulty. Although we rule that Shabbos is not the time for tefillin, this follows Rabbi Akiva's exposition of 'It shall be a sign for you': Tefillin are a sign that we are Hashem's servants and bear His seal like a slave's fetter; Shabbos and Yom Tov are themselves a sign and need no additional sign. If one nevertheless wishes, he may put on tefillin on Shabbos. Thus, even according to our ruling, the reason that he must remove them is needed: Tefillin are not a burden because he may wear them. Rav Safra's wording then needs explanation. What difference is there between the views whether Shabbos is the time for tefillin, if even according to the latter rationale one still needs the concern that he may remove them? Eruvin 96a cites: 'You shall observe this statute at its appointed time, from days to days.' Rabbi Yose HaGelili expounds: days, not nights; 'from days,' not all days, excluding Shabbos and Yom Tov. Rabbi Akiva says this statute refers only to the Paschal offering. The Gemara later cites Rabbi Akiva: One might think a person may put on tefillin on Shabbos and Yom Tov; therefore 'It shall be a sign for you'; those who need a sign, excluding days that are themselves a sign. Tosafos writes that Rabbi Yose HaGelili relies upon the nearby exposition from 'a sign upon your hand.' Their intent is that 'from days' merely indicates some days without tefillin; why choose Shabbos and Yom Tov rather than other days? The verse about a sign identifies them. If so, why is the exclusion 'from days' needed, since Shabbos and Yom Tov are already excluded by 'It shall be a sign'? It must be as Magen Avraham writes: The verse about a sign teaches only that Shabbos and Yom Tov do not require tefillin, but one may wear them voluntarily; 'from days' teaches that it is forbidden. Rav Safra therefore says: Do not think the Mishnah follows only the view that Shabbos is not a tefillin time; meaning Rabbi Yose HaGelili, who prohibits wearing them. Even according to the view that Shabbos is a tefillin time and one is even obligated to wear them, one may not go out lest he carry them four amos. We rule like Rabbi Akiva, under whom one may voluntarily wear them on Shabbos and Yom Tov; Shulchan Aruch therefore needed the reason that he may have to remove them.
