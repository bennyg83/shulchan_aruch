46) With a needle stuck in his garment, etc. All these are implements that craftsmen display as signs of their trades when they go to the marketplace, so people will recognize them.
47) And if he went out, he is exempt. This is not the ordinary manner of carrying it, which is by hand. Even a craftsman carries it this way only when advertising his trade. Above in se'if 8, the first view makes anyone liable for a pierced needle stuck where people sometimes place it on weekdays; here it is stuck where only a craftsman displaying his trade would put it, so even he is exempt according to Rabbi Meir. Gra rules above at the end of siman 252 that the halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah, who makes the craftsman liable. Chemed Moshe argues that Rif and Rosh also incline to this view.
