Se'if 9: According to Rashi, he is exempt. This follows the plain meaning of the Gemara's wording, 'and their reverse for a man.' So too he explained there on folio 67a, s.v. 'royal children': alternatively, etc., he will remove it and show it. A proof for his words is from the Yerushalmi cited by the Rosh there: 'But a man, since he is not,' etc.; 'this applies equally to a man and a woman,' as the Ramban writes; see there.
And according to Rabbeinu Tam. He derives it from the Yerushalmi cited above, which writes on folio 67 that because women are haughty, etc. There is also proof from the Gemara, folio 59b: a kameira is permitted, etc., just as with a belt, etc.; and there on folio 61a: provided that he not tie it with a bracelet and a ring. This shows that a man is permitted to go out with a ring. He explains 'and their reverse for a man' as referring only to whether it is an ornament or not an ornament. Regarding royal children, the Baal HaMaor answered that minors are like women, and others explained like Rashi's first explanation, because they would laugh. The Yerushalmi above refers to an item that is an ornament for both a man and a woman, as it says there: this applies to one made for this and for that, etc. The Ran objected to them regarding their statement that 'and their reverse' is not about liability and exemption, as above, for if so, what is the Gemara asking, 'You answered for a woman; what can be said for a man?' It appears to me that Tosafos was careful about this and wrote Rabbeinu Tam's explanation, that it refers to liability for a sin offering; see Tosafos in Eruvin 69a, s.v. 'since.' Rabbeinu Tam explains, etc.; see there. This is what is meant by 'that it is not,' etc., as stated there in the mishnah.
