34) Which has no seal. Such a ring is an ornament only for a woman, but a burden for a man. This discussion concerns carrying it on his finger; if he carries it in his hand, all agree that he is liable even when it has a seal.
35) And if he went out wearing it, he is liable. Wearing it on his finger is considered the ordinary manner of carrying, because a woman sometimes gives it to her husband to take to a craftsman for repair, and he places it on his finger until he arrives. Thus, even on a weekday it is normally carried this way.
36) According to Rashi he is exempt. This means exempt but rabbinically forbidden. Although a signet ring is a man's ornament, since he ordinarily uses it on weekdays to seal documents, it is nevertheless forbidden lest he remove it to show others and then carry it four cubits, as the Sages decreed regarding women's ornaments.
37) For it is not an ornament, etc. They hold that for a man's ornament we do not decree lest he remove it to show others, because men do not ordinarily do so. An item that is an ornament for both men and women is nevertheless forbidden even for a man, because the Sages made no distinction.
38) See above, siman 303:18. There it is explained how to act nowadays. It implies that one should not protest against a man who wears such rings, but Rabbi Akiva Eiger writes there that a conscientious person should be stringent and not go out wearing any ring.
