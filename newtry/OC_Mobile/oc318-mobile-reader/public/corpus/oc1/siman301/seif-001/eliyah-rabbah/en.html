[Levush] It is written, if you turn back, etc. So wrote the Tur. This requires study, for in Shabbos 113 it derives this from, doing your ways, that your walking should not be, etc.
And it is forbidden to take large steps, etc. This is surprising, for the Gemara there says that taking a large step is forbidden even on a weekday; see there. He overlooked the aggadic novellae there, that on a weekday it is only a danger, because it removes one five-hundredth of a person’s eyesight, but on Shabbos there is also a prohibition. Also there, it seems to me possible that what the Gemara concludes there, that it is restored to him by the kiddush of Shabbos evening, was the answer of Rabbi Yishmael to Rabbi, or the Talmud itself said it, that on a weekday there is a remedy, whereas on Shabbos the prohibition remains. In Tosafot in Taanis 10b, Tosafot explained that it removes only with the first step specifically, but from then on, once he has trodden, he has trodden. Therefore there is a difficulty: what did Rabbi Yishmael answer Rabbi in Shabbos 113, and is it permitted on a weekday, etc.? Perhaps Rabbi’s question was where he had already trodden. More than a cubit, etc. Meaning, there should be half a cubit between one foot and the other, and the sole of the foot is also half a cubit. The Agudah wrote that he should not lift the second foot before placing down the first, and so wrote the Raavan, siman 360.
