1) [Se'if 1] One may not run on Shabbos. It is written, 'If you restrain your foot because of Shabbos,' and our Sages expounded that one's manner of walking on Shabbos should not resemble his manner of walking during the week. Tur. Although the Talmud derives this from the words, 'pursuing your affairs,' one may say that Tur quotes the beginning of the verse while intending its conclusion. Tosefes Shabbos 1 writes likewise; see there. Even if one's strides are shorter than an amah, running is forbidden on Shabbos. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 1.
2) There: One may not run on Shabbos. Even during the week one may not take a large stride, for it removes one five-hundredth of one's eyesight. Magen Avraham 1. Maharsha, Chiddushei Aggados, page 113, asks: If so, what difference exists between Shabbos and weekdays? He answers that during the week there is no prohibition, only harm to the eyes; on Shabbos there is an additional prohibition. Another practical difference concerns a blind person, for whom it is nevertheless forbidden on Shabbos. Machatzis HaShekel. See Tosefes Shabbos 3, however, who writes that some dispute the assertion that a large stride diminishes eyesight. The halachah follows them leniently, and the prohibition applies only on Shabbos; see there.
3) There: Unless it is for a mitzvah purpose, etc. It is written, 'They shall follow Hashem; He will roar like a lion.' This implies that just as a person runs from a lion, so should he run after the mitzvos of Hashem, may He be blessed. Tosefes Shabbos 2.
4) There, in the gloss: It is forbidden to take a stride, etc. It is likewise forbidden to leap. Rambam, chapter 24, law 4.
5) There, in the gloss: It is forbidden to take a stride longer than an amah, etc. This means that there should be one-half amah between one foot and the other, while the foot itself is also one-half amah, as stated in siman 397:2 in Mahari Weil's name. Magen Avraham 2. The passage there explains that an amah is the length of two shoes: one shoe occupies the place where the foot stands, and another shoe's length is between the feet; see there. Accordingly, the statement here that the foot is one-half amah includes the shoe. Agudah writes in Tosafos' name, first chapter of Taanis, that one should not lift his second foot before placing down the first. Magen Avraham, ibid. Raavan 360 writes likewise, as cited by Eliyah Rabbah 4.
6) There, in the gloss: It is forbidden to take a stride longer than an amah, etc. This is forbidden even during the week, except that there it is solely a danger and can be remedied through Kiddush and Havdalah, unlike on Shabbos. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 1. We already wrote at the end of paragraph 2, however, that some disagree and hold that there is no concern during the week; see there.
7) There, in the gloss: It is forbidden to take a stride longer than an amah, etc. Eshel Avraham writes from the sacred glosses that one may take a large stride on Shabbos for a mitzvah. It goes without saying that this is permitted if the mitzvah cannot be performed otherwise; perhaps it is permitted even if this merely enables him to perform the mitzvah with greater alacrity and speed. See there. Orchos Chaim 2.
8) There, in the gloss: If possible, etc. If it is not possible, se'if 3 explains that it is permitted.
