Magen Avraham writes: 'It is ordinarily attached, so the Sages did not burden him.' His point is not merely that no burden was imposed. Beis Yosef has already explained that an item ordinarily attached is subordinate to the garment. Magen Avraham draws on Tosafos, Shabbos 58: Why is a bell woven into a slave's garment permitted, while a seal woven into it is forbidden? Tosafos answers that a seal is not an ornament and is not ordinarily affixed to every garment, so the Sages decreed concerning a woven seal because of an unwoven one. A bell, however, is an ornament ordinarily woven into the garment; the Sages did not require one to cut it off and thereby spoil the garment. This distinguishes the present law from the servant's seal in siman 304.
