122) It is forbidden to go out, etc. Unlike other objects sewn into a garment, the coins are significant and do not become subordinate to it. Nevertheless, unlike coins tied in a pouch above, their carriage is only rabbinically forbidden because this is not the ordinary manner of carrying.
123) In a case of loss. The coins were sewn into his garment before Shabbos. Since carrying them this way is only rabbinic, and according to many poskim we lack a Torah-level public domain nowadays, the Sages were lenient to prevent financial loss: otherwise, anxiety over his money might lead him to bury it in the ground and violate Torah law. If they were not sewn in before Shabbos, he may carry them unusually, between his garment and body or in his shoe. Ideally, sewing them in before Shabbos is preferable.
124) If he must go out. For example, he fears that remaining home all day will reveal that he is guarding his money and cause thieves to rob him.
125) He may not go out. Meaning outside an eruv while wearing the garment.
126) One should be stringent. He should not wear the garment, lest he go out in it.
