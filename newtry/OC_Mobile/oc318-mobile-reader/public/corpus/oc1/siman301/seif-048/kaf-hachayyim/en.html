48) There: Whether it has an eye, etc. Beis Yosef contains a scribal error here, but Shulchan Aruch is written correctly. Magen Avraham 15. See Taz 5 for his emendation and resolution of Beis Yosef's words. Regarding Bach's statement that no opinion holds a man liable for an eyed needle, Magen Avraham writes that this is not compelled. Taz similarly writes that Bach needlessly described Beis Yosef and Shulchan Aruch here as the words of an erring student. Everything is coherent; only Beis Yosef requires emendation. The general principle is that everything depends upon contemporary practice. If it is ordinarily carried this way during the week and is not an ornament, this is its normal mode of carrying and he is liable. If it is not ordinarily carried this way during the week, he is exempt, but the act is forbidden because it is not an ornament. The authorities disagree only about which kind of needle was ordinarily carried in Talmudic times. If most people do not carry it this way, even though people in one locale do, they are exempt because their practice is disregarded relative to that of everyone else, as the Gemara, page 52, states. Magen Avraham, ibid.; Eliyah Rabbah 19; Tosefes Shabbos 17.
48) Tosefes Shabbos writes there: If they have a reason for their practice, and that same reason would lead most people to act likewise were it relevant to them, we do not say in such a case that their practice is disregarded relative to everyone else's; see there. See below, siman 320:1.
