Se'if 3: He may step over and jump, etc., and it is better that he step over. Rashi there writes that it is even permitted to step over, but forbidden to jump; stepping over is permitted. It appears that his intent is as follows: the Gemara is difficult, for it makes the matter depend on whether they were able, etc., meaning on whether he can or cannot, and it does not simply say that it is forbidden to place forty se'ah there. For where he can, even stepping over is permitted, and where he cannot, even if he does not step over it is forbidden. How can this be? One must say that it refers to jumping, where he lifts both feet at once, as stated in the Yerushalmi at the end of Yom Tov, cited by Hagahos Ashri there; see also the Rambam's commentary to Ohalos, chapter 8, mishnah 5. He holds that jumping is lighter than stepping over, for the prohibition here is that he spreads his legs widely, like the large stride mentioned there. But the beginning also implies that the prohibition is in such a case, and this is why he writes that stepping over is permitted, meaning that even stepping over is permitted. So too here the Tur writes, 'and it is better that he step over,' etc., and did not write that it is forbidden to go around, for certainly if he cannot step over it is permitted to go around. But regarding crossing through, he wrote that it is forbidden to cross, for it is always forbidden for a mundane purpose, as written in se'if 4; see there.
