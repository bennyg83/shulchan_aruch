Even if it is wide. This language implies that all the more may one leap over a narrow water channel. I do not know the reason, for se'if 2 states that leaping is forbidden. Perhaps, since one can sometimes fall into a water channel, the Sages permitted leaping over it in every case. This resembles the Gemara's conclusion that since no alternative is possible, it is proper; all such cases are included in having no alternative.
To greet his teacher, etc. Magen Avraham distinguishes between a festival and Shabbos: on Shabbos it is only a mitzvah, while on a festival it is obligatory. This is very forced to one who examines the Talmud. See siman 554. In the version of Rabbeinu Chananel before us, Rosh Hashanah 16, it is explicitly stated that the Gemara reads: If they live far apart, he is obligated only on a festival; if they live close, he is obligated even on Rosh Chodesh and Shabbos. Ritva explains even more clearly that Rabbi Yitzchak mentioned the outermost limit. In truth, the required frequency of greeting depends upon one's proximity to his teacher. If he is in the same city, he must see him every day and less does not suffice. If he is outside the city but nearby, once a week or month suffices; this is the matter of the Shunammite woman. If he is far away, he must see him at least once each festival. See there at length.
