15) [Se'if 3] He may stride and leap, etc. Striding means spreading one's legs apart broadly; leaping means jumping with both feet at once. Gra.
16) There: It is forbidden to cross it, etc. If he cannot go around and cannot leap over it, but can only cross through the water, it is forbidden, since he is not going for a mitzvah purpose. Bach; Eliyah Rabbah 8. Magen Avraham 6, however, implies that if he can neither leap nor go around, he may cross through the water. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 6, writes likewise. The later authorities nevertheless follow Bach. It appears that one may be lenient in pressing circumstances.
17) There: Lest he come to wring it out. Although wetting the garment should independently be forbidden because soaking it constitutes laundering it, Tosafos, Shabbos 111, answers that this principle applies only when the garment contains dirt. One may alternatively say that the principle does not apply to something whose dirty state is not visible. I found, however, that Sefer Yereim, page 39, and Piskei Recanati 113 answer that the case concerns leather garments. This requires examination. Eliyah Rabbah 8. See below, siman 302:9.
