Se'if katan 3: And their running, etc. This is unlike Bach's explanation of Tur. Tur writes: 'Young men who take pleasure, etc.; likewise, one may see anything whose sight gives him pleasure.' Beis Yosef says the latter word 'permitted' is superfluous. Bach adds that the first occurrence of 'to see' is also superfluous. He therefore explains that Tur's two cases differ in the scope of permission. Running for pleasure is not initially permitted: Initially we tell a person not to train himself to derive pleasure from running. If he has already become accustomed to it, however, we do not forbid him to run on Shabbos, because preventing him would detract from his Shabbos pleasure. The initial restriction arises because young men on weekdays designate a finish line and race, with the first to reach it receiving money. If he runs on Shabbos, onlookers may suspect that he is running for money as on a weekday. Running to see something pleasurable, by contrast, is initially permitted, because no one would pay him merely for running to see something he enjoys. Tur therefore separated the two laws because their rulings differ. Magen Avraham disagrees and holds that one who enjoys running may do so initially. Tur's redundant words 'to see' and 'permitted' are resolved by Magen Avraham's explanation in se'if katan 4.
