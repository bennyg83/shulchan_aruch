41) It is forbidden, etc. Even if he now intends to hang it from his neck as an ornament, it is forbidden because of maris ayin. This applies even according to Rabbeinu Tam and Rambam above in se'if 9, who permit a man's ornament, or where the key is firmly attached to the chain around his neck and cannot easily be removed for display; it remains forbidden because of maris ayin.
42) If it is silver. Since keys are not ordinarily made of silver, its primary purpose is ornamental. Although it is also used, it was chiefly made as an ornament and may be worn as such. An iron or copper key is forbidden even if ornamentally fashioned, because its principal purpose is functional, as keys are ordinarily made of those metals. Levush. Although the Mechaber appears to accept the first, stringent view, one should not protest, since Darchei Moshe cites Agur that Ashkenazic communities follow the lenient view for a silver key.
43) It is forbidden to go out with a case. This means even a silver case fixed to a chain and hung around his neck as an ornament. Carrying it by hand is independently forbidden, even if it is a complete ornament. Later authorities.
44) For the spectacles themselves are a burden. The same applies to a silver knife sheath, even if ornamental, when it contains a knife. The knife is not subordinate to the sheath, nor the spectacles to their case; rather, the case is made for the spectacles and the sheath for the knife. This concerns wearing the spectacle case with the spectacles inside it on a chain around one's neck as an ornament. Wearing the spectacles themselves on one's nose where there is no eruv is certainly forbidden, lest they fall and he carry them four cubits. Chayei Adam.
45) There is room to permit this. This applies only when the key is permanently attached at the end of the belt and fashioned like a hook or clasp used to fasten it. The belt and metal key are then one unit, and the key is subordinate to the belt even though it can also lock something. If attached in the middle of the belt, it is forbidden, because it has no connection to the belt and is not subordinate to it. Thus, hanging a key from metal fixed to a belt, and certainly merely tying it there permanently, provides no permission unless the key itself was originally fashioned as the fastening hook. Some permit a silver key attached in the middle because it is an ornament, while others forbid it, as stated in se'if 11. All agree that carrying a watch in one's clothing outside an eruv is forbidden, since it is not worn as clothing; one who carries it into the public domain is liable to a sin-offering. Even attaching it to an ornamental gold necklace does not permit it, because the watch is independently significant and is not subordinate to the necklace.
