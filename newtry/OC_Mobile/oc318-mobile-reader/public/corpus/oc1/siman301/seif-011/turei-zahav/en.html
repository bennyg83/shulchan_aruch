(א) For ornament and for use — for in the baraita it states: you said it is made for this and for this — meaning forbidden; and we interpret it: made for ornament and for use. And some hold: if for man and woman then specifically forbidden, but here permitted since in any case made for ornament; and for a man we do not decree lest he show off.

(ב) Of copper and iron, etc. — the Tur and Maharam permitted if of silver, but not iron and copper even for ornament; and in Sefer HaMitzvot it writes a key is forbidden unless made in a strap embedded in the key handle; but on a chain with a clasp holding the key — some forbid and some permit; and the Avudraham's view is to permit — end quote. Explanation: in my humble opinion there are two ways to permit: one as ornament, as Beit Yosef on the Rashba's responsum forbidding a key at the belt end — there is permission at least as ornament; second, batel to something else, such as tied at the belt end — Maharam permitted silver keys as ornament. Semag distinguished: if the key hangs on a strap — the belt is a strap whose end is embedded in the key handle so it cannot separate, glued to the key iron as one piece — then permitted because the key is batel to a long belt with a pendant, as Beit Yosef in Ashkenazic responsum they were lenient even in iron; but permission as ornament — silver chain or colored string with key held by clasp at the end — forbidden since not one piece with the chain; some permit batel to the chain as ornament, as above. And what Rama writes here that iron even fixed is forbidden — Rashba's view in the responsum Beit Yosef brought, that even at belt end it is forbidden since not needed for the vessel and not use of the vessel, not batel; those who permitted is Maharil in Ashkenazic responsum — leniency in iron and copper only in pendant; and it appears in Rashba's time they did not go with pendants, therefore he considered it a separate matter; but for us he agrees — but fixed in the middle of the belt is not batel to the belt, not related to the belt — therefore those who permit themselves to carry on Shabbat because fixed iron on the belt, and all the more what hangs by permanent knot on the belt — all this is error and they rule not according to halacha; permission only as pendant type, then batel to belt, or silver or hanging on silver chain as ornament per Rosh; in Ashkenaz they make keys and women hang them on neck chains or colored strings and go out — end quote. And it implies even iron when permission depends on chains or colored strings as ornament and key batel to them; and see above siman 303, as my humble opinion.
