Hat that spreads, etc. — Gemara: felt hat permitted, Rashi felt hat; baraita forbidden — which? That has tefach — Rashi spreads tefach below head, tent prohibition; without tefach — if spread cloak tefach, also liable? Rashi placed tallit on head as he wraps, extended tefach below — which? Tight — not tight: Rashi — not tent reason but wind may blow off and carry four amos in public domain; tight on head permitted, not tight forbidden; Rabbeinu Tam: which? Tight — meaning challenge "I spread my cloak tefach" — cloak not tight, bends down, not tent; stiff prominent tefach beyond head is tent, forbidden; so Rambam: hat with rim making shade like tent — permitted worn; if extended from garment around head or face like tent, tight, hard rim like roof — forbidden temporary tent — end quote; so Shulchan Aruch; next seif going out with hat, permits where no fall concern per Rashi; already ruled Rabbeinu Tam tent prohibition seif 41 when Shabbat entered with hat on head — divide fall concern if wants to go out; thus prohibition Shabbat in our britel: Rashi outside lest wind; Rabbeinu Tam even home tent, britel extends tefach, not "everyone prohibits work." My humble opinion: learn permission for all — no d'oraisa tent, only temporary, exempt; but rabbinic prohibition where recognizable temporary tent — hat garment on head but wide brim at garment end, appears for shade and tent; Gemara challenge spreading cloak tefach — recognition, could put tallit on head and not more, spreading tallit beyond head proves tent intent — prohibition unless soft and bends; our britel one surface, covers head in middle only, tent in what extends beyond; sometimes not tefach in front only behind — no fixed tent boundary, not proven tent; we hold R' Shimon: unintended permitted, chapter HaMevi — here intent only cover head; therefore one distressed by flies on Shabbat without britel who takes britel against flies — full prohibition; see siman 315. And it appears: since Rabbeinu Tam did not permit tallit because folded extension teaches hard tallit like grave green not folded flat — forbidden tefach beyond head like hat to Rabbeinu Tam, as my humble opinion. Rashi fall concern — Beit Yosef: Rashi forbids only stiff sun-hat; head-covering hat even loose permitted, will not carry, people do not walk bareheaded — end quote; from this pantofles permitted Shabbat, not like strict Rama above seif 16; also one with small hat under britel as customary — not go out with britel beyond eruv, certainly concern fall and carry, not bareheaded — Taz; Ari did not put kaplush on Shabbat head — Nagid uMitzvah 4; see siman 315 notes 1 and 11 more britel leniencies; see conclusion.
