151) Which projects outward. Only an extremely stiff brim that does not bend is considered a shelter; otherwise it is merely like a garment. The next se'if permits a firmly fitted hat where the brim is flexible, or where it is less than a handbreadth wide.
152) Because of making a shelter. Although a shelter normally requires walls, this brim is a rabbinically forbidden temporary shelter because it was made to shade from the sun. Later authorities offer several reasons why people wear a brim a handbreadth wide: a soft brim that bends downward is not a shelter; one intends only to cover the head, not create shade; or a sloped brim may not be a shelter. Eliyah Rabbah says people rely on Rashi and those who hold that hats never involve making a shelter. Thus, one should not protest where even a stiff, broad hat is customarily worn, but where no lenient custom exists one should follow the Mechaber and not wear it on Shabbos. Arizal is also stringent. Some avoid draping a tallis over a broad hat lest the sides resemble walls. Magen Avraham questions this custom and Machatzis HaShekel defends it. The resulting practice not to cover the head with a tallis during prayer even over a small hat has no basis. The law of carrying an umbrella on Shabbos is explained in siman 315.
