A hat. Rambam, chapter 22, writes: 'A hat with a surrounding brim that shades like a tent over the wearer may be worn. If a projection extends from the garment around his head or opposite his face like a tent, is secured upon his head, and the projecting brim is extremely stiff like a roof, it is forbidden because he forms a temporary tent.' Kol Bo writes that this applies specifically when it is stiff and does not bend. Hagahos Maimoniyos writes that our hats are permitted because they slope downward. Sefer HaTerumah implies that this is what is called a britel in Poland. See siman 315:8 and 11. Some refrain from placing a tallis over their head lest the tallis on either side form walls, as stated in siman 315. This requires examination according to them: Did all the poskim who prohibit the hat, and the Gemara itself, discuss specifically a person with a tallis over his head? Moreover, the Gemara should have answered by distinguishing between one who has a tallis over his head and one who does not. Rather, as Rosh writes in the chapter HaMeivi based upon Eruvin chapter 2, since his primary intent is to form a shelter for shade, it is forbidden even without walls. Rambam's wording is precise accordingly. It therefore appears to me that if the hat is extremely stiff, he should wear it at a slope, as stated above. Bach writes that where the practice follows Rashi leniently, it may continue. Maharil likewise permits it.
