With an amulet. See Taz and the lengthy discussion in Shabbos 61. According to Rashi, if a physician wrote three different incantations in three scrolls for three different people and they were effective, the physician is established as expert for any other incantation he writes. This resembles an ox that gored an ox, a donkey, and a camel, which becomes forewarned regarding all species. Tosafos disagree. Here it may be that he knows and is proficient only in those particular incantations, but not in others. See Rosh there. It appears that according to Tosafos and Rosh, in Rashi’s case neither the person nor the amulet is established as expert, even regarding those three incantations. Rosh’s statement, “Would he then be established regarding these?” merely intensifies the question. In truth, according to Tosafos and Rosh, those three cases establish neither the physician nor the written text. A physician is established only when he writes one incantation in three separate scrolls and heals three different people. He is then established as expert for that incantation in every scroll he writes, even in a different document. See Beit Yosef. This is also implied by the Mechaber here, who describes the physician as established only when one incantation worked three times for three people. He is then established even if he writes that incantation in other scrolls. Likewise, another person suffering from that same illness may go out wearing this particular scroll. The phrase, “but not for other incantations,” means that even if he wrote three different incantations, he is not established for other incantations; the Mechaber was concise. The Mechaber’s statement that the amulet itself is not established, so that another person may not write it, follows Rosh. Taz asks that even when this particular scroll is established, another person still may not write it. The practical difference concerns a loss of established status. While the scroll has not lost its status, another patient with the same illness may go out wearing it. In the second category, where this particular scroll worked three times, even for one person, another patient with the same illness may go out wearing it, but the physician is not established to write the same incantation in another scroll. When both the person and the amulet are established, a practical difference may arise if the physician loses his established status, since this scroll still remains effective. Conversely, if the scroll loses its status because it was tried on three people and failed, perhaps the physician’s efficacy remains and he may write it in another scroll. Magen Avraham, seif katan 38, writes similarly. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained further there.
That the uncertainty of Rav Pappa is ruled stringently indicates that going out into a public domain with a non expert amulet may involve a Torah prohibition. See Magen Avraham, siman 308, seif katan 57. If it certainly does not heal, it is a burden and one is liable to bring a sin offering. The reason one who went out with it is exempt is that perhaps it does heal. Therefore, Rav Pappa’s uncertainty is treated stringently in a public domain, because it concerns a possible Torah prohibition. Although there appears to be a compound uncertainty, perhaps it was effective and three written copies of one incantation for one person establish the physician, and even if this does not establish him, perhaps the amulet nevertheless heals, one may answer that both possibilities are aspects of a single uncertainty. In a karmelis, however, such a case may be viewed as an unresolved D’Rabbanan question and permitted, even according to Magen Avraham in siman 308, seif katan 57. Where a doubtful amulet is prohibited even in a karmelis, the reason is that ordinarily an amulet not established as expert does not heal, and in such a case we do not apply the rule that a D’Rabbanan doubt is treated leniently.
The Mechaber’s statement in seif 26 that the physician is believed to say that he is an expert comes from the Yerushalmi. The same applies if he says that this particular scroll has been established, because one witness is believed regarding prohibitions. See Penei Yehoshua to Shabbos, who discusses a case where the prohibition has been established and suggests that one witness may not be believed against a presumption. In our novellae we wrote that according to the view that a D’Rabbanan doubt is treated leniently even against a presumption, namely Rabbi Yose in Mikvaos concerning an impure person who descended to immerse and a doubt arose, where in a lesser impurity he is pure, certainly one witness is believed against a presumption regarding a definite D’Rabbanan prohibition. Here, a non expert amulet involves a definite D’Rabbanan prohibition even in a karmelis, because ordinarily it is ineffective, yet the physician says that it is expert. To permit carrying it in a public domain against the established presumption may also be allowed according to the view that a non expert amulet involves only a D’Rabbanan prohibition, as Rambam writes in chapter 19 of Hilchos Shabbos, halachah 14, since it is worn as clothing. Tosafos likewise write this regarding Shabbos, siman 308, seif katan 86. See Eliyah Rabbah here, seif katan 44, and Responsa Maharshal, siman 47.
