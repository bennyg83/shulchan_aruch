Se'if katan 15: Pierced. There is a scribal error in Beis Yosef. In the edition before us it has already been corrected. Magen Avraham wrote this according to the version of Beis Yosef available to him and Bach. See Taz, who cites Bach's version and likewise says it contains a scribal error.
Bach writes that nevertheless... The first Mishnah of Bameh Ishah says that a woman may not go out with a ring lacking a seal or an unpierced needle; if she does, she is not liable to a sin-offering. The Mishnah on page 62 says that she may not go out with a pierced needle or a sealed ring; if she does, she is liable. Ulla says, 'Their rules are reversed for a man.' The poskim dispute what Ulla meant. All agree he certainly referred to rings: For a man, an unsealed ring incurs liability and a sealed ring is exempt. Whether it is initially permitted is disputed. Rashi forbids it; Rabbeinu Tam and Rambam permit it initially. The concern that a wearer may remove an ornament to show it and carry it four amos applies to women, who are ostentatious; a man does not ordinarily show off his jewelry. They agree, however, that an item serving as jewelry for both men and women is forbidden to a man too, because the Sages made no distinction, as stated in se'if 9 and in the Yerushalmi cited below in se'if katan 27. The dispute is whether Ulla also referred to needles. Rif and those with him say he did: A man is liable for an unpierced needle and exempt for a pierced one. Rashi says Ulla did not refer to needles. Maggid Mishneh understands Rashi to equate men and women regarding needles. These are the two views in Shulchan Aruch: The first is Rashi and Maggid Mishneh; the reversed view is Rif. Bach explains Maggid Mishneh's equality to mean that both a pierced and an unpierced needle are exempt for a man. The Mishnah's liability for a woman's pierced needle applies only when she places it opposite her forehead, which is the ordinary manner of carrying described in siman 303:9; see Magen Avraham there. This does not apply to a man, who is therefore always exempt. Magen Avraham consequently cites Bach that no view makes a man liable for a pierced needle: Rif exempts because the rules are reversed; Rashi and Maggid Mishneh exempt because even a woman is liable only when it is placed opposite her forehead. Magen Avraham responds that this is not compelled. Rashi and Maggid Mishneh may mean that men and women are equally exempt for an unpierced needle and equally liable for a pierced one.
As stated in Shabbos 92a: One who carries a burden on his head is exempt, because that is not the ordinary manner of carrying. Even a resident of Hotzal who carries a burden on his head is exempt. Although the people of Hotzal commonly carry burdens on their heads during the week, their practice is disregarded in relation to that of everyone else. See below, siman 320.
