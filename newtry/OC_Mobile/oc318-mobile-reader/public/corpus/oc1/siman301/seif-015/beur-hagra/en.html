Se'if 15: That is, that he makes. Tosafos in the name of Rashi.
And he does not make this in order to walk with it, etc., one whose foot is disabled. This is the explanation of Rabbeinu Porat there, folio 65b, in Tosafos, s.v. 'the amputee.'
For in any event he needs his cane. This follows the explanation of Rabbeinu Yitzchak there. Rabbeinu Yerucham combined the explanations. It appears that although according to Rabbeinu Porat's explanation he does not need a cane, as written in Tosafos there, and according to this there is no proof, etc., since he explains that there is a support that he ties to his leg, etc., nevertheless one may also explain it without a support, but with his cane, according to what Tosafos proved there, that even according to his explanation it is permitted to go out with a cane; see there. More likely it appears to me that even though they wrote in explaining Rabbeinu Porat that he walks on the support and does not need a cane, etc., that was only according to the initial assumption. But Tosafos in Yoma 79a wrote that according to what is said about supports on both legs, he necessarily needs a cane. The difference between him and Rabbeinu Yitzchak's explanation is only that according to Rabbeinu Yitzchak he must lean slightly upon it, whereas according to Rabbeinu Porat's explanation he does not need that at all.
Since it is not. This follows Tosafos' explanation there, that it is because it may slip off, as in the discussion in Yoma 78b; and there they write, 'and Rabbeinu Yitzchak says,' etc., and 'his main walking,' etc. But according to Rashi's explanation, the reason is that it is not a shoe, and even for a healthy person it would be forbidden. He should have said, 'since it is possible without this,' to exclude a cane and the supports of a chair. So too it is clear there in Rabbeinu Yerucham, who follows Tosafos' explanation that the concern is that it may slip, for he discusses a wooden shoe and writes that according to the discussion in Shabbos it is forbidden, and only according to the discussion in Yoma is it permitted. But if even according to Rashi, which follows the discussion in Shabbos, as Tosafos in Yoma write; see there, the reason is also that it is not needed for walking, then even according to the discussion in Shabbos a wooden shoe should be permitted, since it is needed for his walking.
