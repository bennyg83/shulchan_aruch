Rama in siman 338 forbids knocking with a door ring even when the sound is not musical, because it is an instrument designated to produce sound. Nevertheless, one must desire the sound, whereas an adult wearing these bells has no interest in their sound. That is the second difficulty; the first concerns Rama's source in Hagahos Alfasi. Hagahos Alfasi appears to require a musical sound in the case of whistling: He permits it both because the person is merely calling a companion and the sound is not melodic, and because he performs no action. Rama may rely on the second reason, that no action is performed, even though we rule that any desired sound is forbidden. Hagahos Alfasi's first reason, however, implies that only musical sound is forbidden. Perhaps there is a distinction between a designated instrument and sound made with the mouth. This requires study, and Eliyah Rabbah raises the issue. Rama in siman 339:3 is lenient nowadays concerning clapping and dancing because people are not expert in making instruments; in siman 338:2 he also permits having a non-Jew play an instrument. Accordingly, nowadays one may be lenient even with sounding bells, because their prohibition is only a safeguard lest one repair an instrument.
