Having an eye. Beis Yosef contains a scribal error here, but Shulchan Aruch is written correctly. Bach writes that no opinion holds a man liable for an eyed needle; see there. This is not compelled. The general principle is that everything depends upon contemporary practice. If people ordinarily go out with it this way during the week and it is not an ornament, this is its normal mode of carrying and he is liable. If it is not ordinarily carried this way during the week, he is exempt, but the act is forbidden because it is not an ornament. The authorities disagree only about which kind of needle was ordinarily carried in Talmudic times. If most people do not carry it this way, even though people of another locale do, they are exempt because their practice is disregarded relative to that of everyone else, as the Gemara, page 92, states.
