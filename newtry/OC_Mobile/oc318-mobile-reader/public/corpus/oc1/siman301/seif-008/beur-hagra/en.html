Se'if 8: With a perforated one he is liable. This follows Rashi's explanation; the Maggid Mishneh and the Rosh write in the name of most poskim that Ulla refers only to a ring.
And some say the reverse. This is the view of the Rif and the Rambam, who explained it also regarding a needle. It is difficult with the Rambam, for he wrote regarding a ring that if it has a seal, a man is permitted to go out with it initially, as written in se'if 9 and above in se'if 4; yet here he wrote that with a perforated needle he is exempt. It is also difficult according to them why the Gemara did not also answer regarding a needle to the objection, 'But this is carrying,' etc. It appears that certainly their reason regarding a needle is not that it is an ornament, for neither one is an ornament; even regarding a woman they said, 'For what is it fit on Shabbos?' etc., see Tosafos there, and this is as the Rosh challenged the Rif here. Rather, the reason is that this is the usual manner of carrying and this is not the usual manner of carrying. One must distinguish this way, for otherwise there is a difficulty from what is written on folio 11b, as Tosafos asked there, s.v. 'and if.' One must say that for a woman this is her usual manner of carrying, and this distinction is explicit in the Yerushalmi, which says there: There Rabbi Meir said that a tailor may not go out with his needle, etc.; if he went out he is exempt, and here he says he is liable. There it is the normal manner of carrying, etc. This is exactly Tosafos' question and answer, and this forced them to say that Ulla also refers to a needle, for necessarily a man is exempt with a perforated needle. This is why they challenged Ulla, 'But this is carrying,' etc., not the mishnah; they challenged him from his own words, for he distinguished, regarding a man, between one needle and another because of carrying in an unusual manner. The Rambam's wording is also precise: regarding a ring he wrote that one with a seal is an ornament for a man and not for a woman, and one without a seal, etc.; but regarding a needle he wrote that a woman who went out with a perforated needle is liable and a man is exempt, while a man who went out with an unperforated needle is liable and a woman is exempt because it is one of her ornaments, etc. He did not also write regarding a man that the reason is because it is an ornament. According to Rashi and the other commentators, who hold that one is liable with a perforated needle, one must say as the Magen Avraham writes in s.k. 19. But it appears that Rashi's view is like the Rosh, that for a man he is exempt with both kinds, and therefore he explained the passage only regarding a ring; the Gemara's question and answer forced him to do so, as above.
