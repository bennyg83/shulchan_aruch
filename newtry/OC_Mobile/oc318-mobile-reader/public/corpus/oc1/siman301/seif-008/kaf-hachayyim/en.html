45) [Se'if 8] He should not go out with a needle, etc. This passage does not concern a craftsman; the law of a craftsman is explained nearby in se'if 12. Beis Yosef; Olas Shabbos 11. Regarding a woman, see below, siman 303:9.
46) There: Inserted into his garment, etc. If carried in the hand, however, both a man and a woman are liable in every case, whether the needle has an eye or does not. Magen Avraham 14; Eliyah Rabbah 18; Tosefes Shabbos 17. The later authorities write likewise.
47) There: Inserted into his garment, etc. It requires examination whether a man may go out with a needle inserted to join the edges of his robe and garments. See below, siman 303, laws 9 and 18, which imply that this is permitted specifically for a woman. See Beis Yosef in this siman regarding coins sewn into a garment: since his intent is not to carry the coin but to fasten his garment, the coin is subordinate to the garment. Mahara Azulai, of blessed memory, in his manuscript glosses; Birkei Yosef 2. See Shulchan Aruch HaRav 4 and 5, however, who writes that even when the needle is inserted for purposes of wearing, if it has an eye he is liable to a sin-offering, and if it has no eye he is exempt but the act is forbidden. All this concerns a man; regarding a woman, see siman 303.
48) There: Whether it has an eye, etc. Beis Yosef contains a scribal error here, but Shulchan Aruch is written correctly. Magen Avraham 15. See Taz 5 for his emendation and resolution of Beis Yosef's words. Regarding Bach's statement that no opinion holds a man liable for an eyed needle, Magen Avraham writes that this is not compelled. Taz similarly writes that Bach needlessly described Beis Yosef and Shulchan Aruch here as the words of an erring student. Everything is coherent; only Beis Yosef requires emendation. The general principle is that everything depends upon contemporary practice. If it is ordinarily carried this way during the week and is not an ornament, this is its normal mode of carrying and he is liable. If it is not ordinarily carried this way during the week, he is exempt, but the act is forbidden because it is not an ornament. The authorities disagree only about which kind of needle was ordinarily carried in Talmudic times. If most people do not carry it this way, even though people in one locale do, they are exempt because their practice is disregarded relative to that of everyone else, as the Gemara, page 52, states. Magen Avraham, ibid.; Eliyah Rabbah 19; Tosefes Shabbos 17.
48) Tosefes Shabbos writes there: If they have a reason for their practice, and that same reason would lead most people to act likewise were it relevant to them, we do not say in such a case that their practice is disregarded relative to everyone else's; see there. See below, siman 320:1.
49) There: Some say the opposite. According to Rosh, whether the needle has an eye or does not, one who goes out with it is exempt because this is carrying in an unusual manner. Since it is not his practice to go out with a needle, he carries unlike ordinary carriers and is exempt, as Tur and Beis Yosef explain; see there. Perishah 7 writes that he is exempt even if he carries it in his hand. This is not the poskim's view, as stated above in paragraph 46.
