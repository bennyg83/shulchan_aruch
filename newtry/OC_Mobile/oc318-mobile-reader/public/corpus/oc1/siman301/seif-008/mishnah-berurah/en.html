30) He may not go out. This se'if applies even to an ordinary person who is not a craftsman. See below, se'if 12.
31) In his garment. If he carries it in his hand, however, both a man and a woman are liable in every case, whether the needle is pierced or unpierced.
32) Whether it is unpierced. A needle is not an ornament for a man under any circumstances, so according to all views its carriage is prohibited by Torah law.
33) With a pierced needle he is liable. This view holds that a man's law is like a woman's below in siman 303:9: at times the normal manner of carrying a pierced needle is to stick it in one's garment. The ruling below in se'if 12 that even a craftsman is exempt is explained there in Mishnah Berurah. For an unpierced needle he is exempt, because it is normally carried by hand, not stuck in a garment. The opposite view holds that a man does not normally carry a pierced needle in his garment, since it is demeaning to be called a tailor, whereas he may sometimes stick an unpierced needle into his garment on a weekday after finding it in the street, when he needs it to pick his teeth or the like. Magen Avraham concludes that everything depends on contemporary practice: if people normally carry it this way on weekdays and it is not an ornament, one is liable; if they do not, he is exempt but it remains forbidden. If most people do not carry it this way, the practice of one locality does not create liability, because their practice is disregarded relative to that of everyone else.
