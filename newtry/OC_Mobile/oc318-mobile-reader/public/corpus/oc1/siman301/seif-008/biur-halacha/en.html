He should not go out with a needle, etc. If he goes out with an eyed needle, he is liable. This is Maggid Mishneh's opinion, cited by Beis Yosef. The final view is that of Rif and Rambam. A third view is that of Rosh and Tur, who exempt a man in both cases; Shulchan Aruch omits it. See Tosefes Shabbos for the reason. Beis Yosef inclines to say that Rashi equates a man and woman for both an eyed and eyeless needle. Gra, however, inclines to understand Rashi like Rosh and Tur: A man is exempt but prohibited whether the needle has an eye or does not. According to this, Rif, Rambam, Rashi, Rosh, and Tur all agree that he is exempt for an eyed needle. This also neatly resolves the difficulty from se'if 12, which Magen Avraham himself raises in se'if katan 19. His answer there is not satisfactory, as Chemed Moshe, Nehar Shalom, and Pri Megadim object.
For an eyeless needle he is exempt. If he needs the needle to join the edges of his garments, it requires examination whether this is permitted for a man. See below, siman 303:9: A woman may use an eyeless needle to secure her fastenings. Magen Avraham explains there that it is needed for wearing; apparently the same should apply to a man. According to the second opinion, however, which makes a man liable for an eyeless needle, perhaps this need does not permit it. The case of a woman is different, since all opinions exempt her for an eyeless needle. Just as this need does not permit an eyed needle for a woman, it may not permit an eyeless needle for a man, since both incur a sin-offering. One can distinguish: An eyed needle is not ordinarily used as a fastener because it slips out, whereas an eyeless needle may be considered an ordinary mode of wearing. This requires examination.
