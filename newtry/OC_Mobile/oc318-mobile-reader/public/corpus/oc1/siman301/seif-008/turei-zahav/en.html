One may not go out with a machat, etc. — in the Mishnah chapter Mah Ishah it states regarding a woman: she may not go out with a machat that is not pierced, and if she went out she is not liable for a chatas; and it states further there: she may not go out with a pierced machat nor with a ring that has a seal upon it, and if she went out she is liable. Ulla said: and their laws are reversed for a man. The Rif explained: if a man went out with an unpierced machat or a ring without a seal he is liable; and he challenged: carrying out kele'acher yad applies when he takes the ring on his finger, since that is his way to carry it out — but anything that is not an ornament he carries in his hand, and if so he is not liable since he did not carry it out in the usual way of carriers. Rava answered: sometimes a man gives his wife a ring with a seal to bring to a box and leaves it in his hand that entire time; and likewise a woman who gives her husband a ring without a seal to bring to a craftsman leaves it on his finger that time — and so ruled Rambam chapter 19. The Rosh wrote on this: I wonder at the Rif — how can a man be liable for an unpierced machat as kele'acher yad? And furthermore, with a ring one can say "and their laws are reversed for a man," for with a woman without a seal it is an ornament and with a seal it is a burden, and with a man the opposite; but with a machat, whether pierced or unpierced, it is a burden for a man — therefore it appears as Rashi explained "and their laws are reversed for a man" that liable for a chatas refers to a ring without a seal, not to a machat — end quote. And it appears from his words that his conclusion is: for a man, pierced and unpierced are equal — both exempt but forbidden, because it is kele'acher yad — meaning our case where the machat is stuck in the garment, as the Tur wrote; and since it is not his way, for it is a burden for him, there is no liability unless he took it in his hand, and then he is certainly liable for anything. And a woman is liable with a pierced machat even though stuck in her garment, for such is her constant way to use an unpierced machat in scarves or to fasten a robe, as in siman 303 — therefore he says in explanation of the Mishnah: if she went out with a pierced one she is liable, for it is a burden for her and a way of carrying out. Beit Yosef wrote to explain the Rosh's words "not a way of carrying out" — meaning since it is not his way to carry out an unpierced machat, he is one who carries out not in carriers' way and is exempt; and it is astonishing, for then anyone who carries something not in his usual way would be exempt — rather certainly as he wrote: exempt when he did not carry in his hand, but if he carried in his hand he is liable for anything. We find the Rosh's view to explain Rashi: for a man, whether pierced or unpierced — exempt but forbidden; exempt because he did not carry in carriers' way in his hand but stuck in his garment, and forbidden because in any case it is a burden; and for a woman, with a pierced one it is a burden and a way of carrying out, therefore liable; with an unpierced one, which is her ornament, forbidden lest she show her friend. Nevertheless Magen Avraham wrote that the Geonim wrote like Rambam, except they disagreed regarding machatim, whose law is equal for women and men, and Ulla spoke only of rings; and Rashi explained thus — if so, for him a man is literally like a woman, that we explained there: with pierced liable, unpierced exempt but forbidden; from "equal for women" we learn women's law is simple and men's is the same; therefore the simple law for women is what the Mishnah states by them — liability with pierced; thus Beit Yosef wrote well that there are three disputes, except there is a scribal error in his words in three places: first he wrote the Rif's explanation that a man with pierced machat is liable and unpierced exempt — it should be reversed: pierced exempt, unpierced liable, as explicit in the Rif itself and in the Rosh's citation of the Rif; and he wrote in the Rabbenu Chananel view: it appears he holds that for Rashi a man with unpierced machat is liable for chatas like a woman — it should be with pierced machat, for a woman's liability is explicit in the Mishnah with pierced; and afterward he wrote in the three disputes: according to Rabbenu Chananel, with unpierced liable and pierced exempt — it should be: with pierced liable, unpierced exempt. Thus are the three disputes: Rif and Rambam — man liable with unpierced, exempt with pierced; Rosh — both exempt; Rabbenu Chananel — pierced liable, unpierced exempt. And what Shulchan Aruch writes here in the first view, if he went out with pierced he is liable — that is Rabbenu Chananel's view that man's law is like woman's with machat; and "some say the opposite" is Rif and Rambam, that for a man the law is reversed from woman also in machat, therefore pierced exempt and unpierced liable. And Mateh Moshe wrote in vain that Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch here are a mistaken student — rather all fits, only needs emendation as written; and woman's law with machat and ring is in siman 303 — see there.
