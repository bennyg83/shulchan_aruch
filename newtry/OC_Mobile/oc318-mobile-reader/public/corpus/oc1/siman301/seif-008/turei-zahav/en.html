He should not go out with a needle, etc. In the Mishnah in the chapter Bameh Ishah it says regarding a woman: she may not go out with an unpierced needle, and if she went out she is not liable to bring a chattas. It further says there: she may not go out with a pierced needle nor with a ring that has a seal, and if she went out she is liable. Ulla said: and the reverse applies to a man. The Rif explains this to mean that if a man went out with an unpierced needle or with a ring that has no seal, he is liable. The Gemara asks that this is carrying in an unusual manner, by taking the ring out on his finger, since it is not his way to carry it that way. Rather, anything that is not an ornament for him he carries in his hand, and if so he should not be liable, since he did not carry it in the way people carry. Rava answers that sometimes a man gives his wife a ring that has a seal to take to a box, and she places it on her hand in the meantime; likewise, a woman gives her husband a ring without a seal to take to a craftsman, and in the meantime he places it on his finger. This is also the view of the Rambam in chapter 19. The Rosh wrote about this: I am astonished at the Rif, how can the man be liable for an unpierced needle? It is carrying in an unusual manner. Furthermore, granted with a ring one can say ‘the reverse applies to a man,’ for by a woman a ring without a seal is an ornament and one with a seal is a burden, while by a man it is the reverse. But regarding a needle, whether pierced or unpierced, it is a burden for a man. Therefore it appears like Rashi, who explained that ‘the reverse applies to a man’ regarding liability to bring a chattas refers to the ring without a seal and not to the needle. It appears from his words that his conclusion is that by a man, pierced and unpierced needles are alike: in both he is exempt but it is forbidden, because it is carrying in an unusual manner, namely where the needle is stuck in his garment, as the Tur wrote. Since this is not his way, for it is a burden to him, there is no liability unless he carried it in his hand, and in that case he is certainly liable for any object in the world. But by a woman, she is liable for a pierced needle, and although it is stuck in her garment, that is always her way to use an unpierced needle with scarves or the opening of her garment, as stated in siman 303. Therefore the Mishnah says that if she went out with a pierced one she is liable, for it is a burden to her and it is the manner of carrying. The Beit Yosef explained the Rosh’s words, ‘it is not the manner of carrying,’ as meaning that since it is not his way to carry an unpierced needle, he is considered to carry not in the manner of those who carry and is exempt. This is astonishing to me, for if so, anyone who carries something that is not his way to carry would be exempt. Rather, it is certainly as I wrote: he is exempt as long as he did not carry it in his hand, but if he carried it in his hand he is liable for anything. According to the Rosh’s view following Rashi, by a man, whether with a pierced or unpierced needle, he is exempt but forbidden: exempt because he did not carry it in the manner of those who carry, in his hand, but stuck in his garment; and forbidden because in any event it is a burden, and although it is not in the manner of those who carry, it is initially forbidden. By a woman, with a pierced needle it is a burden and the manner of carrying, and therefore she is liable; with an unpierced needle, which is an ornament for her, it is forbidden lest she show it to her friend. The Maggid Mishneh wrote that the Geonim wrote like the Rambam, except that they disagreed regarding needles, whose law is the same for women and men, and Ulla spoke only regarding rings; so Rashi explained, etc. If so, according to him, a man is exactly like a woman. Regarding a woman, the Mishnah explicitly taught that with a pierced needle she is liable and with an unpierced one she is exempt but forbidden. Since he said that men are the same as women, the law of women is plain, and the plain law of women is what the Mishnah taught concerning them, liability with a pierced needle. Thus the Beit Yosef correctly wrote that there are three disputes in the matter, but there are textual errors in three places in his words. First he wrote in explaining the Rif that if a man goes out with a pierced needle he is liable and with an unpierced one he is exempt; it should be the reverse: with a pierced one he is exempt and with an unpierced one he is liable, as is explicit in the Rif himself and in the Rosh’s citation of the Rif. Then he wrote regarding the view of the Maggid Mishneh that it appears he holds that according to Rashi, a man who goes out with an unpierced needle is liable to bring a chattas like a woman; it should read ‘with a pierced needle,’ for by a woman her liability is explicit in the Mishnah with a pierced needle. Afterward he wrote regarding the three disputes, according to the Maggid Mishneh, that with an unpierced needle he is liable and with a pierced one exempt; it should read: with a pierced one he is liable and with an unpierced one exempt. Thus the three disputes are these: according to the Rif and Rambam, by a man he is liable with an unpierced needle and exempt with a pierced one; according to the Rosh, whether pierced or unpierced he is exempt; and according to the Maggid Mishneh, with a pierced one he is liable and with an unpierced one exempt. What is written here in the Shulchan Aruch in the first opinion, that if he went out with a pierced one he is liable, is the view of the Maggid Mishneh, that the law of a man is like that of a woman regarding a needle. The ‘there are those who say the reverse’ is the view of the Rif and Rambam, that by a man it is the reverse of a woman also regarding a needle, and automatically he is exempt with a pierced one and liable with an unpierced one. My father-in-law wrote for no reason that the words of the Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch here are the words of a mistaken student. Rather, everything is settled, only it must be emended as I wrote. The law of a woman regarding needle and ring is written in siman 303; see there.
