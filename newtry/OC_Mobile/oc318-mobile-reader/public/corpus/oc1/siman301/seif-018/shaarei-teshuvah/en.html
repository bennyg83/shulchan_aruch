With a staff. See Baer Heitev. Magen Avraham, seif katan 27, explains Tur’s statement that one may go out in a chair as referring to a place with an eruv. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, first edition, Orach Chaim, where he writes that Magen Avraham said this only to resolve Tur against Beit Yosef’s question. According to Rif and Rambam, however, this distinction is inapplicable on Shabbos. In a place with an eruv, carrying a person is permitted even when the public does not need him. He therefore rules that in a walled city with an eruv, where there is no prohibition of carrying, one may be transported in a chair called a patschiza, which is carried between the hands of two people, even when the public does not need him. See there, and in Noda BiYehudah, second edition, siman 28, which I cited above in seif katan 17. Regarding Shulchan Aruch, seif 38, concerning one who goes out wearing a tallis, and where it has valid tzitzis, see Responsa Rif, siman 299; Zaken Aharon, siman 134; and Responsa Radbaz, part 4, siman 618. They rule leniently to permit going out even on Friday night, since this is his ordinary manner of wearing the garment during the week. See there. See Shaagas Aryeh, siman 108, concerning one whose tzitzis broke in a public domain while his fellow noticed it. His fellow is obligated to inform him that he must remove the garment, and this obligation is not displaced because of human dignity. See what I wrote above in siman 13 concerning this matter.
