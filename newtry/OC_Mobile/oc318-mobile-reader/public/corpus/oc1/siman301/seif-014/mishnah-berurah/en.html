52) If he does not wear it, etc. This means that he wears it over his clothes solely to keep them clean; it is nevertheless permitted. The gloss's later examples of a rain garment or hat similarly include wearing an additional coarse, heavy garment over one's clothes only because of rain, or a large hat over one's regular hat to protect it. Since it is worn in the manner of clothing, it is permitted. Beis Yosef at the end of siman 301; Shiltei Gibborim.
53) A cloth over her head covering. This means that she merely places it on her head, like a small piece of linen, rather than wearing it. If she also wraps part of her body in it, it is clothing and is permitted even to protect from soiling. This applies even to an inferior cloth such as sackcloth, as below in se'if 21, and even for a wealthy woman, because an item fit as clothing for the poor is also considered clothing for the wealthy.
54) For this is not, etc. If her intent is to prevent the rain from causing her discomfort, it is permitted in every case, even though it also protects her clothes from becoming dirty, provided she ties it securely.
55) Clothing. Therefore, a man may not cover his hat with a kerchief. Chayei Adam.
