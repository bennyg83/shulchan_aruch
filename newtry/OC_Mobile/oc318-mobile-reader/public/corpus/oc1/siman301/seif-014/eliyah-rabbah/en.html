[32] [Levush] "It is a burden," etc. Beit Yosef writes this in the name of Rashi and Rabbeinu Yeruham. Malbushei Yom Tov expresses surprise that he did not cite Tosafos, who disagree with Rashi on the basis of an explicit Gemara in Yoma, where the reason is that the item may slip off and he may come to carry it 4 amot in a public domain. If so, the prohibition is only rabbinic. I would add that Ran there, Kol Bo, Hagahot Ashiri, Responsa Rashba, siman 607, and Semag write similarly. Semag explains that because it is made of wood and straw it cannot be tightened securely around the foot, and it may fall off and the wearer may come to carry it. This makes Beit Yosef's statement that Semag follows Rashi especially difficult. It also appears that Rashi may have given his explanation only according to the initial understanding of the Gemara, whereas according to the conclusion he agrees with the other explanation. Alternatively, Rashi may mean only that it is not considered an ornament because it cannot be fastened in the normal manner of an ornament. [Levush] "However, if the stump," etc. The Shulchan Aruch omitted this ruling. It appears to be included in what he wrote in seif 16 regarding a pantish, as is implied by Rashba's responsum there.
