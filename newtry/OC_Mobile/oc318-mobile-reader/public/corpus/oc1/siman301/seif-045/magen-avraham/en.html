He should not spread them out. This implies specifically when they were soaked in water. If only a small amount of water fell upon them, one may spread them out, since with so little water there is no concern of laundering. Sefer Yereim somewhat implies this. Moreover, in siman 302:9 some permit initially placing a small amount of water upon them. Se'if 47, however, somewhat implies that it is forbidden, since even perspiration is forbidden.
Even in complete privacy. This follows Rav. Shiltei HaGibborim, chapter Chavis, challenges this from siman 305:11, which permits an animal to walk in a courtyard wearing a bell but not in the public domain because it appears to be going to market. Shabbos 65 states that according to Rav this is forbidden even in a courtyard. Beis Yosef, siman 336:9, writes in Tosafos' name that an act is forbidden in complete privacy only where observers would suspect him of a Torah prohibition; see there. The difficulty is therefore resolved. It remains difficult, however, why the Gemara in Shabbos 65 did not answer this way. Perhaps Tosafos holds that the passage in Shabbos was rejected because of the passage in Kesubos. See Ran, first chapter of Beitzah.
