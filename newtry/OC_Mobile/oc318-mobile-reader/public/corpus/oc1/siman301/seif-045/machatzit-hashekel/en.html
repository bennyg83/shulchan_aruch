Rama permits pierced coins even when they are not bundled together; all the more so are bundled coins permitted even if they are not pierced. According to Rama, coins that are neither bundled nor pierced are forbidden. The 'some permit' cited from Maharil appears to permit even coins that are neither bundled nor pierced, provided they are not carried directly in one's hand.
As stated in siman 334:2, a person is frantic over his money, and if the Sages did not permit him to rescue it he might come to violate a Torah prohibition. Bach explains that the concern is that he may dig in the ground to hide it. Although se'if 9 permits rescue only into a courtyard with an eruv, Maharil follows Rama's lenient view permitting rescue even into a courtyard without an eruv and extends that view to this case outside the eruv.
Maharil's expression 'carry them with him' implies that carrying the coins directly in one's hand is forbidden even when they are pierced.
Where there is danger; for example, the ruler's agents are searching for the money; one may carry it even beyond the eruv because the owner is frantic over it. All the more may one move the muktzeh coins by hand when there is no other way, as in siman 334:2.
