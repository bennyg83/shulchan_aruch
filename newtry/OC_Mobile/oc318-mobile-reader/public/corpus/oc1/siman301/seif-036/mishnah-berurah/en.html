132) Even for another person's need. Although he derives no benefit, it does not appear to be a burden because it is worn in the manner of clothing.
133) Some forbid two belts. Unlike two garments worn for warmth, a second belt provides no benefit and is not ordinarily worn, so it appears to be a burden; all agree the prohibition is rabbinic. Wearing a kerchief as a second belt is certainly forbidden, and Chayei Adam says even the first view forbids it because the evasion is evident, even in a karmelis. Taz advises tying its end with a bow to the belt's end, making one long belt. Alternatively, one may wear the kerchief as a belt over a different garment from the first belt. One may not tie it around the leg, which is not a manner of clothing, but may wrap it around the neck as clothing.
134) One over the other. Where people customarily wear an inner and outer belt because the first is unattractive and the second decorative, or the second is short, it is permitted. Peri Megadim. Chayei Adam says not to protest where this is customary. Later authorities also permit women to wear one belt normally and another to raise a wide garment from mud, since the second serves a need.
() And likewise proper to practice ; therefore on the upper mantle, if he wants to belt over the lower mantle, he should remove the sewn buttons; nevertheless not a burden, batel to garment after sewn, as above seif 23; not so if he belts with both, the belt is like a burden [Peri Megadim].
136) Two hats. This is permitted because on weekdays he normally places a large hat over a small one. Or Zarua.
137) And likewise two stockings. Two on each leg, like wearing two outer garments.
