Se'if 36: It is permitted to go out. Shabbos 120a: he may wear all, etc. And in Eruvin 95b we say: there it is in the manner of wearing, etc.
Whether for a need. Folio 147b: the bath attendants, etc.; and from the law of felt-makers, folio 146b.
Two shirts. From the case of sachnisa, folio 147b; see Tosafos there, s.v. 'he needs,' etc. Although it helps him not at all, the same applies to shirts, and all the more so to cloaks.
Whether, etc. As above: 'he wears,' etc., and 'wraps himself,' etc.
And there are those who forbid. So he learned from Rabbi Yose there, folio 120a. Although we do not rule like Rabbi Yose there, that is because they permitted it due to a fire; otherwise Rabbi Meir concedes to Rabbi Yose. This follows Rashi's first explanation on folio 59b, s.v. 'two.'
Unless. Tosafos there, folio 59b, from the fact that on folio 120a it lists a money-belt and a belt. The first reasoning is like Rashi's second explanation mentioned above.
And it is permitted. There, regarding the eighteen utensils of Rabbi Yose; see Tosafos there, and as above.
And likewise two, etc. According to the explanation above this requires study, for there it lists only one bell. Rather, he does not accept the proof above that we rule like Rabbi Meir; instead he explains 'two belts,' etc., as a prohibition.
