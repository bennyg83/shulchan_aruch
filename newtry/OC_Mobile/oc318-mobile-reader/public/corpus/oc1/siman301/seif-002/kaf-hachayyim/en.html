9) [Se'if 2] Young men who derive pleasure from jumping, etc. This means youths playing together. Agudah, cited by Eliyah Rabbah 5.
10) There: Their running is permitted. Bach writes that this was permitted only after the fact, since they had already adopted the practice of running and jumping for pleasure on Shabbos. If we protest, we deprive them of their Shabbos pleasure. Initially, however, if they ask, permission should not be granted; see there. Magen Avraham 3 writes that it is permitted even initially, unlike Bach, whose interpretation is forced.
11) On Simchas Torah it is permitted to dance, and likewise at a wedding celebration in honor of the groom and bride. Shas, fifth chapter of Beitzah; Eliyah Rabbah 5.
12) There: Likewise, to see anything, etc. This means that one may also run or leap in order to see something from which people derive pleasure. Bach. Olas Shabbos 3 explains likewise. Taz 1, however, writes that one may not run in order to see it; the ruling permits only the viewing itself, meaning that one may see on Shabbos something he enjoys. Magen Avraham 4 explains similarly: Although one may not look at images, as stated in siman 307:15, he may look if the viewing gives him pleasure. Eliyah Rabbah 6, however, explains like Bach. Chemed Moshe 2 and Maamar Mordechai write likewise, and this appears to be the view of the later authorities.
13) There, in the gloss: It is likewise permitted to stroll, etc. This means that his intent is to exercise and warm himself through it; he may not run to warm himself if he does so for medical treatment. Magen Avraham 5. Eliyah Rabbah 6, however, writes that Levush implies that even if he walks rather than runs, it is forbidden when his intent is exercise. He writes that Levush's position appears correct, for it is implied by Tosefta and Rama; see there. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 5, writes that Levush's position is not compelled and Magen Avraham's interpretation may be accepted; see there. Pischei HaDevir 3 likewise challenges Eliyah Rabbah and agrees with Magen Avraham; see there. Tosefes Shabbos 5 appears to agree.
14) There, in the gloss: It is likewise permitted to stroll, etc. A responsum at the end of Chazeh HaTenufah states that strolling on Shabbos in an alley with an eruv without a head covering or outer cloak depends upon local custom. If the custom is not to stroll this way, it is a praiseworthy custom and should not be treated leniently, as proven by Pesachim 50b, chapter Makom Shenahagu. Beis Yosef. In my humble circumstances, these responsa reached me in manuscript. Chazeh HaTenufah is an abridgment of Rosh's responsa. At its end is an abridgment of responsa of Rosh that were not printed. This responsum is number 55, where the text contains a variation and addition: 'If this is their custom, it is a praiseworthy custom and should not be treated leniently, for it is a proper custom to make one's Shabbos manner of wrapping different from that of weekdays. The custom of our ancestors is Torah. Proof comes from the chapter Makom Shenahagu.' Machazik Berachah 2.
