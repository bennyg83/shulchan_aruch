And likewise to see anything they enjoy — in the Tur he concluded after this that it is permitted to see it; and Beit Yosef wrote that this is an extra word, for he already wrote "and likewise to see," etc.; and in Semak these two words do not appear — end quote. And it appears to me that the Tur's text in Semak was also as before Beit Yosef, but he intentionally added these two words so one not err and say that Semak, who wrote permission for running and jumping in order afterward to see something they enjoy thereby, does not truly permit it — for this resembles what Beit Yosef wrote here in the name of Tosefta: one may not run on Shabbat in order to exercise, but one strolls in his usual way even the entire day — end quote. And it is certain that he has satisfaction in what he exercises, for does one run in the market for nothing? Rather he certainly receives pleasure from it for something, such as appetite for food after exertion — and even so it is forbidden; and the reason is that at the hour he runs he has no pleasure, so we do not permit him because of pleasure he will draw afterward — unlike youths' jumping, where the pleasure is at the hour he runs and jumps. If so, also here running to see — the running and jumping were not permitted, but the intent is to permit seeing alone; for one might have thought there is prohibition in this seeing, that the Tur wrote in siman 306 that one may think of his affairs — nevertheless, because of oneg Shabbat it is a mitzvah that he not think of them at all, etc. — end quote; and Beit Yosef brings in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah that perhaps from contemplation comes distraction of the heart, etc.; and likewise we say: "rest of peace, tranquility, quiet and security, complete rest that you desire," etc. — end quote; if so, this is not the rest Heaven desires that one see and contemplate on Shabbat external things — it teaches us that since it is pleasure for him, it is permitted; and this is oneg Shabbat — this is my humble opinion in the extra language of Rabbeinu the Tur, to explain Semak's words: he did not err to say it refers to running and jumping, but only to seeing and walking at ease; and he relied here because in both the reason is his pleasure — in youths in jumping, and in the end of the seif in seeing alone — as my humble opinion, correct.
