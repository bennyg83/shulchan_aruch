(Seif katan 10) With a sword, even girded, etc. So it also appears from the Gemara, page 63, that the Sages obligate one if he went out with one of these, for they hold that it is a burden and not an ornament; and Rabbi Eliezer exempts, for he holds that they are ornaments. The Gemara asks: What is Rabbi Eliezer’s reason? It answers that it is written, ‘Gird your sword upon your thigh, O mighty one, your glory and your splendor.’ Thus Scripture called it glory and splendor. There it is written ‘gird,’ which implies that the main glory and ornament is when it is girded on his loins. In such a case Rabbi Eliezer exempts, and even in such a case the Sages disagree and obligate.
