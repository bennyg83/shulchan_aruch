One may go out with absorbent cloth and sponge. See Mishnah Berurah. The Shulchan Aruch copied all the wording of this seif from the Tur. It appears that when the Tur concluded about this, because they heal, he necessarily understood the Tosefta as speaking of old sponge and absorbent cloth, see below in siman 328, seif 23, which heal. He follows his own view, that something which does not heal, even though it protects a person from pain, is considered a burden, as is proven below in seif 28, as the Magen Avraham explains there, which is also from the wording of the Tur. According to him, the baraisa must be speaking where he had already placed them before Shabbos and they did not fall off, for otherwise it would be forbidden to return them on Shabbos because of healing. But according to the Rambam, that even with something that does not heal but is useful so the wound will not be scratched, one may go out with it, as the Gra wrote in his commentary on this seif, this Tosefta also discusses new absorbent cloth and sponge, which do not heal but are useful so the wound will not be scratched, as below in siman 328, seif 3; see there, where it is also permitted to go out with them, and it is also permitted initially to return them on Shabbos. Indeed, in the Tosefta it did not conclude regarding sponge and absorbent cloth that if they fell off one may not return them, as it did regarding the others. This supports the Rambam’s view, that one may go out even with those that are not for healing but only to prevent pain. The baraisa therefore includes the law of going out with absorbent cloth and sponge in every case, meaning even new ones that do not heal, and for that reason it did not conclude that one may not return them, as with the others, because there is no concern of healing in this.
