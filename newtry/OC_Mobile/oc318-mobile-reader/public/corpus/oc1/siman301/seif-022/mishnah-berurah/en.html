74) With cotton or a sponge. They must have been placed there before Shabbos, as explained below.
75) With a plaster. This is a piece of cloth smeared with ointment and placed on a wound.
76) He may not replace it. Replacing it is like applying it initially, which the Sages forbade as medical treatment on Shabbos, as below in siman 328.
77) On the wound. It is likewise forbidden to wrap a cord or strap around cotton, a sponge, or a plaster. The bandage over a plaster permitted below is specifically a disposable rag bandage, which is insignificant and subordinate to the plaster; a significant cord or strap is not subordinate. Gra explains Rambam as forbidding a cord or strap only when wrapped over the cotton or sponge for carrying, but permitting even a significant wrapping directly on the wound, since it prevents the wound from being scraped and is therefore not a burden. He proves this from Tosefta and appears to rule accordingly, as do several later authorities; see Mishnah Berurah below, se'if 28.
78) He may tie and untie it. A bandage itself is not considered medical treatment, so tying or untying it does not resemble administering a remedy on Shabbos.
