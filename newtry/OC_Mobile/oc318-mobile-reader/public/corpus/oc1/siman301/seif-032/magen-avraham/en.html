One who goes out with money. Maharil permitted carrying pierced coins upon oneself on Shabbos in a time of danger, but not taking them outside the eruv. Some say he permitted this even when they were not pierced. If one fears robbery - for example, the governor's servants are conducting searches - he may take them outside the eruv, as stated in siman 334. Thus, Maharil's initial statement not to take them outside the eruv concerns a case where no one has come to rob him. He is not panicked over an immediate loss; rather, it is generally a dangerous time, and if non-Jews notice his money they may rob or endanger him, as Orchos Chaim explains; see there. Maharil nevertheless implies that carrying the coins in his hand is forbidden. He may only carry them upon himself, which is carrying in an unusual manner. Beis Yosef writes likewise in Agur and Maharil's name. The permission in siman 303:22 to go out with them applies because they were designated for use and are subordinate to the garment. Mere piercing does not help unless the coin was pierced to hang around his daughter's neck, except in a time of danger.
