Se'if katan 19: If it is in the manner of his trade. This means in order to display and publicize that he is a craftsman.
As stated in se'if 8. A craftsman is no better than a layperson. Siman 303 likewise says that a woman incurs liability. Tosafos on Shabbos 11b says that Rabbi Meir holds a craftsman carrying in the manner of his trade is exempt; this is the accepted ruling. A tailor who goes out with a needle stuck in his garment is therefore exempt. Tosafos asks from the Mishnah Bameh Ishah, where Rabbi Meir says a woman who goes out with a pierced needle incurs a sin-offering, and answers that there it is entirely the ordinary manner of carrying. Magen Avraham explains that the woman carried the needle in the place ordinarily used for carrying, whereas the tailor placed it where needles are inserted only to display his trade; Rabbi Meir therefore exempts him. Chemed Moshe raises several objections to Magen Avraham, all easily dismissed, except that he interprets Tosafos differently. He says Tosafos distinguishes not between locations but between a man and woman. A woman ordinarily carries a needle stuck in her garment, because every woman sews and repairs the household garments, as Tosafos Yom Tov explains. For a man it is degrading unless he has a practical need or wishes to display his craft. In my humble opinion this is incorrect. Tosafos indeed asked from the Mishnah's liability for a woman, which Tosafos Yom Tov answers. But what will he answer according to Ulla's rule that the laws are reversed for a man; according to those who apply it to a needle, making a man liable for an unpierced needle; or according to the view that Ulla does not address needles and men and women are equally liable for a pierced needle, except according to Bach? One must therefore explain it as Magen Avraham does.
