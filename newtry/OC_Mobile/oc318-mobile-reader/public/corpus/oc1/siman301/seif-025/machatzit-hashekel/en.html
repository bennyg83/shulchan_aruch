Se'if katan 25: Some are stringent, etc. See Pesachim 51a. This is the source of the preceding law, for the baraisa there states: One may go out wearing kordakisin on Shabbos, but one may not wear kordakisin in Biri.
And an incident, etc. Rabban Gamliel's sons went out wearing kordakisin in Biri. The townspeople criticized them, so they removed them and gave them to their servants rather than tell the people that they were permitted. The Gemara explains: Although Rabbi Chisda says that one may not permit in front of people something intrinsically permitted which they have customarily forbidden only when they are Samaritans, the people of Biri, among whom Torah scholars were uncommon, were like Samaritans. Hagahos Maimoniyos writes that the shoes called panti may be kordakisin or have the same law. Magen Avraham's concluding rule, 'nevertheless, if he forgot,' is not in Hagahos Maimoniyos and must come from Shibolei HaLeket. I have never seen that work. I do not understand: Rabban Gamliel's sons removed them. Rabbeinu Tam likewise told the people of Milan that the residents of Bashkar were not learned but they were. Perhaps in Rome one must indeed remove them. The permission when one forgot refers to a place where the general custom is lenient but one individual personally adopted stringency; if he forgot, he need not remove them. See Peri Chadash, Orach Chaim, who discusses this detail and the incident of Rabban Gamliel's sons at length.
