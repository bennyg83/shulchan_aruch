And it healed three times. This applies even if it healed one person three times, as the poskim write. We do not attribute this to the patient's fortune, as we do below concerning three amulets used for one person. It is more reasonable to attribute the success to the established amulet than to the patient's fortune.
For example, he wrote one incantation, etc. See Mishnah Berurah for our explanation of an expert writer and proven amulet according to Shulchan Aruch. This follows Tosafos, Rosh, and Rabbeinu Yerucham, who disagree with Rashi. Rashi explains that if a writer gives three kinds of amulets to three people suffering from different illnesses, the writer becomes universally expert: Any amulet he gives will be effective. According to the other authorities, this does not establish him at all. He becomes an expert writer only if he heals all three with one incantation, and he is expert only for that incantation. The same dispute concerns a proven amulet. According to Rashi, if an incantation heals three people suffering from one illness, that incantation is established, and anyone who writes it for that illness may permit the patient to carry it. They hold that specifically the same written amulet that healed three people is proven; if the same incantation is written on another amulet, it is not a proven amulet and may not be carried. Although Beis Yosef cites Semag, Semak, and Sefer HaTerumah as supporting Rashi, Shulchan Aruch adopts Tosafos, Rosh, and Rabbeinu Yerucham as primary. I found, however, that Rabbeinu Chananel's extant explanation of Rav Pappa's question clearly follows Rashi. Chiddushei Ran likewise explicitly cites Rabbeinu Yonasan this way; see there, where he explains the rationale and thereby resolves Tosafos' objection. Meiri states this explicitly, and Hagahos Ashri likewise follows Rashi, as Darkei Moshe cites. Since Rabbeinu Chananel, Rashi, Rabbeinu Yonasan, Semag, Semak, Sefer HaTerumah, Meiri, and Hagahos Ashri all follow one lenient approach, and the issue is rabbinic - the Mishnah says one never incurs a sin-offering - it appears that one may rely upon them leniently when necessary. Had Beis Yosef seen Rabbeinu Chananel and Rabbeinu Yonasan, he too would not have ruled unqualifiedly stringently in Shulchan Aruch.
The writer became expert for this incantation, etc. Know that Shulchan Aruch's approach is that of Tosafos and Rosh, as explained above. According to them, even if he wrote several incantations and each benefited several people several times, he does not thereby become expert for other incantations whose effectiveness has not yet been established.
And these amulets became established for every person. One should not ask what difference it makes that the amulets themselves became established, since once the man was established as expert, every person may carry those amulets. The practical difference is where the man lost his established status, for example if he wrote this incantation three more times and it did not help. Even so, those earlier amulets may still be carried because the amulet itself had become established, since each one healed three times and they did not lose their established status.
