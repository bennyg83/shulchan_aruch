And some prohibit. See Taz and Eliyah Rabbah, letter 64. I have already written above that where the custom is to wear an under-gartel and an over-gartel, meaning that one is unattractive and the second is attractive, and they do so in order that it be proper to gird the second over the first, or where the second is short, it is certainly permitted. He writes there in the name of Sefer HaTerumah and Maharam that even according to the opinion prohibiting 2 belts, the prohibition is only rabbinic; see there. See also letter 14, that the same applies where he makes a bow, so that it functions as a long belt, like a sash; see there. See Tosafos, Shabbos 59b, s.v. Trei Hemayni. Where there is benefit and this is the normal manner, a case such as ours is permitted. Tosafos there were troubled by the case of a fire and did not answer that the Sages permitted a rabbinic prohibition because of monetary loss. At first glance this would indicate that they understood the prohibition to be Torah-level. This is not so, for in a case of loss they permitted moving muktzeh, but not carrying out even where the latter is only rabbinic. See siman 334, where this will be explained.
