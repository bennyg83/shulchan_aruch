One may not go out on Shabbos with an amulet that is not established as effective. If it is established, one may go out with it. This applies whether the writer is established but the particular amulet is not, for example, he wrote one incantation on three separate amulets and all three healed three different people, in which case the writer is established for that incantation whenever he writes it, though not for other incantations, and a later amulet is not itself thereby established; or whether the amulet is established but the writer is not, for example, he wrote one incantation on one amulet and it healed three times, in which case that amulet is established for any person. All the more so, it applies when both the writer and the amulet are established, for example, he wrote one incantation on three amulets and each one benefited three people, or one person three times. The writer is then established for this incantation in every amulet he writes, and those amulets are established for every person. {Rama: This is specifically when the two established statuses arose together. But if the writer was already established and afterward he made an amulet that healed three times, we do not attribute those cures to the amulet's independent effectiveness, but to the expertise of the writer, who had already been established. [Hagahot Ashiri; this is also implied by Tosafot according to the explanation cited by Beit Yosef]} But if he wrote three amulets for one person and they healed him three times, neither the writer nor the amulet is thereby established. It is permitted to go out with an established amulet, whether it consists of writing or of medicinal roots, whether for a dangerously ill person or for one who is not dangerously ill. This is not limited to one who has already suffered an epileptic seizure and wears it as a remedy; even one who has not yet contracted the illness, but comes from a family prone to epilepsy and wears it so that the illness should not seize him, may do so. He may tie and untie it in the public domain. However, he may not attach it to a bracelet or ring and go out with it into the public domain, because people would then say that he is wearing it as an ornament, and that is forbidden because it is not an ornament.
