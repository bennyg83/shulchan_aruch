It is permitted to go out in the public domain, etc. This is from the responsum of the Rashba brought by the Beit Yosef later in this siman. He writes: You wrote that in this city they are accustomed to carry the tallis under the cloak around their necks in the public domain, but Rabbi Yochanan said that one who goes out with a folded tallis, etc., is liable. The answer: In all the land of Spain they are accustomed to go out this way for modesty, even in their courtyards, and it is their manner of clothing; it is like what they said, that merchants go out with a scarf on their shoulders, and not only merchants, but every person, except that this is the way of merchants. As for the folded tallis of which Rabbi Yochanan spoke, Rashi explained that after he placed it on his head he lifted its lower edges, etc.; that is not the manner of clothing. It appears clear that the Rashba’s intention was to permit a folded tallis placed around his neck the entire time that he carries it to the synagogue, and there he removes it and wraps himself in it. He permitted this because that was the manner of wearing in that place. Rabbi Yochanan forbade a folded tallis only where its wearing is merely to wrap oneself in its full width, but this one lifts its lower edges after wearing it so it should not become dirty, and it is not at all the manner to wear it that way, since he changed it by lifting it. If so, what is written here in Shulchan Aruch, that it is permitted to go out in the public domain, etc., also means this, that the tallis is folded and placed around his neck. This makes difficult why he wrote in the previous seif, ‘and based on this it is permitted to wrap oneself, etc.,’ which implies that there too he speaks of folding the part that he lifts from below, and permits only because his shoulder and body are covered by it; yet here he permits even around the neck alone. The Levush wrote in explaining the first permission that he wraps its width along the length of his body under the cloak, even though he does not place it on his head in its normal manner, but on his shoulders and below under the cloak to cover it; nevertheless, this is called wearing it in its way. And regarding our tallisos of mitzvah, even if he places it only on his shoulder around his neck, etc. This is not the intention of the Shulchan Aruch at all, for if so, the main novelty would be that he does not place it on his head, and this is not mentioned here in Shulchan Aruch at all. How then did the Shulchan Aruch write ‘and based on this,’ since one matter is not close to the other? There is also no novelty at all in the fact that his head is not covered with the tallis, for it is obvious that on any day the head is sometimes not covered, as written in the laws of tzitzis. Rather, its intention is plainly as I wrote, that although it is folded at its end it is permitted, since his shoulder and body are covered. If so, it is difficult, for afterward he wrote that even around his neck is permitted, although it is folded. I also did not understand what the Rama wrote at the end, ‘although he places the right side on his left shoulder, etc.,’ for in the Rashba’s responsum, which is the source for this, this is not mentioned. It seems to me that the Shulchan Aruch wrote in a manner of ‘not only this, but even this’: first he wrote the permission where he folds it below and his shoulder and body are covered, and afterward he wrote that even when he wraps himself with a folded tallis around his neck alone it is permitted. It seems proper to rely on this leniently, for the Beit Yosef brings in the name of Shibolei HaLeket in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel that there is no prohibition unless he removes it entirely from upon himself, folds it, and carries it on his shoulder.
