He should sew them, etc., or tie them to the garment with a permanent knot. Taz asks: If they hang by a knot and are not on his hands, they are a burden upon him. What is gained by their being tied? They are certainly not subordinate to the garment, etc. Gra raises an even stronger question: How does even sewing help? How does this differ from a cloth without tzitzis and similar cases, as stated in se'ifim 38 and 39? Eliyah Rabbah answers that since he ties them securely, they appear like long sleeves and are not considered a burden; tying therefore helps. I further saw that Nehar Shalom and Tosefes Shabbos write that although tying certainly does not make the gloves subordinate to the garment, he is presently wearing them normally. The only concern is that he may need to remove them and carry them in his hand. Since they are tied, even if he removes his hands they will not fall completely, creating concern that he will pick them up; they will merely hang from him. This is carrying in an unusual manner, for which he is exempt, and the Sages therefore did not decree. Although this reasoning should seemingly permit even a nonpermanent knot, since there is no longer concern that he will carry them in his hand, perhaps the Sages feared the knot might come undone, as Pri Megadim suggests. Taz and Gra reject this answer, apparently holding that hanging them this way is their ordinary mode of carrying.
Securely. They may likewise be attached with hooks. Eliyah Rabbah.
