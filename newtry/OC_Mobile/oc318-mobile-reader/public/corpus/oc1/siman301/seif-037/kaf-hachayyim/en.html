Initially he may not do so, because he appears to be going to war, as Ran writes; this escaped Levush's notice. Regel Yesharah, page 53; Machazik Berachah 4. See Tosefes Shabbos 14, who writes this in Rashi's name and states that it is forbidden even in complete privacy, as stated in se'if 45; see there. In a city where royal law requires Jews and non-Jews to serve in the army, and each night some are selected by lot to stand guard and patrol the city's markets and streets, all bearing swords against thieves and the like, the lot may sometimes fall upon a Jew to perform guard duty on Friday night. He is not excused and must carry a sword and a firearm over his shoulder. Zera Avraham 3:32 was asked about this and concludes that they must make every possible effort to evade the duty and exchange Friday night for a weekday night, so as not to violate the prohibition of handling muktzeh that appears like going to wage war. They must do so even if it costs money. The matter may not be permitted merely because of hostility. If the decree is severe and entails danger, however, there is certainly no muktzeh prohibition; see there. Pischei HaDevir 5.
