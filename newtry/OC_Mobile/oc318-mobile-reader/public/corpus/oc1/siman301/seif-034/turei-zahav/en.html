With folded sudar, etc. — needs explanation why more permitted here folded than tallit liable when folded; Beit Yosef: Rabbeinu Yerucham — permitted in sudar wrapped like a coat; implies if not wrapped forbidden — astonishing, for one goes out with sudar folded on shoulder implying permitted even not wrapped; difference: sudar worn thus, as Rambam chapter 19: folded tallit on shoulder liable, sudar on shoulder permitted — implies where forbidden in tallit permitted in sudar — end quote. I did not know why sudar more permitted than tallit; if shape difference, poskim should state it as Tur on sakinat. My humble opinion: Gemara — R' Yochanan: folded tallit on shoulder on Shabbat liable; Rashi: after on head raised hem on shoulders, not manner of clothing; Mishnah 55; carriers with sudar on shoulders; Oleirin bring women's veil provided head and most covered; sakinat ties on head below shoulders; Rashi forced to explain folded tallit after on head, not simply took folded tallit on shoulder; needed "after on head" as extra word, difficulty tallit forbidden sudar permitted — if found folded and placed on shoulder as wrapping, permitted, manner of sudar worn folded, same tallit; Rashi: forbidden folded tallit not when placed folded — else difficulty sudar folded; here placed first on head then raised and folded on shoulders forbidden; if placed folded and wrapped on shoulder, permitted, no difference sudar and tallit; both permitted folded wrapped, forbidden place on head then raise and fold; Tur as Rashi explicit in tallit, then permits folded sudar: any garment wrapped even folded no prohibition, no difference — Gemara one language R' Yochanan tallit forbid, baraita sudar permit; then linen law if head and majority not covered; sakinat if not tie both ends below — those two no folding issue, not called garment; Gemara forbids making gutter going to public domain, Tur included gathering two ends — Rambam: wraps tallit folding here and there as copied seif 31, gutter law; then folded tallit on shoulder liable, sudar on shoulder even without finger thread; any sudar not covering head and majority forbidden — short sakinat ties below shoulders like belt, permitted — end quote. Thus sudar permit not about folding — needs cover head and majority, said regarding linen without folding, as Tur; Rambam's sudar law is really linen law, not folding; therefore Rambam did not mention folded sudar as by tallit; Beit Yosef: where tallit forbidden, sudar permitted — we proved that only my humble opinion: forbidden folded tallit not as Rashi but simply folded on shoulder though wrapping manner forbidden; then I would think anything not covering whole body not garment even unfolded — therefore wrote sudar on shoulder extending down permitted, needs head and majority — linen in Gemara; Beit Yosef: need not cover actually but measure to cover — very astonishing, Gemara says head and majority covered, not measure; surely Rambam agrees; "on shoulder" not to exclude rest of body — see after; Rashi and Rambam: sudar permit only wrapping; Beit Yosef Yerucham: sudar wrapped like coat, but Beit Yosef on this implies if not wrapped forbidden — astonishing, folded on shoulders implies permitted not wrapped, difference tallit — end quote; I prefer R' Yehuda's words; astonished Beit Yosef's question on folded sudar — phrase only in Tur; rule in my humble opinion: Rashi and Tur — any garment folded and wrapped permitted; Rashba permitted tallit under cloak around neck in public domain — forbidden only on head then raise hem as Rashi, Beit Yosef; incomplete garment like linen even without folding forbidden; head and sakinat by tying makes garment like belt per Rambam; Rambam — anything folded forbidden to place even by wrapping; for practice be stringent, no tallit under cloak unless removes and wraps even if not reaching down, majority body enough; I wondered Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch leniency on our garments; more astonished gathering makes gutter forbidden by poskim when not for adornment — what help taking hands out — my humble opinion; though Rav Chaim did not descend to all this, for Torah study we need.
