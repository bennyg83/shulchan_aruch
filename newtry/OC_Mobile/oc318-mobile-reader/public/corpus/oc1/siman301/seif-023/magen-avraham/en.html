If they are not woven, he may not. According to se'if 9, where we do not decree for a man that he may come to show the item, one must say that the reason here is concern that people may laugh at him because it appears haughty, causing him to carry it. Although the case concerns a minor, for whom no prohibition exists even if he carries it directly, one may say that we are concerned that princes' children will constantly laugh at him. His father will inevitably hear, remove the item, and come to carry it. If princes' children do not laugh at him, however, then even if it breaks there is no concern that his father will carry it, since it is uncommon for his father to be beside him precisely when it breaks. Attached to the garment. It must specifically be sewn with two stitches; tying does not suffice. Beis Shmuel. This requires examination: How does it differ from green disks, which are permitted when only slightly attached? One must say that those are more fully subordinate to the garment. Bach writes that it is subordinate specifically when attached to a garment; attachment to a belt does not help, because it is not subordinate to the belt. Shiltei HaGibborim also implies this. Similarly, one may go out wearing a hood sewn onto the back of a garment, but not with a cloth tied to the cords of his hat for wiping his eyes. Shiltei HaGibborim, chapter 6, paragraph 25.
They do not produce sound. This requires examination, for in siman 338 we rule that only a musical sound is forbidden. Furthermore, Tur writes there that according to all opinions one may place a stone inside a hollow ring so that it makes sound, since he has no need for that sound. Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 22, writes likewise and proves it from the statement on page 28 that a slave may go out wearing a bell even though it has a clapper, since he does not need its sound. One must say that our ruling specifically concerns children. Shiltei HaGibborim writes: 'If it is made to produce sound, it is forbidden.' Tosafos likewise implies that one may not jingle a bell for a child. This applies specifically to a child who desires its musical sound, as stated in siman 339:3. Although the child performs the act, it must be considered like directly feeding him something forbidden. See what I wrote above and the gloss to siman 339.
