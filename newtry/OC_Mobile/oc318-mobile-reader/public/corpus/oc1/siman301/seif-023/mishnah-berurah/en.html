79) Woven into their garments. We therefore do not fear that they will break off and be carried. Even gold bells made only for princes are permitted when woven in, and we do not fear that others will mock the child and cause him to carry them by hand.
80) If they are not woven in, they may not. This is true even if tied to the garment, and certainly if hung around the neck, lest they break off and be carried. Gold bells present the additional concern that people will mock the child for pretension, since such bells belong only to the wealthy, and he will remove and carry them. Tosafos cites Riva that even poor young children may not wear an unwoven gold bell on a garment or around the neck. Their father will inevitably hear the mockery, remove it, and may carry it four cubits in the public domain. Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah cite this.
81) Only for an item, etc. For example, bells were ordinarily used to adorn all garments in their time. When woven in, they are permitted; the Sages did not decree because of unwoven bells, since they did not require one to spoil the garment and make it unattractive.
82) Which is not ordinarily worn this way. They decreed regarding an attached item because of an unattached one. Mordechai adds that it may even be a burden for which one is liable to a sin-offering, since an item not ordinarily attached is not subordinate to the garment. Rashba likewise implies that anything neither needed by nor useful to a garment is not subordinate to it and is treated as carried independently.
83) One may go out wearing them. They are considered clothing because they are worn throughout the week. We do not fear that he will be embarrassed, remove them, and carry them, because he fears the government; nor are they significant enough to create concern that they will break off and be carried like unwoven bells.
84) If it is attached, etc. It must be securely sewn to the garment before Shabbos, or at least with two stitches, so it is like woven material. Otherwise it may break off and be carried. Since such kerchiefs are not ordinarily sewn to garments nowadays, they are not subordinate to the garment, though some permit because an insignificant kerchief becomes subordinate when sewn. Merely tying it to the garment or belt is insufficient even with a permanent knot if he does not belt himself with it. One who wants to take a kerchief that was not sewn on before Shabbos may tie one end with a bow to the end of his belt, making one long belt, and wear it so throughout the street. Some later authorities question whether a nonpermanent knot suffices, so ideally he should make this arrangement before Shabbos with a permanent knot.
85) To the garment. Sewing it to a garment helps, but sewing it to the middle of a belt does not, because it is not subordinate to the belt. It is permitted if sewn to the end and used as part of the belt, as above. See below, se'ifim 36-37, and Mishnah Berurah there.
