Anything with which one goes out, etc. The general rules of carrying derived from this siman and siman 303 are as follows. Something that is neither clothing nor an ornament is considered a burden and is forbidden by Torah law; intentional violation incurs kares and unwitting violation requires a sin-offering. This applies if it is carried in the ordinary weekday manner. If it is not ordinarily carried this way during the week, it is carrying in an unusual manner: one is exempt from a sin-offering but the act is rabbinically forbidden. Something that is clothing or an ornament is permitted by Torah law, but the Sages prohibited several categories. Some were prohibited lest she remove and show the item and then carry it four amos in the public domain. There is, however, a dispute whether this concern of removing and showing applies to a man, as stated in se'if 9. Some items were prohibited because they are loose and may slip off by themselves, leading the wearer to carry them. Some were prohibited lest people laugh at the wearer, leading the wearer to remove and carry them. Some were prohibited because of appearances. Several items were prohibited for a woman because they interpose during immersion; we are concerned that she may have a mitzvah immersion, remove them, and afterward carry them. Anything not made as actual clothing but only to protect against dirt - such as placing a piece of cloth over one's scarf so rain will not soil it - is a burden. If it is full-fledged clothing, however, it is considered clothing rather than a burden even if intended to protect against rain. Know further that even a genuine ornament is considered a burden and carried in the ordinary manner, making one liable, if held in the hand. I have gathered this from Tosefes Shabbos and other sources.
On the foot without the wound. See Mishnah Berurah for the reason. This is Chiya bar Rav's opinion in the Gemara. Rav Huna holds the opposite: one goes out specifically with the shoe on the wounded foot; see there for his reason. Shulchan Aruch follows Rabbeinu Chananel, Rif, Rambam, and Rosh, who rule like Chiya bar Rav. Razah and Responsa Maharam, however, rule like Rav Huna. Eliyah Rabbah cites Malbushei Yom Tov, and Gra adduces proof for their position from Yerushalmi. Many later authorities - Bach, Taz, Eliyah Rabbah, and Malbushei Yom Tov - therefore agree that it is proper to be stringent and never go out wearing one shoe, even when one foot is wounded, whether the shoe is on that foot or the other. It is preferable to walk barefoot when he cannot wear both shoes. Although some are stringent not to walk barefoot on Shabbos, as discussed below in se'if 16, it is permitted here because there is no alternative. Nevertheless, for us, who according to many poskim have no true public domain, it is certainly reasonable not to be more stringent than Shulchan Aruch. Some are entirely lenient in a karmelis, as discussed below in siman 303:18. Pri Megadim; Shulchan Aruch HaRav.
