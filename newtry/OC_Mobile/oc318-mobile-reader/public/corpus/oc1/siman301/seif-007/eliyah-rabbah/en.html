[Levush] And if he went out he is exempt, etc. The Shulchan Aruch omitted this. Olas Shabbos wrote that this is because nowadays there is no practical difference between liability for a chatas and exemption. This is not precise, for in seif 4 and seif 8 the Shulchan Aruch wrote cases where one is exempt, and likewise in many places. In truth, there is a major difference even nowadays between a Torah prohibition and a D’Rabbanan prohibition. Also below, at the end of siman 334, there is a practical difference regarding a labor that is not needed for its own sake, whether one may extinguish an ember in a place where many may be harmed; there the Shulchan Aruch cited a dispute about a labor not needed for its own sake and did not decide. It seems to me that for this reason he did not write here that if one went out he is exempt, because he did not decide, unlike the Levush, who decided in siman 278 like Rabbi Shimon, that one is exempt for a labor not needed for its own sake; therefore he wrote here the law that if one went out he is exempt. So it seems to me. Olas Shabbos, who wrote at length here in ruling that it is not needed for its own sake, overlooked the Shulchan Aruch at the end of siman 334 cited above. There is also some difficulty with what he wrote in siman 316, seif 4. It is also surprising, for below in siman 303, seif 18, the Shulchan Aruch and Levush wrote a distinction whether it is forbidden in our karmelis. Olas Shabbos further wrote: it seems that nevertheless there is a practical difference regarding extinguishing a lamp for a sick person who is not in danger, that according to the one who exempts for a labor not needed for its own sake it is permitted even initially. This is surprising, for the Talmud in Shabbos 30 says according to Rabbi Shimon, who exempts, that initially one may not extinguish for a sick person who is not in danger. So ruled the Tur and Levush in siman 278, even though there they ruled like Rabbi Shimon.
