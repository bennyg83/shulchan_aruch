Se'if katan 7: To greet, etc. This is derived from the Shunammite woman. Rosh Hashanah 16b states: Rabbi Yitzchak said that a person is obligated to greet his teacher on a festival, as the verse says, 'Why are you going to him today? It is neither the New Moon nor Shabbos.' Her husband said this when she went to Elisha. The Gemara infers that on the New Moon and Shabbos one should go. Magen Avraham asks: Rabbi Yitzchak opens with a festival but concludes with the New Moon and Shabbos; the verse itself mentions the New Moon and Shabbos. Moreover, how do we know there is an obligation rather than merely a mitzvah? Magen Avraham therefore explains that 'on the New Moon and Shabbos one should go' means there is a mitzvah. Rabbi Yitzchak then reasons that on a festival, which occurs only periodically, there is an obligation.
See what I wrote in siman 554:12. Magen Avraham there gives one answer that even on a weekday there is a mitzvah and on Shabbos an obligation. He proves this from Chagigah 5b: Rabbi Yaakov of Kfar Chitaya would greet his teacher every day. When he grew old, his teacher told him not to trouble himself with the walk because he was no longer able. He answered, 'Is the reward for steps toward the Sages a small matter?' According to this, Shabbos, the New Moon, and a festival are one category, unlike the inference here. Although greeting one's teacher every day is a mitzvah, the Shunammite woman's husband asked why she was going because he knew that she was not accustomed to greet Elisha daily, only on the New Moon and Shabbos.
