16) Whoever carries out, etc. See Biur Halachah regarding what we wrote in part in the introduction to this siman and to siman 303.
17) And not in the manner of clothing. Had he carried it out in the manner of clothing, he would not have carried it in the ordinary manner of those who carry. An object that is not an ornament for its wearer is ordinarily carried by hand, not worn as clothing.
18) And carried it out in the usual manner, etc. That is, without an unusual modification.
Liable. Know that wherever the laws of Shabbos say that one is liable, if he acted intentionally he is liable to kareis, and if unwittingly, meaning he forgot that it was Shabbos or did not know that this melachah was forbidden, he is liable to bring a chatas. If he forgot an item by him and took it out unwittingly to the public domain, he is not liable for it, since the Torah forbade only deliberate constructive melachah, meaning that he takes it out intentionally but does not know that today is Shabbos or that this melachah is forbidden.
20) It is forbidden to go out wearing it. We are concerned that it may fall and he will come to carry it four cubits in the public domain.
21) Which she commonly removes. We are concerned that while doing so she may come to carry them four cubits in the public domain.
22) Not with a sword. This is so even if it is belted at his waist, because that is the ordinary weekday manner of carrying it.
23) Nor with a club. It resembles a staff, with a round, ball-like head. Aruch.
24) Nor with armor. The term is drawn from the phrase 'scale armor' in I Samuel 17.
25) He is exempt, because they are, etc. Nevertheless, it is forbidden because of maris ayin: observers may suspect that he intends to fight with them on Shabbos. It is forbidden even in the innermost private rooms, as stated in se'if 45; see there.
26) One may not go out wearing tefillin because, etc. This implies that there is no prohibition against putting on tefillin in one's home when not doing so for the mitzvah. Putting them on for the mitzvah is forbidden, as stated above in siman 31; see Mishnah Berurah there, se'if katan 5.
27) That he must, etc. The concern is that he may come to carry them four cubits. Without this reason, it would apparently not be forbidden to go out wearing them, even though Shabbos is not a time for tefillin, because they are worn in the manner of clothing. See below, siman 308, end of se'if 64 in the gloss and Mishnah Berurah there, and above, siman 31 in Biur Halachah.
28) A small person in a large shoe. That is, a person whose shoe is too large for him.
29) On the foot that has no wound. They will no longer mock him, because they know he cannot wear the shoe on the wounded foot; the wound itself makes this evident. See Biur Halachah.
