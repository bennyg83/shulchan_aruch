29) [Se'if 7] It is not the manner of wearing, etc. Only something customarily worn during the week is considered the manner of wearing. Mahari Molcho, manuscript responsum 17; Birkei Yosef 1; Shaarei Teshuvah.
30) There: He is liable. Know that wherever the word 'liable' appears, one who acts intentionally is liable to kares; if he was formally warned, he is liable to stoning. Machatzis HaShekel. If he acts unwittingly - meaning that he forgot it was Shabbos, or knew it was Shabbos but did not know this labor was forbidden - he must bring a sin-offering. If, however, the labor was wholly unintended, such as when he forgot that an object was with him and went out with it into the public domain, he is exempt. Magen Avraham 9. 'Exempt' means even from a sin-offering, because the Torah forbade calculated labor and he had no intention to perform labor at all. Machatzis HaShekel. Regarding the law of the public domain, see below, siman 345:7, and our comments there, with G-d's help.
31) There: Any ornament that is loose, etc. We are concerned that it may fall and that he will come to carry it four amos in the public domain.
32) There: Which she ordinarily removes, etc. We are concerned that in the meantime she may come to carry it four amos in the public domain.
33) There: Not with a sword. Even if it is girded around his waist, he is liable, because this is its ordinary manner of being carried during the week. Beis Yosef; Magen Avraham 10; Eliyah Rabbah 12.
34) There: Nor with an alah. This is like an iron club, one and one-half amos long, with an elongated ball at its end. Be'er HaGolah; Eliyah Rabbah, ibid.
35) There: Nor with armor. As in, 'He was clothed with scale armor' (I Shmuel 17:5). Metzudas Tzion explains there that it is a kind of garment protecting against sword blows, made of iron rings, with the gaps between the rings closed by pieces resembling fish scales. This resembles, 'He donned righteousness like armor.'
36) There: Nor with a helmet or boots. A kasda is an iron helmet, and magafayim are iron greaves used for war. Beis Yosef; Magen Avraham 11.
37) There: If he went out, he is exempt, etc. It is nevertheless rabbinically forbidden. Shulchan Aruch HaRav 3. Initially he may not do so, because he appears to be going to war, as Ran writes; this escaped Levush's notice. Regel Yesharah, page 53; Machazik Berachah 4. See Tosefes Shabbos 14, who writes this in Rashi's name and states that it is forbidden even in complete privacy, as stated in se'if 45; see there. In a city where royal law requires Jews and non-Jews to serve in the army, and each night some are selected by lot to stand guard and patrol the city's markets and streets, all bearing swords against thieves and the like, the lot may sometimes fall upon a Jew to perform guard duty on Friday night. He is not excused and must carry a sword and a firearm over his shoulder. Zera Avraham 3:32 was asked about this and concludes that they must make every possible effort to evade the duty and exchange Friday night for a weekday night, so as not to violate the prohibition of handling muktzeh that appears like going to wage war. They must do so even if it costs money. The matter may not be permitted merely because of hostility. If the decree is severe and entails danger, however, there is certainly no muktzeh prohibition; see there. Pischei HaDevir 5.
38) There: Because he must remove them, etc. We are concerned that he may come to carry them in the public domain. Gemara; Beis Yosef. See above, siman 31, and below, the gloss to siman 308:4.
39) There: A small person should not go out, etc. This means a small person wearing a shoe that is too large for him. Rashi; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 3.
40) There: A small person should not go out, etc. This rule makes no distinction between a man and a woman. With a new shoe or a worn shoe - meaning a torn one - however, there is a distinction between a man and a woman, as stated in siman 303:13. Bach. See Beis Yosef, who writes that Rabbeinu Yerucham, section 2, appears to understand the rule that a small person may not go out in a large shoe as applying specifically to a woman. Semak and Semag, however, appear to agree with Tur that it makes no distinction between man and woman; see there. This is also the view of the later authorities.
41) There: He may go out wearing the shoe on the foot without the wound. There is now no concern that people will laugh at him, for they see that he cannot wear the second shoe because of his wound. Tur.
42) There: He may go out wearing the shoe on the foot without the wound. He may not go out wearing the shoe on the wounded foot, lest the wound hurt, he remove the shoe, and come to carry it four amos in the public domain. This appears to concern a wound with which he can also walk without a shoe; he therefore may not wear a shoe on that foot. If the wound hurts so badly that he cannot walk without a shoe, there is certainly no concern that he will come to carry it. Since he suffers and cannot walk at all without a shoe, he certainly will not remove it. Rashi's words also imply this; see there. Olas Shabbos 10.
43) There: He may go out wearing the shoe on the foot without the wound. This ruling follows Chiya bar Rav. Hagahos Maharshal, however, cites a responsum of Maharam that rules like Rav Huna, permitting the shoe on the wounded foot. Bach therefore writes that because of the dispute among great authorities, one should be stringent and not go out wearing a single shoe, whether on the wounded foot or the unwounded foot. Taz 4 cites this, and Eliyah Rabbah 17 rules likewise. Tosefes Shabbos 16 appears to agree. He should therefore walk completely barefoot when one foot is wounded. Even according to the view that prohibits one without a wound from walking barefoot, as stated in se'if 17, a person with a wound has no alternative. Pri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav 4. Nevertheless, for us, who according to many poskim have no true public domain, it is reasonable not to be more stringent than Shulchan Aruch's ruling. Mishbetzos Zahav there appears to agree.
44) Regarding going out on Shabbos in the nail-studded sandal called kabkab in Arabic, although it is not the sandal mentioned in the chapter Bameh Isha, it should nevertheless be forbidden because it is a burden or because it may slip off and he may come to carry it. It can be remedied by constructing it so that it does not slip from his foot, like the sandals in Europe whose upper portion is covered. They then constitute shoes, do not slip off, and are not a burden. See Radvaz 3, Furth edition, siman 11, who rules that a wooden shoe not covered with leather, called sueikos - kabkab in Arabic - may not be worn outside on Shabbos even into an alley without an eruv. The leather placed over its top often slips off; he can no longer wear it, and there is a substantial concern that he will carry it in his hand. Ikrei HaDat 15:3. See Birkei Yosef 10, however, who writes in Maharikash's name that some permit it. Machazik Berachah 7 cites several poskim who prohibit it like Radvaz; see there. See also below, the gloss to se'if 16, and our comments there, with G-d's help.
