Se'if 16: But with a chair. This follows the reading of Rashi and the Rosh, 'a chair and its supports,' and so it must be read.
And benches. So Tosafos in Yoma proved, because the mishnah does not state 'benches.' Therefore he needs the benches, and the prohibition applies only to the supports; see there.
One amputated in both, etc. This is Rashi's explanation there. But according to Rabbeinu Yitzchak's explanation, and also according to Rabbeinu Porat's explanation, it is not an independent amputee, but that amputee together with the prosthetic leg. According to Rabbeinu Yitzchak the supports are the cane, and according to Rabbeinu Porat they are the support. According to the second explanation of Rabbeinu Porat there, the case of both legs also concerns the prosthetic leg of one amputated in both legs, as the Mordechai and the Rashba wrote; see there.
And likewise it is permitted. Although according to the discussion in Shabbos, where according to Rabbi Yosi a wooden shoe is not a shoe, it should be forbidden, nevertheless according to the discussion in Yoma it is permitted because it may slip off only there, where the foot enters it. The discussions in Shabbos and Yevamos are reconciled by Tosafos as follows: since it slips off, it is not considered a shoe for chalitzah, like a shoe so large that one cannot walk in it; when it is covered with leather it is tightened, and therefore valid. See there. This refers to their sandals, which were tied to the leg and the foot did not enter them, but with ours it is permitted; Rabbeinu Yerucham. The discussion in Yevamos where they disagree about an anpileya is according to the initial assumption; see Tosafos in Yoma. All this is according to Tosafos' method. But according to the Ramban and Rashba, who hold that we rule like Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri, that anything which is not a shoe is forbidden even if it is tightened, as stated in Shabbos there regarding a plasterer's sandal, etc., 'and one may go out with it'; and we rule like Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri, as written in siman 614, se'if 2.
And likewise with pantines. See Magen Avraham. He also holds like Tosafos' method there in the responsum, and if so it is forbidden to go out with them on Yom Kippur, as written there in Yoma, 'and they agree,' etc. But in siman 554, se'if 16, and siman 614, se'if 2, he ruled that wooden footwear is permitted, and only that which is covered with leather is forbidden. So ruled the Rif and the Geonim, and this follows Rashi's method. The difficulty raised against Rashi from Yoma was answered by the Ramban: at first glance Shabbos there is difficult, for Rav Yosef said, 'Who did not concede,' etc., and the Ramban's reading is, 'Rava said: Who,' etc. Why does he move away from Shmuel's explanation? Rather, Rava follows his own view, for he said there in Yoma that according to everyone it is a shoe, and therefore the Rabbis in Eduyos are Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri. If so, Rava's statement in Yoma that according to everyone it is a shoe refers only to Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yosi, about whom they said, 'and they agree,' but the Rabbis disagree with them, like the anonymous teaching in Eduyos. If so, the anonymous passage in Yevamos is also like the Rabbis, and this is what is meant there: 'Whose view is the amputee's prosthetic leg? Rabbi Meir,' etc.; 'in an anpileya of cloth we have come to the Rabbis,' and there is no difficulty with Rabbi Yosi. The statement of Rabbi Meir is because according to his view he explains that it is a shoe. We rule like the Rabbis both regarding Yom Kippur and regarding chalitzah. Nevertheless, here the law is true that it is forbidden because it may slip off, as Rava said there in Yoma; and regarding Shabbos we rule like Rabbi Yosi. However, according to this there is no need for the rationale that when covered with leather it is tightened, for the reason regarding chalitzah is not because of slipping, but because it is or is not a shoe, as above. However, the Rashba wrote that although the sugya in Yevamos is as the Ramban wrote, and one must say so from the fact that it says 'in an anpileya of cloth we have come to the Rabbis,' nevertheless Rabbi Yosi certainly disqualifies chalitzah with a wooden shoe because it may slip off; otherwise why did Rava say, 'Who did not concede? Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri,' about the mishnah in Eduyos, rather than asking on Shmuel's explanation from that passage in Yevamos, that all agree? Rather, there Rabbi Yosi forbids it as above, unlike with a plasterer's shoe, which does not slip off; see the Rashba. One must say that when he wrote that when covered with leather all agree, this is not needed for Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri, but even for Rabbi Yosi, for it is tightened, as Tosafos answered; otherwise Rabbi Yosi would forbid it and it would not be a case where all agree. The principle of the matter is that regarding Yom Kippur we rule like Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri, as above, that it is not a shoe; regarding Shabbos we rule like Rabbi Yosi because of slipping, and perhaps Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri agrees with him; and regarding chalitzah, Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri and Rabbi Yosi are equal, that it is invalid unless covered with leather. If so, regarding Shabbos we rule according to Tosafos' explanation, that anything which slips off is forbidden and anything tightened is permitted. This is what the Rashba wrote in Responsa, siman 607, regarding pantines, because they are tightened. With this all the words of the Shulchan Aruch here and in the laws of Yom Kippur are proper. As practical halachah, the Ramban's view appears primary, and there is no need for the rationale that leather covering tightens it, unlike the Rashba.
