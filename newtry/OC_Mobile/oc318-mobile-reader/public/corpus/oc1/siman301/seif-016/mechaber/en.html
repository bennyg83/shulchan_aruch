An amputee who cannot walk on his legs at all, but sits upon a stool, and when he moves from his place he supports himself upon his hands and his legs and propels himself forward, and makes supports of leather or wood for the ends of his amputated legs or feet that hang [in the air] — and when he leans upon his hands and lifts himself, he leans somewhat upon his legs as well — may not go out with them on Shabbos, for since they hang and do not rest upon the ground, at times they slip off; but with the stool and the small benches in his hands, he is permitted to go out. An amputee in both legs who walks upon his knees and shins, and makes leather supports for them, may go out with them on Shabbos. {Rama: And likewise it is permitted to go out in a wooden shoe into which the foot enters, and there is no concern that it will fall off [Rabbeinu Yerucham]; and likewise in slippers, which slip off quickly and of themselves [Rashba, siman 607]; and some are stringent and forbid [Agur]. And a person should not go about barefoot on Shabbos in a place where it is not customary to go barefoot; and a person should not go out on Shabbos as he goes out on a weekday, without some other thing by which he will remember that it is Shabbos, so that he not come to desecrate it [Kol Bo].}
