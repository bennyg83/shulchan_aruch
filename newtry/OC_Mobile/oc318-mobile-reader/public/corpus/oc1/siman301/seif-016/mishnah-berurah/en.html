57) And the small stools in his hands. He supports himself on them while propelling himself forward. This is permitted, like a lame person using a staff in se'if 6.
58) He may go out wearing them on Shabbos. The concern that they may slip off does not apply here.
59) Of wood. Even when it contains no leather, it is nevertheless considered a shoe. Gra and Peri Megadim question this from siman 614:2, which implies that it is a shoe only when covered with leather. Gra distinguishes between Shabbos and Yom Kippur to resolve the Mechaber's view, but for practical halachah he appears to favor Ramban's position that wood alone is not considered a shoe even regarding Shabbos.
60) And likewise in slippers. These are called pantofel in our language. Although they slip off readily by being removed by hand without untying a knot, their leather covering makes them somewhat snug, so we do not fear that they will fall and be carried.
61) Some authorities are stringent. This refers to slippers, because they may slip off. Taz writes that this is not a concern because people do not walk barefoot in the public domain; Tosefes Shabbos questions his reasoning. In mud one should certainly not be lenient. All agree, however, that if one forgot and went into the public domain wearing them, he need not remove them but may walk home in them. The same applies whenever an item is forbidden only lest one come to carry it.
62) In a place where people do not ordinarily, etc. Darchei Moshe implies that even where people walk barefoot on weekdays, one should wear shoes on Shabbos as a reminder that it is Shabbos. Bach adds that this is part of Shabbos enjoyment. The same care is required on Yom Tov, whose obligation of enjoyment is equal. See siman 2, where going barefoot even on a weekday is discouraged as a matter of modesty.
