Se'if katan 17: Or images, etc.; when raised, even, etc. Kol Bo states this law and concludes: 'unless letters or images are engraved in it, so that the seal it makes is raised.' Beis Yosef writes that a raised seal is not specific; the same applies if the resulting seal is recessed, and Kol Bo merely described the usual case. Magen Avraham responds that Yoreh Deah 141 states that a complete human figure (head and entire body) may not be kept even on a weekday because of suspicion of idolatry. Kol Bo therefore specifically says 'engraved,' producing a raised seal, because that case is unconditionally permitted on weekdays for every type of image. When the seal is recessed, although one may keep it, one may not use it to stamp; it is nevertheless an ornament.
What he writes, 'such as keys'; the same applies to a sheath, etc. A sheath has the same law as a silver eyeglass case. When the eyeglasses are absent, Magen Avraham holds that it follows the dispute concerning silver keys. The same applies to a knife sheath without the knife. If the knife is inside, however, everyone forbids it, just as Rama forbids an eyeglass case containing its eyeglasses according to all views. We do not say the eyeglasses are subordinate to the case; on the contrary, they are primary. One does not say that eyeglasses were made for the case, but that the case was made for the eyeglasses. For the same reason, a knife sheath containing its knife is forbidden according to all.
