63) If he cannot walk without a staff, it is permitted. Since he cannot walk at all without it, the staff is like his shoe.
64) But if it is possible, etc. An elderly person who walks at home without a staff but leans on one outdoors because of weakness may not carry it, even if his body sways, because he can walk without it. If he has become so weak that he cannot walk at all without it, it is permitted.
65) And he does not take it, etc. Taz permits a person to use a staff where rain has made a slope dangerous or where winter ice creates a fear of falling, since he resembles a lame person. Eliyah Rabbah says this is not conclusive, and other later authorities permit it only where there is an eruv.
66) It is forbidden. Even an ornamental staff carried for honor is forbidden, because an ornament may not be carried in one's hand. Thus, dignitaries may not go out carrying staffs on Shabbos outside an eruv. Within an eruv it is permitted when carried for honor or some need, but carrying it with no need at all demeans Shabbos.
() His law is like a lame person ; meaning it also depends whether he can walk without a stick [Beit Yosef siman 522].
