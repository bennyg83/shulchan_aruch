Who cannot walk without a cane. If a nail at the cane's tip makes a hole as he walks, creating an inevitable result from which he derives no benefit, it requires examination whether walking with it is permitted. See Mishbetzos Zahav, which inclines to permit this.
But if he can, etc., it is forbidden. Mishnah Berurah explains that the cane is a burden. Accordingly, carrying it this way in the public domain is a Torah prohibition. Pri Megadim also initially wished to say that it is a burden, but afterward cites Levush as proving that the prohibition is only a decree lest he come to carry it four amos in the public domain - meaning without leaning upon it. Levush apparently holds that by Torah law it is never a burden, since the elderly person leans upon it. One who examines the conclusion of Shabbos 66 will see explicitly that Rava holds an elderly person's cane is not made for support at all. It is inferior to an amputee's prosthetic with a receptacle for pads, which is susceptible to midras impurity because he occasionally leans upon it. The cane is therefore not susceptible to midras, because it is made only to correct his gait. Mordechai adduces proof for his ruling from this baraisa concerning an elderly person's cane. I later found that Rabbeinu Yerucham clearly holds the cane has no status as footwear or an ornament. Or Zarua explicitly writes that it is a burden.
