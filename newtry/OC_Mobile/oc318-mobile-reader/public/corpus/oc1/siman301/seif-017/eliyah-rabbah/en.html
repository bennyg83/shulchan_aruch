Only to support, etc. Like an elderly person, Malbushei Yom Tov. If he has become so old that he cannot walk without a cane, it is permitted, Rashdam. The Taz wrote that if he is walking in a place where there is concern that he may fall, such as when rains have fallen in a sloped place, or he walks in winter on frozen water called ice, it is permitted to go out with a cane, for he is afraid to walk there. This is not compelling. The Magen Avraham wrote that important people who customarily walk with a cane in their hand are forbidden to go out with them on Shabbos, unless it is in a place with an eruv. One should not say that it should be considered an ornament, for we have not found that one may carry an ornament in his hand. Even Rabbi Eliezer, who holds that weapons are ornaments for him, said only that he is not liable to bring a chatas, but there is nevertheless a prohibition. So Tosafot wrote on 62b, that when one carries an ornament in his hand it is forbidden because of appearance, and so wrote the Raavan, siman 349. I say that there is no proof at all from Rabbi Eliezer, for one can say that he speaks where they are girded on his thigh and not in his hand, and so it implies in Lechem Mishneh, chapter Bameh Isha. Nevertheless, there is no proof from there to prohibit, for one can say that a sword and bow are not an ornament when in his hand, unlike a cane, which is an ornament when it is in his hand. With this, the proof of Magen Avraham from a key is rejected; see there and examine it carefully. It also seems to me that there is no proof from Tosafot, for there it discusses a sick person who carried an amulet in his hand, and there is an appearance issue, for people will say that this is an ornament even without illness, since he carries it in his hand, unlike a cane, whose status as an ornament in the hand of an important person is known. I also examined the Raavan there and saw that it is not a proof. It is also somewhat difficult to me that Tosafot asked from something tied with a bracelet, etc.; perhaps something tied with a bracelet is different, for the bracelet proves that it is for ornament, unlike one who carries an amulet in his hand, which is the way of a sick person. It seems that Tosafot also did not decide this as practical law, but only asked how the Talmud knew to compare it completely to the answer above; examine it carefully. Furthermore, nowadays every public domain is a karmelis, as below in siman 303, seif 18; see seif katan 78. The Magen Avraham further wrote that an ordinary man who does not carry a cane for honor, even where there is an eruv, is forbidden, because there is degradation of Shabbos, as written in siman 522. However, from the Bach and Taz in the name of Maharshal, which I cited there, it is clear that on Yom Tov it is permitted even without an eruv, for degradation applies only in a public domain and karmelis. The Tur wrote: a person whom the public needs may sit on a chair and be carried on Shabbos, provided that each one does not place his hand on the shoulder of his fellow with the chair on their arms. In the laws of Yom Tov below, siman 522, he concluded: and if he is afraid to walk without being carried on shoulders, it is permitted by shoulder-carrying. He did not write so here; perhaps on Shabbos he was stringent in this. The Shulchan Aruch and Levush omitted all the words of the Tur; perhaps they were stringent on Shabbos even without shoulder-carrying. The Magen Avraham wrote that even the Tur permits only in a place with an eruv, and so wrote the Perishah. This requires study.
