But if he can ; such as an elder who walks in his house without a cane and when he goes outside leans on his cane from weakness of strength ; forbidden (and even though he sways his body, forbidden ; Semak); the Rosh and so Maharam Maimonides: specifically when he cannot walk at all without a cane, permitted; if so, obvious that important people who walk with a cane in hand for honor are forbidden to go out with them on Shabbos; and one may say it is like an oribid.nt, for we do not find permission to carry an oribid.nt in hand; and even R' Eliezer who holds they are oribid.nts for him only said he is not liable for chatas, but there is still prohibition; and so Tosafotafos daf 62: carrying oribid.nt in hand forbidden initially because of maris ayin; and so Raavan siman 349; and what we say in Yerushalmi ; R' Gamliel, etc., key in his hand ; therefore "in his hand" not literally, for then the decree lest he remove and show to his fellow does not apply; it must be as the Ran wrote, stuck on his finger like a ring ; thus proven forbidden to carry oribid.nt in hand for his fellow; and furthermore it is made for oribid.nt and use, as siman 11; but where there is an eruv, it appears permitted to go out, for he does not carry it except for honor; but an ordinary person not carrying for honor ; even where there is eruv forbidden, for degradation of Shabbos, as siman 422; and it appears what the Tur wrote "one may not go out with a chair," etc., and if many need it, permitted ; also deals with place that has eruv; thus is resolved the rabbi's difficulty.
