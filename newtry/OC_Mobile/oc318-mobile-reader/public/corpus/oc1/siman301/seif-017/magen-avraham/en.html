But if he can. For example, an elderly person who walks at home without a cane but leans upon one outdoors because of weakness may not go out with it. This remains forbidden even if his body sways. Semak. Rosh and Maggid Mishneh likewise write that it is permitted specifically when he cannot walk at all without a cane. It is therefore obvious that distinguished people who customarily carry a cane for honor may not go out with it on Shabbos. It cannot be considered an ornament, for we find no permission to carry an ornament in one's hand. Even Rabbi Eliezer, who holds that weapons are ornaments, says only that one is not liable to a sin-offering; a prohibition remains. Tosafos 62 likewise implies that initially carrying an ornament in one's hand is forbidden because of appearances. Raavan 349 writes likewise. The Yerushalmi's statement that Rabban Gamliel went with a key 'in his hand' cannot mean literally in his hand, for then the concern that he may remove and show it would be inapplicable. See Beis Yosef. It must mean, as Ran writes, that it was inserted on his finger like a ring. This proves that carrying an ornament in one's hand is forbidden; see Beis Yosef. Furthermore, it is made both as an ornament and for use, as stated in se'if katan 11. Where there is an eruv, however, it appears that an honored person may go out with it, since he carries it only for honor. An ordinary person who does not carry it for honor may not do so even where there is an eruv, because this demeans Shabbos, as stated in siman 522. It appears that Tur's ruling, 'One may not go out in a chair, etc.; if the public needs him, it is permitted,' likewise concerns a place with an eruv. This resolves Beis Yosef's question. Rambam writes that if an amputee went out with his prosthetic leg or wooden ankatmin, he is exempt.
