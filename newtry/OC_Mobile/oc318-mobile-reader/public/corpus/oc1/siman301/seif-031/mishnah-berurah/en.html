116) He intended to gather its corners, etc. He takes the front and rear ends on each side, folds them, and raises them on his hand or shoulder. Unlike se'if 29, where the entire rear hem is folded onto his shoulder, here only the ends are folded. It is therefore prohibited but does not create liability, and it depends on his intent.
117) But our garments. The garment itself remains worn in its normal manner. Later authorities nevertheless permit raising it only slightly, not lifting it high and tucking it beneath the arm so that it forms a gutter-like fold.
118) And he takes his hands out of it. If he does not remove his hands, as with certain women's mantles, it is not ordinarily worn raised. Still, one should be stringent only about raising it high; lifting it slightly to avoid dirt remains a manner of wearing.
