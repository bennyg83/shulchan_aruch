44) Regarding going out on Shabbos in the nail-studded sandal called kabkab in Arabic, although it is not the sandal mentioned in the chapter Bameh Isha, it should nevertheless be forbidden because it is a burden or because it may slip off and he may come to carry it. It can be remedied by constructing it so that it does not slip from his foot, like the sandals in Europe whose upper portion is covered. They then constitute shoes, do not slip off, and are not a burden. See Radvaz 3, Furth edition, siman 11, who rules that a wooden shoe not covered with leather, called sueikos - kabkab in Arabic - may not be worn outside on Shabbos even into an alley without an eruv. The leather placed over its top often slips off; he can no longer wear it, and there is a substantial concern that he will carry it in his hand. Ikrei HaDat 15:3. See Birkei Yosef 10, however, who writes in Maharikash's name that some permit it. Machazik Berachah 7 cites several poskim who prohibit it like Radvaz; see there.
