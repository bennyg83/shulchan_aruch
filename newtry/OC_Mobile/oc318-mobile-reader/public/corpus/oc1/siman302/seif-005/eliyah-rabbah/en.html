[11] [Levush] But if it was, etc. Magen Avraham rules categorically that it is prohibited, following Maggid Mishneh cited above. It is surprising that he does not mention Levush's permissive ruling, which is also the view of Tur and Rabbenu Yerucham, and Tzedah LaDerech rules likewise. These are major later authorities. Moreover, I have already established that according to their reasoning, and because according to all opinions the matter is rabbinic, we rule leniently.
[12] [Levush] And he recognizes, etc. This is the wording of Tur, Tzedah LaDerech, and Maggid Mishneh there. It requires study, because the Mishnah there states, "If it was loosened before Shabbos, he loosens all of it and slips it out," which sounds as though even if it was only partly loosened he may loosen the rest. This is also the text in editions of the Mishnah with Rambam and Tosafot Yom Tov. In the Yerushalmi Mishnah the wording is "he releases his garments," and so it appears in Rosh, the Remezim, Rabbenu Yerucham, and Rif.
[13] And other garments, etc. According to this, one must say that the linen garment permitted here is not pure linen (Nachalat Tzvi). Malbushei Yom Tov, however, explains that even a pure linen garment is permitted because it is an undergarment and is not visible from outside. He agrees with Shibbolei HaLeket in one respect and disagrees in another.
