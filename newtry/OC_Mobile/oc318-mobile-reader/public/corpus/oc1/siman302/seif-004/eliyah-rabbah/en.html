[10] And releasing it resembles, etc. Rashi on Shabbos 141a and Tur explain this way. Therefore the text concludes that if it was already loosened, etc., one may slip it out, because then there is no rationale for prohibition. According to this, the first Tanna does not disagree with Tosafot. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 26, however, writes that even according to Rashi the first Tanna may disagree because of a decree lest one come to release it. In my humble opinion this is incorrect, because even when it is not loosened the act does not truly resemble demolition. Proof is that according to Rambam, chapter 26, there is no demolition issue at all, for he gives the reason of muktzeh. Malbushei Yom Tov cites Aruch likewise. It appears possible that even according to Rambam's reason, if it was loosened one may slip it out, for only while it remains in the press is it muktzeh, contrary to Maggid Mishneh. In truth, Rambam is surprising in light of the Yerushalmi at the end of the chapter Tolin, which asks from the law that seals on utensils may be untied and cut, and answers that a press is treated as attached to the ground. Thus the reason is demolition, as Rashi explains, parallel to seals attached to the ground in siman 314, seif 10.
