6) There, gloss: This applies specifically to a new garment, etc. This implies that if it was already laundered, there is no concern. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, writes the same. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2, similarly writes that if it was laundered and he is not particular, shaking it does not constitute squeezing because he does not intend that result; see there. This is also implied by Magen Avraham's words cited in the preceding paragraph, that one is ordinarily particular about a new garment. It implies that only regarding a new garment is one presumed particular. If it was already laundered, the law depends upon his intent: if he is particular, it is forbidden; if he is not, it is permitted. The later authorities write that even where shaking is permitted, such as with an old garment and the like, one must take care not to shake the water from it forcefully enough to cause squeezing. It is known that when a garment is saturated with water, shaking it can squeeze the water out. It must therefore be shaken gently.
