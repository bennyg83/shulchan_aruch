1) [Seif 1] One who shakes out a new tallis, etc. This means that it has not been used extensively and remains as new as when the craftsmen prepared it. Beis Yosef in the name of Rambam; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Bach writes that even if it was laundered, so long as it has not been used extensively, it is still called new and shaking it improves it; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, and Machatzis HaShekel cite this. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1, similarly writes that the law does not depend upon laundering. If it has not been used extensively, shaking it is forbidden even if it was laundered; if it has been used extensively, shaking it is permitted even if it was not laundered; see there. Levush Serad writes the same. See Minchas Pittim, folio 47b, who writes in the name of Yaaros Devash that for us it is forbidden in every case, because we are not proficient in determining how long a garment is called new; see there. Orach Chaim, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, klal 22, paragraph 9; Ben Ish Chai, parashas Vayechi, paragraph 7.
2) There: To remove dew from it, he is liable. This applies specifically to a new, black garment that is improved by shaking, like laundering, and only when he is particular not to wear it without shaking it. If any one of these conditions is absent, it is permitted. Tur; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2.
3) There: To remove dew from it, etc. The same applies to snow. Eliyah Rabbah there in the name of Agudah; Chayei Adam, klal 22, paragraph 9.
4) There: Provided that he is particular about it, etc. Rashba explains in his novellae that for one who is particular, the beneficial effect is an inevitable consequence, while for one who is not particular it is an unintended consequence, since the garment is not fully laundered. These are his words. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 2.
5) There, gloss: This applies specifically to a new garment about which he is particular. This is because it contracts in water. Beis Yosef, siman 301, in the name of Kol Bo. This implies that one is ordinarily particular about any new garment. Although regarding dew the Gemara says that the law applies only when he is particular, water differs because it causes the garment to contract. Sefer HaZichronos forbids this regarding all garments. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. This means black, white, or any other color, as Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, writes in the name of Sefer HaZichronos. Levush Serad writes that this is the consensus of the later authorities, because Kol Bo, the source of the law, explains that the concern is squeezing. There is consequently no distinction. See Petach HaDevir, paragraph 3.
6) There, gloss: This applies specifically to a new garment, etc. This implies that if it was already laundered, there is no concern. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, writes the same. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2, similarly writes that if it was laundered and he is not particular, shaking it does not constitute squeezing because he does not intend that result; see there. This is also implied by Magen Avraham's words cited in the preceding paragraph, that one is ordinarily particular about a new garment. It implies that only regarding a new garment is one presumed particular. If it was already laundered, the law depends upon his intent: if he is particular, it is forbidden; if he is not, it is permitted. The later authorities write that even where shaking is permitted, such as with an old garment and the like, one must take care not to shake the water from it forcefully enough to cause squeezing. It is known that when a garment is saturated with water, shaking it can squeeze the water out. It must therefore be shaken gently.
7) There, gloss: Some say it is forbidden to shake dust from a garment, etc. This applies specifically to a black, new garment, as stated above. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3. This means as stated at the beginning of the seif. The Gemara says that one who shakes out his tallis on Shabbos is liable to bring a sin offering. Rashi explains this as removing dust, while Rabbeinu Chananel explains it as removing dew. The Gemara states that the liability applies specifically when the garment is new and black and the person is particular about it, but there is no concern regarding white or red garments. Thus, whether the passage is interpreted as dew or dust, it applies specifically to a new, black garment about which he is particular. Magen Avraham therefore writes here that it applies specifically to a black, new garment, as at the beginning of the seif.
8) There, gloss: It is proper to account for his view. Bach similarly writes that ab initio one should take care not to shake off either dust or water; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, however, writes that the act is forbidden by strict law; see there. The later authorities likewise write that shaking off dust is forbidden. Chayei Adam, klal 22, paragraph 9; Ben Ish Chai, parashas Vayechi, paragraph 8; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 80, paragraph 9. See above, paragraphs 1 and 2; the same applies here regarding dust. See Petach HaDevir, paragraph 4, who writes that if a person leaned against a wall and plaster dust adhered to his garment, he may strike the garment with a finger snap until it leaves. If, however, thick, wet plaster adhered to it and dried, one should be stringent, as stated below in seif 7 regarding dry mud; see there.
9) Mordechai writes at the end of the chapter Chavis that we are not particular about wearing a garment with dust or dew upon it, and shaking it is therefore permitted. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, cites this. The fact that Tur, the Shulchan Aruch, and the later authorities record the present law, however, implies that we do not follow Mordechai in this regard, and even for us shaking is forbidden.
10) There, gloss: But it is permitted to remove feathers, etc. This is because feathers do not adhere to the garment like dust and earth. This applies when the feathers came from a bolster or cushion and are not muktzeh. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3; Ben Ish Chai, parashas Vayechi, paragraph 8.
11) There, gloss: But it is permitted to remove feathers, etc. Sefer HaZichronos, folio 53, is concerned that this is forbidden. Knesses HaGedolah there; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 4; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 5. It is therefore proper to take care not to remove any feather from a black garment or anything else hanging from it. Ben Ish Chai there.
12) A person whose clothes became entangled in thorns should separate them discreetly and slowly so that they do not tear. If they tear, he is not liable, because he did not intend this. Magen Avraham there; Eliyah Rabbah there; Tosefes Shabbos there.
