Because Taz Shabbat 75b and Rashi there, and Rambam, may his memory be blessed, chapter 10 of Hilchot Shabbat 18 wrote: one who gathers a yavel is liable, but in a business manner is exempt. One who shakes a new tallit to remove white wool hanging on it is liable for a sin-offering; if he is not particular it is permitted. See Lechem Mishneh there, who explains that in Shabbat 147a the shaking is not per R' Chiya from dew, and not per Rashi from dirt, but rather white wool hanging on it; therefore he omitted the law of shaking dew mentioned there, and he is liable for makeh bepatish. And in Lechem Mishneh it appears that the reading is "and to remove the yellow-white wool hanging on it" — he is liable; and this is dew that appears yellow-white on black and is not attractive. See Kol HaGadol in the Hagahot of Beit Yosef and Eliyah Rabbah here. The distinction between one who picks a yavel — he performs an action by hand: whoever is particular and removes intentionally, even if he was wearing it so, is a finish and makeh bepatish — unlike shaking, which does not perform much action; whoever is not particular to wear it is also permitted; but if one picks unintentionally he is exempt but it is forbidden. The Mechaber in siman 1 omitted entirely the view of the Rambam — Rashi and Tosafos do not hold like him; only the explanation of shaking is for laundering per R' Chiya and Rosh and Tur. And regarding picking he ruled like Rambam: all who act intentionally are liable, but if one is not particular to the wearer and it is in a business manner he is exempt but it is forbidden. But Taz ot 2 was not left easy for him in this — since both are to Rambam from makeh bepatish, and automatically the shaker in 147a where the sages are not particular, even intentionally all who wear it so are permitted — the same would apply to yavel: all who wear it so are exempt and permitted, it appears. And Beit Yosef holds that Rambam already warned us in each place in Hilchot Shabbat and so in the Gemara "exempt and forbidden," and necessarily one must distinguish between yavel and shaker. And what is doubted in Beit Yosef and Magen Avraham there regarding the shaker — whether intentionally, even if he was wearing it so, he is liable — if so, in 147a where he shakes intentionally, why is it permitted? One must say that intention means to beautify the garment; they do not intend at all to beautify the garment, and this requires further study. See ot 2 from this, and see there it will be explained in this.
