[1] [Levush] When new, etc. Even if the garment has been laundered, if it has not been used much and still retains its newness as when the craftsmen finished it, it is called new (Bach). The same rule as dew applies to snow (Agudah).
[2] When one is particular about them, etc. Because garments shrink in water (Beit Yosef, end of siman 301, in the name of Kol Bo). According to this, once it has already been laundered there is no such concern. Olat Tamid writes that since the reason is squeezing, there is no distinction whether one is particular or not, or between black and other colors. This is apparently contrary to Kol Bo. It must be that Olat Tamid follows Magen Avraham, who writes that ordinarily a new garment is treated carefully. Although regarding dew the Gemara distinguishes based on whether one is particular, water differs because it causes shrinking. Sefer HaZichronot prohibits this for all garments, and Shiltei Gibborim, cited in seif katan 3, implies likewise.
[3] [Levush] And one should be concerned, etc. In my humble opinion it is prohibited as a matter of law, for Sefer Yere'im, Sefer HaTerumah, Shibbolei HaLeket, and Tanya rule like Rashi. Shiltei Gibborim also interprets Rambam according to Rashi, and the Gemara plainly indicates that the prohibition applies specifically to a new garment. Sefer HaTerumah and Mordechai at the end of the chapter Chaviyot write that since we are not particular about wearing a garment with dust or dew on it, shaking it is permitted. Gedulat Mordechai explains that if both dust and dew are present it is prohibited, but this is not compelled, and Shiltei Gibborim's statement that we may shake it in every circumstance does not imply so. Shiltei Gibborim adds that when shaking off dew or rainwater, one must not shake so vigorously that it causes squeezing, for when a garment is thoroughly saturated with dew or water, forceful shaking extracts the liquid.
[4] To remove a feather, etc. Sefer HaZichronot, p. 53, is concerned that this may be prohibited. It further writes that if even a small part became bent, one may not straighten it. Tearing is a primary melachah. Therefore, if extra garments catch on thorns, one should separate them discreetly so they do not tear. If they nevertheless tear, he is not liable because he did not intend it (Rambam, chapter 22). Levush's statement regarding heavy items, etc., is also written by Rama in siman 337. Taz objects that even if one breaks something, this is merely destructive and exempt. There is no difficulty, because it is an inevitable result, as Shiltei Gibborim writes at the end of the chapter HaMatznia. Destructive activity is nevertheless prohibited initially.
[5] [Levush] Splinters, etc. If flaxseed or cooked food fell onto his garment, he may peel it off (Behag). Regarding removal of the temporary tailor's stitching called a basting stitch, it is proper to be stringent if one is particular about it (Shiltei Gibborim; Magen Avraham).
[6] To wear it, etc. Lechem Mishneh in the chapter Elu Kesharim writes that even if one does not know that he will need to sit again, it is permitted, because there is no fixed measure regarding preparation of clothing.
[7] [Levush] And colored garments, etc. The text should read "or colored garments," and so it appears in Tur. If I have another garment to change into, etc., even if the substitute is not as fine as this one. Folding a garment on a bench is considered like folding it with two people (Yerushalmi; Kol Bo).
[8] [Levush] And therefore, etc. It appears to me that this refers back to what he wrote, "to wear them that same day." Therefore it is prohibited to fold, etc., even new garments when he has no substitute, if they are not needed that day. Tosafot, Mordechai, and Agudah there imply this. In places where people wear tallitot at Minchah, it is permitted regarding new ones when he has no substitute. Maharshal rules in the name of Maharam of Rothenburg that new tallitot may be folded on a Yom Tov that falls on Friday when an eruv tavshilin was made, and all the more so on Chol HaMoed.
