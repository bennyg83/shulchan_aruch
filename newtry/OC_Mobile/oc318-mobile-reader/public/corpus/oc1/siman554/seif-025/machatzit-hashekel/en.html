Seif katan 25, if possible, etc. Tisha B'Av, however, is because of affliction, as Rashi wrote. This is based on the Gemara, where Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says that a person should always make himself like a Torah scholar in order to be afflicted. Terumas HaDeshen wrote that Rashi explained, in order to be afflicted, meaning that idleness from work is affliction for them.
But in some poskim it implies, etc. Terumas HaDeshen proves this and asks on Rashi's explanation that refraining from work is affliction, for the Gemara elsewhere calls doing work distress. One must understand how Terumas HaDeshen understood Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's phrase, in order to be afflicted, without Rashi. It must be that he explained it as stated earlier in the name of the Rav, that one should not divert his attention from the affliction, since by doing work he diverts his attention from mourning and affliction. By refraining from work, he remains afflicted by the prohibited afflictions because his mind is not diverted from them. Terumas HaDeshen must hold that according to the reason of avoiding distraction, one should have permitted work that involves no exertion and is an amateur act, since such work is not a distraction. His conclusion that here too the reason is the prohibition of work means not because it is itself affliction. Still, the work is not inherently forbidden as on Yom Kippur; rather, on Tisha B'Av the reason is that one not divert his attention. Otherwise Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's wording would be difficult. Therefore what the Magen Avraham wrote in seif katan 23 requires explanation, since Terumas HaDeshen also accepts this reason.
Therefore, if it is impossible through a non-Jew, it is permitted, etc. Meaning, in Terumas HaDeshen's view, the primary opinion of the poskim is that the prohibition of work on Tisha B'Av is not because of affliction, as he proved from the Gemara. Therefore, just as on Chol HaMoed a Jew may do it himself, the same should be permitted on Tisha B'Av. Where it is possible through a non-Jew, this is preferable, to take into account Rashi's view that the reason is custom. However, from Terumas HaDeshen's discussion it appears that he did not consider milking a matter of loss, for a matter of loss is permitted on Tisha B'Av even by a Jew, as written in seif 23. This still leaves difficulty with the Rama, who wrote above at the end of siman 533 that milking is permitted because it is a matter of loss, from the Beit Yosef's longer work there. This requires study when there is leisure, with Hashem's help, bli neder.
