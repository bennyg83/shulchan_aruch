Immersion. See Baer Heitev. See Mahadura Basra in the name of Zera Emes, that if she did not wash and comb on Erev Tisha B'Av, after the fact one may permit her to wash and comb on Motzaei Tisha B'Av, and that suffices. See the responsum at the end of Mishnas Rabbi Akiva, page 91d. I am astonished that he questions the Baer Heitev, for he did not see that the Baer Heitev innovated nothing but copied the Magen Avraham word for word. What he wrote, see siman 551, means that there it is explained that this is specifically if it is impossible on Motzaei Tisha B'Av; he did not come to disagree but to explain. The disputant did not answer that this is in the Magen Avraham, and only at the end mentioned the Magen Avraham to learn from him that combing is permitted on Tisha B'Av in every case. In my humble opinion the Magen Avraham intended to point to the Rama as I wrote. There he rules that even initially she should comb on Erev Tisha B'Av and on Motzaei Tisha B'Av comb a little. Then she must be careful not to go entirely barefoot lest mud and mire adhere to her. If she wants to follow the Ramah, that where possible she should comb on Motzaei Tisha B'Av, she should instruct a woman to spend one hour in the combing process and thereby satisfy all views, Rashi and Tosafot. So it is proper to rule.
