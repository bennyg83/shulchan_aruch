Maharshal and the Taz wrote that even if she says she needs to eat, she fasts unless she is somewhat ill. However, Malbushei Yom Tov wrote that Maharshal's words do not appear correct, and Issur VeHeter rules that even after seven days she does not fast when she says she needs to eat. The Bach wrote that the custom is to fast as long as they are not weak and have recovered from childbirth, and even on the other three fasts healthy women have been stringent. The Magen Avraham wrote that where this is the custom, it is the custom, but when Tisha B'Av is postponed one may be lenient. It appears that on other postponed fasts one may be entirely lenient, but on Tisha B'Av one should not be lenient from the custom, since according to Maharshal it is the law. The Taz wrote that within three days of childbirth, even if she says she does not need to eat, it is forbidden for her to fast, and certainly within seven when she says she needs to eat. In my humble opinion, within seven she should not be stringent at all on Tisha B'Av, which is D'Rabbanan. Beis Hillel wrote in siman 265 that a woman after childbirth who does not fast should nevertheless fast for some hours.
