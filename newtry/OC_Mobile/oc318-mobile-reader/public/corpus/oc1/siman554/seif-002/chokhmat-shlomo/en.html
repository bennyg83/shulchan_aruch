(there) seif 3: and some forbid learning by thought. — note: Magen Avraham wrote it is forbidden to rule unless for a sick person who needs now; I was asked by the rabbi of Leszno from what is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 484 that ruling for an individual is permitted — see there. I answered I do not know what is difficult, for Magen Avraham himself cited proof from there, and there the intent is ruling needed now, like he concludes there that it is like many needing him; so too Magen Avraham's intent, only he cited a common case since it is Tisha B'Av and forbidden in eating etc., therefore not ruling needed now, so he cited the sick person permitted to eat who needs now; the same for any ruling needed immediately. Regarding Shulchan Aruch's words forbidding learning by thought — certainly thought alone is also forbidden; the main joy of Torah is in the heart, as written "the upright rejoice in the heart"; since thought is in the heart, joy is also in the heart. What Rama wrote: if one examines a book it is not — besides that this is not sensible, what difference if he examines a book or not, since he does not articulate with his lips, only thinks; and even per Rama's understanding he should have said forbidding learning by examining a book — why language "by thought"? necessarily thought is also forbidden. Why it says forbidden to learn and not forbidden to think — the intent is incidentally if some Torah thought comes, he is not obligated to remove it from his heart, only to fix intent to learn some matter; fixed learning by this thought is forbidden, but incidental by thought is permitted as appears to me. I answered the rabbi of Leszno — examine well.
