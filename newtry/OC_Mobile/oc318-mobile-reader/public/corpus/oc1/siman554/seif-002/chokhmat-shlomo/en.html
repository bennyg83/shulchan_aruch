(There) se'if 3. And there is one who forbids learning by means of contemplation. Note: Now Magen Avraham wrote that it is forbidden to issue a ruling except for a sick person who needs it now; and the rabbi of Leshniv challenged me from what is written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 384, that it is permitted to issue a ruling to an individual, see there. And I answered him that I do not know what is difficult for him, for Magen Avraham himself cited his proof from there, and there the intent is a ruling that is needed now, and as he concluded there that it is like when the many need him; and this is likewise Magen Avraham's intent, only that he took a common matter, since it is Tisha B'Av and one is forbidden in eating, etc.; therefore it is not a ruling needed now, so he took the sick person who is permitted to eat and needs it now; and the same law applies to any other ruling needed immediately, that it is permitted to issue the ruling. And as to what your honor stood upon regarding the words of the Shulchan Aruch, that it is forbidden to learn by means of contemplation: behold, this is certainly so, that contemplation alone is also forbidden, and the essential joy of the Torah is in the heart, as it is written, 'The precepts of Hashem are upright, rejoicing the heart'; and since contemplation is in the heart, joy too is in the heart. And what your honor wrote, whether one who studies in a book: this is not so, for besides that it is not a reasoned distinction (what difference if he studies in a book or does not, since he does not utter with his lips but only contemplates), and also according to your honor's understanding he should have said that it is forbidden to learn by means of studying in a book; what then is the language 'by means of contemplation'? Of necessity, contemplation too is forbidden. And as to why he said 'it is forbidden to learn' and did not say 'it is forbidden to contemplate': the intent is that if casually some contemplation of words of Torah comes to him, he is not obligated to remove the contemplation from his heart; only to set himself with intent to learn some matter by means of contemplation, that is forbidden in a fixed way; but casually, by means of contemplation, it is permitted, as it appears to my humble opinion in this. Thus far I answered the rabbi of Leshniv; and examine well.
