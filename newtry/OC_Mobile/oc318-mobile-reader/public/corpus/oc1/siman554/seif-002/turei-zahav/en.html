And chapter 'These may shave.' In the Gemara they dispute: R' Meir holds that one may read and review anywhere in a place where he is not accustomed, because it is distress for him; and R' Yehuda forbids even in this; and the halacha is like him. And one must in truth give a reason why R' Yehuda forbids, seeing that he has distress. And it appears that his reason is because of the end of the learning: after he exerts himself and understands the plain meaning, he will have joy; and we follow the end of the matter. And this reasoning we find explicitly that R' Yehuda holds so regarding another matter, namely in the first chapter of Moed Katan, folio 9: R' Yehuda says a woman should not plaster her face on the moed, and R' Yehuda admits regarding lime that she can peel off on the moed, that she may apply it on the moed, for although now she is in distress, she rejoices afterward; and it was brought above in our words in siman 546. So too here we follow the end, that there is joy, and it is forbidden. And for this reason it appears to me that even in a place where it is permitted to learn, that means he should learn in the plain sense of the words, but not by way of pilpul; and even by contemplation, to explain a difficult matter is forbidden, for the reason that he will have joy after it settles for him, as it appears to my humble opinion.
