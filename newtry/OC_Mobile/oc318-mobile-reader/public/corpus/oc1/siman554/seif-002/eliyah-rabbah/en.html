And it is forbidden to read, etc. In my Eliyah Zuta I wondered about those who study a set lesson in the synagogue on Tisha B'Av after Maariv while it is still day. The Bach in siman 559 and the Maharil wrote that all matters listed in this seif are forbidden until dark, for they are from the law of the Gemara, and the Shelah wrote similarly. One cannot say that Maariv makes it night for leniency. Although Shibolei HaLeket mentions putting on shoes at Maariv, perhaps they prayed close to nightfall. The Geonim wrote that one who does any of these the whole day of Tisha B'Av is under ban. The custom to say Tehillim at Minchah is no proof, for that is said as supplication. The Kol Bo records an early custom for women to wash from Minchah and onward as a remembrance of the birth of Moshiach, but for others one who is lenient to wash his feet or any place the whole day violates the words of the Sages. The Beit Yosef at the end of siman 554 wrote the custom, but even for women it is forbidden.
