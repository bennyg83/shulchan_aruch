In the Taz seif katan 4 — and it is difficult, for from that he said "estimation is not required" it is implied there is doubt, etc.; and apparently his words are difficult to understand, for in his answer he concludes that it means estimation is not required — even certainly there is no danger in it, etc. Also study is required — what compelled him in his answer to explain even certainly there is no danger, etc., and not explain this "estimation is not required" simply — that it deals with doubt, but that she did not say "I need"; and it appears his intent: the Taz thus challenged — from that he said "she does not require estimation" it is implied it deals with an ill person where we are in doubt whether there is danger in the matter; and he says estimation is not required — perhaps according to my assessment she will be endangered because of the fast; rather they feed her immediately. And this was difficult for the Taz — for if she says "I need," if so what does it teach us that estimation is not required? For behold even if physicians assess her and say there is no danger, nevertheless if a physician says she does not need and the ill person says she needs — we heed the ill person. And what the Taz wrote "there is no danger" — estimation does not apply; one could say if it deals with an ill person where danger does not apply and there is no doubt in the matter that certainly there is no place for danger — if so estimation does not apply; and therefore it must deal where there is doubt whether she will be endangered; if so it is obvious that estimation is not required — for even if they assessed her we pay no attention, since estimation is required; and also one should not explain "estimation is not required" simply and she did not say "I need" — for then it would be truly difficult, since then they would assess her that there is no danger in her it would be forbidden; if so what difference to me Yom Kippur that is d'oraisa, what to me Tisha B'Av that is d'rabbanan — why should we not assess her? And if they say she will not be endangered we should not feed her, since she does not say "needs"; and likewise for an ill person in this manner — for if we knew there is no danger we would not feed her. One may not be lenient without assessment; therefore the Taz explained that it means estimation is not required — meaning that even if they assess that she will not be endangered, nevertheless they feed her even though she does not say "needs," since this ill person in any event there is doubt lest she be endangered through it; we have no need to be strict per the assessment that she will not be endangered, since Tisha B'Av is d'rabbanan; and for this matter it applies well to distinguish between d'rabbanan and d'oraisa — between Tisha B'Av and Yom Kippur; not so if we say when they assess her that she will not be endangered it is forbidden even on Tisha B'Av — we have no need to be lenient without assessment, since it can be clarified even on Tisha B'Av to be strict; such appears the intent of the Taz.
