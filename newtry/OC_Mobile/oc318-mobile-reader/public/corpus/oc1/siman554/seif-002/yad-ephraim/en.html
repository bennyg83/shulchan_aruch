In the Taz, s.k. 4. And it is difficult for me: from that he said 'assessment is not required,' it is implied that there is doubt, etc. And apparently his words are hard to understand; for in his answer he concludes that it means assessment is not required, even if certainly there is no danger in it, etc. Also this requires further study: what compelled him in his answer to explain even if certainly there is no danger, etc., and not explain this 'assessment is not required' simply, that it deals with doubt, except that she did not say 'I need'? And it appears that his intent is as follows: the Taz thus found difficult: from that he said she does not require assessment, it is implied that it deals with a sick person regarding whom we are in doubt whether there is danger in the matter; and he says assessment is not required, lest according to my assessment she be endangered because of the fast; rather they feed her immediately. And on this the Taz found difficult: for if she says 'I need,' if so what does it teach us that assessment is not required? For behold, even when physicians assess her and say there is no danger, nevertheless if a physician says she does not need and the sick person says she needs, we heed the sick person. And what the Taz wrote, 'there is no danger; assessment does not apply': one can say, if it deals with a sick person to whom danger does not apply, and there is no doubt in the matter that certainly there is no place for danger, then assessment does not apply; and therefore it must deal where there is doubt whether she will be endangered; if so it is obvious that assessment is not required, for even if they assessed her we pay no attention at all, since she needs [food] by assessment as well. And also one should not explain 'assessment is not required' simply, and she did not say 'I need'; for then it would truly be difficult: since then they would assess her that there is no danger in her and it would be forbidden; if so, what difference to me Yom Kippur, whose prohibition is according to the Torah, and what difference to me Tisha B'Av, which is rabbinic: why should we not assess her, and if they say she will not be endangered we should not feed her, since she does not say 'I need'? And likewise regarding a sick person of this kind; for if we knew there is no danger we would not feed her: one should not be lenient without assessment. Therefore the Taz explained that it means assessment is not required, that is, because even if they assess that she will not be endangered, nevertheless they feed her even when she does not say 'I need,' since this sick person in any event has a doubt lest she be endangered through it; we need not be stringent according to the assessment that she will not be endangered, since Tisha B'Av is rabbinic. And regarding this it fits well to distinguish between rabbinic and according to the Torah, that is, between Tisha B'Av and Yom Kippur; which is not so if we say that when they assess her that she will not be endangered it is forbidden even on Tisha B'Av: we need not be lenient without assessment, since it can be clarified even on Tisha B'Av to be stringent; such appears to my humble opinion to be the intent of the Taz.
