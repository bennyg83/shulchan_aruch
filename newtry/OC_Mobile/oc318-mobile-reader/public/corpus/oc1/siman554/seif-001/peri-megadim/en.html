And toddlers. See Taz: he wrote this to exclude the Bach's reasoning; see Magen Avraham 2. And it appears that a child of twelve, somewhat grown and understanding, even the Taz admits that it is forbidden for him to learn by himself that which brings him joy; and specifically a small child who has distress in his learning, etc. And see siman 551 in Magen Avraham, letter 38. See Eliyah Rabbah and Or Zarua that Torah study is forbidden all day until nightfall, for all that is forbidden by law; and specifically sitting on the stools, which is custom, is permitted after midday. See in the Tur: one who eats and drinks is given makkat mardut; and this requires further study whether for the other afflictions they administer [makkat mardut]; and this requires further study whether if he ate a half-measure they administer [it], and also at twilight. See Yoreh De'ah 294 regarding one who habitually [ate] goat and shibuta, in Beis Yosef, that they do not give makkat mardut on a doubt; and in the work Shoshanat HaAmakim, in the general discussion of makkat mardut, we wrote about this; and there is a practical difference for those disqualified as witnesses; the Rambam, chapter 10 of Hilchos Edus: if the liability in it was from their [rabbinic] words; see Choshen Mishpat, siman 34. See in the Rambam, of blessed memory, Hilchos Chametz U'Matzah, chapter 1, halacha 7, what he wrote regarding Pesach: Maggid Mishneh implies that for six [he does] not; and in halacha 10, one who derives benefit, it is implied he is not lashed; Maggid Mishneh implies the general rule: whatever, if it were Torah law of this kind, he would not be lashed, the same applies for something rabbinic; and see Lechem Mishneh there. And this requires further study; and in Shoshanat HaAmakim we wrote about this at length.
