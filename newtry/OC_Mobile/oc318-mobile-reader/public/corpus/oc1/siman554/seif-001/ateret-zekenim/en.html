And infants of the beit rabban, etc. And even bad words are likewise forbidden to learn with them, because the infants do not understand well what they utter from their mouths, and their principal joy is in knowing what they are saying; therefore one may not distinguish between them regarding words of comfort versus bad things; therefore if the infant understands what he is saying, it is permitted to learn bad things with him. There is one who wrote that one should not bless on Tisha B'Av and Yom Kippur "who has provided all my needs," because then it is forbidden to wear sandals [Lechem Min HaShamayim]; and if he must go before the minister and they are strict about him if he goes without shoes, he should put dust in his shoes and walk (Mordechai end of Moed Katan). And wherever it is necessary to walk in shoes, he should do so. And the custom that women wash their heads from minchah onward — and the early elders instituted this and made support for the matter based on what was said in the aggadah that the Messiah was born on Tisha B'Av — and one must make a remembrance of the redeemer and comfort them so they do not despair of redemption; and this is specifically for women, because they are weak in believing consolations since they do not know Scripture; therefore they need strengthening (Kol Bo). And commerce — if it is a lost matter, it is permitted as they permitted on the festivals (Rashba responsa siman 591). And any melacha — even that of a layman, even if it is not laborious — if there is something in it that he derives some amusement from it, one should not do it on Tisha B'Av, for thereby he nullifies the affliction (Terumat HaDeshen siman 153).
