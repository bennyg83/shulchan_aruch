(1) [Seif 1] Tisha B'Av is forbidden in washing, etc. Everything that the Sages prohibited is forbidden until nightfall, like eating and drinking. Only things that are merely custom and not mentioned in the Talmud, such as sitting on the ground, in this some have the practice to be lenient after midday. Shelah p. 199b. So too wrote the Bach at the beginning of siman 559. R' Chananel wrote that anyone who does one of all these the entire day should be placed in niddui [excommunication]. The Tanya and the Shelah cited his words. Magen Avraham s.k. 1. Eliyah Rabbah os 2. But those who walk among non-Jews and engage in commerce after midday, they are permitted to wear shoes, and from them everyone has learned to be lenient, and one should protest against them. Magen Avraham ibid. Eliyah Rabbah ibid. Mishna Berurah os 1. See below os 5.
(2) Also during twilight [bein hashmashos] all of these are forbidden, and the same applies to Torah study which is forbidden until the emergence of the stars. Eishel Avraham os 1, Mishna Berurah ibid.
(3) Regarding the matter of smoking tobacco on Tisha B'Av: the Knesset HaGedolah wrote in siman 551 in the Hagahos Beis Yosef os 21 that a case came before him of someone who smoked on Tisha B'Av and he excommunicated him. Likewise in siman 567 in the Hagahos Beis Yosef os 3 he discussed this at length and wrote that on Tisha B'Av it is forbidden according to all opinions, see there. So too wrote in the responsa Darkei No'am siman 9, and Beis David siman 291. The Zera Avraham siman 73 [wrote] to forbid on Tisha B'Av, see there. So too wrote Chayei Adam rule 135 os 27. The Birke Yosef also wrote in siman 567 that in our times the practice has spread to permit (i.e., on other fasts), except on Tisha B'Av where some are strict because of the mourning, see there. However, the Sha'arei Teshuvah siman 559 os 4 wrote that one may be lenient after midday and in private inside his home, see there. So too he wrote above in siman 511 os 35 that some are lenient after midday, see there. However, the Machberes above in siman 210 os 13 wrote that a great authority dreamed with a distinguished Rav and adjured him to tell him what they say in Heaven about smoking tobacco. He answered that one who smokes on Yom Tov is excommunicated, one who smokes on Tisha B'Av is cursed, and similarly one who smokes under the wedding canopy, see there. So too he wrote in his work Mo'ed L'Chol Chai os 212, see there, and I cited his words above in siman 210 os 35, see there. The Ruach Chayyim os 5 also wrote that it should not be permitted except after the Maariv prayer, i.e., after twelve hours, see there. So too he wrote in his work Milei D'Chasidusa siman 10 os 80, see there. See further in Sha'arei Shamayim by Rav Moed Katan p. 71b, who wrote: "Smoking tobacco, the Knesset HaGedolah forbade it and excommunicated over it, and the law is with him, for it is no less than pleasure, strolling, and diverting one's mind from mourning. So too my master, my father, my teacher, of blessed memory, was accustomed to protest and punish one who transgressed deliberately. Nevertheless, for a certain need, when one does not intend for pleasure but only to aid in bodily functions, as is known from its nature and beneficial effect in this regard, in such a case it is obvious to me that it is permitted. However, [it should be done] where no one can see, so that they do not learn to be lenient, and especially at night which is somewhat more lenient in any case regarding melacha, in my opinion. On this I relied and I myself did so once at night when I needed it, etc. Therefore during the day I am afraid to act leniently even for the aforementioned need, although there are grounds to be lenient for others in such a case, and it should not be more stringent than anointing and washing which are permitted when one does not intend to derive pleasure from them." It seems that if one needs it during the day for his bodily functions as mentioned, one may be lenient to smoke it specifically in the privy, in private, out of sight of others. See further below siman 567 os 18.
(4) However, it is permitted to snuff tobacco through the nostrils on Tisha B'Av and on Yom Kippur. But one who has adopted the practice to treat it as forbidden, his prohibition stands and it should not be permitted for him, as stated above in siman 210 os 37, see there. Those who add a pleasant scent to the snuff on Tisha B'Av are not acting properly. Milei D'Chasidusa siman 10 os 74. See below siman 556 regarding pleasant scents and spices.
(5) Ibid. And wearing shoes. It is forbidden to wear shoes the entire day. Magen Avraham s.k. 1 in the name of the Geonim. The Shelah wrote that some bring their shoes in their hands and when they pray Maariv they put them on. The Eliyah Rabbah os 2 cited his words and wrote that it is possible they prayed Maariv close to nightfall. Furthermore, he wrote above in the name of the Geonim that anyone who does one of all these the entire day on Tisha B'Av should be excommunicated, see there. This implies that his view is that even if they prayed Maariv, it is forbidden by strict law. However, see the Machberes os 2 where he discussed at length and questioned the words of the Eliyah Rabbah and wrote that it is merely an enactment, and if their custom all year round was to pray Maariv while it is still day, it is perhaps permitted. However, for practical purposes he wrote that one should wait until nightfall and it should not be permitted beforehand even if they prayed Maariv, as the later authorities wrote, see there.
(6) Ibid. And wearing shoes. To sanctify the new moon on Motzei Tisha B'Av, one should not do so barefoot. Eishel Avraham os 1. See above siman 426 seif 2 in the Rama's gloss and our words there os 29.
(7) Ibid. It is forbidden to study Torah, etc. One who has an established practice of being called up to the Torah on Tisha B'Av and it happens that he is a mourner, he may be called up to the Torah at Shacharis on Tisha B'Av, since it consists of sorrowful matters and everyone is forbidden in Torah study just like him. Beis Yehuda vol. 2 siman 84. This is obvious, for he may certainly be called up to the Torah at Shacharis and read the Haftarah even though he is a mourner. Machberes os 1. The Sha'arei Teshuvah os 1 cited his words and wrote that this implies the same applies even if he does not have an established practice of being called up to the Torah on Tisha B'Av. See below siman 559 os 55.
(8) Ibid. And schoolchildren are idle [from Torah study] on it. Even regarding the sorrowful passages in Yirmiyahu, Kinos, and Iyov, this applies specifically to children who do not understand what they are saying, but a child who has reached the level of understanding what he says is permitted to study the sorrowful passages in Yirmiyahu. Bach. Knesset HaGedolah in the glosses os 1, Ateres Zahav, Shulchan Gavoah os 1. The Taz s.k. 1 wrote that Iyov and other subjects that are permitted for adults are also permitted to study with children, and they only forbade the studies that children learn in their regular curriculum, namely Chumash and Gemaras, see there. This is also the view of the Dagul Merevavah. However, the Magen Avraham s.k. 2 wrote that it is forbidden to study anything at all with others; one may only teach them about the destruction, which is merely a narrative account and breaks the child's heart, see there. This appears to be the view of the Nishmas HaShulchan os 1, and the reason as the Ya'aros P'ri wrote is that it is forbidden to study anything at all with others, because the adult derives joy from studying with others; so too wrote the Machatzis HaShekel. Thus there are three opinions in this matter: according to the Bach, only with a child who understands is it permitted to study the sorrowful passages; according to the Taz, even with one who does not understand, the sorrowful passages are permitted; and according to the Magen Avraham, whether the child understands or not, even the sorrowful passages are forbidden, and it is only permitted to teach him about the destruction. It seems that according to all opinions, if the child studies by himself the sorrowful passages, whether he understands or not, it is permitted. Therefore, one should allow children to study the sorrowful passages on their own so that they do not go roaming the streets and occupy themselves with idle matters.
(9) There is one who wrote that nowadays, since for most children studying causes them distress and being idle is a pleasure for them, it is permitted to teach children the sorrowful passages. His words do not seem correct. Birke Yosef os 1.
(10) Ibid. But one may read Iyov, etc. If he does not want to study at all, he is permitted, and we do not say that since it is permitted he is not allowed to neglect Torah study, as discussed at length by the Mateh Yehuda in Yoreh De'ah siman 384 regarding a mourner, see there. Erech HaShulchan os 1.
(11) Ibid. And the sorrowful passages, etc. But the misfortunes of the nations are forbidden to read. Magen Avraham s.k. 3. Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 124 os 5, Mishna Berurah os 2.
(12) Ibid. And if there are verses of consolation among them, one must skip them. From here it is proven that the consolations written at the end of each kinah must be skipped. So too wrote the Pri Chadash in the collected writings on siman 559 seif 5 that those who say a consolation at the end of each kinah are mistaken, for if so, why did [the Mechaber] write here that if there are verses of consolation one must skip them, see there. So too wrote the Machberes on this siman os 3, Kissei Avraham os 1, Ma'amar Mordechai siman 559 os 4. However, see Milei D'Chasidusa siman 10 os 67, who distinguished between verses of consolation and the recitation of consolations at the end of the kinos. Therefore he wrote that one should not deviate from their custom, for at night they do not say the concluding passages of the kinos, but during the day they say the concluding passages of all the kinos that contain consolations, see there. However, we follow the majority view, and also because passive inaction [shev v'al ta'aseh] is preferable. But if there are those who practice this way, one should not protest against them.
