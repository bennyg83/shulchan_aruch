Anointing, etc., except that which is of pleasure. And accordingly it is simple that to remove filth is permitted, as is clarified in siman 575, se'if 3, regarding a public fast, and likewise regarding mourning in Yoreh De'ah, siman 381; and as the Gra copied from the Yerushalmi explicitly in siman 614, see there. And the Mechaber should have copied here the language that he himself wrote in Yoreh De'ah, siman 381, that to remove filth is permitted, and it goes without saying for the sake of healing, such as when he has sores on his head; but he abbreviated his language. And so wrote Lechem Mishneh in Hilchos Taanis, chapter 5, halacha 10; and so it is implied from the Gra in this siman; and not like Mateh Yehudah, who intended to say that to remove filth is forbidden, and his proof from what is stated in Pesachim 54: there is no difference between Tisha B'Av and Yom Kippur except that this one's doubt is forbidden and that one's is permitted, see there; and his view is that the baraita brought in the Bavli disputes the baraita brought in the Yerushalmi. But in truth one certainly should not push aside a baraita brought plainly in the Yerushalmi in several places on account of some fine inference. And in truth it is no proof at all; for the intent is that things they forbade on Tisha B'Av, such as washing and anointing, they forbade even to wash one limb, such as putting a finger in water or anointing that limb, as on Yom Kippur; and specifically when the anointing is of pleasure; but anointing that is not of pleasure they did not forbid at all. And see in Taanis, folio 30: Our Rabbis taught: all mitzvos that apply to a mourner apply on the ninth of Av, etc.; and regarding anointing and wearing sandal, etc.; and since regarding a mourner it is stated there on folio 13b that to remove filth is permitted, automatically the same applies on the ninth of Av.
