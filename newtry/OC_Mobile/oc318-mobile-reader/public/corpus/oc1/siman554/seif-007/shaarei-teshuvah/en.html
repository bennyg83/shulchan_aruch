To fast. See Baer Heitev. In a manuscript responsum of Pnei Yehoshua, he disagrees with Maharshal's responsum cited by the Magen Avraham, challenges his proofs, and rules like the Shulchan Aruch, that even if Rabbeinu Tam disagrees with Ramban, we rule leniently in a D'Rabbanan matter, and all the more so where it is a possible danger to life. Devar Shmuel, siman 108, wrote that after seven days from childbirth they accepted stringency upon themselves, as the Rama wrote, so long as they have no great pain or concern of danger. Beis Hillel in Yoreh Deah, siman 265, wrote that a woman after childbirth who does not fast should nevertheless fast some hours; Eliyah Rabbah cites this. Everything depends on the matter, and if even this is somewhat difficult for her she need not fast at all. See Zichron Yosef, siman 21, who discusses this at length and concludes practically that if she has already recovered from the weakness of childbirth she should fast and complete the fast like everyone else, but if she has not yet fully recovered, or is somewhat ill or weak, she should not fast within thirty days. If a healthy woman after childbirth is fasting and in the middle of the day feels unusual weakness, one should rule that she not fast for the rest of the day. See also Likkutei Peri Chadash to this siman.
