Seif katan 7, to review, etc. The parsha before he reads it. Eliyah Rabbah asked that this seems obvious, for Ki Solid Banim is relevant to the day. In my humble opinion it was necessary to teach this, because the Magen Avraham wrote that it is forbidden to study a question and answer even in sad matters, since although at first it is distressing, in the end, when he resolves the question or answer, he rejoices. Therefore one might have thought that the reader, who must know the cantillation and vowels by heart before the reading, would likewise have forbidden joy when he masters them. Therefore it teaches that it is permitted, because the reader's joy in knowing the cantillation by heart is not so great.
And see the end of siman 553. Meaning, from there it is somewhat implied as the Magen Avraham wrote, that if Tisha B'Av falls on Shabbos, Torah study is forbidden the whole day, unlike those who say it has the law of Erev Tisha B'Av and, since it is postponed, is forbidden only after midday. For the proper time to read the weekly parsha twice with translation on Shabbos is initially before the meal, which is before midday. If so, what would it teach by saying that reviewing the parsha is permitted? It is difficult to say that it discusses only after the fact, where he did not review it before midday.
