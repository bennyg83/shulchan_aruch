A woman after childbirth, all of thirty days. Meaning: even though regarding Yom Kippur she is [only treated specially] after seven, like other people who are endangered, as written in siman 617, nevertheless here regarding Tisha B'Av they did not decree upon her. Maharshal wrote in a responsum, siman 53, that it deals where she said 'I need [to eat],' and even though regarding Shabbos in siman 330 we do not heed her even if she says 'I need,' and likewise a sick person requires assessment, here we are lenient since it is rabbinic; but if she did not say 'I need,' but is [only] distressed, even if the fast hurts her greatly, she is forbidden [to eat], since she is not [defined as] sick; end quote. And it is difficult for me: from their saying 'she does not require assessment,' it is implied that there is doubt whether there is danger; for if certainly there is no danger, what assessment applies to the matter? And if so, where there is doubt of danger, it is difficult that when she says 'I need' she is permitted even on Yom Kippur, as written in siman 618 regarding a sick person who needs to eat, etc., lest he be endangered, and they feed them on his word when he says 'I need.' And further, why did the Ramban not mention from the start of this law that it deals where she says 'I need'? Therefore it appears to my humble opinion that it deals where she did not say 'I need,' and even so, for a woman after childbirth after seven, and for a sick person regarding whom there is doubt, that sick person does not require assessment saying perhaps there is danger in him; for even if certainly there is no danger, and even if he did not say 'I need,' it is permitted on Tisha B'Av. And Beis Yosef brought the Hagahot Maimoniyot that Rabbeinu Tam permitted a woman after childbirth to eat on a public fast even after seven; and even though we say in chapter 'They clear' that after seven, if she said 'I do not need,' we do not desecrate Shabbos for her, and a public fast is from the words of the tradition, and the words of the tradition are like the words of Torah, even so it is permitted, since if they wish they need not fast, etc., so it is [merely] optional; end quote. This implies that on Tisha B'Av she is forbidden to eat, unlike the Ramban's words; for on Tisha B'Av it does not depend on [communal] will, as written above, since the calamities were doubled on it and it is an obligation. And perhaps for this reason they customarily were stringent, as the Rama wrote; and so is Maharshal's conclusion, that after seven, even if she said 'I need,' she fasts, unless she was somewhat sick; and likewise in a responsum of Maharil.
However, they were accustomed to fast. It appears that this applies specifically after seven; but within three, even if she said 'I do not need,' she should not fast, and it is forbidden for her to fast, since we desecrate Shabbos for her, as written in siman 330; and all the more so after three, within seven, when she says 'I need,' that it is forbidden for her to fast, as it appears to my humble opinion simply.
