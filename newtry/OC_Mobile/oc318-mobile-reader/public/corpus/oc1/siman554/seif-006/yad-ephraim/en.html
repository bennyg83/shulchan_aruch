Magen Avraham s.k. 9 that she should fast after seven; and in Shulchan Aruch there it implies she should not fast — see siman 617 that we require me'al le'mal; and see in Tosafot haDin siman 148 that wrote there Ashkenazim in the long chapter wrote we require me'al le'mal only; and what we do not say doubt of life-threatening situations to be lenient — nevertheless since Ashkenazim did not write so except to reconcile Keneset haGedolah's words, and here Rabbeinu Alfas and R' Yehuda disagree etc.; and even though we rule the day of a baby's creation requires me'al le'mal, one may distinguish between a birth illness and a general illness, end briefly. And it appears to me perhaps he was not stringent here only on Yom Kippur which is d'oraisa and doubt of karet; but Tisha B'Av is d'rabbanan and certainly one may rely on the Rosh who holds we require me'al le'mal; and so appears from Rashal's words that he cited she gave birth on 2 Av — implying if she gave birth 3 Av she is exempt from fasting even though day 1 postponed is after seven; it is strained to say the incident was so, and not to say the reason she is exempt in such a case is because on Shabbat which is Tisha B'Av it is after seven — if it were weekday she would be exempt — if so even on postponed day 1 she is exempt because once the world's fast was fixed on day 1 and then she is after seven we have no reason to exempt her from this; rather it appears he holds we require me'al le'mal; if so if she gave birth 3 Av then seven days did not complete before 10 Av, and when 10 Av entered she was within seven — she may be exempt; and possibly even without postponement one may be lenient in such a case. Therefore there is no proof from Magen Avraham siman 559 that implies she should not fast — namely from what he wrote the woman after birth drinks the cup; one may say there it deals with birth on 3 Av when now seven days did not complete before Tisha B'Av and in such a case one may be lenient; with this it fits what Yoreh De'ah 265 wrote on Tisha B'Av when the woman after birth cannot drink from the cup she does not bless — see there; there it deals with birth at start of night 2, and in Be'er haGolah he distinguishes there it deals when she need not eat and here when she needs; however even per Rashal one may say in siman 559 she is somewhat ill; and it appears in any case in postponement one may be lenient if she gave birth 3 Av when then seven days completed on 10 Av me'al le'mal, and to be lenient per the Rosh's answer that we require me'al le'mal; and see in my books Beit Efrayim Yoreh De'ah on the matter "there is no healing less than three days" what he wrote on this.
