Magen Avraham, s.k. 9. That she should fast after seven; and in the Shulchan Aruch there it is implied that she should not fast. See siman 617 that we require a full twenty-four hours (mei'et le'et); and see in Terumat HaDeshen, siman 148, who wrote there that the Asheri in chapter Merubah wrote that we require a full twenty-four hours, only that Tosafos in Gittin did not wish to answer so; and as to what we do not say that a doubt of life is to be lenient: nevertheless, since the Asheri wrote so only to reconcile the words of Kenesses HaGedolah, and on this Rif and Ri disagree, etc.; and even though we hold that the day of a child's birth requires a full twenty-four hours, one may distinguish between postpartum illness and ordinary illness; end quote, briefly. And it appears to my humble opinion that perhaps he was stringent in this only on Yom Kippur, which is according to the Torah and a doubt of karet; but Tisha B'Av is rabbinic, and certainly one may rely on the Rosh who holds that we require a full twenty-four hours; and so it is implied from Maharshal's words, who took [the case of] one who gave birth on the second of Av, implying that if she gave birth on the third of Av she is exempt from fasting, even though Sunday when it was postponed is after seven; and it is strained to say that the incident happened so, and also not to say the reason she is exempt in such a case is because on Shabbos which is Tisha B'Av it is after seven, and if it had been a weekday she would have been exempt, and if so even on Sunday when it was postponed she is exempt, because once the fast of the whole world was already fixed on Sunday and then she is after seven we have no reason to exempt her on that ground; rather it appears that he holds we require a full twenty-four hours; and if so, if she gave birth on the third of Av, then before the tenth of Av seven full twenty-four-hour days were not completed, and when the tenth of Av entered she was within seven and is exempt; and perhaps even when it was not postponed one should be lenient in such a case. And accordingly there is no proof from what Magen Avraham wrote at the end of siman 559, which implies she should not fast, namely from his writing that the woman after childbirth should drink the cup; and according to what I wrote, one may say that there it deals with one who gave birth on the third of Av, when now the seven days were not completed before Tisha B'Av, and in such a case one should be lenient; and with this it fits well what is written in Yoreh De'ah at the beginning of siman 265, that on Tisha B'Av, when the woman after childbirth cannot drink from the cup, he should not recite the blessing, see there; for there it deals with a case such as where [the child] was born at the start of night Monday night. And Be'er HaGolah distinguishes that there it deals where she does not need to eat, and here it deals where she needs; however, even according to Maharshal's view one may say in siman 559 that she is somewhat sick. And it appears to me that in any event when postponed one should be lenient if she gave birth on the third of Av, for then seven days are completed on the tenth of Av as full twenty-four-hour periods, and to be lenient per the Rosh's answer that we require a full twenty-four hours; and see what I wrote in my work Beis Ephraim, Yoreh De'ah section, on the matter that there is no sircha less than three days, what I wrote on this.
