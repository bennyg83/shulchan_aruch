A woman after childbirth all thirty days. Maharshal wrote in a responsum that a woman who gave birth on Shabbos, the second of Av, must fast on the postponed Tisha B'Av, for only within seven days is she exempt, but after seven, even if she says she needs to eat, she must fast unless she is somewhat ill. The Darkei Moshe notes that the Beit Yosef in siman 559 implies that after seven she should fast, while the Shulchan Aruch there implies she should not. The Levush in siman 602 wrote that she should fast, and so did Hagahos Maimoniyos. The Bach wrote that by law she may eat, but the custom has already become to fast as long as they are not weak and have recovered from childbirth, and even on the other three fasts healthy women have been stringent. Where this is the custom, it remains the custom; but on a postponed Tisha B'Av one may be lenient. Some women after childbirth have the custom to refrain from meat and wine from the seventh of Av and onward, which is proper, for on that day the non-Jews entered the Sanctuary, as stated in the Tur.
