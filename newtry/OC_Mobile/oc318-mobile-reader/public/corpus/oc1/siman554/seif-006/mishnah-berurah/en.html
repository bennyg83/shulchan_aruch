A woman after childbirth all thirty days. Presumably she is considered weak and like a sick person without danger. Therefore, although regarding Yom Kippur after seven days she is like anyone else, as written in siman 617, here regarding Tisha B'Av, which is D'Rabbanan, they did not decree in a case of illness. See the Taz and the other later authorities, who write that even where there is certainly no danger, and likewise the sick person mentioned next, even if they did not say, we need to eat, they may eat on Tisha B'Av. Although Maharshal disagrees with the Shulchan Aruch's ruling and holds that regarding this Tisha B'Av is like Yom Kippur, many later authorities agree that by law she may eat. However, the custom has already become to fast, as the Rama wrote, so long as they have no weakness and have recovered from childbirth. But if she has not fully recovered, or is somewhat ill, or is naturally weak, she should not fast within thirty days. If a healthy woman after childbirth fasts and feels unusual weakness in the middle of the day, one should rule that she not fast for the rest of the day; later authorities.
Sick person. One who was mentally impaired and recovered from day to day may eat meat and drink wine the entire week and does not fast on Tisha B'Av; Maharil.
Who needs to eat, etc. Meaning that he is weak and feels bodily illness, even though there is no danger, and we feed him.
They do not need assessment. This is the correct text, and it applies to both cases. So it appears in Toras HaAdam and the Ran. The meaning is that we do not need to assess and estimate whether she will come to danger if she fasts, for even where there is no danger, since she is included in the category of a sick person, she need not fast. See Likkutei Peri Chadash, who wrote that this means we feed her by default. But if the woman after childbirth knows of herself that she can fast, meaning she sees herself as healthy, then she is obligated to fast.
Nevertheless, the custom is to fast, etc. This is even if she says, I need to eat, and specifically after seven days. But within seven days, even if she says she does not need to eat, she should not fast, and certainly within three days, where she is definitely forbidden to fast.
So long as they have no great pain, etc. The same applies if she is somewhat ill, as above in seif katan 9, even if she did not say, I need to eat. See the Magen Avraham, who wrote that even in a place where the custom is to be stringent on a postponed Tisha B'Av, one may be lenient and she should not fast. See Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote that even a woman after childbirth who does not fast should fast some hours, but if even this is difficult for her she should not fast at all.
Where there was concern of danger. In such a case it is forbidden for her to fast.
One who is lenient has lost nothing. All the more so with a weak person who is sick without danger, it is not proper to be stringent; Chayei Adam.
