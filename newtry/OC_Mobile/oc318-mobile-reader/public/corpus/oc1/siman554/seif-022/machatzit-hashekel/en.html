(Seif katan 22) To his fellow — he explained Torah scholar is missing, and so it should be in the Tur: there is no inquiry of peace to fellows on Tisha B'Av, and laymen who do not know, etc.; and the Bach explained (from that it teaches "fellows" and not "fellow") that it means Torah scholars who are called fellows. (And per this it is implied that laymen are permitted, as with melacha in seif 22.) And we do not know from where, etc. — meaning that "fellows" that the Tur wrote means Torah scholar; nevertheless he did not come to exclude that laymen are permitted. Rather he comes to teach that if one inquired of his wellbeing that he must respond in soft language. And regarding a Torah scholar you do not find this, for both of them know there is a prohibition in inquiring of peace; therefore he wrote there is no inquiry of peace to fellows, and they both know; but laymen who do not know — if it happened that a layman inquired of the peace of a Torah scholar, he responds to him in soft language.
