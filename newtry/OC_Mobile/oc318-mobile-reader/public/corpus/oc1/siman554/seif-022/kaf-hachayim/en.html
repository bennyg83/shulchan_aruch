And it is permitted to review the parasha, etc. It appears that this is what he said — one who is accustomed to review the parasha (Shemot LeVush) on that day — that is, when Tisha B'Av falls on Thursday it is like the order of the day. Taz seif katan 3. And such is the view of Eliyah Rabbah seif 7. And likewise Mahara Azulai wrote in his glosses on Kol Yehudah from there Magen Avraham; only that he disagreed with him and wrote that it is not fitting to be lenient in this. And Beit Din Birkei Yosef seif 3. And likewise Chida seif 3 on the words of the Taz mentioned that there is great strain to explain thus. And likewise Nahar Shalom on the words of the Taz mentioned that it is very difficult to be lenient in this. And Mishna Berurah seif katan 8 wrote to explain the words of Nahar Shalom mentioned — that is, the chazan who reads is permitted to review the parasha before he reads; or it deals with Tisha B'Av that falls on Shabbos, for then even an individual is permitted to review the parasha, as I wrote above siman 485, end of his words. However Eliyah Rabbah there challenged the words of Magen Avraham mentioned, for behold "when you bear children" is of the type of the event. And Nahar Shalom there wrote: it is possible that Magen Avraham on the parasha "and he began" speaks — they practiced to read it at minchah as on the other public fast days; but the parasha "when you bear" and the like — for everyone it is permitted, see there. And Machatzit HaShekel wrote to reconcile that also in "when you bear" it could be that there is joy in what one learns the cantillation and vowels by heart — it teaches us that it is permitted, for there is not so much joy for the chazan in this, see there. And such is the view of the later authorities per the words of Magen Avraham mentioned.
