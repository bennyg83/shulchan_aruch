Not to do. This is so they not divert their attention from mourning; Rav Ovadiah of Bartenura on Pesachim chapter 4. Terumas HaDeshen discussed finding a reason, as below. It appears to me that for this reason work is also forbidden at night, since at night too one is obligated to mourn. Rashi in Pesachim 54b writes that twilight of Tisha B'Av is forbidden in eating and work. This requires study, for in Taanis 13 Rashi implies that work is permitted on the night of Tisha B'Av and forbidden only by day, and the matter is not publicized. From the silence of all the poskim it appears that it is forbidden at night too. It is not comparable to other public fasts, where the reason is that people be free and gather to pray, and therefore they are permitted at night, when they do not gather. Tisha B'Av depends on custom, and the reason is so that one not divert his attention from mourning or because of affliction, so it is forbidden even at night. Therefore any work that does not take time is permitted, since it does not divert his attention.

If possible through a non-Jew. Although it is a matter of loss, as written in siman 533, that case is different, for the prohibition of work there is because of rest, and the Sages permitted any amateur work that is not burdensome. But Tisha B'Av is because of affliction, as Rashi wrote, and therefore even work that is not burdensome and is amateur is forbidden. Yet some poskim imply that here too the reason is the prohibition of work, and therefore if it cannot be done through a non-Jew, one may do it himself as on Chol HaMoed; Terumas HaDeshen.
