And it is permitted to read the entire order of the day. Meaning Shema and its blessings, and everything connected to the blessings of prayer.
And the parsha of the korbanos and the Mishnah, etc. All this is no worse than what is read from the Torah and the Haftarah from the Navi, for all of it is the order of the day. The custom is to say Tehillim and Shir HaYichud at Minchah. Although Torah study is forbidden all day, one must say that they hold that since it is said as supplication, it is permitted. In Pozna the custom is to say on the following day that of the previous day and that of the current day, and this is proper. Ma'amados certainly should not be said. See below, siman 559, seif 4, that Pitum HaKetores should not be said because it is not part of the order of the day, and certainly one should not say the order of korbanos printed at the end of siman 1. What the Shulchan Aruch writes, the parsha of the korbanos, means the parsha of the Tamid.
And it is permitted to review the parsha on Tisha B'Av. This means that the reader may review the parsha before he reads it, even at Minchah. In the morning, Ki Solid Banim is obviously relevant to the day.
