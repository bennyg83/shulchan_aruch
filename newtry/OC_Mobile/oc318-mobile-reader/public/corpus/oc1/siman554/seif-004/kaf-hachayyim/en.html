(19) [Seif 4] One may recite the entire daily prayer order, etc. It is obvious that studying the order of the Ma'amados is forbidden even if one studies it every day, and not like those who mistakenly recite the Ma'amados also on Tisha B'Av. Birke Yosef os 2, Sha'arei Teshuvah os 4. The same applies to one who recites eighteen chapters of Mishnah every day, or the sacrificial passages of Parshas Tzav and other regular studies that a person has fixed for himself and recites daily, they are forbidden. Shulchan Gavoah os 7. Similarly, reciting the verses of Chok L'Yisrael is certainly forbidden. Zechor L'Avraham os 9 on this seif, Milei D'Chasidusa siman 10 os 90. Regarding Parshas Kadesh and Parshas Ki Yevi'acha, some have the practice of reciting them on Tisha B'Av and some do not, and each person should follow his custom in this. Ben Ish Chai Parshas Devarim os 25. See above siman 25 os 30.
(20) In Pozna [Posen] the practice is not to say Tehillim or Shir HaYichud, and only the next day they say [both] the previous night's and that day's [portions]. Hagahos HaLevush. In this country the practice is to say them at Minchah. Even though Torah study is forbidden the entire day, it must be said that they hold that since one recites them as a form of supplication it is permitted to say them at Minchah. Magen Avraham s.k. 6. Mishna Berurah os 7. However, he wrote that the practice of Pozna is the correct one, and if they missed one day's Tehillim they should say it the next day as is done all year round. Eishel Avraham os 6.
(21) Ibid. One may recite the entire daily prayer order, etc. This will be further explained below in siman 559 seif 4, see there.
(22) In the Rama's gloss: One may review the Torah portion [of the week], etc. It seems that this means: one who is accustomed to reviewing the Torah portion (twice in the text and once in the Targum) on that day, namely, when Tisha B'Av falls on Thursday, this constitutes part of the daily order. Taz s.k. 3. This is also the view of Eliyah Rabbah os 6. So too wrote Mahar"a Azulai of blessed memory in his manuscript glosses in the name of Mateh Moshe, except that he himself disagreed and wrote that one should not be lenient in this matter. The Birke Yosef os 3 cited his words. So too the Hagahos Beis [Yosef] os 3 wrote regarding the words of the Taz that it is a great strain to interpret it thus. So too the Nishmas HaShulchan wrote regarding the words of the Taz that it is very difficult to be lenient in this. The Mishna Berurah s.k. 8 wrote to explain the Rama's words as meaning: the chazzan who reads [the Torah publicly] is permitted to review the portion before he reads it; or it refers to a Tisha B'Av that falls on Shabbos, in which case even an individual is permitted to review the portion, as stated above at the end of siman 285. However, the Eliyah Rabbah there raised a difficulty against the words of the Magen Avraham, for [the Torah reading of] "Ki Solid Banim" is relevant to the theme of the day. The Nishmas HaShulchan there wrote that it is possible the Magen Avraham is referring to Parshas Va'ychal, which is customarily read at Minchah as on other public fasts, but Parshas Ki Solid and the like are permitted according to all, see there. The Machatzis HaShekel wrote to reconcile that even regarding Ki Solid, one might think there is joy in learning the cantillation marks and vowels by heart, therefore it teaches us that it is permitted, for the chazzan does not derive so much joy from this, see there. This is the view of the later authorities in accordance with the Magen Avraham.
