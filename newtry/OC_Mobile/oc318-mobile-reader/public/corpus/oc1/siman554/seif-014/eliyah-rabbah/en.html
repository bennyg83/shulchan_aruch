On the road, etc. It appears to me that the reason is that it is a great burden to walk a long road barefoot. But when one goes to a field near the city, it is forbidden to put on shoes, and the same applies to a cemetery, unless there is mud and mire or he is among non-Jews. The Magen Avraham writes that when he sits in a wagon or rides, he must remove them even on a long road, but this requires study. Furthermore, an ordinary field is generally at a non-Jewish house. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes: when he leaves the city he puts them on, and when he enters he removes them. It also appears from Rashi in Taanis 13 that one who leaves the city for the road implies that whenever he has left the city, even if it is not a long road, it is permitted. The reason is that Chazal were stringent about wearing shoes only in the city, just as they did not forbid work at night and the other matters mentioned there in the Gemara. So too it is explicit in Rokeach, siman 310.
