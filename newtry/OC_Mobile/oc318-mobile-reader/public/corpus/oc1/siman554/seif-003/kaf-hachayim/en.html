(ג) Regarding drinking tobacco (smoke-raising) on Tisha B'Av — Keneset haGedolah wrote siman 551 in Hagahot Beit Yosef letter 21: an incident came to him on the 1st that someone drank tobacco on Tisha B'Av and fainted. And so siman 562 Hagahot Beit Yosef letter 3 expanded; and wrote on Tisha B'Av according to all forbidden — see there. And so responsa Darchei Noam siman 9; Beit Din siman 561; Zera Emet siman 73 to forbid on Tisha B'Av — see there. And Chayei Adam general rule 135 letter 27. Birkei Yosef siman 562 that in our time the leniency spread (meaning on other fasts) except Tisha B'Av where some are stringent because of mourning — see there. However Shaarei Teshuvah siman 559 letter 4 wrote one may be lenient after chatzot and privately in one's house — see there. And so above siman 571 letter 35 that some are lenient after chatzot — see there. However Mechaber above siman 210 letter 13 wrote a great one dreamed with an outstanding rabbi and they adjured him to say what they say in heaven about drinking tobacco; they answered one who drinks on Yom Tov they put to death, and one who drinks on Tisha B'Av they curse; and so one who drinks under the wedding canopy — see there. And so in his book Maaseh Buker letter 212 — see there; I brought his words above siman 210 letter 35 — see there. And Rokeach letter 5 that one should not permit except after arvit prayer, i.e., after 12 hours — see there. And so in his book Malach siman 10 letter 80 — see there. And see further in Sefer Shaarei Shamayim by Rabbi Meir Katzenellenbogen daf 71b: drinking tobacco is forbidden in Keneset haGedolah and one is ostracized for this, and the law agrees with him, for it is no less than enjoyment and strolling and removing the mind from mourning. And so Maharal z"l used to protest and punish one who transgresses intentionally; nevertheless for a slight need when not intending enjoyment but to help do needs — as known from its nature and good effect in this — it is obvious to me it is permitted; however where others do not see, lest they learn to be lenient, and especially at night which is somewhat lenient otherwise regarding melacha in my opinion. On this I relied and did an act myself once at night when I needed etc.; therefore by day I fear to do an act to be lenient even for the aforementioned need, although one may be lenient for others in this manner and it should not be stricter than anointing and bathing which are permitted when not intending enjoyment therein, end of his words. It appears if needed by day for doing his needs as mentioned, one may be lenient to drink it specifically in the bathroom privately because of onlookers. And see further below siman 562 letter 18.
