There. 'That sick person does not require assessment lest there be danger in him; for even...' This requires further study. And it appears that his intent is: at first he explained that necessarily it deals where there is doubt whether there is danger, that is to say, that we enter the category of doubt in this; for if no doubt at all falls for us in the matter, assessment does not apply; rather, certainly we are in doubt; and for this he said that assessment is not required, to ask the physicians about this; rather they feed him according to our doubt. And therefore it was difficult for him against Maharshal, who explains it as where she says 'I need'; for in that case it is obvious that even on Yom Kippur they feed him without physicians' assessment, as clarified there, that even if a hundred say he does not need, we heed the sick person. Therefore he wrote that it deals where she does not say 'I need'; and in this it fits well to say that regarding a woman after childbirth or a sick person about whom we are in doubt whether there is any concern of danger: in such a case on Yom Kippur, if the sick person says 'I do not need,' then assessment from experts is required; but on Tisha B'Av assessment is not required for a physician to say there is concern of danger if he fasts; for even if the physician says that certainly there is no danger, and the sick person also does not say 'I need' (in which case on Yom Kippur they would not feed him), nevertheless on Tisha B'Av they properly feed him; for since we enter doubt regarding him such that one needs to ask the experts, the Rabbis did not decree upon him. And this is the intent of the Taz: 'assessment is not required to say there is danger in him,' that is to say, it is not required that the physician say there is concern of danger if he fasts; for even if he says that certainly there is no danger, and the sick person also does not say 'I need' (in which case on Yom Kippur they do not feed him), nevertheless on Tisha B'Av they properly feed him; for since we enter doubt regarding him such that one needs the experts' inquiry, the Rabbis did not decree upon him. As a matter of law this requires further study whether one should be lenient in such a case; and it appears that in any event it is required that ordinary people who are somewhat expert be in doubt with concern of danger.
