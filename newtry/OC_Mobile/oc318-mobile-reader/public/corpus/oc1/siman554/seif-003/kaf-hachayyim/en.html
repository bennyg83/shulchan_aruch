(16) [Seif 3] There is one who forbids, etc. The Beit Yosef wrote this in the name of the Agur who wrote in the name of Maharil Moelin, and it is in the aforementioned responsa of the Maharil. See Shem HaGedolim by the Chida, section on Great Ones, letter yud os 238, who wrote that the Maharil is Mahari Segal and he is Mahari Moelin, see there. The reason he wrote this in the Shulchan Aruch in the name of "there is one who forbids" even though the Beit Yosef did not cite a dispute on this, is because generally we hold that mental contemplation is not equivalent to speech, except under duress, as stated above in siman 47 and siman 62, see there. Additionally, one can say that this is the manner of the Beit Yosef, sometimes when he finds a position held by only a single authority, he writes it in the name of "there is one who says," as written by Yad Malachi in the rules of the Shulchan Aruch os 12, see there.
(17) Ibid. There is one who forbids studying through mental contemplation. Even though mental contemplation is not equivalent to speech, in matters of mourning it is different, because the primary reason [for the prohibition] is because of joy, and mental contemplation involves joy. Responsa of the Maharil ibid. It seems to me that for this reason it is forbidden to study any discourse, or a question and answer, even on the sorrowful passages, because it brings him joy. It is also forbidden to render halachic rulings unless it is for a sick person who needs it now, as stated in Yoreh De'ah siman 384 seif 1, for a ruling is considered like mental contemplation, as stated above at the end of siman 85. Magen Avraham s.k. 5. Eliyah Rabbah os 4 and os 5, Yad Aharon in the Hagahos Beis Yosef. Eishel Avraham os 5, Chayei Adam rule 135 os 1, Mishna Berurah os 5. See above siman 553 os 17.
(18) It is forbidden to adjudicate monetary cases, for legal rulings constitute Torah study. However, if the litigants cannot wait until the next day because they need to travel, it is perhaps permitted since it is a case of public need. Magen Avraham ibid. Eliyah Rabbah ibid. Yad Aharon ibid. Chayei Adam ibid. Mishna Berurah ibid. However, in Siddur Amudei Shamayim by R' Yaakov Emden, he wrote that it is forbidden to sit in judgment, and even if the litigants are pressed for time, for who can compel the judge in this matter? Let the litigants wait or go on their way, see there. The Tosfos Chayyim on Chayei Adam ibid. os 3 cited his words. Pischei Teshuvah os 5.
