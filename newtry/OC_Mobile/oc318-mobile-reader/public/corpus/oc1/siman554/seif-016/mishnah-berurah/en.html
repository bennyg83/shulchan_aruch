But of cloth or wood, etc. Meaning, although it protects his foot and is made in the actual form of a shoe, it is permitted, for only leather is called a shoe.
And wood covered with leather. Meaning covered on top, and even though the soles are also wood, it is nevertheless forbidden. The same applies if it is cloth and covered with leather on top, or if its soles are leather; it is forbidden. See Shaarei Teshuvah and the later authorities. Although wearing shoes is forbidden on Tisha B'Av and Yom Kippur, many later authorities agree that one may recite the blessing, Who has provided me all my needs.
