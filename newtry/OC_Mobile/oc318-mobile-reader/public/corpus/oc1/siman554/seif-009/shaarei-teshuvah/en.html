In Shulchan Aruch, seif 9, and if. If he relieved himself, etc. See Responsa VeShav HaKohen, siman 3, who discusses at length and concludes that on Tisha B'Av and Yom Kippur, if one wants to pray and relieved himself, even if he did not wipe, or if he urinated and rubbed, in which case he must wash by law, he washes until the end of the finger joints, even if during the rest of the year he usually washes the whole hand. But if he did not rub, so that washing is not required by law but only because of preparation for prayer, he should wash only a little at the tips of his fingers. Even if he wants to be stringent and not wash with water at all, he has whom to rely on, since it is not mentioned in the Shulchan Aruch or Rama. If he does not want to pray but only to recite Asher Yatzar or say a matter of sanctity, he washes only the soiled place if he relieved himself and wiped or urinated and rubbed. But if he did not wipe and did not rub, one should be stringent not to wash his hands with water at all, and should clean his hands with a pebble or anything that cleans. He also wrote there that the washing of a kohen for duchaning on Yom Kippur must be until the joint where the hand meets the arm, since it is required by law; this will be explained in its place, with Hashem's help.
