Seif katan 9, a woman after childbirth, etc. Maharshal wrote in a responsum, etc., and disagrees with the Shulchan Aruch.
However, in Beit Yosef, siman 559, it implies that she must fast. He writes that regarding the halacha, we rule to recite the milah blessing without a cup; but on the seventeenth of Tammuz, the third of Tishrei, and the tenth of Teves, where the woman after childbirth may drink, they bless over a cup and she drinks from it. Thus only on the three fasts do they bless over a cup because the woman after childbirth can drink, but on Tisha B'Av they do not bless over a cup. It must therefore be that he holds that on Tisha B'Av the woman after childbirth may not drink.
And see there in the Shulchan Aruch, etc. Meaning, from the very place where the Darkei Moshe proved from the Beit Yosef's longer work that she should fast, the Beit Yosef's words in the Shulchan Aruch prove that she should not fast. In siman 559, seif 7, he wrote that if the woman after childbirth is the one present at the milah and heard the blessing, she drinks, even if the milah is on the eighth day, and the same applies after seven days, since those seven days need not be complete twenty-four-hour periods. Even if one says that in the Shulchan Aruch he retracted from his longer work, his words in the Shulchan Aruch also contradict one another, for in Yoreh Deah, siman 265, seif 4, he wrote that on the three fasts, when a woman after childbirth does not fast, one may recite over a cup, but on Yom Kippur and Tisha B'Av, where she cannot drink, one does not bless over the cup. Thus he wrote that on Tisha B'Av she fasts.
Therefore the Bach answered that what the Beit Yosef wrote in Yoreh Deah and in his longer work here was according to the custom, as the Rama wrote that the custom is to fast. See Bach, siman 559. The Taz in Yoreh Deah wrote similarly. But as a matter of law, the Beit Yosef holds that she need not fast, as he wrote here.
One may be lenient. See the end of siman 559, where they are lenient regarding a postponed Tisha B'Av for those involved in a bris, permitting them to eat.
On that day, etc. As the Tur writes in siman 558, and this is from the Gemara at the end of Taanis.
