There is no greeting. In Yoreh Deah, siman 385, some say that what we call giving shalom is not called the greeting of their times. This requires study, for the Tur there cites the Yerushalmi explicitly saying shalom aleichem, and the Gemara says that a student's greeting to his rebbe is shalom alecha, my teacher. Therefore one should not be lenient. It appears to me that it is also forbidden to send a gift to one's fellow, for this is included in greeting, as written there in Yoreh Deah.
To his fellow. In the Tur the text is to his fellows. The Bach explains this means Torah scholars do not greet one another; but the Mordechai writes to his fellow, implying that anyone who is his fellow, even if not a Torah scholar, is forbidden. I do not know what he means, for it is certainly forbidden to greet anyone, only that ignorant people who do not know and give greeting are answered softly.
