Levush: even on Shabbos, etc. The Shelah wrote that there is a dispute whether immersion at its proper time is a mitzvah, and the Beit Yosef, siman 196, wrote that when her husband is in the city it is a mitzvah to immerse at the proper time, for Yehoshua was punished for neglecting one night of procreation. Therefore he permitted a woman to immerse and have relations on Shabbos night when Tisha B'Av falls then, although the Rama was stringent, for a mitzvah is different. In my Eliyah Zuta I challenged this, for the dispute whether immersion at its time is a mitzvah applies even without relations; but for us, who rule that it is a mitzvah only when her husband is in the city because of neglecting procreation, what is gained on Tisha B'Av when relations are forbidden? One may answer that it speaks specifically of the immersion night, where there is neglect of procreation, for a woman conceives only near immersion. Yet this is difficult, since on a weekday Tisha B'Av it is forbidden even on the immersion night, and since we rule that private mourning applies as by a mourner, and by a mourner it is certainly forbidden on an immersion night that falls on Shabbos. The old customs cited by the Shelah mean, in my humble opinion, that she immerses, but relations are forbidden. Proof comes from the responsum of Mahari Bruna cited by Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah, that one should not immerse on Friday night lest he have relations with her, since private matters of mourning apply. Its meaning is that one might have thought she should immerse in order to have relations after Tisha B'Av and not distance combing from immersion, as written in siman 551, seif 16. It teaches that this is forbidden lest he have relations. The old customs did not make that decree, but certainly there is no difference between an immersion night and other nights of Tisha B'Av regarding relations. Mahari Bruna forbids explicitly, and had the Shelah seen him he would not have been lenient against him. I also heard that my grandfather, the Gaon Maharash, and my father after him, ruled to forbid, and this is the custom. See siman 574, seif katan 7, the Magen Avraham on the Shelah, and Sefer Chassidim, which may hint that nowadays she may not immerse.
