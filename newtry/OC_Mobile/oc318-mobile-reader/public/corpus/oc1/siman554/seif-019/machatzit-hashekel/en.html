Seif katan 19, with, etc. The Magen Avraham writes that by day one may be lenient. Although in siman 613 regarding Yom Kippur he is stringent and forbids even by day, he is lenient on Tisha B'Av because it is D'Rabbanan. There is also some reasoning: the Taz writes there that on Yom Kippur the concern is greater at night because it may lead to intimacy, but on Tisha B'Av even at night it would be permitted, for specifically on Yom Kippur, when women are adorned for the honor of the day, there is concern for arousal, unlike Tisha B'Av, when people wear worn garments. Therefore the Magen Avraham holds that on Yom Kippur, because of adornment, there is concern even by day; but on Tisha B'Av, where there is no adornment, we are not concerned by day, although at night there is more concern that it may lead to something. If, however, they are adorned and not in worn garments, there is concern even on Tisha B'Av, and we are stringent.
