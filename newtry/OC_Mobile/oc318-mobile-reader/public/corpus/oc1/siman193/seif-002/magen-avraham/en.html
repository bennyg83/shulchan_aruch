Fixed together. Meaning at one table as written siman 167 seif 11; for if not, one does not discharge his fellow whether in hamotzi or birkas hamazon, as written siman 167 seif 11. And so wrote Rosh and Tosafot chapter "Who ate" and Rif chapter "Three who ate" and Terumat Yisrael and on "parach zimun," etc. And so in Remazim and in Ravyah 175:4, and so in Terumat HaDeshen that there is a mitzvah to divide. And it is implied in the Gemara that whoever one does not discharge his fellow, they are not permitted to invite zimun and afterward each blesses for himself — for Rav's students tore their garments, and it is difficult: they should have invited zimun together and afterward each blessed for himself as they did in berachat hamotzi; if so, since they are not fixed together they are not permitted to invite zimun. And so Rama siman 195, see there, and so Bach there. And one must say that what he wrote here in the gloss "it is preferable to invite zimun" means that if they eat a little at one table so they become obligated in zimun; and so too Bach's words, for in siman 195 he wrote explicitly that they cannot invite zimun unless they entered from the outset for this purpose — "three who ate" we taught, etc., and that response Rabbeinu Yonah brought on this siman — possibly he holds that since poskim are divided whether berachat hazimun requires reclining or not, therefore the permission is in their hands to invite zimun; but we hold it requires reclining, as stated above in the name of all poskim in Shulchan Aruch here — if so they are not permitted to invite zimun. And it appears to me for this reason Rabbeinu Yonah omitted this response.
