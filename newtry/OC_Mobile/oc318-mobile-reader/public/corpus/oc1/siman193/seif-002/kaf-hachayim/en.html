However Rashi wrote that one fulfills even though he does not understand the language, and brought proof from what we learned in Megillah: a foreigner who heard in Hebrew fulfills. But Rosh in chapter 3 "who ate" wrote on this that one should reject it — publicizing the miracle is different, as written in the Gemara there — see there. And Beit Yosef brought it and wrote: and so Tosafot and R' Yosef and Mordechai. And Rama wrote in Darkei Moshe letter Alef: therefore it is good that women bless for themselves; however the custom in this time is like Rashi's words, and I never saw anyone who protests this matter — until here. And so Bach: the custom is like Rashi, that in all matters women are obligated — whether in prayer, Megillah, or birkas hamazon — they rely on their mother who hears from others who intend to discharge the women as well, even though the women do not understand at all — until here. And they brought Shulchan Aruch HaGavohar in Hagahot Beit Yosef letter Bet; Eshel Avraham in this siman letter Bet; Magen Avraham s.k. 2; Rabbi Akiva Eiger letter Bet. And Magen Avraham wrote there: nevertheless it is good that they say word for word with the one making Kiddush and the one blessing, for it is impossible to intend and hear, as written in siman 183 seif 7 — see there. And it appears thus: if it is possible for them to say with the one blessing, and the one blessing waits for them until they say with him — but if not possible, one may rely on Rashi even l'chatchila, for this is more preferable than that they not bless at all, as Taz wrote s.k. 2 — see there. However if they know how to bless or pray — plainly they should bless for themselves if they do not understand, and not rely on others to fulfill per all views. And even if they understand lashon kodesh, it is better they bless for themselves, as written above in siman 183 seif 7 and in our words there letter Lamed Zayin — see there.
