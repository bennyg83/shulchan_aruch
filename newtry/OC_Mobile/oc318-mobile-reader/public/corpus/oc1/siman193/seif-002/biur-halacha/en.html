It is preferable to recite zimun. See Mishnah Berurah. Biur HaGra also implies that according to the Mechaber’s earlier ruling, that even a third person who arrives near the end of the meal and joins them becomes part of the zimun, there is no source for this ruling. It cannot be derived from the incident of Rav and Shmuel on 47a, for there they were legally obligated because they were together at the end of the meal. Nor is there proof from 53b, where Beis Hillel holds that one person recites the blessing for everyone, as the Rosh explains there. This position is tenable only according to Rabbeinu Yonah, who holds that they are obligated in zimun only when they began together. He must therefore resolve the incident of Rav and Shmuel and the incident of King Yannai on 48a by saying that zimun is optional in every case. Magen Giborim writes likewise.
