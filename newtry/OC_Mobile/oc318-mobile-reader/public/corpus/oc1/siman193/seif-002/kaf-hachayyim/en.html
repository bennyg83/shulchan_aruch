2) There: If he understands Lashon HaKodesh, etc. The Tur writes that women therefore do not fulfill their obligation by listening if they do not understand. Rashi, of blessed memory, however, writes that one fulfills his obligation even if he does not understand the language, and brings proof from the Mishnah in Megillah that one who speaks a foreign language and hears the Megillah in Ashuris fulfills his obligation. Rosh, of blessed memory, in the third chapter of Berachos rejects this proof because publicizing the miracle is different, as the Gemara states there; see there. Beis Yosef cites this and writes that Tosafos, Rabbeinu Yonah, and Mordechai rule likewise. Rema, of blessed memory, writes in Darkei Moshe, paragraph 1, that women should therefore preferably recite the blessing themselves. Nevertheless, the present custom follows Rashi, and he never saw anyone protest; see there. Bach likewise writes that the custom follows Rashi: regarding every obligation incumbent upon women, whether prayer, Megillah, or Birkas HaMazon, they rely upon listening to others who intend to discharge the women as well, although the women understand nothing. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 2; Olas Tamid, paragraph 2; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2. Magen Avraham writes there that it is nevertheless proper for them to repeat every word with the person reciting Kiddush or the blessing, because it is impossible to concentrate and listen properly, as stated in siman 183, seif 7; see there. It appears that this applies specifically when they can repeat after the person reciting the blessing and he waits for them to say it with him. If this is impossible, one may rely upon Rashi even initially, because this is preferable to their not reciting a blessing at all, as Taz, paragraph 2, writes; see there. If they know how to recite the blessing or prayer, it is obvious that if they do not understand they should recite it themselves and not rely upon others, thereby satisfying all opinions. Even if they understand Lashon HaKodesh, it is preferable that they recite the blessing themselves, as stated above in siman 183, seif 7, and in our words there, paragraph 37; see there.
