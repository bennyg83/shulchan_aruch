It is preferable, etc. Magen Avraham explains that they should eat a small amount together at one table so that they become obligated in zimun. Otherwise they may not make a zimun, since each person must recite Birkat HaMazon for himself. He proves this from the students of Rav who tore their garments out of uncertainty: if separate Birkat HaMazon after a shared zimun were permitted, they could simply have made zimun together and then each blessed separately. In my humble opinion this is no proof. Magen Avraham himself writes above that when making zimun one must hear Birkat HaMazon from the leader, so their uncertainty concerned Birkat HaMazon itself. One could still ask why the leader could not bless quietly while the others listened, as written in siman 183, but Magen Avraham does not accept that solution. Note this well. His inference from siman 195 is also not a proof. There the rule may concern obligation, whereas as an enhancement it is preferable to join, as the Taz writes.
