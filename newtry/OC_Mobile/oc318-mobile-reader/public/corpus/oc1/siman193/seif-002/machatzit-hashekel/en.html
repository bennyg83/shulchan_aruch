And Rama siman 62 — it implies that Kiddush is lenient regarding understanding; and Bach wrote even Kiddush.

Some say that even on the seven species, etc. — this is Ra'avyah's view in the name of R' Hai Gaon; Mordechai brought it in chapter "Three who ate," but he did not write there reason and proof. And one could bring a little proof from what is taught in Berachot daf 37: whoever is of the seven species — R' Gamliel says three berachot and the Sages say one berachah resembling three. And the incident of R' Gamliel and the elders, etc., and they brought before them scriptures and they ate. And R' Gamliel gave permission to R' Akiva to bless; he jumped and blessed — R' Akiva one berachah resembling three, etc. And Tosafot wrote: and you should ask on R' Gamliel — behold we hold one does not invite zimun on fruits! And one can answer that R' Gamliel held he should bless on each of the seven species three complete berachot; and when R' Akiva blessed resembling three only for himself — for we hold one does not invite zimun on fruits. And the others reclining blessed each for himself — end Tosafot. And Ra'avyah in the name of R' Hai — possibly he holds: since this was not mentioned in the Talmud regarding this too, they did not act per R' Gamliel's words, that he commanded to invite zimun and they blessed each for himself. Or that in truth on this they agree with R' Gamliel that on the seven species — even though he holds one berachah resembling three suffices — nevertheless one invites zimun on them; so one can answer. And in any case one can learn from this from what we say in the baraita there on rice: if they ground, baked, and cooked it — even though the slices remain (meaning there is a kezayit in each slice, as above siman 168) — first he blesses borei minei mezonot and finally one berachah resembling three; and it challenges: but it was taught thus at the end and it is nothing (and Rashi: rather borei nefashot); and it answers: this baraita that teaches after it a berachah resembling three comes per R' Gamliel, for we hear from him that he holds thus regarding rice; and it challenges: can you establish it per R' Gamliel? If so the first part of this baraita on wheat is difficult: if they ground, baked, and cooked it — if the slices do not remain, he blesses first borei minei mezonot and finally one berachah resembling three. And if R' Gamliel now on porridge and pap holds that he blesses three berachot — all the more so when slices do not remain one can say three berachot — see there; behold when slices do not remain it is preferable to the seven species; if so per Ra'avyah's view, just as we invite zimun even per our view on the seven species — all the more so when slices do not remain, which is preferable. And likewise pat haba'ah bekisnin. But this is not so — for regarding pat haba'ah bekisnin: if he ate a quantity upon which others fix a meal, behold he blesses hamotzi and birkas hamazon; and the law of bread is plain that one invites zimun even for those who disagree with Ra'avyah. And if he did not eat so much — even if he fixed a meal upon it, his intent is nullified and he blesses borei minei mezonot and resembling three, as above siman 168; therefore one must explain there is no fixation — and if so how can they invite zimun, since there is no fixation?

And the same applies when slices do not remain — for even if he ate a quantity upon which others fix a meal, he blesses borei minei mezonot and resembling three; therefore it is not called fixation and one cannot invite zimun; so one can answer per my view, as a possible way.
