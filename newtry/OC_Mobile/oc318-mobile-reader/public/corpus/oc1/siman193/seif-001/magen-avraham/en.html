Therefore it is good that women bless for themselves; however the custom in this time is per Rashi that they fulfill even though they do not understand, and I never saw anyone who protests this matter [Darkei Moshe]. And so too Bach wrote that the custom is thus, and even in Kiddush which is d'oraisa women fulfill through hearing; and nevertheless it appears to me that nevertheless it is good that they say word for word with the one making Kiddush and the one blessing, for it is impossible to intend and hear as written siman 183 seif 7 and siman 199 seif 7 and siman 62. Berachat zimun. But if they can hear berachat zimun, he can bless the rest of birkas hamazon for himself, as written siman 183 seif 7. And in Darkei Moshe he wrote that this case is different there, for even though he blesses in a whisper, nevertheless the listener also hears the one blessing and answers amen on each berachah; and even though we say that berachat hazimun is only until ha-zan as written siman 200 — that is when required; but when not required it is better to hear all the berachot from the mouth of the one blessing and answer amen, as written siman 59 regarding berachot of Kriat Shema, etc. — end of his words. And I do not know why he retracted in Shulchan Aruch and omitted siman 195 like Rabbeinu Yonah, and Bach wrote that one should not bless at all in a whisper but only hear from the mouth of the one blessing; and in my humble opinion we should conduct as Darkei Moshe wrote, that if they cannot hear birkas hamazon they should invite zimun three by three, and so is the plain meaning of the Gemara and poskim that when they invite zimun we require that they join together for all of birkas hamazon with God's Name — examine this. And even per one who says that berachat hazimun alone suffices, they must [hear] until ha-zan as Rama wrote siman 200; and l'chatchila they should give to bless to one whose voice is strong so all reclining hear, for in a multitude of people is the glory of the king; see end of seif 2.
