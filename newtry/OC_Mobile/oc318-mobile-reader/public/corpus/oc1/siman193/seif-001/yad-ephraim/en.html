In Taz s.k. 3 — from division of berachot etc.; and that in chapter One Who Ate that we follow multitude of people — he held it deals where there is a great one among them fitting to bless to discharge others because of multitude of people, similar to zimun; and they also erred and there is no obligation of zimun at all among equals. And Mareimar revealed to them that even among equals there is zimun and one discharges all; and if so, that of "these matters" also deals even among equals that one discharges all because of multitude of people; and therefore automatically through Mareimar's answer it is proven that multitude of people is preferable to division of berachot — since there is no distinction between great and equal. And what Taz wrote "and then there is merit" etc. — it is not precise that even when there is no zimun at all multitude is preferable to division of berachot, as proven from that of "these matters" and as he himself wrote above; but rather his intent: since zimun applies even though all are equal, it is proven there is merit in multitude of people even without zimun; and all the more so through zimun it is preferable to division of berachot.
