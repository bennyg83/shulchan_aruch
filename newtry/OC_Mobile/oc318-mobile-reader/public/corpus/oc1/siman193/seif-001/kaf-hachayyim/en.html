1) [Seif 1] Two who ate together, although one can discharge the other with the blessing of HaMotzi, etc. The distinction between the initial and concluding blessings is that at the beginning of the meal, when they recline and establish themselves together, they intend to join. Therefore, each fulfills his obligation through the other's blessing. At the end of the meal, however, the established framework has ended and they separate from one another. Therefore, each recites the blessing for himself. R' Yosef explains in the name of Rashbam that the reason is that Birkas HaMazon is of Torah origin, whereas the initial blessing is rabbinic, and the Sages were not as stringent about it, so one may fulfill his obligation through another's blessing. Rosh, third chapter of Berachos. Tosafos, Mordechai, Rabbeinu Yonah, and Beis Yosef write the same. Levush writes that we do not follow the latter reason, because according to it, one person could discharge another with every blessing over fruit, both initial and concluding, since they are all rabbinic. Rather, the first reason is primary: specifically bread has an established framework, so if they established themselves together one may discharge another, but this does not apply to other types, neither for the initial nor concluding blessing; see there. Perishah, paragraph 1, cites this. Taz, paragraph 1, however, writes that even according to the latter reason distinguishing Torah and rabbinic law, fruit must be treated separately. Although its blessing is rabbinic, each person must recite it for himself, as stated in the gloss to siman 213; see there. All this follows Rema, of blessed memory, in the gloss to siman 213, seif 1, who writes that with fruit, even for the initial blessing, each recites for himself, because sitting together is ineffective for one to discharge another. According to Maran, of blessed memory, there, however, sitting together is effective for one person to discharge another with the initial blessing even over fruit; see there.
