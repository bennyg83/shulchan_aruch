[Levush] Rather, the matter is clear, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov and Lechem Chamudot, folio 57, write that even if the Levush accepts the latter reason, fruit is different because reclining does not establish a formal group for fruit and there is no zimun for fruit, as implied in siman 213. The Bach writes at the beginning of siman 192 that one should be careful not to have three people establish themselves together over one of the Seven Species, in order to avoid the dispute, because the Raavyah rules that zimun applies even to the Seven Species.
[Levush] The women, etc. Above in siman 185, seif 3, one must say the case concerns an unlearned man who understands Hebrew but cannot himself recite Birkat HaMazon. The Bach writes, however, that the custom follows Rashi: for every obligation incumbent upon women, they rely on what they hear even if they do not understand at all. Birkat Avraham, folio 265, and Rabbenu Yerucham, folio 202, rule likewise. See below siman 199, seif katan 3.
They may divide, etc. The Taz holds that there is not even a preference based on 'with the multitude of the people is the King's glory,' because each subgroup will still have its own zimun. This differs from the Rama and Levush in the next seif, where if they divide there will be no zimun at all. Olat Tamid, however, writes that it is preferable not to divide, and cites Lechem Chamudot, Seder HaYom, and Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah. I examined these sources and did not find this in Seder HaYom or Lechem Chamudot. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah writes that Seder HaYom, the Levush, Bach, and Lechem Chamudot say it is preferable not to divide. The Bach, however, writes this in the name of the Rama. There is no doubt that Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah understood the Rama to refer to this seif too, while Olat Tamid mistakenly read the citation as referring to Seder HaYom. This also explains why Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah cites the Levush and Lechem Chamudot, who merely quote the Rama, and understood them likewise. The result is that none of these great authorities independently rules that they should not divide. There is no preference either way. I also find proof from Berachot 50, where the Gemara infers that if a larger zimun formula were preferable, why would they be allowed to divide? Note this well.
[Levush] Even from seven, etc. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah and the later authorities rule likewise.
To hear the blessings of the zimun, etc. This follows the Levush's view in siman 183, seif 7. According to what I wrote in seif katan 15 there, however, they must hear the entire Birkat HaMazon. If they cannot hear all of Birkat HaMazon, they may divide even if they could hear the zimun formula itself. Magen Avraham rules accordingly. He writes that initially they should appoint as leader someone with a strong voice so all diners can hear. He further writes that if they must leave to perform a mitzvah, they may divide into groups of three.
