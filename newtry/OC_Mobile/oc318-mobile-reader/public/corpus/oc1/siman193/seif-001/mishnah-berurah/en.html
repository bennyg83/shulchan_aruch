(1) One discharges the other, etc. — meaning that in hamotzi even l'chatchila each one can fulfill through his fellow's berachah.
(2) It is a mitzvah to divide — meaning that even though b'dieved in birkas hamazon he certainly fulfills through his fellow who discharges him, nevertheless l'chatchila it is a mitzvah to divide. The reason they distinguished between birkas hamazon and hamotzi is that at the beginning of the meal when they establish to eat together their intent is to join, unlike at the end of the meal when their intent is to separate from one another; therefore each must bless for himself. And there is another reason because birkas hamazon is d'oraisa they were stringent regarding it. And see in Peri Megadim who wrote it is proper to be concerned for the first reason; and therefore even if he did not eat to satiety, where his obligation is only d'rabbanan, nevertheless it is a mitzvah to divide and each bless for himself.
(3) One knows, etc. — see below siman 197 seif 4 in Mishnah Berurah.
(4) That he intend to discharge him — and the listener too must intend to fulfill, as written siman 213 seif 3; and see what we wrote there.
(5) But if he does not understand, etc. — and per this women should bless for themselves; and the same applies to an ignoramus when he does not understand berachat hamotzi. And there are poskim who hold that in lashon kodesh a person fulfills his obligation through hearing even though he does not understand the language (unlike other languages, where all agree he does not fulfill if he does not understand the language). And so is the custom that women fulfill through hearing from the one blessing even though they do not understand at all, and even in Kiddush which is d'oraisa — for women, all agree they fulfill through hearing; and so an ignoramus even though they do not understand the words of the one making Kiddush [Acharonim]. And nevertheless it is better that they say after the one blessing and the one making Kiddush word for word in a whisper if possible for them, for thereby they fulfill per all poskim; and if not possible, it is proper to do so per all views, for it is impossible to intend and hear well, as explained above siman 183 seif 7 — see there.
(6) They are not permitted to divide — for they already became obligated in zimun from when they ate together. And know that the obligation of zimun applies specifically when they ate bread upon which they are obligated in birkas hamazon; but if they ate fruits, and even if they are of the seven species, it is not as we hold in siman 213 that there is no zimun on fruits. And there are those who are stringent on seven species to require zimun; therefore it is good not to fix three together upon seven species in order to remove oneself from the dispute [Bach]. And Birkei Yosef wrote that the custom is simply to fix several people upon fruits of seven species, and likewise to eat pat haba'ah bekisnin.
(7) It is a mitzvah that they seek him — meaning if he is with them in their house, it is a mitzvah to give him something to eat so he joins with them. And it is implied in the Gemara that even if he is a servant who serves them at the meal, where it is not their way to eat with him, it is a mitzvah that they call him to eat with them so they can invite zimun.
(8) After a third — and it is plain that if he is one person, they need not seek two.
(9) That all of them became obligated — meaning even though if one goes away three remain and can invite zimun, nevertheless he does not fulfill his obligation, for he too became obligated in zimun.
(10) They may divide — ten here and ten there, and it turns out that all of them invited zimun with God's Name.
(11) They are not obligated to divide — meaning we do not say it is better to divide in order to multiply berachot. And see in Darkei Moshe that his view is on the contrary: it is a mitzvah from the preferred [path] not to divide, whether with twenty or with six, because of "in a multitude of people is the King's glory"; only that by law there is no prohibition in the matter if they divide. And there are those who disagree on this and hold that since in each group there is also somewhat of a multitude of people, and the mitzvah of berachat zimun is fulfilled in each one — therefore they are balanced and one may do as he wishes even l'chatchila.
(12) After ten — so that they may be able to bless with God's Name. And see in Acharonim that even if they are seven, it is a mitzvah to seek three more; and see what we wrote above in s.k. 7.
(13) To hear berachat hazimun — for if they could hear berachat zimun from the mouth of the one inviting zimun, even though they cannot hear all of birkas hamazon from afar, they were not permitted to divide into groups of three; rather they hear berachat zimun alone from the mouth of the one inviting zimun, and in birkas hamazon each blesses before himself in a whisper, as explained above siman 183 seif 7.
(14) And they are not permitted, etc. — for if they could divide into tens of ten, they were not permitted to divide into a group of three, so they not lose mentioning zimun with God's Name.
(15) In a loud voice — in order to make heard berachat hazimun to all ten.
(16) And he resented them — for making groups for themselves. And the same applies if they need to go for a matter of mitzvah and they do not have time to wait lest the mitzvah be nullified — they are permitted to divide three and three; so too Magen Avraham. And in Kaf HaChayim he wavers on this and rules that in any case when ten do not remain without them, it is certainly forbidden to leave even for a matter of mitzvah. And nevertheless if it is a d'oraisa mitzvah, it is possible there is room to be lenient in every manner; and see in Peri Megadim.
(17) Berachat zimun — for behold, etc. — but if they could hear berachat zimun they were not permitted to divide, as likewise in s.k. 13. And Acharonim disagree on this, because even though we hold in siman 183 that nowadays each of those reclining blesses all of birkas hamazon in a whisper, nevertheless they must hear well also the words of the one blessing and say with him in a whisper word for word and answer amen on each berachah; and in any case until ha-zan et ha-kol they certainly must listen also to the words of the one blessing, for until there is berachat hazimun, as below in siman 200 in the gloss. And if so, even if they can hear berachat zimun from the mouth of the one blessing — whenever they cannot hear word for word from the mouth of the one blessing until ha-zan et ha-kol, it is better for them to divide into three and bless without God's Name than not fulfill the obligation of zimun at all by law. Acharonim wrote: where there is a large reclining group, one should give berachat hazimun to one whose voice is strong so all reclining hear — and so likewise.
