Then. See Taz. Darchei Moshe explicitly writes that with twenty people it is preferable not to divide. He cites Rashi to the Mishnah on Berachos 50a, who explains “up to ten” as meaning “if they wish,” but does not write this regarding six. The reason is that with six the choice is theirs, whereas with twenty it is preferable not to divide because a zimun of ten mentions Hashem’s Name, and greater attendance is preferable, as in a synagogue. At the beginning of 50a, however, Rashi explains “six may divide” to mean that they may do so if they wish. This means only that there is no obligation, although the choice remains theirs. The Gemara therefore proves that the wording “Let us bless” is preferable. If with six it were preferable not to divide, although division remained permitted, how could this prove that “Let us bless” is preferable? Perhaps “Bless” is preferable. I wrote this in my novellae, and I now see that Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 3, noted the same difficulty. The Rama’s wording here, that twenty may divide if they wish, follows Rashi’s explanation of the Mishnah. Since Rashi did not explain this regarding six, it implies that with six the choice is entirely theirs. His earlier words “if they wish” merely exclude an obligation. This is the meaning of the Rama’s statement, “It appears to me,” that no obligation exists. Levush’s wording is exceptionally clear: six may divide if they wish, whereas twenty are permitted to divide. Wherever seif 2 says “permitted,” it means that it is nevertheless preferable not to divide. A difficulty remains, however, because in the Gemara Rashi explains “six may divide” as “they are permitted,” while he does not use “permitted” regarding twenty in the Mishnah, the reverse of the usage here. Taz reasons that since the Mishnah does not say “six are permitted,” nor does it say so regarding twenty, the choice is theirs. His assertion that there is an advantage in a larger group over multiplying blessings is somewhat difficult, since in any event a zimun blessing requires three. See Magen Avraham, siman 90, seif katan 15, and siman 690. The distinction between a synagogue and this case requires study. See also what I write, with Hashem’s help, in seif katan 4.
