But Olat Tamid wrote this language: it is preferable not to divide; and so wrote Lechem Chamudot and Seder HaYom — see She'eirei Kenesset HaGedolah — until his language. And indeed I wonder, for I examined and did not find thus in Seder HaYom, nor in Lechem Chamudot; and I examined in She'eirei Kenesset HaGedolah — this is his language: but it is preferable not to divide — Seder HaYom and Levush and Bach and Lechem Chamudot — until his language. And behold Bach wrote thus in the name of Rama, and there is no doubt he understood Rama's words; and Olat Tamid erred and thought it was Seder HaYom and did not examine closely. And now it is settled what She'eirei Kenesset HaGedolah wrote in the name of Levush and Lechem Chamudot — because he brought Rama's words, and per his understanding it applies also to this seif, as I explained, and he ruled thus. It emerges that it is not found in any of the great ones mentioned above that they ruled one may not divide, but rather there is no preference in this; and so it appears to me to prove from the Talmud [Berachot] daf 50, who inferred that if blessing is preferable, why divide? — examine.
