4) There: If he does not understand, etc. This applies specifically to listening: one who does not understand does not fulfill his obligation. A person who himself recites the blessing, however, fulfills his obligation even if he does not understand what he articulates. Levush; Olas Tamid there; Shulchan Gavoah, paragraph 1; Shaarei Efraim, siman 13. We wrote the same above in siman 124, paragraph 4; see there. Although Rosh in the third chapter of Berachos and Rabbeinu Yonah there imply that even one who articulates the words must understand them, one may say that this applies initially, but when no alternative is possible he fulfills his obligation; see there. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2, however, distinguishes as follows: when reciting in Lashon HaKodesh, one fulfills his obligation even without understanding the language; when listening, which requires intent, one must specifically understand Lashon HaKodesh; and in any other language, even the person reciting must understand it. See there. Tosafos in Sotah 32a appear to agree; see there. We wrote the same above in siman 62, paragraph 3, and siman 101, paragraph 16; see there.
