And it appears to me. See Taz. This follows his position in seif katan 3, and Rashi writes similarly in the Mishnah there. Earlier in the Gemara, however, Rashi writes regarding six that if they wish they may divide. See what I wrote in seif katan 3, that a distinction may be drawn between twenty and six. See Bach regarding this.
It is obvious to me. The Mishnah’s ruling that six may divide applies only when none of them has already recited Bircas HaMazon. If one already recited it independently, they may not divide. Although siman 194, seif 1, rules that two may form a zimun by including a third who already recited Bircas HaMazon, that applies after the fact, not initially. This is certainly so if one of them already formed a zimun with two others. This resolves the Beit Yosef’s citation from the Tosefta that twenty may divide provided none among them has already been exempted from zimun, whether by forming a zimun of three or by reciting Bircas HaMazon alone. Although apparently, all the more so, ten could form a zimun by including him, nevertheless they should not arrange matters this way initially. I saw, however, that Perishah writes that if one already recited Bircas HaMazon, they may include him in their zimun, implying that this may be done even initially. If three already formed a zimun, however, even though they remain obligated in a zimun mentioning Hashem’s Name, Perishah agrees with Beit Yosef that they may no longer be included in a zimun of ten. It would appear that even if three each recited Bircas HaMazon independently, according to Perishah twenty may divide in such a case, whereas according to Beit Yosef they certainly may not. Panim Meiros, part 2, siman 6, comments on Perishah’s ruling. We have already explained that after the fact this certainly works. Even if three each recited Bircas HaMazon independently, seven may include them in their zimun, because they are no worse than one who ate vegetables, as stated in siman 196, seif 2. Initially arranging a division of twenty in this manner, however, may be prohibited, as Beit Yosef writes. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained further in simanim 194 and 196.
