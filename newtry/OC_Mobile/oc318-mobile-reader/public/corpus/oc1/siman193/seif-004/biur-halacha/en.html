Even if each one eats from his own loaf and even, etc. — see Magen Avraham, who brought in the name of Bach that when there are two reasons for leniency they may divide; and behold Bach derived this from Tur's language, who wrote one must be concerned for two versions; and Bach understood that if we emend the Gemara "each eats from his loaf," then we need not be stringent regarding less than a kezayit; and likewise if we emend the Gemara such as "they sat to eat," then we need not be stringent regarding each eating from his loaf — see there in Bach at end of his words; and therefore he holds one need not be stringent with two stringencies. And indeed this is not Rif's law regarding each eating from his loaf — it is true for all — see Tosafot Rabbenu Yehudah on Berachot, who wrote Rif's law from R' Chananel, and derived it from explicit Gemara daf 42b — see there; and so too Rashba and Ra'ah in their novellae on this sugya [and even though one could reject Rif himself in chapter 7 regarding hamotzi, that for him the whole sugya there deals with the first berachah, and it is possible regarding the final berachah we require that all eat from one loaf — nevertheless Rosh and Rashba and Tosafot and Semag all wrote explicitly that the sugya on daf 42 deals also with the final berachah — see there; and so too in this siman 193 it is explicit that also regarding zimun we are not particular that all eat from one loaf]. And so too it is proven per my view from the mishnah in chapter "Three who ate," "all untithed," etc. — behold it deals with eating from one's loaf; and nevertheless specifically in a case where it is impossible at all to eat together they do not join, but otherwise they do join. And regarding less than a kezayit it is explicit in Yerushalmi, and from where do we reject it from halacha? And so explained Eshel Avraham, who has the girsa like Rif; and nevertheless he brings the Yerushalmi matter for this law — thus both exist [unless we are forced to say that even though both laws are true, nevertheless possibly the Yerushalmi's stringency regarding less than a kezayit is when both eat from one loaf — and from where is this? Also Bach's language does not imply thus, for because of this he is lenient — see there]. And see Mordechai's article, who also sides not like Bach; and even though Tur's language is somewhat strained, nevertheless this is not compelled for the law; and likewise the second proof Bach brings from Tur is no proof at all per my view; and therefore one should not deviate from Shulchan Aruch's ruling.
