Even if each one eats from his own loaf, and even, etc. See Magen Avraham, who cites Bach that when both disadvantages are present they may separate. Bach derives this from the Tur’s statement that one must account for both textual versions. He understands that if the Gemara’s reading is “each one eats from his own loaf,” there is no basis to be stringent where each ate less than a kezayis; and if the reading is “such as when they sat down to eat,” there is no basis to be stringent when each eats from his own loaf. Bach therefore holds that one need not combine both stringencies. In truth, however, the Rif’s ruling that each may eat from his own loaf is accepted by all. Tosafos Rabbeinu Yehudah to Berachos cites it in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel and derives it from the explicit Gemara on 42b. Rashba and Raah likewise write this in their novellae to that passage. Although one might distinguish the Rif himself, since in his view that entire discussion concerns the first blessing and perhaps the after-blessing requires everyone to eat from one loaf, nevertheless the Rosh, Rashba, Tosafos, and the other poskim explicitly write that the passage on 42b also concerns the after-blessing. This is likewise explicit in seif 3 here, showing that for zimun too we do not require everyone to eat from one loaf. In my opinion, this is also proven by the Mishnah at the beginning of the chapter Three Who Ate, which discusses one who ate untithed produce, and therefore plainly concerns each person eating from his own loaf. Nevertheless, they fail to combine only where it is entirely impossible for them to eat together. The rule concerning less than a kezayis is explicit in the Yerushalmi, and there is no basis to reject it from halachah. Or Zarua also adopts the Rif’s reading yet cites the Yerushalmi as law, proving that both rules apply. One could perhaps force a distinction and say that the Yerushalmi’s stringency concerning less than a kezayis applies only where they eat from one loaf, but there is no source for this, and Bach’s wording does not imply that this is the basis of his leniency. Maamar Mordechai also inclines against Bach. Although the Tur’s wording is somewhat strained, Bach’s conclusion is not compelled as practical law, and his second proof from the Tur is no proof at all in my opinion. One should therefore not depart from the Shulchan Aruch’s ruling.
