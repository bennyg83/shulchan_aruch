For the berachah of zimun until "Who provides" implies they need not hear more. Therefore if one pauses for two when they reach "Who provides," he need not pause more and may eat.

And I did not know why he retracted, etc. — for Rabbi Yitzchak wrote there that they must hear the words of the one blessing — the berachah of zimun with understanding. And Rama wrote on this: and the rest of birkas hamazon each blesses for himself — and this is like Rabbi Yitzchak's words here.

And Bach wrote one should not bless, etc. — I already wrote his reason above in siman 183 s.k. 12, that he holds one must answer amen at the end of the berachah of the one blessing, as written in siman 183; and the reason Rama wrote there: if they bless with the one blessing in a whisper, they should finish before the one blessing so they can answer amen.

And lest it not be possible for them to finish quickly before the one blessing, and they cannot answer amen and "through His Name"; and even per one who says etc., they must [hear] until ha-zan — and so too above siman 183 s.k. 12, for this is the essential point.

And in my humble opinion we should conduct as Darkei Moshe wrote, etc., and so is the plain meaning of the Gemara.

And this is what we say on daf 45: Judah bar Ma'arava and Mar bar Rav Ashi and R' Elazar of Difti would wrap themselves in a tallit together — there was no one distinguished among them, and they challenged him: that which we taught, "three who ate are obligated to invite zimun" — this applies where there is an elder among them; but where they are all equal, division of berachot is preferable, etc. And one could say that if they invite zimun, each one can bless in a whisper by himself — why did they bless there each by himself and not invite zimun, as concluded there, because of division of berachot? If so, even when they invite zimun there would also be division of berachot, for each one would bless in a whisper. Rather certainly when they invite zimun they must hear and not bless, and there is no division of berachot. This proof was brought in Bach.
