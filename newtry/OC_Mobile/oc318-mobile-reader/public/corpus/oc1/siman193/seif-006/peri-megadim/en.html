Taz — Tosafot Berachot 45a s.v. shelosha: Yerushalmi — you say they are obligated to invite zimun and you say they are not permitted to divide (on daf 57) — initially he explains like Rosh, i.e., like the challenge in Bavli "what does it teach us" — it is not difficult and it is irony (and he says "and this is not difficult" meaning irony); afterward they explained Yerushalmi challenges itself and raised this distinction that Taz wrote from them; but Rosh at beginning of chapter Three Who Ate and daf 57a explained Yerushalmi like Bavli "what does it teach us" and answers etc.; and there are two laws: if they were established in hamotzi — per their view reclining, and per our view sitting (as siman 167 seif 11) — even if they did not eat a kezayit they must further finish and invite zimun; and if initially they were not established and afterward were established at the end of the meal — they are obligated to invite zimun; and Beit Yosef brought in seif 2 and 4 the words of Rosh; but in plain speech and they did not eat at all they did not become obligated (unlike the implication of Rav Hai Gaon, Abudraham) — and he concludes: since they are established at the end of eating; and so is Rosh's language there chapter Three Who Ate. And nevertheless the Mechaber should have brought explicitly Tosafot's language that if they were not established in hamotzi there is permission to finish the meal earlier provided he ate a kezayit earlier (and one could say less than kezayit); and even though Rosh's plain meaning is like Tosafot, nevertheless it is possible he agrees to this law; and since it is a rabbinic matter, as most poskim hold zimun is d'rabbanan, he should have ruled like Tosafot; and see Eliyah Rabbah ot 5 and what I wrote — end of his words.
