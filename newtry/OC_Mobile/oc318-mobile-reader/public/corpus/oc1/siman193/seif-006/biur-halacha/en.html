Since their companions invited zimun — Gra wrote: it appears to me this is specifically if they invited zimun before these three joined together; then the one who left their group has no place to fulfill zimun by himself, and therefore the zimun that the three remaining in their house invited applies also to him, and zimun departed from him. But if these three joined to one place from the outset and afterward the others remaining in their house invited zimun — these three may invite zimun, and zimun did not depart from them, since here they have a place to invite zimun by themselves; they separated completely from their group, and the zimun does not apply to them. And a little proof for this from the Rama gloss here, who wrote: but if in every group there were four, one may separate, etc., and they did not stipulate that they would invite zimun first, etc. — see there end of his words. And even though Shulchan Aruch's language does not imply thus — for it wrote "and they joined another group and invited zimun," which implies they joined first and then invited; and likewise afterward it wrote "and these three joined and one came," etc., which implies they joined first — and nevertheless zimun departed; nevertheless the truth is with him, for so Ramban wrote explicitly in his Lekutim on Berachot — see there.

But if in every group, etc. — Chayei Adam wrote: four who ate together at one table and two ate at another table — one of the four may join the two and invite zimun with them; and if the three remaining already invited zimun, zimun departed from the one and he does not join, etc. — see there. And his words are difficult: how does the one join the two for zimun afterward when they are not obligated at all? And it is not similar to the Rama gloss here, where each one is obligated after separating from a group of three obligated in zimun. And one could say his intent is where the one will now eat a little in their group and zimun is created thereby; but if so it is difficult on what he wrote afterward: if the three remaining already invited zimun, zimun departed from the one and he does not join again. And if it deals with eating — he should be able to join in any case, since he did not fulfill his zimun obligation through the group's zimun, as we wrote in Mishnah Berurah in the name of Gaon R' Akiva Eiger. And one could say it deals initially when they see one another partially — if so, why did he conclude at end of his words? But if there were two groups and in each group three, one may not join, etc. — see there; and the matter requires study.
