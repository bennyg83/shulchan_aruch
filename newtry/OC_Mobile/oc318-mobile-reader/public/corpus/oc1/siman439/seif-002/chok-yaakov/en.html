And one pile of chametz. The same law applies where there are two piles, one of matzah and one of chametz. The Gemara compares this to two people who walked on two paths, and Taz in Yoreh Deah, siman 111, and Pri Chadash write likewise.
He need not inspect again. According to Tur, this applies specifically where he nullified the chametz, as explained above, siman 438, seif katan 2. Even according to Rambam, the ruling is the same, as implied by Maggid Mishneh, chapter 2 of Hilchos Chametz. Bach and Magen Avraham likewise write that this is Rambam’s view.
But the conclusion of the other poskim is not so. See Tur and Beit Yosef for the many distinctions in this law, and also the law where a mouse entered a courtyard and it is unknown whether it entered the house. Shulchan Aruch omitted and abbreviated these laws because, as Rama writes above in siman 438, seif 2, they are uncommon. Rif likewise omitted them for this reason, as Rosh and Ran write in the first chapter of Pesachim. I have therefore also been brief.
