(א) He need not return and check — it deals when he nullified, and it is a rabbinic doubt to be lenient; and one should not challenge — behold Rambam holds above regarding nine communities, etc., that he must return and check even if they nullified, because in such rabbinic doubt checking is required, as written below; and one might say there we see with certainty a change before us in this house, and there is no doubt at all in this house that a mouse entered, only doubt whether it is chametz or not — not so here where we doubt the change itself, for we do not know into which house it entered; therefore even if they come to ask at once about those two houses, he need not check, since they nullified it is rabbinic doubt — not like doubt of tum'ah which is d'oraisa doubt; and Levush distinguished — above regarding nine communities he has nothing to rely on for the obligation of checking except doubt whether he is obligated to check, therefore we go stringently as the law of all bedikat chametz that one must check from doubt; not so here, each relies on the other. And it is difficult — why rely on doubt of entry more than doubt chametz matza; and Rambam wrote further a law he did not copy here in Shulchan Aruch: where he left chametz and a mouse came and took it, and doubt whether it entered this house or did not enter — checking is required; and it is difficult — for we said above it certainly entered, only doubt into which house it entered — no checking is required; and all the more here where the doubt is on the entry itself — no checking; and one might say here it deals where the chametz was in his courtyard and his house too, since he is in that courtyard we say he is close to certainty the chametz is there, and all houses in that courtyard we doubt and must check them — it is literally as written in a valley that all fields in that valley are doubted — so too here; not so above where they are two houses and one domain does not belong to the other. Nevertheless he answered: above, since there is one house that certainly requires no checking, therefore they need not check them — not so here; and see there. And it is challenged — on the contrary, say the opposite: since here there is one that requires checking and nevertheless you say no checking is required, all the more one that requires no checking where the doubt is whether it entered; and furthermore, if so, when one person has two houses and checked one and does not know which he checked, one might also say no checking is required, since he certainly checked one of them — one cannot say so, for then the poskim should have mentioned this great novelty; and per the answers I mentioned it is not a difficulty at all — for there the presumption is from himself that he does not know which he checked, therefore checking is required; not so regarding a mouse that entered, the presumption is from another side.

(ב) Such are Rambam's words; but the conclusion of the poskim is not so. This is Tur's view, who wrote regarding a large loaf: if he nullified, specifically he need not check; and so for a small loaf it helps even if he did not nullify; and for one community he wrote it does not help if they come to ask in eight houses even if they nullified; and if one-and-one, then nullification helps specifically because then it is rabbinic — one might say for tum'ah, because doubt on Rosh Hashanah is pure; and it is difficult from what is said in Kesubos 27a regarding a city captured by troops — they said there if there is one hiding place it saves all the women; R' Yehudai asked: it only establishes one, etc., and challenged from two paths, etc., and Rava said they did not disagree except when they come to ask about it and its fellow — so too here, that it is permitted for all of them like one case; and it implies if they were one-and-one it would help; and there it is d'oraisa, and here Tur explained specifically nullification helps one-and-one because then it is rabbinic. And furthermore in the first chapter of Temurah he brings Maharash in the name of Yerushalmi that for terumah too one-and-one helps like two paths; and terumah is d'oraisa; and in Yoreh De'ah 111 I elaborated; and it is further difficult that in Yoreh De'ah 109 dry in dry one-and-one helps even though d'oraisa — therefore it appears there is no rule of this d'oraisa doubt here for those doubts here — one can repair the d'oraisa doubt by nullifying and it becomes rabbinic doubt; so too after prohibition time when nullification is impossible, at least checking is possible — why enter doubt; not so there where the doubts cannot be repaired, as stated.
