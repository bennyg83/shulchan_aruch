Taz s.k. 3: since he certainly checked one of them, etc. — in my humble opinion it appears to distinguish: two houses that are not checked are different, for since presumption of prohibition was established — five are obligated in checking by rabbinic enactment — it does not cease from doubt and what one certainly checked does not help — not so regarding a mouse entering here, properly we say since the houses were already in presumption of checked, and you cannot remove both from their presumption since at least one is certainly checked, therefore we establish each in its presumption and say the mouse did not enter it. And similarly Tosafot wrote regarding doubt of tum'ah in a private domain — we do not learn from sotah "I was not," for it can be; but for something impossible to be impure for both we do not say so; and see what Eshel Avraham wrote on Maharsha's words in Chullin 8 that even in a private domain in such a case we establish each in presumption of purity; and elsewhere I wrote to resolve his difficulty; and if so, here too — since it is impossible to impose the obligation of checking on both, we establish each in his presumption.
