Nevertheless, if the loaf is small, etc., we presume leniently, etc. The Tur explains that this is because there is a compound doubt: perhaps it was matzah rather than chametz, and even if it was chametz, perhaps the mouse ate it. The Tur further writes that if the loaf is too large for the mouse to consume entirely and one did not nullify it, one must search; but if one nullified it, no search is required under any circumstances. Beis Yosef explains the Tur's reasoning: once one has nullified it, the matter involves a doubt concerning a rabbinic law, which is resolved leniently. Rambam, chapter 2, however, writes without qualification that one must search. Maggid Mishneh writes there that this is also the opinion of Rashi and the Geonim. In the name of Ra'ah, he explains that although this involves a doubt concerning a rabbinic law, the original obligation to search for chametz was itself instituted because of a doubt. The Sages therefore treated a doubt concerning this obligation more stringently than other doubts involving their enactments, and this is the primary ruling. Beis Yosef cites his words. Rambam's unqualified language thus implies that one must search in every case, whether the loaf is small or whether one nullified it. This also appears to be the view of the Shulchan Aruch, which states Rambam's ruling without qualification and does not cite the Tur. Rema should therefore have presented his ruling as the opinion of some authorities. However, it is Rema's practice in many places to state a ruling without attribution when the Shulchan Aruch did not explicitly state the contrary. Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 2, writes likewise. Erech HaShulchan, subsection 2, likewise writes that Maran's opinion is to prohibit even when there is a compound doubt. Maharikash, however, writes that if one nullified it, no search is required because of the compound doubt. Chok Yaakov, subsection 3, writes that one may rely on Rema where one nullified it. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1, likewise writes that one may rely on this where one nullified it or can still nullify it. See there. With a large loaf, however, one may not be lenient on account of nullification. Levush rules likewise, and Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, agrees.
