He must inspect again. Magen Avraham writes that even if he already nullified the chametz, according to Rambam he must inspect. This is unlike the case where he inspected part of the house and nullified, where we attribute the found loaf to the one carried in by the mouse. There no flaw appeared, whereas here the loaf was originally in one corner and was later found elsewhere. This explanation is incorrect. There too the mouse took the loaf from one place and dragged it into the house, while here the concern is likewise that the mouse took the loaf from one corner and dragged it elsewhere. The Gemara and Rashi and Tosafos make clear that this is the essential concern. Magen Avraham overlooked Tosafos, Pesachim 10a, who raise this exact difficulty. They answer that in the earlier case he found a loaf identical to the one the mouse took. Here, however, the case is where he does not know how many loaves had been placed there, and inspection is required because perhaps he did not find them all. Rambam’s wording, which follows the Gemara, may also be understood this way. According to Tur, no difficulty arises at all. In a case where a loaf was left in one corner and found in another, no inspection is required if the chametz was nullified. Inspection is required only if it was not nullified, because then the doubt is from the Torah. Even if he knows how many loaves were left, he must inspect because of the doubt, just as when a mouse entered and he inspected only part of the house, inspection is required where the chametz was not nullified. This is so according to Rambam as well, as explained above, seif katan 8. For this reason Rashba and those who agree with him rule concerning meat that disappeared from sight that, where there is a Torah concern of neveilah or treifah, if it is not found in the place where it was left, even within the house it is prohibited because of the doubt. This is clear. Pri Chadash, siman 63, seif katan 2, discusses at length to reject the early authorities and concludes that where a loaf was left in one corner and found in another, no nullification is needed, all the more so no inspection. He was imprecise. Certainly, if it is still before the sixth hour, nullification suffices. After the sixth hour, if he did not nullify it, he must inspect again because of the doubt, as is clear from the early and later authorities. Pri Chadash himself concludes in practice that one should be stringent. See there.
