(13) He should not sit with his face, etc. This is the correct text. This is the view of the Taz and the other later authorities.
(14) Rather, toward the north, etc. The same applies when his back faces the holy site, in which case urinating is permitted. See Biur HaGra, who agrees that even beyond the point from which Yerushalayim is visible, urinating while facing the holy site is forbidden. One may not openly relieve himself while directly facing a synagogue or beis midrash, lest he be among those concerning whom it is written, “with their backs toward the Sanctuary of Hashem.” Likewise, one should not construct a privy directly opposite a synagogue or beis midrash in a manner that leaves the anal opening exposed toward them. This is stated in Sefer Chassidim, siman 814, and is cited by Eliyah Rabbah and the glosses of Rabbi Akiva Eiger. If one builds an intervening wall between the privy enclosure and the synagogue, it would appear that there is no need to be stringent. See Orach Chaim.
