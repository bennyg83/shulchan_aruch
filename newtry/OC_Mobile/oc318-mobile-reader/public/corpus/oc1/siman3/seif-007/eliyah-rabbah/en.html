[6] He sits with his face, etc. This implies that having one's back toward the sacred place is forbidden, as the Beit Yosef wrote. It is possible to align the words of the Levush accordingly, or to say that he holds that the primary explanation is like the first explanation in the Beit Yosef, that it states a novelty: even facing it, where he exposes two tefachim, is permitted, and certainly with his back toward it. Nevertheless, the Levush overlooked the words of the Kesef Mishneh, chapter 4 of Hilchos Beis HaBechirah, that he retracted, because there is a scribal error, and the text in the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch should read: he should not sit with his face toward the sacred place, but rather to the north or to the south, or he should move the sacred place to the sides; end quote. The Olas Tamid wrote that even now there is a practical difference, for the sanctity remains in its place, as stated in Megillah. In the book Sheyarei Kenesses HaGedolah he wrote that this law was written unnecessarily and that he was not precise; end of his words. I do not know why he wrote this about the Sheyarei Kenesses HaGedolah, for he copied his words from the Bach; see there. Also, regarding what he challenged from the Gemara in Megillah, one must say that only regarding matters forbidden in the place of the Mikdash itself do we say that its sanctity remains in its place. Know this, for we say in Berachos 62 that nowadays there is no prohibition to act frivolously from Tzofim inward, and so it also implies in Lechem Chamudos, page 75; examine this carefully. Sefer Chasidim, siman 620, writes that when one leaves the privy he should look at the place where he sat, so that there should be no filth, lest his fellow come suddenly, or at night, and sit on it. A person should not make a privy aligned opposite a synagogue, so that the anal opening should not be exposed opposite the synagogue or beis midrash.
