[8] And not with the right hand, etc. Likewise, not with the middle finger of the left hand, because one places tefillin on it; Shelah. All of this concerns wiping. But regarding rubbing away droplets, it is explained in Beit Yosef, siman 43, that it is permitted even with the right hand. It is possible that the same applies, that it is permitted to kill a louse with the right hand. The reason for the prohibition against wiping with the right hand is that he ties tefillin with the right hand. Another reason is that he shows the cantillation reasons of the Torah with it. According to these reasons, if he is left-handed, he should wipe with his right hand. But according to the reason that the Torah was given with the right hand, even for a left-handed person we follow the right hand of all people, and he must wipe with his left. It seems to me that even according to the reason that one shows the cantillation reasons of the Torah with them, it is permitted to show the cantillation reasons of the Torah with the left hand, even though it is designated for wiping, for a left-handed person wipes with his left hand even though he shows the cantillation reasons of the Torah with it. So it seems to me. However, in Beit Yosef, siman 43, he wrote that it is forbidden to wipe with the right hand; therefore one must say that a left-handed person also wipes with his own right hand, for his left hand is considered his right even according to the reason that the Torah was given with the right hand. So wrote the Taz. In Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 149, he disagrees with what the Taz wrote, but regarding the reason, etc., and he did not examine the end of the Taz's words. However, this requires study, for one can say that what he wrote, that Beit Yosef holds it is forbidden to wipe with the right hand, refers only to an ordinary person, but a left-handed person is different.
