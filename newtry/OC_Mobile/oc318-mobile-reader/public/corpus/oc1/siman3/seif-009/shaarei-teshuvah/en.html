Right hand — Baer Hetev. Bikkur Shor 110 disagrees with Magen Avraham and distinguishes: between one accustomed to writing sacred matters and one accustomed to cantillation marks, or not fit / accustomed to both; a left-handed person who writes with his left and does other acts with the right of most people should wipe with his [writing] “right,” which is most people’s left. If he is not accustomed to writing sacred text but is accustomed to trope, even if he sometimes writes sacred text there is no reason to worry—he is like everyone. If he is accustomed to writing sacred text but not trope, he wipes with his left, which is everyone’s right, and not with his right hand with which he writes. If he is left-handed who writes with the right and other acts with the left: if accustomed to writing but not trope, he wipes with everyone’s left; if accustomed to trope but not writing, with everyone’s right; if accustomed to both or to neither, he is like everyone and wipes with everyone’s left—this is the upshot of his words. See Machazik Berachah citing Mor Uktzia that a woman too should not wipe with the right, and he wrote not to be strict with her on this—see there. See Yad Ephraim on explaining the Taz; but Beit Yosef in siman 43 wrote it is forbidden to wipe with the right—which is correct—and those after him strained to explain his words.
