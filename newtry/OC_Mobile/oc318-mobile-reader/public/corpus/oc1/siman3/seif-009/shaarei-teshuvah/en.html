Right — Baer Hetev. And Bikkur Shor folio 110 disagrees with Magen Avraham on this and wrote to distinguish between one accustomed to writing sacred matters and one accustomed to cantillation marks, or one not fit or not accustomed to both; and if he is left-handed, who writes with his left and his other actions with the right of most people — he should wipe with his right, which is the left of most people. And likewise if he is not accustomed to writing sacred matters but is accustomed to trope marks, even though it may happen at some time that he writes sacred matters, there is no reason to be concerned for this, and behold he is like every person. And if he is accustomed to writing sacred matters and is not accustomed to trope marks, he wipes with his left, which is the right of every person, and he shall not wipe with his right with which he writes. And if he is left-handed, who writes with his right and his other actions with his left — if he is accustomed to writing and not to trope marks, he wipes with the left of every person; and if he is accustomed to trope marks and not to writing, he wipes with the right of most people; and if he is accustomed to both or is not accustomed, behold he is like every person and wipes with the left of every person. This is the upshot of his words, may his memory be a blessing. See Machazik Berachah in the name of Mor Uktzia that a woman too shall not wipe with the right, and he wrote about this that one should not be stringent with her, etc. — see there. See Yad Ephraim on what he wrote explaining the words of the Taz; but Beit Yosef in siman 43 wrote that it is forbidden to wipe with the right — see there — which is correct, and those who came after him strained in explaining his words, may his memory be a blessing.
