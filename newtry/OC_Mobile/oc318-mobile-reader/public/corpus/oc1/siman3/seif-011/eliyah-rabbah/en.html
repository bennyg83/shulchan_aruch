[9] With earthenware, etc. In Eliyah Zuta I wrote that it seems to me that the explanation of the Gemara at the end of chapter HaMotzi is that one who wipes with the rim of an earthenware vessel has no concern of sorcery. With this, all the questions of the Bach and the later authorities here and below in siman 312 are resolved, as well as what the Bach asked there against Rashi's explanation. Examine this carefully, for I have been brief, and below it will be explained further, with the help of Heaven. It is stated in the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter HaMotzi: one does not wipe with the mouth of a dog, nor before a dog, nor with water from which a dog lapped, nor with water that does not contain forty se'ah, nor with an item that entered fire and came out, nor with bathhouse water. See Eliyah Zuta for an extended discussion of this.
[10] And so they practiced. See the Levush. The Malbushei Yom Tov wrote: it appears that the nature of lands or times changes, as he wrote in Even HaEzer, siman 156; end quote. In the Shelah, page 79, he wrote in Shabbos, chapter HaMotzi: there are three things which, if one does them, make him vulnerable to sorcery: first, one who pulls a vegetable from a bundle; second, one who kills a louse on his garments; third, one who wipes with earthenware. Therefore one who guards his soul should distance himself; end quote. They further warned: three do not pass between others and others do not pass between them: a dog, a palm tree, and a woman; some say also a pig. If this occurred, Rav Pappa said: begin with a Divine Name and conclude with a Divine Name, 'God brings them out,' etc., until 'what has God wrought.' Or begin with 'not' and conclude with 'not,' such as 'God is not a man,' etc., until 'and He will not fulfill it.' He further counts many matters that involve danger, such as one who relieves himself between a palm tree and a wall when there are not four amos between them; see there, and below, siman 170.
