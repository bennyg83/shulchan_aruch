(s.k. 12) “At night,” etc.—relieving in the street: so Berachot 62a; or Tosafos s.v. Rabbi Elazar—if a corner is nearby he should withdraw there at night as by day, and not relieve exposed—so Rashi there, cited by Taz.

“But not before gentiles”: Avodah Zarah 17a. If one’s house adjoined an idol-house and the [shared] wall fell, he may not rebuild in a way that benefits idolatry—how then may he build? He sets back four amot into his own [property], leaves four amot vacant, and builds his wall. The Gemara asks: that still leaves four amot toward the idol-house; it answers he builds a privy there. It asks: a privy needs modesty—he should build a privy wall toward the idol-house; it answers: a nighttime privy. It asks: at night one need not distance, yet modesty still applies; it answers: a privy for a girl. Rashi on the modesty question: “here there is no modesty concern from idolaters’ view,” etc.

…permitted, etc.—if necessary, Bekhorot implies one is obligated [to relieve] when there is danger.

“A woman should not stand [exposed] before a child,” even if she does not uncover the way women usually avoid uncovering—there is still an immodesty concern—so it is stated there.
