b) [Section 2] He shall be modest in the privy, etc.; one who is modest in the privy is not ruled by an evil spirit or death; it is among things that lengthen life; for adults; for urination one need not be so modest. Yafeh Lev ch. 1 s. 2.
c) There in hagaha: Two men should not go together. If one is afraid he may place his hand on his head through a window. Gemara Berachot 62a. Magen Avraham s.k. 1, Raavad 2; one who inserts his hand into the privy needs netilat yadayim with three pourings. Ruach Chaim 3; see our words below siman 4 s. 6.
d) There in hagaha: Two men should not go together; and likewise two women should not enter together. Chasdei Lemalachim wrote to warn children in this; partly so they grow accustomed to modest matters and partly lest they grow accustomed to immodesty; even young children who “do not know evil”; for they said in the Gemara a woman should not rise to urinate before a nursing infant, kal va-chomer somewhat older children. Bnei Chayim year 1 Vayeitzei s. 6.
e) There in hagaha: He should not speak there. But the Sages enacted that women may converse in the privy because of yichud; where they are in a city there is no concern. Magen Avraham s.k. 2; where they are in a city two women need not avoid walking alone, unlike Perisha/Magen Avraham; see Chayei Olam on Magen Avraham s.k. 2. See Ben Ohev Chayim there.
f) There in hagaha: He should not speak there, for there is a kind of spirit that incites a person if he speaks there. Or Yisrael in the name of the Arizal. Amudei HaShulchan on Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 4 s. 3, and Rav Salat Balula s. 2, wrote that speaking in the privy is forbidden so as not to give external forces a hold on a person’s voice and speech (for he is holy. For great need before having relieved, it may be permitted) see Eliyahu Rabbah s. 2 with a case there. Many works of mussar wrote it is good to think other thoughts in the privy so Torah thoughts do not intrude. Ruach Chaim wrote it is good on weekdays to think of one’s livelihood and household needs; on Shabbat to think of fine buildings and forms. Salat Balula s. 8.
g) There in hagaha: He should close the door; even at night in darkness and by candlelight; nevertheless if the privy has a very bad odor and he opens the door slightly for air, one may be lenient in night darkness when he cannot be seen. Ben Ohev Chayim there s. 4.
h) When entering a privy he should not enter with his face straight backward or forward [toward holiness] but sideways. Masseches Derachei Eretz, Likutei Pri Chadash.
