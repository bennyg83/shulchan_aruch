“He shall be modest.” See Berachot 62a–b, where the Gemara distinguishes degrees of modesty; Semak counts modesty as a Torah mitzvah. I have seen places where people neglect building proper public privies—this causes many pitfalls: besides lacking modesty, many evils follow—(1) hundreds of blessings and Holy Names are recalled at all times for naught, for it is ruled below siman 92 that if one cannot stand a full hour and a fifth his prayer is loathsome; when a privy is nearby and he needs his openings before the Amidah he must not be lazy to go relieve himself—unlike when there is no privy and he must remove tefillin to go home, in which case a common man will surely be lazy and restrain himself until after prayer. Even a Torah scholar should not be lazy to seek some place or go home for this, for it still causes neglect of communal prayer and much neglect of Torah study. There is also embarrassment when people meet, and danger to life because a returning pillar [amud] can lead to hernia [drakon], and many more ills. Therefore it is a great mitzvah to help remove these obstacles from Israel so they may mention His Name in holiness.
