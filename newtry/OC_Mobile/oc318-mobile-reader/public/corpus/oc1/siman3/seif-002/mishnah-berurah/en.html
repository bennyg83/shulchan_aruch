(2) Until he sits — and the same applies that he should not stand while he is exposed.

(3) And they should not go — and if one is afraid, one may place his hand on his head through the window, provided he does not see his exposure, as below in siman 8. To enter there openly is forbidden even with his wife; but with a minor who lacks understanding, entry is permitted. [Aratzot HaChaim]

(4) He should not speak — and if it is for a great need, it may be that speech is permitted as long as he is not yet relieving himself; but while he is relieving himself, even for a great need it is forbidden. If the door cannot be closed and another man wishes to enter, they should grunt to one another and not speak. Only women, when they go to the fixed public privy — like the privy in the field in their days, or in the courtyard of the synagogue — in these lands the Sages enacted that they should converse with one another so their voices are heard from outside and a man will not enter and be secluded with them [Sanhedrin 19]. It implies somewhat there that they also enter two by two to the privy.
