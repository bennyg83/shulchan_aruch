“Between east and west.” Rashi explains one should arrange the bed so head and feet lie one toward north and the other toward south. So too Sefer Hasichos of Rabbi Moshe of Coucy; Magen Avraham in siman 3 ruled like the Zohar, Parashat Bamidbar: head and feet one east and one west, the bed between north and south; he wrote the head should be west—for a kabbalistic reason. The Levush wrote that even when one sleeps in a partitioned place it is forbidden, for it is a great disgrace—this needs study: is there really more disgrace in sleeping than in a privy? It appears to me [vele’aniyas da’ati] that during sleep one is not as aware of partitions, for that is one’s usual way—unlike a privy, where one typically relieves in a field: if there are partitions, there is “recognizability.” Alternatively: the bedroom is also a place for the Divine Presence to rest, unlike a privy—even angels do not enter there, as explained in siman 1.
