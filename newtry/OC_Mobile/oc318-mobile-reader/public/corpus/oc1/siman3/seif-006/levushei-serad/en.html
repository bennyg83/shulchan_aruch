Ibid.: And why is north–south permitted in Judah unlike those other directions? The wording is somewhat tangled—that is: why is north–south in Judah permitted unlike east–west when those directions themselves are at issue? The difficulty is: why would the “sides” be “dragged” to forbid north–south—should they also be “dragged” to point T so north–south would be forbidden? Fair enough: place T is not “dragged” to the sides (a “dragging of dragging”), as explained—unlike the “sides” themselves: why should they not be “dragged” to both places to forbid both north–south and east–west? Hence one must say “a majority is not dragged after a minority,” therefore the sides are not dragged after place T ; and what drags to forbid north–south is that Jerusalem occupies more than half of Judah’s width, so the sides are a minority for north–south—and therefore Rashi wrote Judah is a narrow strip, meaning Jerusalem holds more than half its width as above, but “majority after minority is not dragged.” On this Raavad asked: for Rabbi Akiva who forbids outside Eretz Yisrael, there the sides are a majority—why are they dragged after a minority? He should forbid only the minority facing Jerusalem, since a minority is not dragged after a majority for leniency, as proved from place T , which is not dragged after the sides for leniency—but still, outside Eretz Yisrael’s sides, why are they dragged? The countries east and west of Eretz Yisrael—for which we forbid east–west—should likewise be forbidden north–south if they “drag” to the other side—yet “majority after minority is not dragged”—then east–west, after whom is it “dragged”? This is what Taz wrote: why is east–west forbidden there more than north–south? Understand everything Taz wrote here thoroughly: his words cannot be explained otherwise than as set forth—study it.
