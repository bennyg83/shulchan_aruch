[ו] “He sits with his face [toward the Sanctuary],” etc. It implies one’s back may not face the Sanctuary, as Beit Yosef wrote. One may explain the Levush that way, or say he held the main point is Beit Yosef’s first reading: he stated a stringency—even the front while exposing two tefachim is permitted, all the more so the back; still the Levush overlooked Kesef Mishneh Hilchot Bechirah ch. 4, who retracted—there was a scribal error—and one must read Rambam and Shulchan Aruch as: he does not sit facing the Sanctuary but north or south, or he turns the Sanctuary direction aside—end of quotation. Olas Tamid wrote that even now there is a practical difference of “holiness stands in its place” as in Megillah. Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah wrote he did not need to record this ruling and his wording to that point was imprecise. I do not know why he criticized Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah—he copied that author’s words from the Bach—see there. As for his difficulty from Megillah: one should say “holiness stands in its place” applies only to items forbidden in the Temple itself. Note: we say in Berachot 62a that nowadays there is no prohibition to raise one’s head from the lookout toward the Temple; so too Lechem Chamudos 75a—see there. Sefer Chasidim siman 820: when leaving a privy one should check the seat for filth lest another come suddenly or at night and sit on it. One should not build a privy aligned with a synagogue so that one’s anus is exposed toward the synagogue or study hall.
