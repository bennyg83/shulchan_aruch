“To see.” — Taz, as in Gemara Berachot 62a. Issi b. Natan said: behind a fence where one does not hear a sneeze; in an open field likewise where one does not see exposure—as Rabbi Elazar concludes—in a field one needs distance for sneezing and also not to see exposure. Ulla said: behind a fence immediately; in a field it suffices that one not hear a sneeze. Rif and Rosh ruled like Ulla for a fence immediately, in a field like Issi; Rav Ashi holds distance for sneezing does not help unless one does not see his exposure—how then did he write that if one hears a sneeze it is permitted? So too Levush explicitly permits hearing a sneeze. I understood from Levush from what he wrote: therefore “behind the fence immediately”—meaning since we follow Ulla “behind the fence immediately,” for sneezing what difference is there fence or not? Rather we infer: whenever one does not see his exposure we do not care about sneezing; in a field where there are two factors—sneeze and exposure—Ulla was lenient: not hearing a sneeze suffices; Issi is strict—we require both not hearing and not seeing. Since we rule like Ulla behind a fence—we do not care about sneezing when there is no visible exposure—in a field like Rav Ashi we are strict about exposure—hence when there is no visible exposure even if one hears someone sneezing no further [distance] is needed, for where there is no visible exposure we rule like Ulla (Eshel Avraham). From this one sees how great are our master the Taz’s words: with this Beit Yosef’s citation in Rabbi Yochanan’s name is settled—for a fence immediately and a field where one does not hear a sneeze or does not see exposure he held entirely like Ulla; when there is no visible exposure it helps and we do not worry about sneezing as with a fence; why Beit Yosef wrote he was uncertain—one can say, see there, because three disputes exist: Rambam Hilchos De’os 5:6 rules exactly like Issi—for a fence where one does not hear and a field where one does not see (both required); Rif and Rosh ruled like Ulla—for a fence only; in a field we require not seeing, and one factor suffices—“what is not revealed is not revealed”; R. Yehoshua b. Levi rules entirely like Ulla—in a field one of the two suffices, and if one does not hear sneezing that also suffices. Perhaps this is Rashi’s method Berachot 62a s.v. Rav Ashi said—do not read until below where he establishes Issi b. Natan thus per Rif and Rosh’s text: Ulla said fence immediately and field where one does not hear; Rav Ashi said field where one does not see—in a field we require not seeing and that suffices; Issi teaches fence where one does not hear sneeze and field requires both (no need for what Kesef Mishneh and Beit Yosef wrote about “what is revealed,” etc.); they ruled like Rav Ashi, and likewise Rif and Rosh had that version—they omitted everything, only Rav Ashi said—see there—and understand. Rashi holds like R. Yehoshua b. Levi; hence he wrote Rabbi Elazar establishes Issi thus and for himself disagrees—no need to extend. Levush and Taz wrote in a field yes, in a house one must not hear sneeze—that does not appear so from the poskim; likewise Bach brings the [case of] Rabbi Chiya’s daughter placing her hand on her head—yet for Levush there is no difficulty: one can say his wife is as his own body even if she hears and even sees his exposure. Needs study: exposure forward is also forbidden, and behind the fence one does not see—not near the fence.
