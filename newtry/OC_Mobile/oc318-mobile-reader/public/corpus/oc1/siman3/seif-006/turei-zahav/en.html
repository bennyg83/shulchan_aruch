“Even when his wife is not with him.” — The Tur inferred this from Rambam; Tosafos in Beit Yosef suggest even with one’s wife, without relations, it is permitted—because the Shechinah “dwells” along the east–west axis and lying with one’s wife for relations would be unseemly. The Taz asks why the Tur did not cite Rambam’s prohibition of sleeping with one’s wife along that axis. Berachot: Abaye ben Menasseh was distressed that his bed lay north–south—apparently a praiseworthy orientation, not forbidden; Beit Yosef answered he feared a different prohibition (distant). The Taz resolves: the Tur permits relieving with partitions even east–west per Yerushalmi/Tosafos (R. Akiva) when there is no wall—relations are no stricter than bodily needs; in a privy one may not think Torah, and at relations one should cleave to holy thought (R. Chaim Vital, citing Ramban)—so relations do not apply in an exposed privy and the Tur need not mention them. Rambam does not prove he ignores “wall vs no wall”—Beit Yosef says our Bavli argues on Yerushalmi; we do not rule like Rabbi Yossi’s “wall” leniency but like R. Akiva, equating relations and bodily needs: both are forbidden east–west even with partitions. Abaye ben Menasseh is then mere extra piety (Rashi). The Shechinah is toward west or east, so one should rotate the bed if easy (Maharshal)—not that east–west sleeping is forbidden per se. Maharshal’s gloss about disgrace next to one’s wife explains Abaye’s wording as custom, not prohibition—even for Rambam who forbids relations in a room along east–west if Rabbi Yossi’s “fence” view were accepted; we rule like R. Akiva, so Rambam would forbid; practically we permit bodily needs with partitions per Shulchan Aruch, hence relations with partitions too—yet Shulchan Aruch forbids relations like Rambam: needs study. Maharshal then suggests relations require greater sanctity—yet it seems odd to permit filth-relieving without Shechinah concern but restrict marital holiness; the Taz thinks the Tur fully permits and omitted prohibition; one who cannot easily rotate north–south is not forbidden east–west. Maharshal’s Zohar-based note (head east, feet west for sons) is not Shulchan Aruch’s plain sense—so at minimum there is no clear prohibition.
