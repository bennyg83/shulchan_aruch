“Married.” — It appears to me that if his wife is not with him or she is a niddah, it is forbidden. Tosafos: when we say our holy Rabbi did not place his hands below his belt, that was his greatness—he was married and stringent on himself—implying “below the belt” is not literal but means he did not place his hands on his organ; for if it meant literally below the belt, that would be mere ordinary piety for Rabbi… (and from Taz Even HaEzer 182 it seems he did not see these Tosafos). Similarly Avodah Zarah 17a: “Hand to his mother—cut off” for placing his hand below his navel—answered, a thorn lodged in his belly—should he not remove it? Yet his belly would split open. Tosafos: if he does not remove the thorn his organ may rupture—so “belly” means his organ; likewise the Gemara that began with “organ” and ended with “belly.” In Even HaEzer 23 we rule like Rambam—see there. The Gemara: where one is afraid it is permitted to hold, since fear does not lead to [forbidden] thoughts; the poskim omitted this because we are not expert as to which degree of anxiety prevents thoughts—it depends on the person (Beit Yosef). This needs study: Even HaEzer 184 rules anxiety stops menstrual flow, yet we do not say “we are not expert in anxiety”; it is strained to distinguish fear from anxiety, unless rabbinic veset rules are lenient unlike here where the sin is severe. See Shabbat 41a.
