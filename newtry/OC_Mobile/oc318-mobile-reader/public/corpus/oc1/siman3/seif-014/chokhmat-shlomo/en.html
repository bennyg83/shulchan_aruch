(S. 14) “He shall take care not to hold his organ while urinating,” etc. Editor’s note: See Magen Avraham’s astonishment at Beit Yosef regarding “we are not expert in the measure of anxiety” unlike Niddah, etc.; see Chiddushei Mei Niddah 14, Chiddushei L’YD omissions EH 184 on resolving this, Chiddushei L’Shas 9, and Tosafos Niddah 14 s.v. ha’ochez—even where holding is permitted, when pressed it is forbidden—see there; poskim did not spell this out clearly. In this connection I saw in Machatzit HaShekel this siman: he challenged the Gemara’s proof from bolas—we say in Ketubot 27b that for intercourse there is “leisure” because the yetzer for arayos is very strong, so one might say the same here and the proof from libation is weak. In my humble opinion it is not difficult: there the case is gentiles and villains suspected of arayos—reasonably fear might leave their hearts and they would sin. Here, are we dealing with deliberate evildoers forming [forbidden] thoughts? Surely not—if he could intend that, he could sin even without urinating; we do not assume deliberate intent, only that while holding the organ involuntary thoughts may come and his heart is “compelled”; since he does not deliberately remove fear from his heart, as long as he remains afraid he will not come to [forbidden] thoughts by coercion while afraid—and a coerced thought here is no worse than coerced libation there; if there libation from his own mind even when “beloved” does not come from fear, so too a coerced unwanted thought will not arise while he is afraid—unlike Ketubot, where we deal with a deliberate sinner who at the moment of intercourse removes fear from his heart because of the yetzer for arayos that is “beloved” to him. Similarly Taz Yoreh De’ah 184: one must worry that at relations fear leaves the heart—see there; this fits; study it.
