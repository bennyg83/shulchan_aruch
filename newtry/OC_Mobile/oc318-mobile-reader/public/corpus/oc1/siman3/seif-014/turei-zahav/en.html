“Unless he is married.” — The Tur wrote “even if he was married”; Beit Yosef called it piety; Magen Avraham disagrees. The Taz: the Tur explains the Gemara’s “married is permitted”—specifically when at that time he is regularly with his wife (“bread in his basket”). The extra words “if he was married” are not obvious (being married once would never suffice years without a wife); they teach he must have a wife then—“if married” means at that moment. This matches the Gemara’s precision about Rabbi Yochanan: “married” meant then married and regularly with his wife—so we rule stringently: who counts as “regularly with his wife” for the leniency? People sometimes travel from their wives; the Tur therefore stringently treats every married man like the Gemara’s other strict cases (anxiety; unclear measure of anxiety per Beit Yosef). Hence binding halacha and fear of Heaven, not optional piety as Beit Yosef said—Shulchan Aruch as the Taz understands.
