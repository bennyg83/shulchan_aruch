He may not uncover himself except one handbreadth behind and two before him; a woman [has different measures]. Some say one before and two behind—this is the Tur’s text and Sefer HaYirah for R. Yonah, Orchot Chayim, and Rashi in the Alfasi, chapter HaRo’eh. Reason favors this reading: before him he can take extra care not to soil his clothes with urine, since he sees what is before him—one tefach suffices; behind he does not see—two are needed. Still, one may be stringent like the stricter textual versions: one tefach before and one behind.
