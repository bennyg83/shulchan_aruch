“Even.” — The Taz expanded; his intent is that Rambam did not learn from Abaye ben Menasseh like Beit Yosef—for then it would be only piety as “I was distressed”; rather Rambam argues from the law of relieving and holds sleeping is likewise forbidden as unseemly, and on his view even with partitions relieving is forbidden so sleeping is too (Beit Yosef himself [says] for Rambam even with partitions it is forbidden, and Rambam there wrote forbidden to relieve or sleep east–west; all the more fixed privies, etc.—unnecessary—rather to say even fixed partitioned stalls like privies, and the wording “I followed Rabbi Yehoshua” implies partitions; Abaye ben Menasseh distressed alone follows Rabbi Yossi in Berachot that with a fence it is permitted, while we rule like the first tanna and Rabbi Akiva hold even with a partition it is forbidden—hence Rambam wrote correctly, unlike Shulchan Aruch who ruled with partitions relieving is permitted, all the more relations. Bach, Levush, and Magen Avraham letter 7 already reconciled this. What Abaye ben Menasseh “was distressed” implies is extra piety, not law: two matters—(1) his prayer should be near his bed so he not do work—that is forbidden by law; if we say “near his bed” means to study per Rashi, that is extra piety; if after reciting some songs and praises—works. But the Mechaber in siman 89 per his approach [in] se’ifim 13 and 16 works—and in my chidushim I elaborated.
