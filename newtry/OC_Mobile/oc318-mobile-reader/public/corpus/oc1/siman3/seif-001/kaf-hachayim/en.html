(א) [Section 1] When he enters, etc.—nowadays people do not recite it, for we do not presume ourselves to be yerei Shamayim such that angels escort us (Bach, Taz s.k. 1). Raavad holds 1—but Matzat Shemura 28d wrote one should be careful every time he enters a privy to say Hitchabdu, etc., contrary to Radak; it is good to say it to remove from him an impure spirit and evil thoughts and keep his heart open always to Torah—to learn it in holiness and purity—see there; they cited from Chavruta responsa 2, and wrote further from the great kabbalist Maharam Papirash in Or Tzadikim: one should say Hitchabdu, etc., even though Shulchan Aruch says we do not say it nowadays; Arizal innovated to say it now too, and by this one is saved from Torah thoughts in the privy—see there. So too Ruach Chaim 1 in the name of Maggid Meisharim to Maran in Metzot u-Masa’ei: the maggid told the holy Maran to say Hitchabdu mechubadim. So Yafeh Lev ch. 3 s. 1—but they wrote these words only for individuals whose craft is Torah, not others, for it looks arrogant; yet Pri To’ah Yoreh De’ah 246 wrote arrogance applies only to something done before many—not here.

(ב) This is the wording: “Be honored, honored ones, holy servants of the Most High—guard me, guard me; help me, help me; wait for me until I enter and leave, for such is human custom”—and so one says each time he enters. Tur.
