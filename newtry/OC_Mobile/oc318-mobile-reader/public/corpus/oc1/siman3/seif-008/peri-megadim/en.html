“With the hand.” — Taz: for three reasons a left-handed person wipes with his left, which is everyone else’s right; for the fourth reason Rava, who is latest, said Torah was given with the right—one can say we follow the world’s right and everyone wipes with the left, which is his “strong” hand (Magen Avraham 8). He wrote we derive law for the three reasons that one may show Torah trop with the left even though he wipes with it—for the fourth reason a lefty wipes with his left, which is his strong hand, and with it shows trop—one can say the other reasons do not apply here and we do not unravel the dispute. Beit Yosef wrote later siman 43: entering a fixed privy he holds it in his right—we are not concerned he will wipe with it, for wiping with the right is forbidden—hence a lefty wipes with his left, which is everyone’s right, even for the fourth reason “Torah was given with the right,” for regarding a lefty his left is considered his “right” and counts properly as “Torah with the right,” and we do not follow the world’s right. Hence one should not show trop with the left he wipes with—no proof. Eliyah Rabbah, letter 40, explained his precision: he said “holds in his yemino” not “in the right”—implying we follow his right hand—not a good precision per Magen Avraham. It appears to me the meaning is there are four reasons and we are uncertain for a lefty—hence necessarily a lefty may wipe whichever way he wishes because of doubt, and a rabbinic doubt without definite resolution is lenient even here (understand); why then not omit and write in Magen Avraham a lefty may not hold it in his hand at all lest he come to wipe—hence we infer even for the fourth reason we follow his right and he holds in his right; hence where he may wipe as he wishes per Magen Avraham 8, it is forbidden to hold it in his hand—will be explained with Hashem’s help.
