[ח] “Not with the right [hand],” etc. So too not with the middle finger of the left, because one lays tefillin on it (Shelah)—all this for wiping. Rubbing off urine splatter is explained in Beit Yosef siman 43 as permitted even with the right; the same may permit killing a louse with the right. The prohibition on wiping with the right is because tefillin are tied with the right and because one indicates Torah trop with it. For those reasons a left-handed person wipes with his right; for the reason “Torah was given from the right,” even a lefty follows everyone’s right hand and wipes with his left. It appears to me one may show trop with the left even though it is designated for wiping—a lefty wipes with his left while showing trop with it—so I thought; but Beit Yosef siman 43 forbids wiping with the “right,” so a lefty must wipe with his own right, which counts as most people’s left, even for the “Torah from the right” reason. So wrote Taz; Responsa Beit Yaakov 149 disputes the Taz’s wording but not his reason—and did not study Taz’s conclusion. This needs study: perhaps Beit Yosef’s “forbidden to wipe with the right” means everyone’s right, and he read the Gemara about tefillin in a privy—“he holds them in his right”—as most people’s right, not the lefty’s “right”—and that is no proof, for Berachot 23a parallels “holding in his hand” to “holding in his garment.” Magen Avraham wrote: if he writes with his left and does other work with his right, he wipes with his right—two reasons: writing and tying tefillin with the left—end of quotation. That is not obvious, for the left also has two reasons—feeding mouth and trop. The “Torah from the right” of most people is as he himself wrote there.
