“Exposed” [no partitions]. In a courtyard, any orientation is permitted (Beit Yosef). Still, it appears to me [nireh li] that a city surrounded by a wall is not called “partitions” for this purpose in Beit Yosef; the Levush wrote that one partition toward the west suffices. Because the Shulchan Aruch says “partitions” (plural), it implies two are required—for some hold east is also forbidden, as the wind opposite west [ruach shekeged ma’arav] (Beit Yosef). In Bava Batra ch. 1 they say the east is called “air of G-d” [avir YaH]. Yet according to the Rema’s gloss that urinating [in any direction] is permitted, we are not concerned about exposure before him while relieving, and therefore one partition behind him suffices.

“Toward the south.” Specifically: his face to the south and his back to the north—but not the reverse (Heichal Hakodesh)—see there for the reason.
