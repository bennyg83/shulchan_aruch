[ג] “That it has no partitions,” etc. If it does have partitions, even one wall toward the west suffices because of the Shechinah. Nachalat Tzvi wrote: in a city that lies directly opposite Jerusalem to its north or south, north–south [relieving] is forbidden and east–west permitted—end of quotation. It implies that even a city surrounded by a wall is not counted as a “partition” for this rule.
[ד] “Between east and west,” etc. Rashi: feet toward east and head toward west—or the reverse—but [the bed’s width lies] between north and south. The Bach and Taz cite the Zohar, Parashat Bamidbar: “between north and south” means head east and feet west—end of quotation. Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah wrote that therefore people are not careful about this nowadays at all—end of quotation. Olas Tamid wrote: “some do this, some do that”—end of quotation; see Tosafos Shabbat 61a s.v. dav… I found in Responsa Menachem Azariah siman 3 that early authorities explained the bed’s width should run north–south so that conception resembles the formation of Adam; the reason is not the Shechinah—on the contrary, one should think holy thoughts at relations. Moreover, even in a privy the prohibition applies only where there is no fence; relations are permitted only where there is a fence and the whole body is covered. For practice he concluded the head of the bed should be west and the northwest corner highest—end of quotation. For Rashi and the plain Shulchan Aruch his head is north or south—so wrote Bach explicitly. In Lechem Chamudos I found he requires the head specifically toward north—hence he mentions north before south even though south is to one’s right—end of quotation. This needs reconciliation with Tehillim 89: “North and right You have created”—where north is listed first. Rabbenu Chananel explained: in the Gemara “north” is called cold and “south” heat—meaning one should use his bed about midnight, when the body has cooled from the food’s heat and temperament is calm: one born from a cold drop is a fool; from a hot drop, wrathful; from an average drop, wise and sociable—“You have given your maidservant men’s offspring.” The Levush wrote that even where there are partitions one must be strict—unlike relieving above, for it is more disgraceful to stretch one’s legs toward the Shechinah—and the Bach answered that at relations one must sanctify oneself more.
