(א) Seif 5, "in an open place..." Yerushalmi there, and only where there is no wall:

(ב) "He shall orient..." Like R' Akiva, for Rabbah acted accordingly:

(ג) "They shall be..." Yerushalmi there teaches: one who urinates—this one turns his face to the north; one who shields his legs—this one turns his face to the south. R' Yosi says: from Scopus inward. R' Eliezer permits everywhere, provided there is no wall. It teaches: one who shields his legs shall not turn his face eastward and his back westward, but to the sides. R' Yehuda says: at the time of the Temple. R' Yosi says: from Scopus inward, etc. Beit Yosef wrote, and the poskim omitted this because since in our Gemara the anonymous teaching is stated, it implies that everywhere is permitted—and this is what "they shall be" means, etc. Magen Avraham. But the main point is that there the case is in the Galilee, and the Yerushalmi follows his view that one is concerned only when shielding behind him, as stated in the baraita after it—and his back to the west—and there the case is in Judea, and R' Yehuda and R' Akiva disagree, and we do not hold this baraita at all. So too there in Tosefta Megillah, which states below: one who shields his legs—his face toward the people; one who urinates—textual error; it should read toward the Sanctuary. R' Yosi refers to both of them, for the first tanna is the first tanna of our Gemara, who forbids only toward the Sanctuary; but in the Tosefta he is concerned only for his back. So too in the Yerushalmi he said he shall not turn his face... and his back westward; but in our Gemara he is concerned also for his front because of the circumcision two tefachim:

(ד) "And to urinate..." etc. So Beit Yosef infers from the Yerushalmi there, which teaches: one who shields his legs shall not turn his face eastward, etc., from what it says "one who shields" etc. But this is not so, for the baraita above there implies that even for one who urinates it is forbidden; and also what he infers from the Rambam's words in chapter 7, "one who urinates" etc.—the reading in Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch is "he shall sit and his face toward the Sanctuary" or remove, etc. And there is no proof from this that east refers to the Temple, nor is the reading correct as stated above; the main point is that westward is forbidden because the Shechinah is in the west, and turning is forbidden for both directions because of the two exposures:
