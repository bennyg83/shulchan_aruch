(7) Exposed — but in a courtyard, in any manner it is permitted; so Beit Yosef and Magen Avraham. But Taz agreed with Mahari Abuhav that in a courtyard too it is forbidden in the middle of the courtyard between east and west, for is he not exposed there unless he draws near to one side of the walls; so too Drisha and other Acharonim.

(8) That has no partitions — but if it has partitions, even only one partition on the west side, he sits at the side of the partition close to it [that is, at least within four amot], with his back to the west toward the partition, and relieves himself there. Similarly if the wall is on the east, he sits close to it with his back to the east. Some are stringent in this because his exposure before him is toward the west and the Shechinah is in the west; where possible it is good to be stringent. Peri Megadim wrote there is no minimum height for the partition — only wherever his exposure is not uncovered is it permitted.

(9) Or the reverse — and some say not the reverse; it is good to be careful about this.

(10) Between east, etc. — because the Shechinah dwells in the west. Even if he turns his face to the west it is forbidden, for the east, which is opposite the west, also has holiness.
