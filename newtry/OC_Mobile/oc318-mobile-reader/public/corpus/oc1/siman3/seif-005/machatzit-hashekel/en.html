(s.k. 5) “Exposed,” but in a courtyard: once there are partitions—even far from where one relieves—it is not called “exposed.” Still, it appears that a [whole] city [wall alone] is too distant to count as those partitions.

Toward the west—because the reason is honor toward the Shechinah, whose primary [“dwelling”] is toward the west.

…which is the “east wind” opposite the west—Beit Yosef there. Rambam: it is forbidden to defecate or sleep between east and west because the Sanctuary is in the west; therefore one should not turn his front or back toward east or west, since that aligns with the west [axis toward the Sanctuary], etc.

Darkhei Moshe also wrote: for us, who live west of Eretz Yisrael, east is forbidden because it faces the Sanctuary; in Bava Batra 25a “east” is called [by a certain name], implying the Shechinah’s primary [axis] is eastward—see there.
