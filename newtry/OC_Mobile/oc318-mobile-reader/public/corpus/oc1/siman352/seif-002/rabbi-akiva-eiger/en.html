seif 2: forbidden to roll it back to him. I ask: behold even if it were not bound in his hand, nevertheless as the scroll is now — part of it is above 19 — if he rolls it back there is only shevut since part is bound in a makom patur like the case in first chapter of Shabbos s.v. in a basket that it is impossible the reed peel will not rise above 19; and one cannot say that if it falls from his hand presumably all of the scroll will fall below and everything will be below 10 — for if so when the wall is not sloped why is it permitted when it did not rest — behold it will all fall to the ground and be placed; there is no answer except that we judge only that the scroll is now in the air and they decreed only when bound in hand lest it not be in hand; if so it is difficult; and I saw in Rashba chapter 8 Shabbos 99 on what they say there: a post in reshus harabbim 10 high and 4 wide, threw and rested on top — he is liable; he asked: behold the object placed on it — part is in the air above 10; to permit like in the basket; and he wrote there to distinguish: specifically in the basket the vessel itself consumes the entire domain and also it is bound above 10; but when the body of the vessel is not so high as to consume the entire domain, binding does not help from makom patur — see there; and if so it is fine here; and although Rashba's question there does not apply only to his approach and Ramban's that on a post not enclosed by partitions there is no air rising to the sky, but according to Rashi and Tosafos there, in the sugya it appears they hold that also on a post we say it rises to the sky — Rashba's question does not apply; nevertheless it may be said they distinguish like Rashba's distinctions; nevertheless still insufficient; for if so when the wall is not sloped and the scroll rolled until it reached the ground — to permit, behold the scroll consumes the entire domain, and it is bound above 10 — he should not be liable even if not bound in hand; strained to say we judge only the scroll as it is now in the air and we do not follow the end that it will fall to the ground; nevertheless we judge the head of the scroll that above as if rolled and bent and the lower head reached — requires study:
