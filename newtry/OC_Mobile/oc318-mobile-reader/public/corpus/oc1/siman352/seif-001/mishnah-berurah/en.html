(א) One who reads in a scroll — all their books were made in a scroll like our Torah scroll:
(ב) on the threshold — that is, even if it were reshus hayachid such as when it is 10 tefachim high and 4 wide and reshus harabbim passes before it as below — one might think we should decree not to be lenient to roll the scroll back because perhaps all of it fell from his hand and he will also come to bring it there where there is chatat liability; it teaches that we do not decree here because of disgrace to sacred writings; and although it is explained in seif 2 where one stands at the edge of the roof and reshus harabbim passes before it and one end of the scroll rolled from his hand to the ground — it is forbidden to roll it back because of this decree — here it discusses a threshold that many tread on as explained in the gloss, and there is greater disgrace to sacred writings; therefore they were lenient:
(ג) and one end of the scroll rolled etc. — that is, and it rested on the ground; for if removed from the ground even slightly it is not considered at all like falling and there is no decree because of falling:
(ד) even if it rolled etc. — it is also permitted to roll the scroll back to him and we are not concerned lest he come to be lenient to bring the 4 amot, even where all of it fell from his hand where there is a d'oraisa prohibition:
(ה) even reshus harabbim — and specifically where one side is bound in his hand; for if the other end also fell to the ground — even where it fell to karmelit — it is likewise forbidden to bring it to him, and they did not permit this because of disgrace to sacred writings [Bach and Magen Avraham]:
(ו) in reshus hayachid — it should read reshus hayachid:
(ז) because of disgrace — for from Torah law if he threw an object to reshus harabbim and the other end is in his hand it is permitted to bring it to him [for there is no placing since the other end is in his hand and he can bring it to him; if so when he pulls and raises it from the ground it is not considered uprooting]; only the rabbis decreed not to be lenient even where it fell near him, and here because of disgrace they permitted:
(ח) because many tread — and there is greater disgrace:
(ט) but in other etc. — that is, also when one end remained in his hand it is forbidden to bring it to him:
(י) only to karmelit — and the reason: also in this we decree lest it fall entirely to the ground and nevertheless he will bring it to him; and know there are several later authorities lenient in this; nevertheless it appears that in our reshus harabbim one should not be lenient, for several poskim hold that even among us there is d'oraisa reshus harabbim — as above siman 345 seif 7:
