(ב) In the Tur — Taz, one who examines well in the Tur and Beit Yosef here and in siman 480 in Taz there will understand that Taz according to his reasoning there holds like the Tur: on the fourth cup he says Hallel and says yehalelucha until ata Adonai and afterward nishmas, yishtabach, and he concludes "Who chooses song and melody"; therefore he was compelled to explain the words of the Tur here that the meaning is: Rav Saadiah Gaon said that one makes a fifth cup, and on the fourth cup he says Hallel without any blessing at all, and he says Nishmas, yehalelucha, yishtabach, and concludes "Who chooses song and melody"; and such is the meaning of Taz, that certainly there is no concluding blessing on Hallel — meaning, not as Beit Yosef wrote that he concludes "the King Who is extolled with praises," but rather "Who chooses song and melody"; and nevertheless it is somewhat applicable also to Hallel afterward; and therefore the disciples came and hold that the final blessing is required on every cup — so too the blessing "Who chooses song and melody" should not refer to the Hallel before it; for this reason etc. And what Taz wrote for us, "if he erred" etc., is somewhat difficult, for it appears that this is as the Mechaber wrote in siman 480: to say Hallel until yehalelucha and Nishmas, and say yishtabach without concluding, and afterward yehalelucha and conclude "the King Who is extolled with praises" — and how did he write afterward "if he erred" etc., "he shall say the Great Hallel until the concluding blessing and no more"? Behold he already said the Great Hallel! Rather thus it should have been read: if he erred and finished the blessing of yishtabach, he should not again finish saying yehalelucha, but he shall say yehalelucha until ata Adonai; and in Magen Avraham in siman 480 he explains this at length, see there.
