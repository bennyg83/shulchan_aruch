And it appears from the words of the Tur that the Geonim disagree only whether the fifth cup needs a concluding blessing on its own or must include it in the concluding blessing of the fourth cup; but without any concluding blessing at all, merely by reciting the verses of the Great Hallel, it is not sufficient to permit drinking the fifth cup. And according to this, this ruling of the gloss applies only specifically if he has not yet finished the concluding blessing of "the King Who is extolled with praises" — for once he has finished, it is again impossible to drink the fifth cup, for by reciting hodu alone it is not permitted to drink and to conclude with a blessing, as the Tur already concludes that it is forbidden since there is a dispute among the great authorities on this. And it does not appear so, for behold they labored to find this leniency for one who is delicate and thirsty to drink, and this is very common after he has already finished the entire order and has already ceased drinking. And so from the plain meaning of the words of the Rambam and Rabbeinu Yeruham it also appears explicitly that by reciting the verses of the Great Hallel alone, without any concluding blessing, it is permitted to drink the fifth cup. And I found that the Gra also explains as we say in the intention of the Shulchan Aruch, who wrote that one must say the Great Hallel and Nishmas until the concluding blessing, see there — except that he added a stringency here, that all this is when he did not say the Great Hallel on the fourth cup; but if he said the Great Hallel on the fourth cup as is our custom, we do not permit him to drink the fifth cup by reciting Hallel. And the reason appears: since there is no renewal or additional praise here, for behold he already said these verses once; and so the plain meaning of Magen Avraham implies to be stringent on this. However I found in Peri Megadim that it does not seem right to him to be stringent on this, see there, that he aligns the words of Magen Avraham to a different intention. And see in Hagahos Maimoni in the name of Rav Amram, and it appears there too that if he has a fifth cup he should not say the Great Hallel on the fourth cup, and per the plain meaning of Magen Avraham, see there. Afterward I found in Shiltei HaGiborim who wrote that from the words of the Rif and the Rambam it appears that without any concluding blessing at all it is permitted to drink the fifth cup by reciting Hallel; but from the words of the Tur it does not appear so, see there — and this is exactly as we say, except that it appears from his words that he had a different version in the Tur in the words of Rav Shalom and in the matter of R' Akiva, see there, and this requires further study.
