In the Tur he brings the words of Rav Hai regarding a fifth cup: One who wishes to drink a fifth cup should say Hallel HaGadol over it and leave "Yehalelukha" to say afterward. Meaning, over the fourth cup he says Hallel and leaves "Yehalelukha" for the fifth cup, over which he says Hallel HaGadol, and there he concludes "Yehalelukha." But now the students came and said that every cup requires a blessing afterward just as before it, so he says "Yehalelukha" after Hallel and concludes "King praised with praises," drinks the fifth cup, says Hallel HaGadol over it, and concludes with Nishmat Kol Chai until "Who chooses songs." The Beit Yosef discussed this at length. It appears to me that this is what it means: certainly there is no conclusion over Hallel. Initially, the conclusion "Who chooses songs" related also to the Hallel said over the fourth cup, just as the final blessing over the vine relates also to the third cup. So too here with this conclusion. But according to the students, who said that "Al HaGefen" is required for each cup, the conclusion "Who chooses" relates only to Hallel HaGadol of the fifth cup and not to the Hallel of the fourth cup; according to this, Hallel has no conclusion. Therefore they concluded over the fourth cup with "King praised," and over the fifth with "Who chooses songs." He answered that this is not proper, for the Rabbis did not establish two blessings, meaning two conclusions, but only one of them. If so, according to our practice of only four cups, where we conclude in "Yehalelukha" with "King praised," if someone erred and after Yishtabach concluded "Who chooses," he should not afterward conclude after Nishmat with "King praised," for even with two cups there would be only one conclusion, all the more so with one cup. Rather, he should say Hallel HaGadol until the conclusion and no more. This appears simple to my humble mind.
