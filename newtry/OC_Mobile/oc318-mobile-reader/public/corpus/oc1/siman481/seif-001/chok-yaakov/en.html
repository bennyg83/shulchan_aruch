One may not drink wine. The Raavad in Tamim De'im, in his critiques of the Baal HaMaor on tractate Pesachim, siman 30, wrote the reason that they do not drink wine after the four cups: the custom is for the sake of the poor, for whom they allocate four cups from the communal soup kitchen for two nights, so that he not drink in one night the wine that was given to him for two nights; end quote. However, in the Tur he wrote that the primary reason is so that one not become intoxicated, because a person is obligated to engage all night on Pesach night in the Exodus from Egypt until sleep overtakes him, and not because of nullifying the taste of matzah; and his words are primary, and so it is proven in the Gemara, and it is also proven from the fact that they drink two cups and also a fifth cup for one who practices thus. And so Shiltei Gibborim elaborates at length in se'if 7 to prove this, and that such is the view of many poskim. And according to this reason it would be permitted to drink after sleep has already overtaken him, and likewise it is permitted to drink all beverages that do not intoxicate; and this is the main ruling, and so ruled Nachalas Tzvi; and some of the later authorities are stringent in the matter. And in Darkei Moshe he wrote that they are accustomed to permit drinking lakritz in the vernacular, and even Apfel-trank is permitted; end quote. And Magen Avraham wrote that that trank which they make in Ashkenaz, whereby they crush apples in the press and it has a strong taste, one should forbid; and ginger water, which they call Ingber-Wasser, one should permit.
