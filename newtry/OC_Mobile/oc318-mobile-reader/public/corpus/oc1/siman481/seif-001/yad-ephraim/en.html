In Taz at the beginning of the siman — and it appears to me that the meaning is etc.; it is possible that it should read that the meaning is Rabbeinu Hai, that there is no concluding blessing etc., for the word "certainly" is not understood; and therefore it appears to me that a printing error fell, and it should read Rabbeinu Hai — and this is a close printing error — and the meaning is that the questioner there, in the responsum of Rabbeinu Hai said that there is no concluding blessing on Hallel — meaning that one does not need a concluding blessing on Hallel by itself; rather initially his concluding blessing was "Who chooses" etc., because Taz holds per the opinion of the Tur that they conclude with "Who chooses," see siman 480. And what Rabbeinu Hai wrote, "he shall leave yehalelucha and say it afterward" — meaning that he shall say yehalelucha until the concluding blessing, and afterward begin yishtabach and conclude "Who chooses"; the reason that he leaves yehalelucha on the fourth cup is in order to combine it with the Great Hallel that he says on the fifth cup, and there will be great praise; or it is possible that the meaning is that he shall say yehalelucha after drinking the fifth cup, as for the disciples who drink first the fifth cup and afterward say Hallel etc., see in Beit Yosef (but in Bach he wrote the correct text: and they drink the fourth cup and pour the fifth cup and say upon it etc., see there). And what he wrote, "if so, why do we practice" etc. — his words require further study; and it is possible that it is a printing error and it should read: if one erred and concluded after Hallel yishtabach with "Who chooses" etc., and the meaning is that after the Egyptian Hallel he said yishtabach with its concluding blessing — he should not again say the Great Hallel and Nishmas with the concluding blessing of yehalelucha, but he shall say the Great Hallel and yehalelucha until the concluding blessing; and according to what I thought, that it is not proper for the ending to be without a concluding blessing, therefore he learned this from his explanation in the words of Rabbeinu Hai that he shall say yehalelucha afterward — and this is without a concluding blessing, for the concluding blessing is in "Who chooses"; but according to other explanations that Beit Yosef and Bach wrote, even though it is also proven that Rabbeinu Hai agreed that one should not conclude two blessings, nevertheless the ending must be in a blessing — meaning that he shall say yehalelucha with the ending of the blessing; which is not so according to what Taz wrote here — the explanation of the words of Rabbeinu Hai proves that even if he finishes without a concluding blessing, we have no concern; or it is possible that a printing error fell at the end of the words of Taz and it should read: rather he shall say yehalelucha until the concluding blessing and no more — except that the language he wrote, "he should not again finish saying Nishmas" etc., requires further study, for there is no need to say "after Nishmas," for behold it deals with after yishtabach, and automatically it is after Nishmas; and one can say that also after the concluding blessing of yishtabach, after Nishmas, is called "after," and he meant that he should not say yehalelucha with the concluding blessing of "the King Who is extolled with praises" — and this requires further study.
