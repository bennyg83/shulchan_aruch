In the Taz. At the beginning of the siman, and it appears to me regarding "this is what he meant," etc.: it is possible that one must read "this is what Rav Hai meant, that there is no closing formula," etc., for the word "dudai" is not understandable; and therefore it appears to me that a scribal error fell in, and one must read "Rav Hai," and it is a near scribal error; and that is to say, that the questioner there in the responsum of Rav Hai meant that there is no closing formula on Hallel, meaning that one need not recite a closing formula on Hallel separately; rather, initially his closing was HaBocher, etc., because the Taz holds according to the view of the Tur that they conclude with HaBocher; see siman 486. And what Rav Hai wrote, "he should leave Yehallelucha and say after it," means that he should say Yehallelucha until the closing formula, and afterwards begin Yishtabach and conclude with HaBocher. The reason he leaves Yehallelucha for the fourth cup is in order to join it to Hallel HaGadol which he says over the fifth cup, so that there will be a great praise. Or it is possible that he means that he should say Yehallelucha after drinking the fifth cup, as [he writes] for the students: he drinks the fifth cup first and afterwards says Hallel, etc.; see Beis Yosef. (But in the Bach he wrote that the correct text is: and they drink the fourth cup and pour the fifth cup and say upon it, etc.; see there.) And what he wrote, "and if so, why regarding our practice," etc.: his words require further study; and it is possible that it is a scribal error and one must read: "if someone erred and after Hallel concluded Yishtabach with HaBocher," etc.; and that is to say, that after Egyptian Hallel he said Yishtabach with its closing formula: he should no longer say Hallel HaGadol and Nishmas with the closing of Yehallelucha; rather he should say Hallel HaGadol and Yehallelucha until the closing. And according to [the idea that] one might have thought it is not proper that the conclusion be without a closing formula, therefore he learned this from his interpretation of the words of Rabbeinu Hai: that he should say Yehallelucha after it, and that is without a closing formula, for the closing is with HaBocher. For according to the other interpretations that Beis Yosef and Bach wrote, even though it is also proven that Rav Hai agreed that one should not conclude two blessings, nevertheless the conclusion must be with a blessing, namely that he should say Yehallelucha with the conclusion of the blessing; which is not so according to what the Taz wrote here as the interpretation of Rav Hai's words: it is proven that even when he concludes without a closing formula, we are not concerned. Or it is possible that a scribal error fell at the end of the Taz's words, and one must read: "rather he should say Yehallelucha until the closing, and no more"; except that the language he wrote, "he should no longer conclude, having said Nishmas," etc., requires further study: why did he need to say "after Nishmas," for he is dealing with after Yishtabach, and automatically that is after Nishmas. And one may say that even after the closing of Yishtabach he calls it "after Nishmas," and he meant to say that he should not say Yehallelucha with the closing "Melech Mehulal"; and this requires further study.
