He makes havdalah in the prayer. And the Magen Avraham wrote, and this is his language: One who begins to eat on Motza'ei Pesach while it is still day, and continues until night, and has not yet prayed and made havdalah: this requires further study whether he is permitted to eat chametz; for at first glance it appears that it is permitted, since once it has grown dark, night is weekday for all matters, etc.; and therefore once it has grown dark, it is permitted to eat chametz; end quote. And so it appears to me as the main ruling, that the prohibition of chametz does not depend at all on havdalah; and so there is some proof from the fact that they are lenient in several matters on the last day of Pesach regarding the prohibition of chametz, since it is only a doubt as to the day (sefeika d'yoma), and it was not mentioned by any of the poskim to be stringent for the reason that he has not made havdalah, [as though] if so the prohibition of chametz of the day is still upon him; rather, certainly there is no concern regarding the prohibition of chametz with respect to havdalah.
