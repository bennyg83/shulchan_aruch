He must observe the fast, etc. This refers to a case in which he was in the place where they were fasting at the time the fast was accepted, for otherwise the decree of his community certainly did not take effect upon him, since he is not there on the day of the fast. Taz, ibid.; Eliyah Rabbah, os 1. This refers to a case in which they decreed a fast because of a calamity that it should not come, or as an act of repentance. In such a case, it is relevant whether he was there when the fast was accepted or not. However, regarding a fast observed by a particular community because of a decree that had occurred in the past, such as the 20th of Sivan in Poland and the like, no acceptance is required at all, for it is considered a communal fast. He then has the law of one who travels from one place to another, and they impose upon him the stringencies of the place from which he departed if he intends to return.
