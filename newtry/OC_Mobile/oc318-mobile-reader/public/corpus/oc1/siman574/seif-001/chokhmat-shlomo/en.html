Seif 4: It is a mitzvah to starve oneself in a year of famine, and it is forbidden to use one's bed for marital relations, etc. We asked the community of Rimlov why we are not accustomed to forbid marital relations in years of famine; the questioner wrote that perhaps we rely on what Peri Megadim wrote in the name of Eshel Avraham and Shach — I answered that this is not so, for we decree that even one who is not in such straits, if he intends for the sake of the mitzvah, he still does not separate; in my opinion it is among sins people trample with their heels, since there are many sides on which it is permitted, such as on the night of immersion or for childless couples, and since there are lenient paths it was permitted entirely. Regarding what R' Moshe asked how many years count as famine — it seemed to me that this depends on the ruling how one may leave Eretz Yisrael for chutz laAretz, for keeping the mitzvah of onah is a mitzvah and dwelling in Eretz Yisrael is a mitzvah. Therefore for one who holds that only if he cannot buy food at all may he leave, the same here one need not separate only if he cannot buy at all; but for one who holds that even if there were four se'ah for a sela he may leave, the same here he must separate. See Bava Batra on the dispute in this, and if you say one need not separate only when he cannot buy at all — Tosafot's question why Levi used his bed can be answered that Yosef estimated that by natural course the grain would not suffice for the whole world, especially the seven famine years, but Yosef in his righteousness did not trust that blessing would come through his hands; therefore there would be no grain in the world, so he separated. But Yaakov and his sons saw that there was grain in Egypt and thought there was enough for the world naturally, though expensive — therefore Levi used his bed, and it fits well; examine. One may also say that even though Yosef knew there would be enough anyway, this is a miraculous path, for all grain rotted except his, and it is forbidden to benefit from miracles except for eating, since it is pikuach nefesh, and this prohibition does not override Torah prohibitions, all of which are set aside for pikuach nefesh; but marital relations are not pikuach nefesh, and if he engages he benefits from a miracle — therefore Yosef separated. But Yaakov's sons did not know if it was miraculous, and therefore they used their beds since grain was available — and it fits. What Shulchan Aruch wrote, “It is a mitzvah to starve oneself,” etc., “and forbidden to use one's bed,” etc. — see Magen Avraham where he disagrees with Beit Yosef and wrote we do not hold like R' Yochanan that a woman conceives only near her immersion, etc.; but see Maggid Mishneh chapter 15 of Laws of Avel and Sotah, for from there it is proven we hold like R' Yochanan that a woman conceives only near her immersion; see my composition on Niddah 10b, and examine.
