And it is forbidden to use one's bed, etc. Tosafot chapter 1 of Taanit challenged: yet Yokheved was born between the walls, and that time was famine, and therefore they used their beds in famine years. One may answer that for all it was not forbidden except for one who wishes to conduct himself with piety, and Yosef did not engage, but others engaged, end of their words. It is difficult: if so, from where do we learn the prohibition from Yosef — perhaps Yosef acted as piety and not because of prohibition? And when Yitzchak engaged in famine years, as Rashi explained on “Yitzchak jested with Rivkah his wife,” he forced to answer that there the place where he stood there was no famine. The Rambam in parashat Miketz challenged Tosafot's question from Yokheved from his own reasoning and did not mention Tosafot; he answered that since to fulfill peru urevu he needs a daughter, childless couples are permitted, and Levi did not yet have a daughter — therefore it was permitted. Beit Yosef answered on this: if so, Yosef too was permitted to engage since he had no daughter — if so he should have counted her in the seventy souls, end of his words. I say that per the Rambam's answer Beit Yosef's question is good, but in the essence it seems we do not depend on fulfilling peru urevu — for they used the wording childless couples, implying he has no children at all, otherwise it should have said “one who did not fulfill peru urevu is permitted”; rather certainly they permitted only one completely childless who goes barren — a great pain. Tosafot's words are primary, and it seems they can be resolved: certainly before matan Torah everything was permitted except the seven Noahide laws; only Yosef practiced piety from his own reasoning. They repeated that we see Scripture noted this trait in our Torah — we learn from Yosef that from matan Torah onward this matter was renewed like other mitzvot. With this, what the Mizrachi wrote from Yitzchak who engaged in famine years, as Rashi on “Yitzchak jested,” is also resolved; he forced to answer that there the place where he stood there was no famine — the Rav was not precise, for what is written that where Yitzchak was there was no famine is not so, for it is written “Yitzchak sowed in that year” and Rashi explained it was a hard year — we learn that in that region too it was hard. What he challenged from Elimelech is very puzzling, for certainly even in the famine land itself one may marry a wife, for even if already married one may engage for childless couples, kal vachomer to marry when he has no wife. According to Leket Kemach from Yitzchak, this prohibition was renewed only from matan Torah onward, and Beit Yosef also wrote that before matan Torah it was permitted; that they bring from Yosef is merely an asmakhta, and he proved this — what proof from Yosef, behold he was childless? Rather certainly it is merely asmakhta; and in my humble opinion everything is settled: one must say that the permission for childless couples does not involve piety to cancel it, since fulfilling it involves a great mitzvah.
