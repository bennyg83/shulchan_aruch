Se'if 4. It is a mitzvah for a person to starve himself in a year of famine, and it is forbidden to engage in marital relations therein, etc. Marginal note: I was asked from the holy community of Hrimelov why we do not customarily forbid marital relations in years of famine; and the questioner wrote that it is possible that we rely on what the Peri Megadim wrote in the name of the Eliyah Rabbah; and I answered that this is not so, for we decree that even one who is not capable of intending for the sake of the mitzvah also does not separate; and in my opinion it is among the sins that a person treads with his heels, for since there are several grounds by which it is permitted, for example on the night of immersion, or for those without children, and since there are lenient paths, therefore it became entirely permitted. And regarding what His Honor asked, how much is considered famine: it appeared to me that this depends on the law of in what manner it is permitted to leave Eretz Yisrael for outside the Land; for fulfilling onah is also a mitzvah, and dwelling in Eretz Yisrael is a mitzvah; therefore, according to the one who holds that specifically if he finds nothing at all to buy, then it is permitted to leave, the same applies, that he need not separate except when he finds nothing at all to buy. But according to the one who holds that even if there were four se'ah for a sela it is permitted to leave, the same applies, that he must separate; and see in Bava Basra the dispute about this. And if we say that one need separate only when he finds nothing at all to buy, one can resolve the difficulty of the Tosafos: why did Levi engage in marital relations? For one can say that Yosef estimated that according to the natural course the grain would not suffice for the whole world, especially for the seven years of famine; only that in truth blessing was sent into his hands; but Yosef in his righteousness did not trust that blessing would be sent into his hands, and if so there would be no grain at all in the world; therefore he separated. But Yaakov and his sons saw that there was grain in Egypt, and they held that there was grain there sufficient for the whole world by the natural course, even though it was expensive; therefore Levi engaged in marital relations; and it works out well; and examine this carefully. And one can say further: even though Yosef knew that there would be enough, nonetheless this was by way of a miracle, for behold all the grain spoiled and only his remained; and it is forbidden to benefit from miraculous acts; except that for eating, since it is pikuach nefesh, this prohibition is no stronger than all the prohibitions of the Torah, which are all set aside before pikuach nefesh; but marital relations, which is not [averting] pikuach nefesh through a miraculous act, is not pikuach nefesh, and if he has relations he would be benefiting from miraculous acts; therefore Yosef separated from marital relations. But the sons of Yaakov did not know whether it was a miraculous act, and therefore they engaged in marital relations, since [grain] was available to buy; and it works out well; examine carefully. And regarding what is written in the Shulchan Aruch, that it is a mitzvah for a person to starve himself, etc., and it is forbidden to engage in marital relations therein, etc.: see in the Magen Avraham what he disputes against the Beis Yosef, and wrote that we do not hold like R' Yochanan that a woman does not conceive except close to her immersion, etc. But see the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 15 of Hilchos Issurei Biah, and in Sotah folio 27; from there it is proven that we do hold like Rabbi Yochanan in this, that a woman does not conceive except close to her immersion; and see my composition Mei Niddah on tractate Niddah, folio 10b; and examine carefully.
