In the Shulchan Aruch: In a year of famine. See above, siman 240, that the same applies to other calamities that are like famine; and see Birkei Yosef in the name of Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah, who wrote that nowadays we rely on what the Tosafos wrote, that it is only a measure of piety, and Levi did not observe this; and even if you say that we do observe a prohibition, it suffices in a year of famine, etc.; and see there that my father, my master, of blessed memory, in a responsum was uncertain about his words, etc. And see Responsa Beis Yehudah, siman 80, which was cited above in siman 240. But in the Sefer Birkei Yosef they wrote in the name of his father the Rav, of blessed memory, who wrote in a responsum that when foodstuffs are available, only that they are at a very high price, this is not famine, and one need not refrain from marital relations; and the sages of Jerusalem (may it be rebuilt and reestablished speedily) agreed with him; and it implies that even at double the price there is no [prohibition] in it. And see Divrei David of the Gaon, the author of the Taz, on Parashas Mikeitz, who wrote that specifically marital relations for mere pleasure is forbidden, but for perpetuating the species, or where his inclination overwhelms him and there is concern of wasting seed, there is no prohibition; and this matter is included within those without children, except that he stated a clear-cut case, end quote. And the Eliyah Rabbah cited it, and from this he wondered at what the Taz wrote here, that even one who has not fulfilled peru u'revu is forbidden, which is not so, as we have written; and so wrote the Sefer Tzidah LaDerech; see there. And see Birkei Yosef in the name of his father the Rav, of blessed memory, that one who fulfilled peru u'revu from his first wife, but from the second he did not merit to build [a family], and he wants a wife as a staff for his hand, etc., is not included among those without children, and it is forbidden; and see there. And it requires somewhat further study, for the mitzvah of onah is according to the Torah; and even though they forbade [relations] in years of famine, that is because from the outset she reasoned and accepted [marriage] on that understanding, that at a time of prohibition he would separate from her; which is not so [here], for in any case it is a doubt whether she accepted upon herself with that intent; and especially according to what we wrote above in the name of Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah, one should not be so stringent. Therefore it appears that also on the night of immersion, concerning which the Magen Avraham is stringent, and the Eliyah Rabbah who rules to be lenient in years of famine, we do not dismiss him; and all the more so regarding other calamities, as is implied from Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah above. And see Responsa Shevus Yaakov, volume 3, and siman 74, who also wrote to dispute the Taz from Rashi's commentary; and he also brought from Eruvin folio 28 regarding gudgedaniyos, etc.; see there; and he wrote that since it is from the measures of piety, possibly in a case of concern that if he does not have relations he will come to a matter of sin, whether he or his wife, if they yearn for it, it is possible that it is permitted to have relations; see there.
This requires some study, for the mitzvah of onah is biblical. Although they prohibited relations in years of famine, that is because from the outset she understood and accepted on that basis, that at a time of prohibition he would separate from her. Here, however, at the very least it is doubtful whether she accepted upon herself such a condition. Especially according to what I wrote above in the name of Kenesset HaGedolah, one should not be so stringent. Therefore it appears that even on the night of immersion, where the Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah are stringent, a decisor who rules leniently in years of famine should not be rebuked, and all the more so in other calamities, as is implied by the Kenesset HaGedolah cited above. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, vol. 3, siman 30, who also challenged the Taz from Rashi, and also brought proof from Eruvin 28 regarding gudgudaniyot, etc.; see there. He wrote that since this is a matter of pious practice, perhaps where there is concern that if he does not have relations he or his wife may come to sin, if they desire it, perhaps relations are permitted; see there.
