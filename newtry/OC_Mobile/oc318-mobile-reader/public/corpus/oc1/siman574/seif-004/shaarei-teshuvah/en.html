In Shulchan Aruch: In a year of famine. See above, siman 240, that the same applies to other calamities that are like famine. See Birkei Yosef in the name of Kenesset HaGedolah, who wrote that nowadays we rely on Tosafot's statement that this is only a pious practice, and Levi did not observe it; and even if one says that we practice a prohibition, it is enough in a year of famine, etc. See there, that my father, my teacher z"l, in a responsum expressed reservations about his words, etc. See Responsa Beit Yehudah, siman 40, cited above in siman 240. But in Birkei Yosef he wrote in the name of his father the Rav z"l, who wrote in a responsum that when food is available but very expensive, this is not a famine and one need not refrain from marital relations, and the sages of Jerusalem, may it be rebuilt and established, agreed with him. It implies that even if it is twice as expensive there is no concern. See Divrei David by the author of Taz, Parashat Miketz, who wrote that specifically relations for mere pleasure are prohibited, but for preservation of the species, or where his inclination is strong and there is concern for wasting seed, there is no prohibition; this is included in childless people, but the text stated a fixed example. Eliyah Rabbah cites this, and from this objected to what the Taz wrote here, that even one who has not fulfilled procreation is prohibited; this is incorrect, as I wrote. So too wrote Tzeidah LaDerekh; see there. See Birkei Yosef in the name of his father the Rav z"l, that one who fulfilled procreation with his first wife but did not merit children from the second, and she wants a support for her old age, etc., is not included among the childless and is prohibited; see there.
This requires some study, for the mitzvah of onah is biblical. Although they prohibited relations in years of famine, that is because from the outset she understood and accepted on that basis, that at a time of prohibition he would separate from her. Here, however, at the very least it is doubtful whether she accepted upon herself such a condition. Especially according to what I wrote above in the name of Kenesset HaGedolah, one should not be so stringent. Therefore it appears that even on the night of immersion, where the Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah are stringent, a decisor who rules leniently in years of famine should not be rebuked, and all the more so in other calamities, as is implied by the Kenesset HaGedolah cited above. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, vol. 3, siman 30, who also challenged the Taz from Rashi, and also brought proof from Eruvin 28 regarding gudgudaniyot, etc.; see there. He wrote that since this is a matter of pious practice, perhaps where there is concern that if he does not have relations he or his wife may come to sin, if they desire it, perhaps relations are permitted; see there.
