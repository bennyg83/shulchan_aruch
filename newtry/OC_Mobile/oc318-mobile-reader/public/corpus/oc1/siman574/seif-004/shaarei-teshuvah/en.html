In Shulchan Aruch, “In a year of famine.” See above siman 240 that the same applies to other troubles like famine; see Rashi in the name of Sefer Chasidim that nowadays we rely on what Tosafot wrote that it is only a measure of piety and Levi did not practice this, and even if you say we forbid in famine years, etc., see there that Abba Mari z"l in responsa wavered on his words, etc.; see responsa Beit Yehudah siman 40 brought above siman 240. But in Beit Yosef they wrote in the name of his father the Rav z"l in responsa that when provisions exist but are very expensive, this is not famine and one need not refrain from marital relations; the sages of Jerusalem agreed — see. It implies even at double price it is unnecessary; see the words of David the Gaon, author of Taz, parashat Miketz, that only relations for pleasure alone are forbidden, but for procreation or if his urge is strong and there is concern for wasting seed there is no prohibition — this is included in childless couples, except that a clear matter is stated, end of his words. They brought this in Eshel Avraham, and from this he wondered on what Taz wrote here that even if he fulfilled peru urevu it is forbidden — it does not exist, as written; so too in Sefer Tzidah laDerech, see there. See in Beit Yosef in the name of his father the Rav z"l that one who fulfilled peru urevu with his first wife but from the second did not merit children and wants a younger wife for childbirth, etc., is not in the category of childless couples and it is forbidden — see there. It is somewhat difficult, for the mitzvah of onah is Torah law, and even though they forbade in famine years, that is because initially she accepted knowing that in the time of prohibition he would separate from her — unlike here where there is at least a doubt whether she accepted herself on that basis, especially according to what is written above in the name of Sefer Chasidim not to be so strict; therefore it seems that even on the night of immersion, where Magen Avraham and Eshel Avraham are strict, one may rule leniently in famine years, all the more in other troubles, as Sefer Chasidim implies above; see responsa Shevut Yaakov volume 3 siman 164, where he also challenged Taz from Rashi, and brought from Eruvin 28, “Beardlets,” etc., see there; he wrote that since it is a measure of piety, where there is concern that if he does not engage he may come to sin, or he or his wife long for it, perhaps it is permitted to engage, see there.
