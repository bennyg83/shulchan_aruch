On Monday and Thursday. See Taz and Tosafos at the end of the chapter Merubah. See Peri Chadash, who explains that they are days of judgment, as stated at the end of the chapter Mefanin: one who has ancestral merit should undergo bloodletting on Monday or Thursday, when the Heavenly and earthly courts sit in judgment. See there. Therefore, supplications are increased. See Magen Avraham, siman 580, letter 9, who writes that we rule that a person is judged every day and that Monday and Thursday are days of favor. See there. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 1, who writes that on Monday and Thursday the Heavenly and earthly courts are aligned in judgment. One may say that this explanation follows the opinion that a person is not judged every day. According to the opinion that he is judged every day, when judgment is conducted below there is no judgment above, and these are days of favor.
In Levush, regarding decreeing a fast on Monday and Thursday, see siman 572, seif 1, that one does not initially decree the fast for Thursday. See Perishah here.
They recite Kel Erech Apayim. Levush explains that in one place we say “save us” and in another “rescue us”: “save us” from what we presently suffer, and “rescue us” so that distress should not come.
