[1] On Monday and Thursday, etc. During the forty days in which the second Tablets were received, which were days of favor, Moshe Rabbeinu ascended on Thursday and descended on Monday. This is also alluded to in the verse, “These are the generations of the heavens and the earth when they were created,” which may be read, “He created them with the letter hei,” and in the verse, “Seek Hashem when He is found,” whose initials are beis and hei. Yam Shel Shlomo, at the end of the chapter Oso Ve’es Beno, writes that those who refrain from eating meat on the night after Monday and Thursday, yet eat meat on the preceding night, do not act properly, because the halachic day follows the preceding night. However, I heard that the reason for the fast is that the Heavenly beis din sits and judges capital cases on those days. According to this, their custom is proper, because capital cases are not judged at night. On the following night, however, they do not eat meat, because once a capital case has begun, it may be completed even at night. End quote. In Eliyahu Zuta I objected that there is an explicit Mishnah in Sanhedrin 32a: monetary cases are judged by day and may be completed at night, whereas capital cases are judged by day and completed by day. See there in Eliyahu Zuta. Perhaps the reason is that the end of that Mishnah states that capital cases may be completed on the same day for acquittal, but only on the following day for conviction. We therefore fear that perhaps, Heaven forbid, the defendant is liable, and his judgment is delayed until the next day; for this reason meat is not eaten that night. Nevertheless, the question on Maharshal remains, for the judgment is certainly not completed at night. Nor is the case judged at night; rather, each judge individually reviews and discusses it at night, as Rambam writes there. Afterward Magen Avraham was printed, and I saw that at the end of siman 580 he emends Maharshal’s words from “capital cases” to “monetary cases.” This is astonishing, since Maharshal repeats the phrase “capital cases” several times. It is also astonishing to explain the passage this way when one examines Maharshal itself. Magen Avraham further challenges Maharshal: on the contrary, we rule that a person is judged every day, and the fasts on Monday and Thursday are because they are days of favor, as Tosafos and Rashi write at the end of the fourteenth chapter of Shabbos, the chapter Mefanin. Accordingly, there is no reason to fast on the night following them. End quote. This is no difficulty, for the Gemara there states that the Heavenly and earthly courts are aligned on Monday and Thursday. Rashi explains that since these are days of judgment and accounting, our sins are recalled. The judgment on Monday and Thursday is therefore more severe than on most other days, since the earthly beis din also sits in judgment. See siman 334, seif katan 28. Minchas Yaakov writes in Hilchos Niddah, siman 185, that the reason is that fasting substitutes for a korban, and with sacred offerings the night follows the preceding day, as established in Chullin 83a. Yam Shel Shlomo further writes there that some have the custom not to eat meat or drink wine on both the night before and the night after. One should not object when healthy people do so, but one should object when young women do so, and certainly a husband should object. End quote. Damesek Eliezer writes that this applies specifically the first time, for otherwise it would not be considered “on the day he heard,” and she would require three people to release the vow. Magen Avraham writes there that during summer, when they eat before nightfall, there is no need to be stringent on the preceding night unless they accepted upon themselves to regard it as night after praying Maariv.
