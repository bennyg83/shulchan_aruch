(1) The custom is to increase, etc. In some places the custom is to recite the Thirteen Attributes of Mercy and confession before Vehu Rachum.
(2) And they recite Vehu Rachum. Its institution arose through a great miracle, as brought in Kol Bo and printed in the siddurim. It must be recited with concentration and deliberation, not hastily.
(3) And he is called one who breaches a fence. Some say that he is called one who breaches a fence only if he does not recite it at all. If he recites it while not standing, he is not included in this designation.
(4) To recite it while standing. Likewise, the passage Kel Erech Apayim, recited after Half Kaddish, must also be said while standing, because it contains the words “we have sinned,” and confession must be recited while standing.
(5) Quietly. Like the Amidah, which is recited quietly. The reason for the first opinion is to arouse concentration. Each locality should follow its established practice in this matter. Some say that if one finishes Vehu Rachum before the chazzan, he should wait at “merciful and gracious” for the chazzan and perform nefilas apayim together with him. Shaarei Teshuvah writes that nowadays most congregations do not wait for the chazzan, and each person performs nefilas apayim when he finishes. See there.
(6) Days of favor. During the forty days in which the second Tablets were received, which were days of favor, Moshe Rabbeinu ascended on Thursday and descended on Monday.
(7) To fast on them. See below at the end of siman 580 and what we write there in the Mishnah Berurah.
