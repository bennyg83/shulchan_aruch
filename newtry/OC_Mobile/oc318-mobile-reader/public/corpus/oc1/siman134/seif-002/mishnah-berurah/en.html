(8) He shows, etc. The Mechaber placed this law before the laws of Torah reading because the Sephardic custom is to lift the Sefer Torah before the reading. See the gloss below, which states that our custom is to perform hagbah after the reading. The one who lifts the Sefer Torah from the bimah to show it to the people should open it to three columns and then lift it. It is possible that three is meant specifically. Magen Avraham. It appears to me that this depends on the strength of the person lifting it, namely how widely he is capable of lifting it while it is open.
(9) The face of the writing of the Sefer Torah. The later authorities write that when the Sefer Torah is shown to the people while the person stands on the east side of the bimah, he should turn from east toward south, as written in siman 128 regarding the kohanim; see there. Before taking out the Sefer Torah, Kaddish is recited through “Le’eila.” On a day that has Mussaf, however, Tiskabel is recited before taking out the Sefer Torah.
(10) For it is a mitzvah, etc. Even if one was initially standing outside, it is a mitzvah to enter the synagogue to see the Sefer Torah when it is taken out and returned, because “in the multitude of the people is the King’s glory.”
(11) To see the writing. It is proper to see the letters closely enough to be able to read them, for the kabbalists write that through this a great light is drawn upon a person.
(12) And to say, “And this is the Torah.” One should say “And this is the Torah” only while facing the writing of the Sefer Torah. Later authorities.
(13) The chazzan says, etc. The universal custom is to say Berich Shemei when the Sefer Torah is taken out, both on weekdays and on Shabbos. If it was not said then, it may be recited until the Sefer Torah is opened for reading. Later authorities. The Torah should be raised three times, namely when saying Shema, when saying Echad, and when saying Gadlu. Magen Avraham in the name of Maseches Sofrim.
(14) In the right hand. This is based on the verse, “And His right hand embraces me,” and also because the Torah was given with the right hand. On Shabbos morning, when returning the Sefer Torah to the heichal, the custom is to recite the psalm “Give to Hashem, children of the mighty,” etc. At Shabbos Minchah, as well as on Yom Tov and weekdays, the custom is to recite Tehillim 24. Later authorities.
(15) “Blessed is He Who gave,” etc. Afterward they recite “The Almighty, His way is perfect,” etc., which contains forty letters, corresponding to the forty days Moshe was on the mountain. Magen Avraham in the name of Darchei Moshe.
