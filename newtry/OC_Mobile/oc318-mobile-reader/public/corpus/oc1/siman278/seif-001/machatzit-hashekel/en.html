And if you say he changes — injuring where he needs the blood for his dog, and kindling where he needs its ash, and it is repair — the Gemara challenges R' Eliezer, and we learn: all who damage are exempt — implying even injuring and kindling; and it answers: this is R' Yehudah and this is R' Shimon. And Tosafot explain: R' Yehudah holds that even damaging in injuring and kindling is exempt, and R' Shimon holds liable as he expounded from Scripture there. And Tosafot challenged R' Shimon — why is he liable in injuring and kindling even she'eina tzricha legufa for his dog and for its ash? Nevertheless in these two, even when damaging he is liable; nevertheless it is melacha she'eina tzricha legufa that is exempt per R' Shimon, and we find several sugyot that for R' Shimon even damaging in injury requires tzricha legufa, and one must say that you find it where it is nevertheless damaging, such as he injured and needs it for his dog, and he kindled and needs its ash, and it is needed for its own sake per his later thought — it becomes known they are items forbidden for benefit and forbidden to give to a dog or benefit from the ash — it turns out retroactively he was damaging; nevertheless it is called needed for its own sake, for per his thought it was needed; but in Bava Kamma Tosafot explain it deals even where he does not need it for his dog or its ash, and nevertheless per R' Shimon he is liable — just as generally damaging is exempt, nevertheless in injuring and kindling he is liable — so too here he is liable even though not needed for its own sake, even though generally exempt — so holds R' Eliezer, and these sugyot prove that even in injuring and kindling one requires melacha tzricha legufa — they come according to R' Yochanan; behold per Tosafot in Bava Kamma, for R' Eliezer according to R' Shimon in injury even without needing blood and it is damaging and also not needed for its own sake — nevertheless he is liable. And their dispute stems from what is stated in Shabbat 105b: one who tears in his anger on Shabbat is liable, and we conclude it is according to R' Yochanan, who holds melacha she'eina tzricha legufa is liable; and it challenges: nevertheless is he not damaging? And it answers that it is repair, for he satisfies his inclination; and it challenges: it was taught — one who tears his garments in anger should be in your eyes like one who worships idols; and it answers: for example, he did it to instill fear in his household. And Rashi explains the questioner's difficulty: since it is like idol worship, how can it be called repair? And so explains the Raavad, and it answers: for example he did it to instill fear — it is not like idol worship, and therefore it is repair; but if he intended a transgression it is damaging. But the Rambam explains the Gemara's question was: since it is like idol worship, the baraita should conclude and this thing is forbidden even on a weekday. And it answers: for example he did it, etc. — he did not commit a prohibition, but in any case even if he committed a transgression in his act, nevertheless it is called repair in that he satisfied his inclination, and he is liable regarding Shabbat and exempt even per R' Yehudah — meaning who holds she'eina tzricha legufa is liable — nevertheless he is exempt because he is damaging.
