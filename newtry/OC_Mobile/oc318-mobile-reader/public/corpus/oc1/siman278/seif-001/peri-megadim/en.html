(א) For a liturgical pen — Kai stands for forbidden, per R' Shimon, and for a dangerously ill person it is permitted l'chatchila, and the same for a gentile and bandits where there is danger (or doubt, as in siman 329, see there — and behold doubt of pikuach nefesh — one may say establish it on the presumption that it is life-threatening, and perhaps this does not help either; there is an answer, but that one does not follow the majority in this is explained there, see there) — also permitted. But in the Mishnah Shabbat 29b we establish it according to R' Yehudah, and therefore where there is danger it is permitted l'chatchila, and therefore see there; and the Tur ruled like R' Shimon, and the Mechaber omitted this — he only permitted where there is danger. In Shulchan Aruch, siman 327, opinion 1: in general she'eina tzricha legufa is exempt; however, from what the Rambam wrote there is no need to say he ruled like opinion 1 in such a case, and if not so in general laws it will be explained, and in the Levush he ruled here like R' Shimon and brought the Mechaber's wording there in Shulchan Aruch, see there. And melacha she'eina tzricha legufa — there is a rabbinic prohibition, and what he wrote regarding a wax candle, etc., see siman 274 on this. And behold where there is no danger it is forbidden l'chatchila to extinguish it, even though she'eina tzricha legufa is rabbinic, and for illness without danger rabbinic violations are permitted even through a Jew — at first glance one might say that she'eina tzricha legufa is very close to a Torah prohibition and they did not permit such; and to say it deals where it is possible to take the candle outside even gently and he will not kindle (Magen Avraham 276, letter 10) — therefore l'chatchila it is forbidden; but if not so, it is permitted l'chatchila — for if so, because of the gentile it should have said it is permitted even l'chatchila; and in truth in Shulchan Aruch 327 regarding a wooden candle it is permitted l'chatchila in a place of distress — it requires study somewhat, for there too there is no life danger, only slight damage, and in Turim with Bach the Pappadma edition has the words exempt, and if there is danger permitted — so too in Bach there; and see Pnei Yehoshua and in our novellae we wrote from this, and Magen Avraham if not so will be explained.
