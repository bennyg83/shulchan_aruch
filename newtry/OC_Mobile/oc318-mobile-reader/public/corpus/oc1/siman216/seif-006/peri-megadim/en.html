Over oil. See Taz. If one recited Borei Atzei Besamim, he fulfilled his obligation; see Magen Avraham, letter 10. See Levush, who writes that it is not fit for eating. His explanation is difficult, for even if it were fit for eating, because of its importance, since it grows in Eretz Yisrael around Yericho, which is named for this, as Rashi explains in Berachos 43a, they established a distinct blessing for it. One might say that they should have established the blessing “Who creates a pleasant fragrance in fruits” for balsam oil, just as they established Borei Peri HaGefen. I have not presently found any posek who says this. Accordingly, if one recited “Who creates a pleasant fragrance in fruits,” he would not fulfill his obligation. I have neither seen nor established this ruling.
One who smells fragrant fruits. Eliyah Rabbah, letter 6, writes in the name of Olas Tamid that one should first recite the blessing for eating and afterward the blessing for fragrance, because the blessing for eating takes precedence. See there, and see Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman. Nevertheless, it appears that he should eat immediately so there is no interruption, then recite the blessing for fragrance and smell the fruit. He should not intend to derive benefit from its fragrance until after eating. So it appears to me. See Eliyah Rabbah. According to what I have written, this is correct.
