[Levush] Therefore it is proper, etc. Bach and Nachalat Tzvi hold that one should smell it and recite Borei Minei Besamim. Bach's proof is clearly from Rambam, who calls the anointing oil by the same term as fragrant oil over which one says Borei Minei Besamim, as Beit Yosef explains; the anointing oil was certainly strained. I do not understand Taz, who attributes to Bach a position that never occurred to him, as though Shulchan Aruch's wording were Rambam's, which is not so. In practical law Bach and Nachalat Tzvi appear primary. The Rosh, Rabbenu Yonah, Kol Bo, Rabbenu Yerucham, Abudarham, and other poskim cite Raavad that if the spices were removed one says Borei Minei Besamim, without limiting this to a case where some substance remains, as the Tur does. They plainly speak of oil that was strained. Moreover, the opinion brought by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch to recite "Borei Shemen Arev" over it is, after examining the poskim, a solitary view. Riaz in Shiltei Giborim likewise rules Borei Minei Besamim. This does not resemble siman 217 seif 3, where the oil absorbed only smoke; here it absorbed the actual essence and moisture of the spices. One can also distinguish between a fruit that absorbed fragrance and a vessel that absorbed fragrance. See the end of the siman and Tanya.
[Levush] Rather, he should smell, etc. This remedy was added by the Levush on his own. Lechem Chamudot writes that it helps only if he takes balsam and blesses over it, because those who require a blessing on this oil say Borei Shemen Arev. If this is what the Levush intended, he should have explained it. Or Chadash wonders that the Levush should have specified that he is in Eretz Yisrael, unless he means balsam brought outside the Land. This is not precise. Since balsam is important and grows in Eretz Yisrael, a special blessing of Borei Shemen Arev was established over it. Certainly that blessing applies even if it grows outside the Land, just as the seven species praised in Eretz Yisrael receive Me'ein Shalosh even when grown outside the Land. Perhaps balsam simply does not normally grow outside Eretz Yisrael. As to Lechem Chamudot's main question, it appears the Levush follows Bach that after the fact Borei Atzei Besamim exempts balsam oil. Thus if balsam itself is unavailable, this is considered like an after-the-fact situation. Furthermore, since many poskim hold that this oil itself receives Borei Atzei Besamim, as Beit Yosef writes, the Levush holds that initially one may use another item whose blessing is Borei Atzei Besamim. This is clear.
Lavender. This is the plant called spikenard in Polish, which people commonly place in the water with which kohanim wash before duchaning; one must recite Borei Atzei Besamim over it. Orchot Chaim says it is called spika nardi, and some call it lavender, which is of the same species.
