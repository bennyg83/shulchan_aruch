The musk — see in Mishna Berurah, and it is proven from this that something forbidden in eating is permitted to smell; for until now Rosh and Rama did not disagree except regarding eating, but regarding smell it is clear in the Talmud that one blesses on it as well, and we learn from this that it is permitted to smell even if we say it is the blood of a live animal; and likewise in Be'er Heitev in the name of Damesek Eliezer, and likewise in Ma'amar Mordechai in the name of Perach and Perishah in Yoreh De'ah siman 108 regarding smell — it is a separate matter. However, it is reasonable that not everything is equal in this: a thing like musk and the like, whose primary purpose is to smell and not to eat — even if they put it in a pot, its primary purpose is certainly to smell — we are not concerned when he smells lest he come to eat it; which is not so regarding a thing whose primary purpose is to eat, and because of its good taste he smells it — in this it is reasonable l'chatchila not to smell, since its smell too is only the taste of the dish that smells because of its goodness and its oil and the like; certainly one should be concerned lest he come to eat it. And an example of this is found in the Talmud: we say to a nazir, "Go around, go around — do not approach the vineyard," lest he come to eat it; and all the more so to smell it directly, to arouse desire for that thing; and all the more so if it is a matter in which there is danger of weakness, like that sugya in Ketubot 60b [and it was brought above in siman 169] — certainly it is forbidden l'chatchila to smell it, lest he weaken because of its smell and then be forced also to eat, like that incident in the Talmud there; and Asheri explained to us in the name of Ra'ah there, because we judge him like a pregnant woman who smelled on Shabbat:

(ב) When he took it to smell it — and specifically he took it in order to smell it, for in this it becomes as if the fruit stands to smell, as the Acharonim wrote in siman 217; but if it was placed before him on the table and he enjoys the smell and intends to smell, he need not bless, for behold the fruit's primary purpose is not to smell but to eat, as in Tosafot and other Rishonim; and a thing that was not made for smell — even if he intends to smell — he does not bless, as long as he did not take it in his hand, as below in 217:

(ג) Or to eat it and smell it, etc. — see in Biur HaGra s.v. veham — who wrote: see Tosafot 43a s.v. ve-al, to exclude, etc. — meaning that one does not bless at all [that is, not only that one does not bless borei atzei besamim, but even the blessing borei minei besamim with which one fulfills b'dieved on everything [and likewise the blessing asher natan] — one also does not bless when eating them even though he enjoys their smell], for otherwise it should have said "to exclude musk and everything similar." And see Tosafot there 43b s.v. hai — from what is explained in 43a, to say that wherever it was not made for smell one does not bless on it; and here too, when he takes them to eat, we do not bless on them. And see in Rosh, who wrote that "this one who smells an etrog" deals with one who took it to smell it, or to eat it and smell it, etc.; but, etc. — not like Rashi and Tosafot; but the main thing is according to Rashi and Tosafot — end of his words, Biur HaGra. And see in Damesek Eliezer, who wrote that the intent of Biur HaGra is that Tosafot and likewise Rashi there in 43a disagree with Rosh in what requires blessing when he took it to eat and smell it; and he explains that what Tosafot wrote "to exclude apples and quinces that are not primarily for smell but to eat" means that even if one takes them to smell and eat, one does not bless [and likewise Rashi 43a s.v. ein mevarchin — not when brought within the meal, meaning even if brought for both]. And I do not know how we can say this according to Tosafot — behold Tur and likewise Rabbeinu Yerucham and likewise Rabbeinu Meir [in Berakhot, Maharam] all wrote explicitly in the name of Tosafot that when he takes it to eat and smell it he is obligated to bless. And in truth, in Tosafot Rabbeinu Yehudah on Berakhot [printed recently], where almost the entire language of our Tosafot appears, it seems their intent is as the simple meaning: because their primary purpose is not eating, therefore they did not establish for it the blessing of atzei besamim even when he intends only to smell; but regarding the blessing asher natan they did not discuss at all — and for this one must bless even when he intends to eat and smell. And likewise R' Yitzchak explicitly in his Tosafot, chapter Elu Devarim, like Rosh — only where he does not intend except to eat, even though he enjoys the smell, he does not bless at all, as he wrote there; and likewise Ra'avyah in his laws on Berakhot also wrote explicitly that when he takes it to eat and smell it he blesses. And likewise from Rashi 43a there is also no proof, for the intent of Rashi is even when brought for both; therefore it appears to me that the intent of Biur HaGra is only to attack the language of Rosh in what he concludes "but, etc." — since he did not intend to smell, it implies that if he afterward intends to smell he blesses; and in this Biur HaGra holds that the intent of Tosafot 43b s.v. hai is not like him, but rather since it came initially for eating, even if afterward he intends to smell he does not bless; and likewise it appears more on 43a in Rashi and Tosafot there, from the conclusion of their words, that where it comes within the meal for eating, even if at the end he also intends to smell, he does not bless; and likewise is the explicit view of Shitah Mekubetzet in chapter Elu Devarim: "And even though one smells an etrog he blesses she-natan reiach tov, etc. — there, when he takes it likewise to smell it; but one who eats it and smells it — no, and even if he afterward intends to smell it, etc." — end of his words. But if at the time of his taking he intends for both, Biur HaGra also concedes to all the aforementioned poskim that he blesses [and one should not challenge our words from siman 14 regarding smelling an etrog of mitzvah, where Biur HaGra agrees there to the second view that one should not bless, because its primary purpose was not made for smell — and there certainly, even if at the time of taking for the sake of the mitzvah he also intended to smell, he does not bless; and likewise the second view regarding bread, where Biur HaGra also agrees there not to bless, because its primary purpose does not stand to smell; and there it also appears explicitly in Biur HaGra that even if at the time of taking the bread he took it to eat he also intended to smell, nevertheless he need not bless the blessing asher natan — for regarding etrog, since his taking is obligatory for the mitzvah, the thought he considered then also for smell is nullified with it, and we do not say that through the thought he considered then also for smell it becomes as if the etrog was made for smell, as I expanded below in Biur Halacha s.v. hamariach — see there; and likewise regarding bread, since by default it stands only for eating and the smell in it is only momentary while it is hot, therefore we do not say that because he thought at the time of taking for eating also for smell, it suffices that it become as if made for smell — which is not so regarding etrog [during the rest of the year] and an apple whose good smell is permanent, if he intends for smell it becomes as if made for smell]. And know further that even according to Shitah Mekubetzet and Biur HaGra — specifically when he took it initially to eat and afterward wants to smell it, they hold that he is exempt from blessing then; but if he wants initially to smell and afterward to eat, it appears to me that certainly he must bless according to all views, for his taking was now for the sake of smell — and what do we care if he wants to eat it afterward? And this is a fortiori from what Shulchan Aruch ruled: when he took it to eat and smell it, he must bless [and even according to Damesek Eliezer above he must also bless in this, and support for this from the words of Beit HaGadol that I brought in Sha'ar HaTziyun sign 7 — see there]:

(ד) And he did not intend to smell it — this language is from Rosh, and I already wrote that Biur HaGra questions his words; and in truth the law is with him, for we have established below in 217 that wherever it was not made for smell, even if he intends to smell he does not bless unless he took it in his hand for the sake of smell — and likewise here, since at the time of his taking it was for eating, even if at the end he also intends to smell, he cannot bless; and it appears to me that Magen Avraham also intended this in sk 5 s.v. eino mevareich — delo l'reicha avida — end of his words; meaning that if so, even if he intends to smell he does not bless. And were I not afraid, I would say that Rosh also concedes in this, and his intent in what he wrote "he did not intend to smell" is that at the time of taking he did not intend for the sake of smell — and therefore even if at the end he also intends to smell it does not help, as Shitah Mekubetzet wrote above; and with this all the words of Rosh will be precise — see there; for otherwise his words are not directed to their beginning. But the language of Shulchan Aruch, which concludes "even though it gives off good smell," is not so precise accordingly — for it should have concluded a greater novelty: "even if he afterward intended to smell":

(ה) Even though it gives off good smell, etc. — and likewise when they brought before him within the meal oil for eating, and the oil is spiced with minei besamim that smell and give off good smell — since the main intent in bringing it was for eating, he need not bless [thus it is proven in Rashi 43a at the end — see there]:
