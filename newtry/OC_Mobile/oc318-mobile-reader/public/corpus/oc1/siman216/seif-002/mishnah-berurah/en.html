(5) If this, etc. — he wished to explain how one blesses on it; and for this he said "if this, etc."

(6) Or species of tree — meaning even though it is not a complete tree, since it is soft; nevertheless, since it produces leaves from the tree, one blesses borei atzei besamim.

(7) Such as musk — and in foreign tongue phizem; and in besamim this comes from an animal; therefore one blesses on it borei minei besamim. Know that this musk — some say it comes from an animal's sweat and some say it comes from blood of a certain animal that gathers in its neck and afterward dries and becomes perfume; and according to this last view some wanted to forbid it at least to put in a pot to flavor the dish with its smell, since blood is forbidden in eating; and many poskim permit even to put in a pot, for we follow its present state and now it is not blood but mere dust that gives good smell; and see in Eshel Avraham who tends to rule as they [the permissive poskim] said, and in Pri Megadim in Mishbetzot Zahav he also tends that one may rely on their words to be lenient even l'chatchila when there is sixty times its amount against it in the dish.

(8) Fit for eating — like etrog and apple, or a ground fruit that smells good; and even if it is not fit to eat by itself except through mixtures, as will be explained at the end of the seif.

(9) He who gives, etc. — see in Biur Halachah who wrote that from the Talmud and poskim it implies that one must say asher natan in past tense.

(10) Or to eat it and smell it — which of them one blesses first; see in Biur Halachah who concludes that in such a case where smell and eating are in one fruit, it is better to bless on the smell first, for the enjoyment is immediate — as soon as he holds it in his hand he smells — and afterward he blesses on the eating; and similarly Mordekhai there. In Pri Megadim he gave another advice in this: taste a bit of the fruit and bless on it the blessing of eating without intending then to enjoy the smell, and afterward bless the blessing asher natan etc. and smell.

(11) Even though he, etc. — he does not bless; for this is like something not made for smell, on which one does not bless even though it gives smell and one enjoys, as explained below siman 217 seif 1.

(12) And on all of them — whether the smell is in a tree, or in a herb, or in fruit that smells.

(13) Has fulfilled — for the language of this blessing includes everything, like the shehakol blessing which includes all kinds of eating and drinking. And some Acharonim hold that b'dieved if one blessed shehakol on smell he has fulfilled, for smell whose pleasure is slight is not preferable to eating and drinking whose pleasure is greater, and nevertheless one fulfills with shehakol; and Magen Giborim holds that even b'dieved he did not fulfill, for this is not the nature the Sages established in blessings, and they did not enact the shehakol blessing except for eating and drinking.

(14) Nutmeg, etc. — meaning if he wishes to smell it.

(15) And on cinnamon — which they call zimering.

(16) And cloves — which they call neigelach; and even though all these are species of besamim, nevertheless their primary purpose is for eating [Ra'ash]; and see in Acharonim who raise doubt regarding cloves, and many of them agree that it is better to bless borei minei besamim on its smell. Acharonim wrote: one who smells coffee while it is hot and it is warm and its smell wafts, and a person enjoys that smell — he must bless the blessing asher natan etc.
