And I am like one who adds to the matter, with God's help: what Rosh wrote — his proof needs proof; behold his proof is explained in the Talmud Avodah Zarah according to Ran's explanation above, and the sages revealed to us that it is not specifically spoiling, but any thing that changes before putrefaction and disappearance to permitted matter; and nevertheless, in any case we require two good qualities — that it change to a permitted thing, such as spoiling or honey; but blood that curdled and became cheese — even though presumably it first putrefied, nevertheless the novelty is that it does not change to a permitted thing. And see in Keritut 5a: "bread" — is it not written "I want"? He did not say "bread did not change" — if so, even pure flour should be permitted; and see Avodah Zarah 46b and Tosafot beginning of 42a. Rather, perforce, since there is no putrefaction, change does not help except that it was mixed in water; and if not for the verse "bread," he would not be liable on an olive-size of bread, but per the measure of wheat in it; and likewise the law for one who vows from wheat — he is not liable on an olive-size of compressed bread because of the measure of water in it; and likewise here, if it were not written "bread." And still this requires further study regarding the law of water in bread; and see Rashi's language in Pesachim 33b end, s.v. tamei met she-sacht — and one can distinguish; and this requires further study. And in any case, it is not said in Keritut 5a except per the needs of the sugya and an erroneous notion — but not according to truth, Heaven forbid that such a change should permit prohibitions. And see in Chullin 120, who wiped milk, etc. — there it is not because of change but because "eating" is written, and drinking is not included in eating except in wine and olive oil; but all liquids that return into the food and one eats properly is liable even though it changed, as Tosafot wrote in Bekhorot 6b end, s.v. rotev, etc. And I wondered at the words of the gaon Mekor Chayim on this — see there; and Peir Yeshua in Piskei Chadash preceded:
