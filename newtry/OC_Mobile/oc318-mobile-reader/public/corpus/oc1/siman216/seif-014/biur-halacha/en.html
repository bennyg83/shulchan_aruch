One who smells an etrog, etc. — and some say he does not bless, etc. See in Mishna Berurah what we wrote in the name of Magen Avraham in the name of Maharshal, and likewise the gaon R' Mordechai Benet in his commentary on Mordechai; but R' Ovadiah Yosef in siman 653 and Mordechai's statement and Mateh Yehudah disagree with him — and according to them, per the second view, all the days of the festival one should not bless on its smell, whether at the hour when he takes it for its mitzvah or during the rest of the day, because it was set aside for its mitzvah and it does not stand to be smelled; and likewise appears from Levush. However, in my humble opinion the matter is astounding, since in the Talmud it is stated explicitly that an etrog is permitted to smell, for he did not set it aside except from eating, which stands in its primary purpose for that; but for smelling he did not set it aside, and it was properly in his mind to enjoy its smell — if so, why should he not bless on it at least when he is not taking it for its mitzvah? Granted, at the hour when he takes it for its mitzvah, it is possible to say with the reason of the second view: since in any case he is obligated to take it in order to fulfill with it, it is like something that was not made for smelling, and even though he also intends to smell, he does not need it, and it is preferable to take it to eat and smell — for there the permission is the main purpose of his taking, and when he takes it to eat and smell, who can say that the main purpose of his taking is for eating and the smell comes incidentally, and not the reverse, that the main purpose is for smell, or perhaps for both enjoyments equally — which is not so here, where certainly the main purpose of his taking was for the mitzvah in its time, since he is obligated in this; if so, the enjoyment is secondary according to all views. But when not at the time of the mitzvah, what can be said? [And we cannot answer and say that since its primary purpose is not to smell but to perform a mitzvah with it all seven days, therefore we cannot bless on it the blessing asher natan — this is not so, for an etrog during the rest of the year, when one takes it to eat it, even though he enjoys its smell, all the sages also hold that he should not bless on it because its primary purpose is not to smell but to eat; and nevertheless, when he takes it only to smell, according to all views he should bless; and likewise here, even though its primary purpose stands for the mitzvah and not for smell, now when he took it only to smell he should bless.] And there are among the later authorities who wish to say in this manner: since it does not stand to smell in its primary purpose, therefore he should not bless on it atzei besamim or minei besamim, as we conclude in the Talmud in chapter 43 [per the variant of Tosafot in the name of Beit HaGadol]: "The hadas was taught, and everything similar to it, that stand in their primary purpose to smell" — which is not so regarding etrogim and apples — see there in Tosafot and Rashba; and if so, he should bless on it only asher natan reiach tov ba-perot, like any thing whose primary purpose is eating; and here on the festival, since it was set aside from eating, therefore one should not bless even this blessing [this is the essence of their intent, even though they did not mention this sugya of "the hadas was taught" — see in Kenesset HaGedolah and Bigdei Yeshu and Ma'amar Mordechai]. But in any case it is not reasonable at all that because he set himself aside from eating this fruit, it no longer stands for eating in its primary purpose and it is not called a fruit — especially according to what Rashba wrote, brought in Beit Yosef siman 653: if he stipulated that it should not be forbidden in eating or in benefit, it helps — see there well; if so, certainly eating was not nullified from it by his setting himself aside from eating it. Also, one who examines Tur will see that R' Shimshon, who holds the second view, did not write at all "since it does not stand for eating," but on the contrary wrote "since it does not stand to smell" — see there; and therefore it appears that the main thing is as Magen Avraham for the law: that during the rest of the times, on all the days of the festival, one must bless when smelling according to all views. Nevertheless, after the aforementioned later authorities were stringent in this, and Biur Halacha and Chayei Adam copied their words, one should refrain from smelling an etrog all the days of the festival. And know that if one handles an etrog and fragrance remains on his hand or garment, he does not bless on that smell at all according to all views, even during the rest of the year, for that smell has no primary source [Acharonim]:
