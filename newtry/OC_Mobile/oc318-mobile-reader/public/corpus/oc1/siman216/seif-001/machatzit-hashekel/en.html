(3) The bee, etc. And the Rama was — thus it should read.

And it appears to me that even his proof requires proof — meaning his proof that he brought from honey into which forbidden matter fell is permitted, for the forbidden matter itself — from where is it permitted? And even though we permit the honey when the wings and feet of bees are mixed in it — the reason is not because the wings and feet became honey and we follow the present state; rather the reason it is permitted is that there is no taste of prohibition in wings and feet, and it is like the bones of a donkey that do not impart food impurity, for they do not have the status of food; and the same applies here regarding prohibition — they do not have the status of food; and see in Lechem Mishneh there in Berakhot.

Regarding "milk is a novelty," etc. He brought there a verse to forbid milk of an unclean animal. And he challenged: I would have a verse — behold since it is written "the unclean" five are left over, and we expound to forbid their gravy and sauce — and milk is not less than gravy and sauce; and on this he answers: milk is a novelty, for even pure milk would have been fit to forbid, and nevertheless the Merciful One permitted it; therefore one could say that even unclean milk is permitted; therefore an exclusion was needed; and he concludes: what is its novelty? And he said at the beginning — because blood coagulated and became milk; and it would have been fit to forbid the milk because of blood, and nevertheless the Merciful One permitted it; and if so we learn that we follow the original state — for if we followed the present state, what is its novelty? For now it is milk and not blood.

And you could also derive from milk — meaning that external reasoning should follow the original state; and if so milk as novelty is fine; except that now that the Torah permitted milk, the verse informs us to exclude external reasoning — rather we follow the present state, and we derive from it generally to follow the present state; and this is to reject. If so, what we say there is fine per the view that blood coagulated and became milk (meaning that the reason the nursing animal does not see blood) and it is in the presumption that blood was removed; but per the view that limbs break down, etc., until after twenty-four months — what would he answer? And he concludes that the novelty is that it is fit to forbid it, for it is something that comes from a living creature and is like a limb from a living creature or flesh from a living creature; and since we hold like the view that limbs break down, as is in Yoreh Deah siman 184 and 185 — and per them we do not say blood coagulated and became milk — if so from where do we permit honey and the bee? From where do we learn that we follow the present state? And he answered that per the view that limbs break down we say it is reasonable to follow the present state and a verse was not needed; if so there is no place for Magen Avraham's difficulty, that the Talmud calls it a novelty what originally was blood — for we do not hold like that view, but like the view that limbs break down, and the novelty is another matter as above; perforce one must say Magen Avraham holds that originally he had to say the novelty is that blood coagulated, etc. — perforce he must say external reasoning should follow the original state; from where shall we say the conclusion that limbs break down, etc.? He retracted from this and it appears to him that external reasoning should follow the present state; and if so it is possible to reject — there is no derivation from milk, for it is difficult for us since we hold like the view that limbs break down, etc.; and as above; and one must say Magen Avraham is more pleased to reject as plainly — for we do not derive from a novelty; and see what I wrote to resolve in Beit Heaven in tractate Chullin.

For then all permit it. And he means that Rosh is not disputing the matter — as the Rash who permitted nursing, etc., as is in Chullin daf 116b and in Shulchan Aruch Yoreh Deah siman 87.
