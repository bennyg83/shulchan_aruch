(s.k. 9) Trees of spices, and likewise anything whose stalk is hard (Shaarei Teshuvah). And these are Shaarei Teshuvah's words: almost the chapter "How does one bless" — all spices whose stalk is hard like flax stalks — one blesses on them "Who creates trees of spices," as it is said "and you hid them in the flax of the tree"; and whose stalk is soft like a vegetable — one blesses on them "Who creates herbs of spices," as brought in Riaz's proofs, end of his words; and likewise further in the s.k. before this, see there. However our eyes see his proof — from what is written "and you hid them in the flax of the tree," thus the Torah called flax a tree, and therefore we require similarity to flax whose stalk is hard. Per this I did not understand what Tosafot wrote in Shabbat daf 27b s.v. kol hayotzei on this that we learned: all that comes from a tree one may not kindle with it except flax. And Rashi explained on this that we learned that all that comes from a tree one may not kindle — such as hemp and grapevine — and it does not appear plausible to Rabbenu Tam, etc.; and Rashi said that certainly one may kindle with hemp and grapevine, for they do not come from a tree but are seed, as we say in Menachot, etc.; and flax too is a species of seed like hemp, except the mishnah needed to permit flax because it is called a tree, as written "and you hid them in the flax of the tree," and like the tree of knowledge per the view that wheat existed and it called it a tree, etc.; and further that they said in "How does one bless": whenever — if you take its fruit and the tree does not again produce, one does not bless hanoten on it; and flax, hemp, and grapevine — if you take their fruit, which is the seed, it does not again produce — therefore they do not come from it, end of Tosafot's words. But flax itself — Tosafot wrote that it is seed — how does the author of Shaarei Teshuvah infer that it is not called a tree until it is similar to flax whose stalk is hard? On Shaarei Teshuvah this is not a difficulty — one may say he holds like the Shitah Mekubetzet in Shabbat that both hemp and grapevine and flax are called a tree; but Magen Avraham, who brought it for practical law, is difficult — for it implies he does not dispute him, yet Tosafot dispute, and we hold like Tosafot's view, as written siman 264 seif 1 that we kindle with grapevine — perforce it is not a tree, and the same applies to hemp and flax. And further — Magen Avraham himself brought in s.k. 14 in the name of Rashba: even though we do not care about something crushed, since it produces from its tree, etc. — behold Rashba holds that smell and eating are equal, and just as for eating one does not bless "Who creates fruit of the tree" except on what produces from its tree, as written siman 203 — the same applies regarding the blessing on smell, to bless "Who creates trees of spices," and it does not depend whether the stalk is hard or soft; and perhaps one may say that Magen Avraham certainly holds that Shaarei Teshuvah holds like Tosafot's view, as we hold as above, and agrees that flax is seed — only that it is a difficulty for the author of Shaarei Teshuvah on the verse: why did it call flax a tree since it is seed? Perforce because it is similar to a tree — meaning because its stalk is hard; if so from this it is proven that a plain tree — its stalk is hard, and one cannot rely on this rule — and flax proves that its stalk is hard yet it is seed and not a tree; and likewise conversely that the stalk is soft and nevertheless it is a tree, as Magen Avraham wrote s.k. 14. However it appears that since the Torah called flax a tree, perforce most trees — their stalk is hard, and most vegetables — their stalk is soft; and perforce he holds that if a spice comes to his hand and he does not know whether it produces from its tree or from its roots — it is a doubt, and he blesses "Who creates species of spices"; nevertheless he may check: if its stalk is hard — one may rely on the majority and say it produces from its tree and it is a tree species; and if its stalk is soft — we follow the majority that produce from their roots and it is a herb species.
