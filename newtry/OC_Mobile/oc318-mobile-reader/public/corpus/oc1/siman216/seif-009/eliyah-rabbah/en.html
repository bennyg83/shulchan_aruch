(9) [Levush] And cinnamon, etc. He omitted what Shulchan Aruch wrote — kannila and cloves — and wrote in their place cane and cinnamon. And it appears to me because he holds kannila is cinnamon and it is cane and cinnamon as above at the end of siman 202. And what Shulchan Aruch wrote — on cinnamon one blesses "Who creates trees of spices" — Lechem Chamudot explained on daf 45. And it appears to me that was not cinnamon but only cinnamon bark [kinamon]; and accordingly Levush does not dispute Shulchan Aruch; and in Malbush Yom Tov he wrote that he disputes Shulchan Aruch — and it is not clear. And that he omitted cloves, which are nutmeg — it appears to me Levush holds they are not fruit, for one does not bless on them for eating; and so Nachalat Tzvi explained the words of Shulchan Aruch. And the cloves Shulchan Aruch mentioned here are a different species and not nutmeg — and not like Olat Tamid, who wrote that on nutmeg one blesses "Who gave good smell in fruits," and wrote the reason that they are primarily fruit and roses are not primarily fruit, until here. And this is not correct — for Rosh and Tur wrote the reason regarding roses that they are not primarily for eating. However the Taz wrote that nutmeg is primarily for eating and one blesses "Who gave good smell in fruits," and one must say this is through a mixture that makes it fit for eating; and so it appears in Magen Avraham — and study is required. And further, in any case many poskim hold to bless also on cinnamon "Who creates trees." And from doubt one may bless on nutmeg "Who creates species," as the Agur wrote — also on cinnamon; and on kidah called ginger see below siman 298 in Havdalah. Olat Tamid wrote — and it appears one should bless on cinnamon "Who gave good smell in the fruits of the earth," for one should bless explicitly on a species of earth and on a species of tree; and so Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rosh — and in a place where it is not known if it is of a tree one blesses simply "Who gave good smell in fruits," until here. And in Rosh only fruit of the earth is mentioned; but regarding fruit of a tree he wrote that one blesses simply "Who gave good smell in fruits," and so wrote all the poskim. And further — even regarding fruit of the earth Tur wrote that one blesses simply; and it is also possible that Rosh did not speak precisely but said it is fruit of the earth like etrog from a tree — to exclude poskim who bless "Who creates trees of spices" — see there and examine. And thus Tur does not contradict his father's words. And so I found in Hagahot Semak and Shiltai Gibborim that they wrote in the name of Rosh that one blesses simply — but certainly as I explained.
