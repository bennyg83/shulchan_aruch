And regarding shehecheyanu — Radbaz vol. 2 siman 4 and in Chadashot siman 297 wrote that on good smell renewed from year to year — such as damask rose, Indian jasmine, hyacinth, rose, and the like — I am accustomed to bless "shehecheyanu" at the time I enjoy the fruit; see there. And they brought Kenesset HaGedolah in Hagahat Tashbetz siman 225 and Magen Avraham there s.k. 12; and so wrote Shevut Yaakov vol. 2 siman 38, and Shaarei Teshuvah brought it there letter 11. But Lechem Chamudot chapter 24 letter 120 wrote that one does not bless shehecheyanu on smell — see there — and Magen Avraham brought it in this siman s.k. 1. And so wrote Shakh in Kenesset HaGedolah in this siman in Hagahat Tashbetz letter 2 — see there; and see Berkei Yosef letter 1 what he wrote on this and concluded that for halachah one should not bless, since it is stated in dispute, and he wrote that so is the custom — see there. And so in his book Machazik Berachah letter 3, and Shaarei Teshuvah brought it letter 2. And likewise on roses preserved in honey one should not bless shehecheyanu on eating them, since they are not fit for eating by themselves. And so Maaseh Rokeach chapter 9 of Laws of Blessings din 1 wrote the reason — because they were not planted for this; people did not intend thus to preserve them in honey but for smell, and therefore they did not enact to bless shehecheyanu also on eating them — see there.
