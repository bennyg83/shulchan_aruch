He blesses on the hadas first — see in Be'er Heitev who questioned Tur, who wrote regarding afarsimon oil: if it happened to him together with hadas, he blesses first on the hadas — for according to Beit HaGadol's reason, because hadas is present in essence, this does not apply except regarding oil whose name is in grasses of spices, where the oil only absorbed smell from the spices, which is not so regarding afarsimon. And his words are difficult: why did Rambam rule regarding olive oil [that has smell] and hadas, that hadas is primary — and therefore this too is not considered present in essence regarding blood, since the body of the tree exists; and likewise regarding afarsimon, as we wrote in Mishna Berurah. And know that Ra'ah also wrote in this reasoning that it deals when neither one is more cherished to him — see there; but afterward in the sugya where they brought him oil and wine he also wrote that it deals when neither is more cherished to him, and he concludes: or alternatively, per the halacha here — since their blessings are not equal, even if one of them is more cherished to him, we follow the more important one, and in this they dispute which of them is more important — see there. However, per what we concluded above in siman 211 in Biur Halacha, that most poskim hold that even when their blessings are not equal we still follow the more cherished — perforce the words of Shulchan Aruch should be the law only when both are equally cherished to him.
