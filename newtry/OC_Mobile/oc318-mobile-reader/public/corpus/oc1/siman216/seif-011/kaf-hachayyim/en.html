Regarding Shehecheyanu, Radbaz, volume 2, siman 4, and new responsa, siman 297, writes that over a pleasant fragrance renewed from year to year, such as Indian jasmine, jasmine, orange blossom, rose, and similar species, he is accustomed to recite Shehecheyanu when deriving enjoyment from the fruit; see there. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, siman 225, and Magen Avraham there, paragraph 12, cite this. Shevus Yaakov, volume 2, siman 38, writes the same, cited by Shaarei Teshuvah there, paragraph 11. Lechem Chamudos, chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, paragraph 120, however, writes that Shehecheyanu is not recited over fragrance; see there. Magen Avraham in this siman, paragraph 1, cites this. Knesses HaGedolah in this siman, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 2, writes the same; see there. See Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1, who discusses this and concludes that as practical halachah one should not recite the blessing, since the matter is disputed. He writes that this is the common practice; see there. He writes the same in Machazik Berachah, paragraph 3, cited by Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 2. Likewise, one should not recite Shehecheyanu when eating roses preserved in honey, since they are unfit to eat by themselves. Maaseh Rokeach on chapter 9 of Hilchos Berachos, law 1, further explains that people do not plant them with the intention of preserving them in honey, but for fragrance. Therefore, the Sages did not institute Shehecheyanu even when they are eaten; see there.
