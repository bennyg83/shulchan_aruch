There were before him atzei besamim. In Tur it is written — and it appears to me that if they were before him, etc. — it appears to interpret the words of Tur: certainly if he blessed on species of spices and intended to exempt everything, it is obvious he fulfilled — for there is no difference whether one species is before him and he blessed "Who creates trees of spices" or "Who creates grass of spices," and he blessed on it borei minei besamim — he fulfilled as at the beginning of the siman; or if many were before him — since he intended on all of them, it is forbidden for him to bless in vain on the rest with his specific blessing. And if he intended when he said it in Mishna Berurah on a matter whose blessing is thus alone, as above — certainly he must still bless on the rest only his blessing; and it is comparable to one who says peh"a and there is also fruit of a tree before him and he did not intend on it except on peh"a — he must bless further on peh"e on peh"e; so too here. However, if he comes l'chatchila to consult how to conduct himself in this — Tur wrote he should see that he bless borei minei besamim on all of them, since there is in any case one among them whose blessing is thus; and even though with peh"a and peh"e, if both are before him he blesses on each its blessing — why not do so here as well? And so Beit Yosef challenged — one must say smell is different, for the smell comes to him without his choice, for the smell enters by itself, and behold he brought all of them to smell in them. And even if he chooses l'chatchila to smell this one, it is possible the smell from the second comes to him first — perforce he blesses on a matter that includes all of them, since there is in any case one among them whose blessing is thus; not so with eating, which depends on his choice — if he wishes he eats first from this one, he blesses on it specifically. And that which Tur wrote "and he blesses borei minei besamim exempts everything" — not to say specifically b'dieved, for behold he concluded afterward with words of l'chatchila in all that he brought in this law. And so Rambam and Rav Amram — all deal with language of l'chatchila, for they did not mention any matter of whether he blessed first in Mishna Berurah; rather plainly that Tur too at the beginning deals with l'chatchila, and so Beit Yosef, and as implied here from the words of Rama on this; and on this Tur also brought the view of Rambam who wrote "if there are no species of spices he blesses on each one" — implying if there are species of spices he blesses in general; so too. But Rav Amram holds that here too it is like with fruits regarding eating that we mentioned, and he does not distinguish between smell and eating — for even though smell enters by itself, nevertheless he is not obligated to bless until he intends to smell it specifically; perforce he blesses first on what he wishes to smell first, and afterward on the second and third. And if he wishes to smell first on Mishna Berurah and does not intend explicitly to fulfill on all of them, he must still bless also specifically on the others; and likewise with eating regarding shehakol — certainly the law is so — one does not fulfill by saying "all" on what one must bless with peh"a, since he did not intend explicitly also on it. And Maharshal z"l explained another way and wrote on it "and it is permitted for him from his Master," and I say — a man whom his Master helps — and his explanation is true; but what he challenged from fruits to eating, etc., and ruled here like Rav Amram — was already settled well in our words, with God's help.
