If there were in it trees and grasses. Ramban wrote until "and if we sifted it" — which is the language of Shulchan Aruch from Tur — that Rambam does not deal at all with sifting, but holds that anything that was not ground — there is no smell in it at all; and if ground until it has smell — one blesses borei minei besamim; and if not ground and by itself there is no smell from itself, only that it was soaked in spices that produce smell — we follow those spices, and this is all that are in it. But if we sifted and smell remained without primary substance — there is a dispute as Shulchan Aruch wrote. And Maharshal z"l wrote that Rambam deals with sifting and brings proof from the words of Beit Yosef — and he was not precise, for Beit Yosef did not write thus on Rambam at all. Also what Tur wrote after the words of Rambam, and Ramban wrote plainly on all of them borei minei besamim — this too does not deal except when they still exist visibly, not when sifted.
