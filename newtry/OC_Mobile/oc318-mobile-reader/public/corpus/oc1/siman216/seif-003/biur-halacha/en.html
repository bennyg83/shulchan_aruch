Atzei besamim — Magen Avraham wrote, and likewise in anything whose stalk is hard [Semag] — and it appears from this language that even if it finally withers in the ground and nothing remains in winter, nevertheless anything whose stalk is hard one blesses borei atzei besamim, even though regarding the blessing of eating it is not so — see siman 203 seif 2 in the gloss. However, in sk 14 of Magen Avraham it appears the opposite, that we do not bless the smell blessing of atzei besamim until it has the signs that it is a tree, like regarding eating — and this requires further study; and Peri Megadim already raised this. And behold in Chayei Adam it appears that he explains the intent of Magen Avraham in the name of Semag is only as a general sign to know whether it is a tree or grass — that is, first of all we must see if the stalk is hard [for if it is soft it is presumably grass], and nevertheless, even if it is hard, we must afterward see if it produces from its wood and persists year to year — see there; however, the simple meaning of Semag's language does not appear thus — see there in Semag in the name of Ri'az. However, in truth there is a dispute among the Rishonim in this: Ra'ah in his laws, and it is also brought in Semak on Berakhot, wrote explicitly on 43a: simlak and chilfa dema are grasses with smell in their bodies, and they are not soft like grasses but hard like a tree and grow on the ground; and if they had fruit, certainly with the opening of the mouth one would bless on them and not with the closing of the mouth, because the tree mentioned in the closing of the mouth means a full fruit-bearing tree, and these are not a full fruit-bearing tree; but nevertheless, anything that is hard is called a tree in the language of the world, and since these too are hard and have good smell in their bodies, they are called a tree and we bless on them borei atzei besamim; and he brings proof that even something that is not a full fruit-bearing tree, only because it is hard, is called a tree — as it is written "and you shall hide them in the stalk of flax," and certainly flax is not a full fruit-bearing tree and it is called a tree — end of his words [and likewise it appears somewhat in Rashi 43b s.v. simlak, who wrote that it is a type of grass that has three rows, etc. — and it was brought below in seif 7; however, one can reject that the intent is that it is very soft like grass, but nevertheless that its wood persists year to year — and know that this explains on jasmine, and Arukh's view too is that simlak is jasmine; and Arukh himself holds that we require that it have signs of a full fruit-bearing tree, as below]. However, according to what is explained in Arukh on chilfa in the name of the Geonim, the law of the smell blessing depends mainly on the law of a full fruit-bearing tree — whether it is a tree or a vegetable; and he wrote there: the Rishonim explained that these chilfei dema — we are not certain what they are — and we establish like this baraita [in Tosefta Kilayim]: whatever at the beginning of its growth produces a tree — it is a full fruit-bearing tree, and on everything that is like this matter one blesses borei atzei besamim — end of his words — see there; and likewise in Or Zaru'a in the name of Arukh, and he concludes on this; and likewise R' Yitzchak Alfasi — we establish like this baraita: whatever at the beginning of its growth produces leaves — it is a vegetable [and know that in many places he brings the words of R' Yitzchak Alfasi and they are not in the Alfas before us, and perhaps they are found in his responsa]; and likewise in Eshkol in the name of the Geonim; and likewise in Chiddushei Rashba in the name of Rav Achai and in the name of the Geonim — see there well; and likewise I found in Ma'amar Mordechai, who wrote in the name of Or Zaru'a and in the name of Avudraham, in this method; and if so, we do not bless atzei besamim unless the rules of the gloss above in siman 203 are fulfilled [and perforce what the Gemara brought as proof from flax for chilfei dema — behold chilfei dema per this method certainly persist year to year and at the beginning of their growth produce a tree, which is not at all a rule in flax stalks; rather the proof is only that even though they are soft, they are called a tree — and like flax stalks that are called a tree even though they too are not hard — thus Arukh, and Biur HaGra brought it in seif 7; and what appears from Ra'ah above that flax stalks are hard — this does not contradict, for relative to other grains and grasses they are hard, and relative to a tree they are soft].
