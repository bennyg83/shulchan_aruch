Regarding what he cites from Responsa Rashba, see letter 4. Rashba similarly writes that when one purchases new utensils for himself and the members of his household also use them, he recites both Shehecheyanu and HaTov VeHaMeitiv. Perishah, however, distinguishes that Semak wrote this specifically regarding new utensils, because the benefit does not come to everyone equally at the same moment, but to each person when he uses them. Therefore, he also recites Shehecheyanu. Regarding a field and similar property, where the benefit comes to both equally, HaTov VeHaMeitiv suffices, for Shehecheyanu is included within it. See there. Magen Avraham, letter 7, explains Semak as discussing one who purchased separate items, some for himself and some for his household: over his own he recites Shehecheyanu, and over theirs HaTov VeHaMeitiv. Bach likewise explains it this way. Beit Yosef himself, however, does not explain it so, as will be clarified there, with Hashem’s help. The proper understanding of the language appears to be as follows. At first glance, one could say that Beit Yosef and Rama do not disagree. Beit Yosef discusses a woman who gave birth, knew that she had borne a son, and then died, in which case the husband recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv. Rama discusses a woman who died without knowing that she had given birth. Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah writes this, as cited by Eliyah Rabbah, letter 2. According to this, Beit Yosef would explain Rashba’s responsum as referring to a woman who died during childbirth. Taz, however, writes that the reason is that it remains a benefit to her after death. According to him there is no distinction, Rama disagrees, and Rashba holds that both HaTov VeHaMeitiv and Shehecheyanu are recited, contrary to the Mechaber’s ruling in seif 2 that HaTov VeHaMeitiv is recited in place of Shehecheyanu. See Berachos 59b: The Gemara initially states that if one has land he recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv. Yet Shehecheyanu is also recited. The Gemara’s question is that the language implies HaTov VeHaMeitiv is recited only when there is a partner, and it answers that indeed the case involves a partner. It is also true that both blessings are recited, etc.
