[1] Levush: They told him, etc. This implies that the ruling applies even when the husband is in another city. Beit Yosef writes that the same applies if the husband is present and sees the birth himself. Magen Avraham writes that he should bow toward the east and recite the blessing, citing Masas Binyamin and Emek Berachah. It appears that the same applies to all these blessings. Sefer Chassidim, siman 846, writes that this also applies when a son is born to one’s son or daughter. Even when a son is born to a righteous person whom he loves because of his righteousness, he recites the blessing. It further states there that when a person receives news that a son was born to him, he should not thank the bearer of the news until he has first blessed the One Who graciously granted him a male child. It further states that if the bearer finds him in a bathhouse or a filthy place, and the listener is not suspected of mentioning Hashem’s name in a filthy place, he should wait until the person leaves and washes his hands. If the recipient is a righteous person, however, he should tell him immediately, and when he leaves he should recite the blessing.
[2] Levush: If she has died, etc. So writes Rama. Bach and Lechem Chamudos write that Rama disagrees with Beit Yosef, who rules that even if the wife died, the husband recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv, because it was nevertheless a benefit to her that she gave birth. Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah writes that they do not disagree: Rama discusses a case where she died during childbirth and did not know that she had given birth to a son. I saw, however, that Perishah explicitly writes in the name of Darchei Moshe that Rama disagrees with Beit Yosef. As practical halachah, Lechem Chamudos rules that Rama’s view is primary. Taz, however, rules like Beit Yosef, though for a different reason: even after her death it is a benefit to her that she left a remembrance in the world. This appears reasonable. Rama writes that some authorities state that the custom is to be lenient regarding this blessing, because it is not obligatory but optional, and from this many people have become lenient regarding these blessings. Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah writes that Lechem Chamudos understood Rama to refer to the blessing of Shehecheyanu, not HaTov VeHaMeitiv, and that Rama’s phrase “these blessings” means all the Shehecheyanu blessings mentioned in the chapter HaRoeh. I do not know how he attributed this to Lechem Chamudos, for on page 72 he cites Rashba and then records Rama’s words without qualification, as is his practice. It is possible that Rama also refers to HaTov VeHaMeitiv. As practical halachah, since Levush omits this leniency, it appears that one should not be lenient at all regarding these blessings. This is also evident from Beit Yosef. Moreover, Rashba’s reason, as stated in siman 245, is the dispute concerning one who purchases items similar to those he already owns, or already possessed items like them. In all these cases we plainly rule that he recites the blessing.
