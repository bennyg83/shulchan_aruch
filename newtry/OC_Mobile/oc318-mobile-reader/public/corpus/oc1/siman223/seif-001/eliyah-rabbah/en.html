[1] Levush: They told him, etc. This implies that the ruling applies even when the husband is in another city. Beit Yosef writes that the same applies if the husband is present and sees the birth himself. Magen Avraham writes that he should bow toward the east and recite the blessing, citing Masas Binyamin and Emek Berachah. It appears that the same applies to all these blessings. Sefer Chassidim, siman 846, writes that this also applies when a son is born to one’s son or daughter. Even when a son is born to a righteous person whom he loves because of his righteousness, he recites the blessing. It further states there that when a person receives news that a son was born to him, he should not thank the bearer of the news until he has first blessed the One Who graciously granted him a male child. It further states that if the bearer finds him in a bathhouse or a filthy place, and the listener is not suspected of mentioning Hashem’s name in a filthy place, he should wait until the person leaves and washes his hands. If the recipient is a righteous person, however, he should tell him immediately, and when he leaves he should recite the blessing.
