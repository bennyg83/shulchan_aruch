His wife gave birth to a son; he recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv. Beit Yosef writes that even if his wife died, he recites HaTov, because she nevertheless benefited when she gave birth. Rama disagrees. It appears to me that Beit Yosef is correct, though not for his reason, for how can one bless over something no longer applicable at the time of the blessing? Rather, even after her death she benefits from having left a male child in the world. We find this blessing recited over a posthumous benefit, for in the third chapter of Berachos it is stated that HaTov VeHaMeitiv was instituted in Bircas HaMazon because the slain of Beitar were granted burial and did not decompose. Responsa Rashba, siman 245, writes that one to whom a son is born must recite Shehecheyanu even if he already has sons, just as over new utensils below. He writes that he did not see great authorities practice this. Perhaps they regarded the case in the chapter HaRoeh as optional, like the new gourd in Eruvin, because it does not recur at fixed intervals. Rama found difficulty because the Gemara states that one recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv, not Shehecheyanu, and therefore explains the Rashba as referring to a case where the mother died in childbirth, so there is no benefit to another and he recites Shehecheyanu. This does not appear from the Rashba’s unqualified wording. Rather, the Rashba understands the Gemara’s requirement of HaTov VeHaMeitiv as additional to Shehecheyanu, similar to the Semak cited by the Tur below, that if one purchases for himself and his household he also recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv, implying that he recites Shehecheyanu as well. Likewise, when one’s father dies and the Gemara says he recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv, it means after he first says Dayan HaEmes.
