(1) His wife gave birth, etc. This applies even if he did not see it himself but merely heard the news while in another city. The same applies to the cases discussed in seif 2.
(2) A male. The unqualified wording of the poskim implies that even if he already has several sons and longs for a daughter in order to fulfill the mitzvah of procreation, if a daughter is born he does not recite this blessing. Nevertheless, it appears obvious that the first time he sees her he recites Shehecheyanu. She is no worse than a friend whom one sees after thirty days and whose appearance brings him joy, over whom one recites Shehecheyanu, as below in siman 225, seif 1.
(3) He recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv. “Who is good” to him, because he is pleased with a male child, and “Who does good” to her, because she too is pleased with a son. This applies even if they already have several sons. Peri Megadim writes that even if he did not bless immediately upon hearing, he still blesses according to all opinions, because the benefit continues.
(4) If his wife died, etc. Some say that the Mechaber disagrees and holds that even if his wife died during childbirth he recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv. Taz also rules this way, because she still benefits from having left a male child in the world. Mor U’Ketziah disagrees, maintaining that HaTov VeHaMeitiv was instituted only for physical benefit, not solely spiritual benefit. Lechem Chamudos, Levush, and Chayei Adam all rule like Rama.
(5) During childbirth. This wording seemingly implies that if she died several hours after giving birth, he recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv, since she was pleased before her death. See Biur Halachah.
(6) He recites Shehecheyanu. This is over the birth of the son. First, however, he must recite Dayan HaEmes over her death, because that blessing is obligatory, as above in siman 222, seif 3, whereas some hold that Shehecheyanu here is merely optional, as explained below.
(7) Regarding this blessing. Meaning Shehecheyanu, because it is merely optional, since the primary institution of Shehecheyanu concerns matters that recur at fixed intervals, such as festivals. From this, many became lenient regarding all similar blessings. This is incorrect, for only what the Gemara explicitly stated to be optional is optional.
