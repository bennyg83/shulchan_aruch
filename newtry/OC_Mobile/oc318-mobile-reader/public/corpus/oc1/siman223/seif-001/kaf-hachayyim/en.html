1) [Seif 1] If his wife gave birth to a son, he recites HaTov, etc. The reason is that his wife shares the good with him, for she too is pleased with a son. Beis Yosef; Olas Tamid, paragraph 1. There is no distinction between seeing for himself and hearing that his wife bore a son. Olas Tamid there; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. This applies even if the husband is in another city. Beis Yosef; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. When he arrives and sees the child, he recites Shehecheyanu. Halachos Ketanos, volume 1, siman 201; Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef. See below, paragraph 6.
2) There: He recites HaTov, etc. He should bow toward the east and recite the blessing. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. Eliyah Rabbah there cites this and writes that the same applies to all these blessings. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2; Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 2. It appears that this applies to those who pray facing east. Those who pray facing west should bow toward the west, and this is obvious. While bowing, he should say, 'Give thanks to Hashem, for He is good,' etc., and then straighten and recite the blessing. See above, siman 222, paragraph 9.
3) There: He recites HaTov, etc. The same applies if a son was born to his son, to his daughter, or to a righteous person whom he loves because of his righteousness: he recites the blessing. Sefer Chassidim, siman 843; Eliyah Rabbah there. It appears that one who takes into account the lenient practice concerning these blessings cited by Rema, of blessed memory, should recite it without the Name and kingship. Chida writes the same there in his commentary on Sefer Chassidim. See below, paragraph 6.
4) One who receives good tidings that a son was born to him should not express gratitude to the person who informed him until he first recites the blessing. Sefer Chassidim, siman 844; Eliyah Rabbah there. See above, siman 222, paragraph 10.
5) There: He recites HaTov, etc. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2, writes that here all agree that even after the time required for a short greeting, he may recite the blessing, because the good remains present in the world; see there. It appears that this applies specifically while the intensity of his joy remains in his heart. Afterward, he should recite it without the Name and kingship.
6) There: He recites HaTov, etc. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 4, writes that the practice is not to recite this blessing, but only Shehecheyanu at the circumcision. Alternatively, when the child's father returns from a journey, he recites Shehecheyanu. Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1, writes the same. His statement that the blessing is not customarily recited follows Rema, of blessed memory, at the end of this seif, who writes that many are lenient concerning these blessings. His statement that only Shehecheyanu is recited at the circumcision follows Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah, siman 265, seif 7, which says that some practice reciting Shehecheyanu at the circumcision; see there. He means that with the Shehecheyanu over the circumcision one should intend to cover the Shehecheyanu over the child as well. His statement that when the father returns from a journey he recites Shehecheyanu follows Halachos Ketanos cited above at the end of paragraph 1. According to Rema, of blessed memory, however, who writes that the practice is to be lenient regarding this blessing, it appears that even when returning from a journey one should not recite it except in a place where the practice is to do so. Ben Ish Chai, of blessed memory, parashas Re'eh, paragraph 8, similarly writes that if the child's father was absent during the circumcision and later returned from a journey, he should not recite Shehecheyanu unless he clearly knows that the local practice is to recite it. If the custom is uncertain, he should recite the blessing over a new fruit or garment and intend to cover the Shehecheyanu for the child; see there. It appears that one should act likewise when a son is born to one's son or daughter, covering it with a new fruit or garment.
