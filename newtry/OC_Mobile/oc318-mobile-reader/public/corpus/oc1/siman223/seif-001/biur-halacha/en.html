His wife gave birth, etc. See Eliyah Rabbah, who cites Sefer Chassidim that the same applies when a son is born to one’s son or daughter, or when a son is born to a beloved friend who is righteous and pious. However, from Responsa Rashba, part 4, siman 77, it is evident that he disagrees. He concludes that one does not recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv whenever both he and others derive pleasure, for if so one would recite it even over a new gourd. Why, then, did the Gemara ask, when one’s wife gives birth to a son, who else benefits with him, and answer that his wife also benefits? It could have answered that his children and household rejoice with him, and indeed everyone rejoices at the birth of sons. Rather, this blessing applies only where one has concrete benefit and enjoyment, such as rainfall, inheritance from relatives, or an abundance of wine from which others also drink with him, as above in siman 175. Likewise, when his wife gives birth to a son, both father and mother derive benefit, for he becomes a staff for their hand and a spade for burial, and he is considered an extension of both father and mother, while every person desires an heir. These reasons do not apply to the cases mentioned by Sefer Chassidim, nor are they cited by the other poskim. It is therefore preferable to limit these blessings to cases stated explicitly.
A male, etc. See Mishnah Berurah. As a practical matter, it requires study whether one who already has several sons but no daughter, and longs for a daughter, should recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv when another son is born, since ultimately he is not pleased with the male birth. This requires study.
During childbirth. This wording seemingly implies that if she died some time after giving birth and he had not yet recited the blessing, he may still recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv after her death because of the benefit she previously experienced. One may reject this, however, for since she died before he recited the blessing and now has no physical benefit from her son, he can no longer recite this blessing. This resembles one who finished eating and then remembered that he had not recited HaMotzi, who may no longer bless over the prior enjoyment. Consider also one whose father died and who has a brother, so that he should recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv over the inheritance, but the brother died before he recited it and he now inherits his brother as well because the brother left no son. Could he recite HaTov because his brother previously benefited? This is certainly implausible. Rama writes “during childbirth” because otherwise he would have blessed immediately after the birth, and Rama did not wish to construct a case in which he failed to bless earlier. See above, siman 175, Beit Yosef, concerning a change of wine and whether the blessing must be recited before the benefit. This requires study.
Some write, etc., that it is merely optional. Meaning, some write that it is optional because it does not occur at fixed intervals and resembles the case of a new gourd in Eruvin 40b, which the Gemara regards as optional. Many poskim, however, hold that it is obligatory, as is evident from Darchei Moshe at the end of this siman. Their reason is that where Chazal instituted a blessing because of heartfelt joy, time is irrelevant. Whenever an acquisition brings joy to his heart, he blesses Hashem for enabling him to reach a moment of joy.
