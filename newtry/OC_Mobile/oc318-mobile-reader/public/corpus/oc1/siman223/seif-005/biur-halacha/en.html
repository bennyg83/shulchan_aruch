He purchased utensils that they use, etc.; he recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv. It is obvious that even if he already possesses similar utensils, he nevertheless recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv, as above in seif 3.
He recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv. This refers to the recipient of a gift. According to all opinions, the giver does not recite a blessing. Later authorities.
Because it is beneficial to him and to the giver. See Mishnah Berurah for the reason, and see Magen Avraham’s challenge to the Rosh. Eliyah Rabbah and Biur HaGra resolve his challenge, and Derech HaChaim and Chayei Adam therefore rule like the Shulchan Aruch. However, Responsa Perach Shoshan and Biur HaGra rule that the Babylonian Gemara disagrees with the Yerushalmi on this point. Several Rishonim likewise imply this, including Tosafos and the Rashba, who writes that this is also the Raavad’s view. Thus, three Rishonim disagree with the Rosh, and the omission of this Yerushalmi by Rif and Rambam also implies the same. Practically, we ruled that one should not recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv because of these authorities, for we certainly follow the Babylonian Gemara. He should recite Shehecheyanu alone. Moreover, even according to the authorities who allow HaTov VeHaMeitiv, if he recited Shehecheyanu it is not a blessing in vain, because Shehecheyanu was instituted for one’s own joy from a benefit or good tidings that affects him. Even over a new gourd the Gemara permitted it. HaTov VeHaMeitiv applies only when the joy is greater because others also benefit. The greater category includes the lesser. In addition, some poskim hold that where HaTov VeHaMeitiv is required one must also recite Shehecheyanu. Although we do not initially rule this way, Shehecheyanu is not thereby in vain. By contrast, if one recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv and we follow the Babylonian Gemara, it is a blessing in vain. I therefore ruled in Mishnah Berurah that he should recite Shehecheyanu and not HaTov VeHaMeitiv.
