And his household. But if he bought utensils for himself and utensils for his household, he recites Shehecheyanu over his own, and over those of his household he recites HaTov VeHaMetiv. Bach. It appears to me that this means his sons, but for his male or female servant it is as if he sold them to them, for a hired worker comes for his wage. Magen Avraham.
And for the giver. In Responsa Perach Shushan, rule 1, siman 12, he wrote that Tosafot, Rif, and Rambam do not hold like this Yerushalmi, and certainly had Beit Yosef seen all these authorities he would not have ruled like the Yerushalmi. Therefore he concludes there that in places where they make clothing for orphan boys, the orphans recite Malbish Arumim and Shehecheyanu, and not HaTov VeHaMetiv; see there. So writes Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah. Maharshal Algazi ruled that the orphans recite Malbish Arumim, Shehecheyanu, and HaTov VeHaMetiv; see his responsa printed at the end of Shema Shlomo. According to all opinions, only the recipient, not the giver, recites HaTov VeHaMetiv. This is from Perach Shushan. See Magen Avraham, Taz, Bnei Chiya, and Yad Aharon.
