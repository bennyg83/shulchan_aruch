Seif 5, "one purchased," etc. This is as explained above in seif 2 and as stated in the Gemara concerning harvesting, etc. "Which is," etc. This is the view of the Rosh. His words are weak, for if the distinction depends on whether others share the benefit, then the giver should also recite a blessing, apart from the other difficulties. The Yerushalmi, however, holds that the distinction does not depend upon whether there are others who share the benefit. Rather, where a genuinely new benefit comes to a person, such as a found object, a gift, the birth of a male child, an inheritance, and similar events, the blessing is HaTov VeHaMeitiv. When he purchases an item, exchanging money for utensils, the blessing is Shehecheyanu. This was also the assumption of the questioner in our Gemara from the passage discussing whether there are others who share in the benefit, and from the cases of building a house and the baraita concerning harvesting. This indicates that the rule applies specifically to cases resembling building or acquisition. Rain, by contrast, is always associated with HaTov VeHaMeitiv. See Magen Avraham, who left the matter requiring study. See also the first chapter of Kiddushin, where the case concerns specifically a wealthy and distinguished person, and his difficulty is thereby resolved.
