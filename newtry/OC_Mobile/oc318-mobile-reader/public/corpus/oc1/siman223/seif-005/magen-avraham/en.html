He and his household. If he purchases utensils for himself and separate utensils for his household, he recites Shehecheyanu over his own and HaTov VeHaMeitiv over those of his household. Bach. It appears to me that this refers to his children. Regarding his servant or maidservant, it is as though he sold the items to them, since a hired worker receives his wages.
To him and to the giver. If the recipient is poor, it benefits the giver because Hashem enabled him to possess money with which to give charity. If the recipient is wealthy, the giver rejoices that he accepts from him, and it is therefore also beneficial to the giver. Rosh. This requires study, for Even HaEzer, siman 27, seif 9, states that only when the recipient is an important person does the giver derive benefit from his acceptance. Otherwise, many wealthy people extend their hands to receive gifts, so what benefit does the giver obtain? This is also implied in Choshen Mishpat, siman 190, seif 6, and Yoreh Deah, siman 160, seif 15. This requires study.
