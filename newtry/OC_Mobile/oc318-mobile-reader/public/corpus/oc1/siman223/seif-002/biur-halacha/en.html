If he has no brothers, he also recites Shehecheyanu. See Mor U’Ketziah, who writes that this refers to one who has no wife or children. Otherwise he recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv, as stated below in seif 4. According to Magen Avraham above, siman 221, seif katan 1, however, even if he has a wife and children he recites only Shehecheyanu and not HaTov VeHaMeitiv, for they have no share in the inheritance and he may not give them any of it, unless utensils were left as an inheritance and they all benefit by using them immediately.
If he has brothers, etc. This applies even if their father had already divided his property by instruction and each brother has a separate field. Nevertheless, the inheritance benefited them all, and HaTov VeHaMeitiv therefore applies. So writes the Rosh. See Peri Megadim in siman 221, who writes that the Mechaber also agrees.
