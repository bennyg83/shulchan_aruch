(8) His father died. The same applies to another upright person whose death distresses him, for it is included among bad tidings. The common practice is to bless without the Divine Name and kingship, but this is incorrect. At minimum, over the death of a Torah scholar, concerning which one must certainly mourn and grieve, and over relatives for whom one is obligated to mourn, he should certainly mention the Divine Name and kingship.
(9) He also recites Shehecheyanu. First he recites Dayan HaEmes, as explained above in seif katan 6, and afterward Shehecheyanu over the property left to him. Rambam. Although he would have preferred that his father not die and that he not inherit him, he may nevertheless recite Shehecheyanu, because this blessing does not depend upon joy but upon receiving benefit, even when sorrow and grief are mixed with it. Responsa Rashba, siman 245.
(10) Instead of Shehecheyanu. Meaning, he no longer recites Shehecheyanu, because the inheritance benefits his brothers as well and he may recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv. If they tell him that his wife died and left money, he recites Dayan HaEmes over her death and Shehecheyanu over inheriting her.
