6) There: He recites HaTov, etc. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 4, writes that the practice is not to recite this blessing, but only Shehecheyanu at the circumcision. Alternatively, when the child's father returns from a journey, he recites Shehecheyanu. Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1, writes the same. His statement that the blessing is not customarily recited follows Rema, of blessed memory, at the end of this seif, who writes that many are lenient concerning these blessings. His statement that only Shehecheyanu is recited at the circumcision follows Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah, siman 265, seif 7, which says that some practice reciting Shehecheyanu at the circumcision; see there. He means that with the Shehecheyanu over the circumcision one should intend to cover the Shehecheyanu over the child as well. His statement that when the father returns from a journey he recites Shehecheyanu follows Halachos Ketanos cited above at the end of paragraph 1. According to Rema, of blessed memory, however, who writes that the practice is to be lenient regarding this blessing, it appears that even when returning from a journey one should not recite it except in a place where the practice is to do so. Ben Ish Chai, of blessed memory, parashas Re'eh, paragraph 8, similarly writes that if the child's father was absent during the circumcision and later returned from a journey, he should not recite Shehecheyanu unless he clearly knows that the local practice is to recite it. If the custom is uncertain, he should recite the blessing over a new fruit or garment and intend to cover the Shehecheyanu for the child; see there. It appears that one should act likewise when a son is born to one's son or daughter, covering it with a new fruit or garment.
