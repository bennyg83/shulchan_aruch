(11) He built, etc. The same applies if he purchased a house. This is specifically when he built or purchased it for himself. If he has partners in it, each partner recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv. Gemara. Therefore, when a community builds or purchases a synagogue, the chazzan should stand and recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv aloud to discharge everyone.
(12) A new house. The same applies if it burned down and he rebuilt it. If he demolished it and rebuilt it, the later authorities disagree, and in doubt one is lenient. If he added even one row to its height, all agree that he recites the blessing.
(13) New utensils. This includes clothing and utensils for use, drinking, eating, and the like, provided they are things that gladden a person’s heart, each according to his circumstances, the poor person with what is appropriate for him and the wealthy person with what is appropriate for him. See seif 6. If one is so wealthy that even expensive and important utensils are insignificant to him and do not bring him joy, he does not bless. The later authorities disagree whether one blesses over purchasing sefarim, because mitzvos were not given for physical enjoyment. Chayei Adam inclines that if he sought this particular sefer and rejoices in acquiring it, he blesses, because this blessing was instituted for joy, not for use, as explained below in seif 4. Mor U’Ketziah writes likewise. One should therefore not protest against one who blesses.
(14) He already had, etc. This includes utensils inherited from his ancestors. Nevertheless, this utensil is new with respect to his acquisition. He further writes that even if he purchased and then purchased again, so that it is not new even regarding acquisition because he already owns similar purchased utensils, this particular utensil is nevertheless new to him.
(15) Shehecheyanu. He should take care to bless immediately, before he becomes accustomed to the item and the joy departs.
(16) Excluding if he sold them, etc. Even if they are new and were never used, once he has become accustomed to them there is no longer joy.
