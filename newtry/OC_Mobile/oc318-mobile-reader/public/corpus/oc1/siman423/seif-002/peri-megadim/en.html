The custom is. See Taz. In siman 25, seif katan 15, it is stated that on Rosh Chodesh in a place where Kesser is not recited, one may remove his tefillin after Mussaf. See there. The custom, however, follows Rama’s gloss there, that tefillin are removed everywhere before Mussaf. See Responsa Mabit. When we say, “the Mussaf offering of this day,” and mention the offering, it is proper to have removed one’s tefillin. In a leap year we add, “for the atonement of willful sin,” until Rosh Chodesh Adar; from Rosh Chodesh Nisan onward it is no longer said. Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 6, writes that the expression “in a leap year” implies that it should be recited throughout the entire year. Nevertheless, I have seen the custom to recite it only until Rosh Chodesh Nisan. See Beit Yosef, siman 452. Psalm 150 contains twelve expressions of praise corresponding to the twelve months, and the phrase kol haneshamah is repeated corresponding to the additional month. Peri Chadash objects that Hallel is not recited on Rosh Hashanah. See there. At first glance, there are thirteen expressions of praise, counting the two occurrences of Halleluyah and the word tehallel. Siddurim note that this alludes to the thirteen attributes through which the Holy One, blessed is He, governs the world. This requires study.
