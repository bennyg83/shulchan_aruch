3) [Se'if 2] For the Levi, one repeats and reads, etc. The reason repetition is necessary is that this first passage contains only eight verses. If each of the first two readers reads three verses, only two verses remain, and one may not leave fewer than three verses at the end of a passage. If two readers complete this passage, six verses still need to be read by two people, but they are divided into two passages: the passage U'V'Yom HaShabbos contains two verses, and the passage U'V'Roshei Chodsheichem contains five verses. If one person reads U'V'Yom HaShabbos and one verse from U'V'Roshei Chodsheichem, one may not begin a passage with fewer than three verses. If that person reads U'V'Yom HaShabbos and three verses from U'V'Roshei Chodsheichem, only two verses remain. Therefore, three readers must read from the passage Tzav. Although it contains only eight verses, when the middle reader repeats one verse it is treated as nine. The fourth reader then reads U'V'Yom HaShabbos and U'V'Roshei Chodsheichem. Alternatively, the third reader reads the remaining three verses as well as U'V'Yom HaShabbos, and the fourth reads U'V'Roshei Chodsheichem, as Rambam and the Tur write. This is the accepted custom. Beis Yosef.
4) The reason the Levi repeats part of the kohen's reading, rather than having the Yisrael repeat part of the Levi's reading that follows it in the passage, is as follows. The rule that one may not leave fewer than three verses at the end of a passage is a decree on account of people who leave, while the rule that one may not begin a passage with fewer than three verses is a decree on account of people who enter. This is the emended text of Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 1. Here there is no alternative, so the middle reader repeats. There is no concern regarding those who leave, and regarding those who enter, anyone who enters will ask what occurred. Beis Yosef in the name of Shibolei HaLeket. See above, siman 138.
