(ב) And the levi repeats — per the Geonim; and the halacha is that skipping refers to Rosh Chodesh, and 'the middle one' means the second, for it cannot refer to positions, for if so the halacha would be for the messiah. But Ran in chapter 4 of Megillah wrote that Ramban disagreed and asked: if so what did the Sages accomplish in their enactment? Behold the concern of those entering still stands — one who sees him begins at the third verse in the first section and says the first read only two; and if we are not concerned for those entering when impossible, etc., let the second read in parashat Tzav and the third read 'And on the Shabbat day' and one verse from 'And on your Rosh Chodesh days' — what concern is there since one may not begin fewer than three, etc.; and this is only because of those entering; and since even with skipping this concern exists, why skip — let him read normally and not be concerned for those entering; since impossible — therefore he explained that Rav and Shmuel's dispute refers to positions where there are only eight verses, therefore impossible except by skipping — end Ran there. His question is certainly great; but the more spacious view appears primary — practice as Mishna Torah chapter 17 halacha 9: on the month itself one reads three in the tamid offering — first 'And he spoke,' 'Command,' 'And you shall say'; second 'The one lamb,' 'A tenth,' 'Burnt offering,' 'And its libation,' 'And the lamb'; third returns and reads 'Burnt offering of the tamid,' 'And its libation,' 'And the lamb,' 'And on the Shabbat day' — 'Burnt offering of the Shabbat' — because they said one may not read fewer than three in a section, and some say one may not leave fewer than three — end. Per this there is no concern of entering and leaving. What Megillah says 'halacha to skip and middle ones skip' refers to positions with only three men — 'middle' fits; if Rosh Chodesh it should say 'and the second'; Rashi explained positions. Since our Gemara on Rosh Chodesh is unresolved, practice Mishna Torah plainly without dispute or concern; see Tosafot Berakhot 18a s.v. tomorrow; Mishna Torah; Megillah 31b s.v. Rosh Chodesh; so we practice per Mishna Torah (what is difficult — 'halacha for messiah' — Tosafot asked in Zevachim 45a from many sugyot where halacha is decided for messiah without the Gemara asking; they conclude from tekhelet chapter R' Yosi follows Abba Yosei ben Dostai on High Priest's loaves; Rashi and Ramban follow R' Chananel that they only ask 'halacha for messiah' with two factors — messiah and practical prohibition — see Sanhedrin 101b s.v. halacha):
