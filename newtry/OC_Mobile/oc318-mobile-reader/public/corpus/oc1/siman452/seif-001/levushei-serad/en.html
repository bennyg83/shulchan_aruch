Magen Avraham s.k. 3 For Beis Yosef also agrees; all of this is explicit in the Beis Yosef in this siman in the name of the Geonim and Toras Chatas, without any disputant, that [hagalah] helps only at the time when [the water] raises a boil; see Peri Chadash here. And what the Mechaber wrote in siman 251, se'if 3, regarding knives that one does hagalah in a kli rishon even if it is not by the fire, and it suffices when the water is yad soledes bo, as the Magen Avraham wrote there in s.k. 7 (see there): one must say that the reason is that the primary use of knives is in a kli sheini; and even though they are also used sometimes in a kli rishon, he holds, of blessed memory, in siman 451 that for every vessel we follow its majority use; therefore according to the strict law it would suffice to kasher in a kli sheini, and in the Gemara as well it says simply "in boiling water"; yet nevertheless he was stringent regarding a knife, because much becomes stuck and adhered to it, and he requires a kli rishon; and in any event he does not require that [the water] raise a boil; see Chok Yaakov s.k. 7. But a vessel whose use is in a kli rishon, and which by law requires hagalah, must have [the water] raise a boil: so one must say; and not as I saw someone who was troubled here from the law of knives above. However, as a matter of practical law, according to what we hold that a solid item has the status of a kli rishon for as long as it is yad soledes bo, as the later authorities wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 34 and Magen Avraham siman 447 s.k. 9, therefore knives with which one cuts hot solid items have the same law as anything whose use is in a kli rishon, and [the water] must raise a boil, and it does not suffice otherwise; and examine carefully.
