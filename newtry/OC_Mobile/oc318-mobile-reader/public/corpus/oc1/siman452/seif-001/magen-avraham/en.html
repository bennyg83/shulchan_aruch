If the utensils are ben yomo. Even if a utensil is ben yomo and the water does not contain 60 against it, it is nevertheless permitted before the fifth hour because this is a secondary transfer of permitted flavor. After the fifth hour this rationale no longer applies. A difficulty arises from siman 447, seif 5, where Rama prohibits foods cooked in chametz utensils and does not permit them because of a secondary transfer of flavor. One may answer that here there are several transfers: from the food to the utensil, from the utensil to the water, and from the water to the second utensil; therefore it is permitted. Whose absorption is slight. For example, spoons and cups. There is concern that they may absorb from what bowls and pots expel. But many disagree, etc. The authorities differ whether one should insist upon 60 in the water, whether the utensils must be not ben yomo, and in what order the utensils should be inserted. The custom is to observe all the stringencies. The utensils should be cleaned thoroughly, allowed to remain unused for 24 hours, and kashered while the water is actively boiling. One should first kasher utensils whose absorption is slight and afterward those whose absorption is greater. One should not keep a utensil in the water longer than necessary, and the water should not cease boiling. The hagalah vessel is kashered at the beginning and at the end. If the hagalah vessel itself is not ben yomo, it appears that technically it need not be kashered at the beginning, for it is no worse than the other utensils. If it was kashered at the beginning, it need not technically be kashered at the end when the utensils are not ben yomo. Nevertheless, the custom is to follow all the stringencies even before the fifth hour, as Bach writes. A utensil should not be inserted when the water has stopped boiling. Those performing hagalah must therefore take care not to work so rapidly, one utensil immediately after another, that the boil is lost. Bowls and spoons, whose ordinary use is through pouring, have more room for leniency; a copper pot and other utensils used as a kli rishon require true kli rishon hagalah. One should not pour water for hagalah by drawing it with another vessel from the boiling pot, because the drawn vessel is a kli sheini. If the drawing vessel is left inside the boiling pot until the water in it comes to a boil, it is considered a kli rishon. Mateh Moshe writes that hagalah should be done 3 days before Pesach; this depends upon custom. Sefer Chasidim, siman 730, writes that a Torah scholar expert in hagalah should conduct it.
