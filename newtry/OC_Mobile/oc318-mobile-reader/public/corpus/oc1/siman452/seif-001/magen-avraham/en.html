If the vessels are ben yomo. For even though the vessel is ben yomo and there is not sixty times [as much] water against it, nevertheless it is permitted, because it is a giving of taste after a giving of taste of what is permitted, which is not so after the fifth hour. And it is difficult: for in siman 447, se'if 5, the Rama forbids things cooked [in such vessels] and did not permit on the grounds of a giving of taste after a giving of taste; so why does he permit here ab initio? One may say that here, since there are many [transfers of] taste from the food to the vessel and from the vessel to the water and from the water to the vessel, it is permitted.
Whose absorption is slight. Such as spoons and cups; and one must be concerned that they will absorb from what the bowls and pots expel.
However, many dispute, etc. The words of the Rama are puzzling, for Beis Yosef also agrees to this; rather, after its prohibition, once the water has ceased boiling it is forbidden to bring it to a boil a second time, for so long as they are boiling they have not absorbed from the hagalah water, and if they ceased boiling they absorbed; but before the time of prohibition it is permitted, for it is a giving of taste after a giving of taste of what is permitted; and so wrote Hagahot Maimoniyot; and so wrote Maharshal in a responsum.
And that one not insert. So that it not absorb from the hagalah water.
At the beginning and at the end. Meaning that it suffices when one does hagalah at the beginning or at the end.
One must be careful in all, etc. Meaning that one should see that the vessels not be ben yomo; and if it is ben yomo, there must be sixty times [as much] water against it; and if one does hagalah for many vessels one after another, there must be sixty against all of them, for the prohibition returns and reawakens (Beis Yosef and Darchei Moshe in the name of the Rashba, siman 262). And in Issur VeHetter, [klal] 58, he wrote that after the fact it is permitted, for some say that even regarding food it does not return and reawaken, and if so regarding a vessel one should be lenient; and so wrote Eliyah Rabbah; and the Shach in Yoreh De'ah siman 99 disagrees with them; see there. And it appears to me that if one vessel absorbed meat and the other fish, it does not return and reawaken, since they are distinct tastes, as is written in Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 98. And one should first do hagalah for vessels whose absorption is slight, and afterward vessels whose absorption is great; and one should not leave them [in] longer than necessary; and the water should not cease boiling; and one does hagalah for the cauldron at the beginning and at the end. It appears to me that if the cauldron is not ben yomo, one need not do hagalah for it at the beginning, for it is no worse than the vessels themselves; and if one did hagalah for it at the beginning, one need not do hagalah for it at the end, for the vessels are not ben yomo; yet the practice is to be stringent with all the stringencies even before the fifth [hour], and so wrote the Bach. And this requires further study: why did the Rama specify after six, for even after four one must be careful, as is written above, for even though it is a rabbinic prohibition, nevertheless a giving of taste after a giving of taste does not apply; see siman 447, se'if 3, that regarding something that has in it only a taste of chametz we do not say it returns and reawakens; not like Olas Shabbos.
