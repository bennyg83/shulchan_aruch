Before the fifth hour. That is, the beginning of the fifth hour, the end of the fourth hour, as above in siman 443; and so it is explicitly in Tosafos and the Rosh in chapter "One who hires workers," and in chapter 25, and in chapter "Kol Shaah," and the Mordechai and the Ran and all the other posekim; and so is the wording in the Tur before us; and this is unlike some Tur volumes into which an error fell, which wrote "before the sixth hour"; and so is the consensus of the later authorities, a matter with reason: since from the fourth hour it is in any event forbidden by rabbinic law, it is not called a giving of taste after a giving of taste of what is permitted; one must be careful in these matters that there be sixty times [as much] water against the vessel, or that the vessel not be ben yomo. And if so, there appears to remain a question on the Rama: here he rules as the Mechaber without any disputant at all, and at the end of this se'if he wrote, "And when one does hagalah before Pesach after six, one must be careful in all these matters," which implies that before six one need not be careful; and how can he decide to be lenient against the implication of most of the posekim? And the Taz and the Magen Avraham already sensed part of this question, and left it as requiring further study. And one cannot say that the view of the rav is to be stringent from the sixth hour and upward, that even if the vessel is not ben yomo or there is sixty, nevertheless one must be careful in all the other matters: that is not so; for according to what we hold, and so he decided above in siman 446, se'if 2, that from six and upward until Pesach it does not forbid in any amount, and a detrimental giving of taste is also permitted, then if there is sixty or the vessels are not ben yomo, one need not be careful at all any longer, for it is a giving of taste after a giving of taste of what is permitted; and if there is not sixty and they are also ben yomo from the fourth [hour] and upward, one must likewise be careful in all the matters, since there is no giving of taste after a giving of taste of what is permitted, for it was forbidden at least by rabbinic law. And in truth the Kol Bo (and some of his words are also cited in the Beis Yosef) likewise wrote in the language of the Rama, "before six" and "after six"; but in truth there is no proof from his words there, for he follows his own view there, that he was drawn after the posekim who hold that from six and upward it is forbidden in any amount, and a detrimental giving of taste is also forbidden. (And what some editions of the Shulchan Aruch write above in siman 446, se'if 20, in the gloss on the ruling that a detrimental giving of taste is permitted, that this view follows according to everyone: that is certainly not so, for the posekim who hold that it becomes forbidden in any amount also forbid a detrimental giving of taste, for the one depends on the other; see there in the Beis Yosef, and like the view of the Kol Bo; and so wrote there Kenesses HaGedolah and Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah.) Therefore he ruled to be stringent, that if one does hagalah after six, even with a vessel that is not ben yomo and there is sixty, nevertheless one must be careful in all the matters as during Pesach; but regarding the rav, this requires further study. And it is possible that the intent of the rav is to be stringent like the view of the Kol Bo, that after six, even if there is sixty and the vessels are not ben yomo, one must be careful in all the matters; and even though he ruled above that from six and upward it is nullified in sixty, and a detrimental giving of taste is permitted, that is specifically after the fact, so as not to forbid, but regarding hagalah ab initio one should be careful and be concerned for the view of the Kol Bo and those who join him; and like this appears to be the main intent of the rav.
So that one need not scrutinize whether the vessels are ben yomo. For even if they are ben yomo it is also permitted, because it is a giving of taste after a giving of taste of what is permitted. And even though above in siman 447, se'if 5, in the gloss he forbids things cooked in their vessels and did not permit on the grounds of a giving of taste after a giving of taste, one may say that is because regarding what is cooked we hold to forbid a giving of taste after a giving of taste ab initio, as is explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 95 and in my books Minchas Yaakov, klal 50 and 58; see there. And there it is called ab initio, since one can eat it before Pesach or keep it until after Pesach; and some also say that chametz, since its name is upon it, is called absorbing issur, as I wrote above in siman 450, s.k. 14; which is not so here regarding vessels, where there are many givings of taste from the food to the vessels and from the vessels to the water and from the water to the vessel, and it will again expel from the vessel into the cooked dish during Pesach; therefore they permit here. And similarly wrote the Rosh in chapter "Kol Shaah," and so wrote the Magen Avraham, unlike some later authorities who left this as requiring further study.
Or if there is sixty times [as much] water against the vessel being hagaled. And even though one does hagalah for many vessels one after another, we do not say regarding vessels that [the issur] returns and reawakens, since it is only hagalah of vessels by means of several givings of taste of what is permitted, as I wrote in s.k. 2; and so is clearly implied from the words of the Rosh in chapter "Kol Shaah" and many other posekim, who permit to do hagalah if there is sixty against the vessel even though it is ben yomo, and even though the usual way is to do hagalah for many vessels one after another; and so wrote Issur VeHetter, klal 58, din 23. But the Shach in Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 99 challenged Issur VeHetter on this, and his words are not compelling at all to push aside the words of Issur VeHetter; and especially since the Rosh wrote explicitly in chapter "Kol Shaah" that for every vessel that one inserts, the expulsion of its issur is nullified in sixty. And even though the Rosh ruled at the end of chapter Gid HaNasheh that we say it returns and reawakens, whether it became known in the meantime or not, as is also written in Toras Chatas, klal 85, din 10, and in my books Minchas Yaakov there, s.k. 31, and not like the Peri Chadash in Yoreh De'ah siman 99, who wrote in his name the opposite: one must say that there is a distinction between the taste of expulsion and absorption of vessels versus food, and it is easy to understand. But in the Beis Yosef and Darchei Moshe he wrote in the name of a responsum of the Rashba to be stringent even regarding vessels as to returning and reawakening; however, from what the rav wrote in the gloss it appears that his view is to be lenient regarding vessels, like the view of Issur VeHetter and the implication of most of the early posekim.
And likewise if one does hagalah for vessels. Meaning that in all of this one need not be careful when one does hagalah before the fifth hour; which is not so after the fifth hour, when one must be careful in all this if the vessels are ben yomo and there is also not sixty against the vessels; but if there is sixty or the vessels are not ben yomo, one need not be careful in all this, only from six and upward, as I wrote in s.k. 1 according to the view of the rav in the gloss.
He does not leave them so long. At first glance this has no explanation or relevance here at all; and if his intent is like the view of the Kol Bo cited in the Taz s.k. 2, that one must be careful not to hurry so much to remove the vessel from the cauldron, so that it have time to expel: if so, even when one does hagalah before the fifth hour one must also be careful in this. It is possible that this is what he means: when one does hagalah before the fifth [hour], then one need not be careful not to leave the vessels too long, nor be careful if one does not leave them so long, [but rather] in a middle way; for since then it is permitted to leave them longer than necessary, one needs no further warning; which is not so when one does hagalah afterward, when one must be careful not to leave them too long, and also not to hurry so much, but only in a middle way; and it is easy to understand.
However, many dispute. And the later authorities wrote that here the rav erred, for Beis Yosef's view is also so; rather his intent is only that one not leave the vessels in them after they have ceased boiling, for [then] they absorb from the hagalah water.
That they not cease boiling. For then it is forbidden to do hagalah in them; nevertheless, if they again raise a boil, it is permitted to return and do hagalah in it, not like those who err in this; so wrote Maharshal in a responsum, siman 26. And he wrote in Terumat HaDeshen and Agur: when one inserts the vessel to do hagalah, the water ceases boiling; therefore for every vessel one must wait until the water raises bubbles; end quote. And my brother-in-law the rav wrote in Eliyah Zuta: and those who do hagalah must be careful in this law, for they practice lightness in this and do hagalah quickly one after another and are not careful to see whether [the water] raises a boil; and it is possible to find merit for them, for it is not the usual way to do hagalah except for bowls and spoons whose use is by pouring, for which hagalah helps in water that is yad soledes bo, etc.; nevertheless, regarding a copper pot and things whose use is by means of a kli rishon, it appears to me that even after the fact this hagalah does not help; end quote. And in Sefer Chasidim, siman 730, he wrote that a Torah scholar expert in hagalah is the one who should do the hagalah; and I have not seen [people] practicing thus, but rather they rely on the women who do hagalah; and possibly the reason they are lenient is that for the most part it is not the usual way to do hagalah except for what is used by pouring and a kli sheini; also they first do hagalah at home by means of a white-hot stone, as I wrote above in siman 421, s.k. 67; and regarding a copper pan, where they do not practice to do libun by means of a white-hot stone, nevertheless they practice to first do libun for the handle and insert it into the cauldron, by means of which it raises a boil greatly.
That one does hagalah with it at the beginning and at the end. The Bach ruled to be stringent even when one does hagalah before the fifth [hour] and the vessel is not ben yomo, to do hagalah for the cauldron at the beginning; and in truth from the outset they practice to do hagalah, and not at the end; and this too is only as a stringency, since they do hagalah before the fifth hour and [the vessels] are not ben yomo; and even though the Taz at the end of this siman is stringent in this, he follows his own view according to what I wrote in s.k. 7, and in truth he erred in this, as I wrote in s.k. 11. "For then chametz [forbids] in any amount, etc.": meaning on Chol HaMoed; for on Yom Tov it is forbidden to do hagalah: Taz.
One must be careful in all the matters. Meaning even if the vessel is not ben yomo and there is also sixty, as I wrote in s.k. 1.
