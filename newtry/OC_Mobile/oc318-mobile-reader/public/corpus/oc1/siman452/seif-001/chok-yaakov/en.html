Before the fifth hour. This means before the beginning of the fifth hour, namely, by the end of the fourth hour, as stated above in siman 443. This is explicit in Tosafos and Rosh in Perek HaSocher Et HaPoel, in Perek Kol HaBasar, and in Perek Kol Sha'ah, as well as in Mordechai, Ran, and the other halachic authorities. This is also the reading in our copies of Tur, unlike some copies in which a scribal error reads "before the sixth hour." The later authorities agree, and the reason is clear: from the fifth hour chametz is at least rabbinically prohibited, so it is no longer considered a secondary transfer of permitted flavor. One must therefore ensure either that the water contains 60 times the utensil or that the utensil is not ben yomo. Accordingly there is an apparent difficulty in Rama. Here he accepts the Mechaber's ruling without dispute, while at the end of the seif he writes that when one performs hagalah before Pesach after the sixth hour he must be careful about all these matters, implying that before the sixth hour he need not be careful. How can he rule leniently against the implication of most authorities? Taz and Magen Avraham already sensed part of this difficulty and left it requiring study. It cannot be answered that Rama means that from the sixth hour onward one must be careful even when the utensil is not ben yomo or there is 60, for according to the accepted halacha those factors remain relevant. The wording therefore requires careful interpretation. The practical rule is that before the fifth hour, when the absorbed chametz is still permitted, several successive transfers of flavor may be treated leniently. From the fifth hour onward, however, one must observe the safeguards stated by the authorities. Where the utensils are ben yomo, the water must contain 60 against them; alternatively, the utensils must be allowed to remain unused for 24 hours. The utensils should be thoroughly cleaned before hagalah so that no tangible chametz remains upon them. The water used for hagalah must remain boiling. One should not insert utensils one after another so rapidly that the boil ceases. If the water stopped boiling, one should wait until it boils again before inserting the next utensil. This is especially important for pots and utensils whose ordinary use is in a kli rishon. Bowls and spoons whose ordinary absorption is through pouring are more lenient, but one should nevertheless perform hagalah correctly. A utensil should not remain in the hagalah water longer than necessary, because once it has expelled what it absorbed there is concern that it may reabsorb from the water. On the other hand, it must remain long enough for the boiling water to act upon it and cause the absorbed flavor to be expelled. Thus the language of Tur that one inserts and removes it "immediately" does not mean literally without any pause. Utensils whose absorption is slight should preferably be kashered before utensils whose absorption is greater, so that the water remains cleaner of absorbed flavor. One should likewise ensure that the utensils are dry from cold water before immersion, so the hagalah water is not cooled. Regarding the large vessel in which the hagalah is performed, the authorities discuss whether it must itself be kashered before and after the procedure. Where it is not ben yomo and hagalah is performed before the fifth hour, there is substantial basis to be lenient. Nevertheless, the custom is to kasher it at the beginning as a stringency. Some also require kashering it afterward, especially under circumstances where the water absorbed prohibited flavor. The later authorities strongly criticize the common practice of conducting hagalah carelessly and rapidly without verifying that the water remains at a full boil. A Torah scholar knowledgeable in the laws of hagalah should ideally supervise the procedure. Sefer Chasidim, siman 730, writes likewise. Rama's statement that "then chametz prohibits in the slightest amount" refers to Chol HaMoed. On Yom Tov itself hagalah may not be performed. When the prohibition of chametz has already taken effect, one must be careful with all the stated conditions, even where the utensil is not ben yomo and even where there is 60, in accordance with the stringent practices recorded by the later authorities.
