2) Regarding how long a utensil must remain in the cauldron during kashering, Rif in the chapter Kol Sha'ah writes that it should remain there until it expels what it absorbed. Ran writes likewise there, as do Rambam, chapter 5, halachah 23; Raavad in Sefer Tamim De'im, siman 25; and Rashba in Toras HaBayis, bayis 4, sha'ar 4. Radbaz in Leshonos HaRambam, siman 241, demonstrates that this is Rambam's view. See there. Tur, however, writes that the custom is to insert the utensils and remove them immediately. He writes likewise in Yoreh De'ah, siman 121. See there. Mordechai, chapter Kol Sha'ah, siman 579, writes likewise, as cited by Beis Yosef. Levush, se'if 2, also writes likewise. Peri Chadash, at the end of se'if 6, writes that the Jewish people's custom has the force of Torah. Taz, subsection 2, however, writes that Tur's statement to remove them 'immediately' is not literal; they must remain at least briefly. Kol Bo likewise writes that one must be careful not to hurry to remove a utensil from the cauldron, allowing time for the boiling water to enter it and extract what it absorbed. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4; Ben Ish Chai, parashas Tzav, subsection 12, write likewise. See below, subsection 14.
