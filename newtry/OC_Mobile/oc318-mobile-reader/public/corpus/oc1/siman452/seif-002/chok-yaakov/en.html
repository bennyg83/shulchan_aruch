Meat vessels and milk vessels together. See above siman 509 in the Magen Avraham, s.k. 11, for the reason of those who practice not to do hagalah for a milk vessel in order to eat meat in it, or the reverse; and see there regarding the law of hagalah and libun on Yom Tov.
One of them is not ben yomo. And we do not decree ab initio a pot that is not ben yomo on account of one that is ben yomo, in the way that we forbid cooking in a vessel that is not ben yomo for that reason: for there, if one cooks in a pot that is ben yomo it is forbidden according to the Torah, and also what they cook at home, people would easily err if we permitted ab initio to cook in a vessel that is not ben yomo; but here, even with a ben yomo vessel, if it returns and absorbs a little of what was expelled, it is a small matter and it is a giving of taste after a giving of taste; and also one who comes to kasher a vessel is not accustomed to err. All this the Rosh wrote in chapter "Kol Shaah"; and the Taz in s.k. 7 sensed this difficulty and entered into a strained answer, and it was unnecessary, for these words of the Rosh escaped him.
Of them is not ben yomo or there is sixty against it; and all this during the rest of the days of the year or on erev Pesach before the fifth hour; which is not so afterward, when one must be careful with each of them that it not be ben yomo or [have] sixty, as is explained in se'if 1; and see in the Levush.
And all the more so a vessel of issur. That it must be that they are not ben yomo, or that there be sixty times [as much] water against it; and then it is permitted to do hagalah even for the vessel of issur first; and so wrote the Beis Yosef in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot; and so it is in a responsum of Maharam of Rothenburg, siman 429; and in Issur VeHetter, klal 58, din 56.
Any vessels that are ben yomo. Even alone, and before the fifth hour, and even if there is sixty. And the Magen Avraham wrote: even tables, one should scrub well and wait after the scrubbing twenty-four hours so that they not be ben yomo and so that they be dry of the cold water, lest the boiling [water] be cooled by them; and one should be careful to pour upon them with alacrity, and not by throwing through the air, for that is not considered pouring; and one should be careful to pour upon them from a kli rishon in which one boiled the water, and not by drawing from a kli rishon, unless one needs to leave the vessel inside the kli rishon, for then it too is called a kli rishon; end quote, in the name of Maharil. And I have not seen [people] careful in all this, because they do hagalah by means of a white-hot stone that maintains the heat so that it not cool, and it is like a kli rishon, as is explained above in siman 451, se'if 6, in the gloss; and also regarding waiting twenty-four hours they are not careful by the tables and benches, since most of their use is by cold; and if it is known that boiling was poured upon it within twenty-four hours and the like, one needs a wait of twenty-four hours from that pouring. But as for what he required to wait twenty-four hours from the scrubbing, I do not know a place for this stringency; for every vessel that is not ben yomo means from the use in which something was used upon it, and there is no difference at all from what adhered upon it in cold until now, as I wrote in my books Minchas Yaakov, klal 58, s.k. 15; unless we be concerned that perhaps by the scrubbing, when one scrubbed hot water upon it, it absorbed some thing stuck upon it: this too is a remote concern, etc. Maharil; and in Mateh Moshe and in the minhagim [it is written] that one should do hagalah three days before Pesach.
