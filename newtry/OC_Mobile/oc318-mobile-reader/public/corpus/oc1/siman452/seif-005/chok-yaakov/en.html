Hagalah is not performed with any other liquid. The same applies to water mixed with ashes, called lye. So writes Beit Yosef. See my Minchas Yaakov, klal 58, seif katan 26, and Peri Chadash, siman 95, seif katan 19, who rules accordingly. Therefore, the kashering performed at home on tin utensils by means of heated stones and lye-water does not constitute proper hagalah, especially since people do it while the utensils are dirty. See the end of siman 451, seif katan 67. One must therefore be careful with large utensils that are not inserted into a cauldron, to clean them thoroughly and use water alone, unlike the common practice with small tin utensils.
One may not perform hagalah again. After the fact it is permitted, as with other liquids, though Ran requires study. Issur VeHeter, klal 58, din 72, writes that one should not initially use water in which forbidden utensils were kashered, even where the water contained sixty times the utensil, because intending to use the water constitutes deliberately nullifying a prohibition.
