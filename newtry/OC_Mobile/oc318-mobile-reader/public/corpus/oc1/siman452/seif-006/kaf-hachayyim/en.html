6) There, "so that one will not need," etc. This means that before the fifth hour one need not ensure that the utensil is not ben yomo. Even if it is ben yomo and the water does not contain 60 against the utensils, it is permitted because chametz is still permitted and this is a secondary transfer of permitted flavor. Tosafos, Chullin 108b and Avodah Zarah 76a; Rosh at the end of Avodah Zarah and in Perek Kol Sha'ah; Mordechai, Perek Kol Sha'ah; Ran at the end of Perek Kol HaBasar. Tur and Beit Yosef cite them. Olat Shabbos, letter 1; Magen Avraham, seif katan 1; Chok Yaakov, letter 2; Chok Yosef, letter 1; Mekor Chaim, letter 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, letter 13; Chayei Adam, rule 125, letter 33, write likewise. Although Rama above in siman 447, seif 5, prohibits food cooked in a chametz utensil and does not rely on secondary transfer of flavor, here there are several transfers, from the food to the utensil, from the utensil to the water, and from the water to the second utensil, and it is therefore permitted. Magen Avraham, Chok Yaakov, and Mekor Chaim there. All this is the strict law, but the custom is not to kasher any utensil that is ben yomo, as stated below in Rama to seif 2. See below, letter 21, regarding whether the hagalah vessel itself must first be kashered.
