Even if it was used that day and the water does not equal sixty times the utensils, it is permitted, because chametz is still permitted and this is a secondary transfer of permitted flavor. Tosafos, Chullin 108b and Avodah Zarah 76a; Rosh at the end of Avodah Zarah and in the chapter Kol Sha'ah; Mordechai, chapter Kol Sha'ah; Ran at the end of the chapter Kol HaBasar. Tur and Beis Yosef cite these authorities. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1; Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Chok Yaakov, subsection 2; Chok Yosef, subsection 1; Mekor Chaim, subsection 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 13; Chayei Adam, there, subsection 33, write likewise. Although the gloss above in siman 447, se'if 5, prohibits foods cooked in a chametz utensil and does not permit them because of a secondary transfer of flavor, here there are several successive transfers: from the food to the utensil, from the utensil to the water, and from the water to the second utensil. It is therefore permitted. Magen Avraham, there; Chok Yaakov, there; Mekor Chaim, there. All this is the strict law, but the custom is not to kasher any utensil that was used that day, as stated below in the gloss to se'if 2. See there.
