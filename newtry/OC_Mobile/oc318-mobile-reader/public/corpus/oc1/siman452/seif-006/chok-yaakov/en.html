He should form a rim at its mouth with clay. During the rest of the year one may form a rim from dough, as stated in Kol Shaah. Beis Hillel, Yoreh Deah 121, seif katan 1, writes that Rosh in Kol Shaah clearly implies that only regarding chametz for Pesach, where permitted matter was absorbed, is such a rim effective for a large utensil. For other prohibitions, a large utensil must first undergo hagalah once, and afterward a rim is needed for its lip. According to him, this distinction applies only to other prohibitions, whereas for chametz before Pesach a first hagalah is unnecessary before refilling it with boiling water and using the rim. He adds that Rif in chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah, who cites the case of Mar Ukva, does not imply such a distinction. These words are astonishing. Rosh contains no implication whatever supporting such a distinction. His reference to performing hagalah on the cauldron twice concerns a case where utensils are kashered within that cauldron, so the cauldron must undergo hagalah before and after, as the Mechaber wrote above in seif 1. Likewise, the claim regarding Rif is even more difficult, for Rosh also cites that case at the end of HaSocher Es HaPoel. The rule is that there is no distinction at all in this law, except that a dough rim cannot be used during Pesach itself, as is evident from the Gemara and all the authorities.
And the water should rise over its rim. If a utensil is used on its outside, such as a bowl that is sometimes inverted over a pot, or a vessel used to draw food from a pot, its exterior also requires hagalah. Magen Avraham writes in the name of Maharil that a narrow-necked utensil that cannot be scrubbed inside, or a utensil with tubes, cannot be kashered through hagalah.
