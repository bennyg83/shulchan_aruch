A rim around its mouth with clay. During the rest of the year one makes a dough rim, as stated in Perek Kol Sha'ah. Beit Hillel on Yoreh De'ah, siman 121, seif katan 1, writes in the name of Rosh that specifically regarding chametz before Pesach, where the utensil absorbed permitted matter, a rim is effective for a large utensil. For other prohibitions, however, a rim by itself is insufficient. The large utensil must first undergo hagalah once, and afterward, because of its lip, a rim is used. For chametz before Pesach, according to this view, the first hagalah is unnecessary before refilling it with boiling water and using the rim. These words require study. Rif in the second chapter of Avodah Zarah, who cites the case of Mar Ukva, does not indicate this distinction, and neither does Rosh. The Rosh's statement about kashering the hagalah vessel twice concerns a vessel in which other utensils are kashered, which requires hagalah before and after, as the Mechaber writes above in seif 1. There is therefore no basis to distinguish this law between chametz and other prohibitions. The only clear difference is that during Pesach itself a dough rim cannot be relied upon, as appears from the Gemara and the halachic authorities. The boiling water must rise over the utensil's rim. If a utensil is used also on its exterior, such as a bowl that is sometimes inverted over a pot, or a vessel used to draw food from a pot, its exterior likewise requires hagalah. Magen Avraham writes in the name of Maharil that a narrow-necked utensil that cannot be scrubbed inside, or a utensil containing tubes, cannot be kashered through hagalah.
