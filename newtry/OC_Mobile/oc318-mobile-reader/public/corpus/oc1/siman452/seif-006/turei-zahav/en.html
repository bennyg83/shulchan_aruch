(א) A large vessel etc. — meaning one wants to kasher it and is concerned perhaps there is chametz on its lip where water does not reach; and the same for a pot after one kashered vessels in it and wants to use on Pesach — one must make a lip; but initially before kashering vessels and kashering the pot first from prohibition in it — one need not make a lip, for regarding hagalah of vessels done in it there is no concern either way: if water power does not reach the lip to kasher it, also the lip absorption has no power to expel and absorb in vessels kashered by water; and if water has power to expel from there — then boiling in the pot also helps the lip to kasher it; not so in kashering a pot after hagalah — we certainly are concerned perhaps water did not reach the lip in all places it already received, and afterward when one cooks in the pot on Pesach one fills it and the lip forbids the cooked food; therefore one must make a clay lip; and so Tur words in the name of Geonim: rinse a large pot, kasher in boiling, kasher it, rinse in cold, expel first from chametz, then boil boiling water without measure (meaning one need not fill it except as needed for kashering vessels), kasher in it small pots and knives and wooden spoon and vessels needing hagalah in kli rishon; and be careful the water not cease from boiling, for if they cease it is forbidden to kasher in them and noten taam lifgam; rinse in cold; afterward fill the large pot, surround its lip with clay, and kasher per the first method and pour and rinse — end of his words; behold one need make clay only for what one wants to kasher afterward for Pesach; but initially when coming to kasher the pot to kasher in it — one did not need this, for the reason I mentioned; and even though Tur wrote at end per first method — this refers only to hagalah not the lip; and now the practice is to make first also a clay lip or insert a stone, and this is on the better side lest the kashered vessel touch the pot lip; and so concludes my teacher and father zl.

(ב) And the water reaches its lip — we say in the Gemara regarding this: as it absorbs through sparks so it expels through sparks — Rashi explained they do not absorb except through sparks that boiling splashed there, and it was not inserted into another pot at time of prohibition absorption; so at time of expulsion, even though not inserted into another pot, since it filled well and raises sparks on its lip — it expels; however Gednafa requires, for otherwise perhaps sparks do not ascend on the lip this time; but prohibition was cooked in it many times, and if it did not raise sparks every hour it raised once — end of his words; it implies where chametz entered inside the pot, boiling alone does not kasher it, but specifically to insert it into a pot to kasher it — a great rule: as its use so one kashered it. Mateh Yehuda zl wrote: accordingly bowls inverted sometimes on the griddle or drawn from the pot with a bowl, as he wrote in his heart — one must kasher it by inserting into another pot; but to pour boiling from kli rishon on them, even placing white-hot stones on them — all this is called only irui, not kli rishon — end of his words; and this stringency with white-hot stones is not from law but general stringency, for Shulchan Aruch siman 451 seif 6 wrote this kashering is hagalah in kli rishon; and Rama brought there seif 5 on large bowls to place white-hot stones etc.; therefore certainly this helps for all needing hagalah in kli rishon, provided boiling goes over all of it and its sides. Beit Yosef wrote: it appears to me per what our rabbi wrote in the name of Rosh requiring kashering the large pot — specifically when vessels kashered in it were ben yomo and there was not sixty in the water against them and hagalah was after four hours which is chametz prohibition time; not so if the pot was kashered before kashering in it — one need not return and kasher it — end of his words; and it appears one should be stringent l'chatchila also in this, for the essence of permission is because it is noten taam bar noten taam d'heter; we already mentioned Yoreh De'ah siman 95 seif 1 in the name of Semak that it is forbidden l'chatchila to make noten taam bar noten taam, and forbidden to cook fish in a meat vessel so afterward one eats with kutach — so too forbidden l'chatchila the pot and relying that it will be noten taam bar noten taam; one may say this is to kasher a vessel that is not ben yomo with a ben yomo vessel, as seif 2 there — absorption is not certain since the vessel is busy expelling — perhaps it did not absorb at all, as written there; not so here in a pot that certainly absorbs from vessels kashered in it, for it is not removed from them always — expulsion of the vessel remains — therefore forbidden l'chatchila; and so the practice is to kasher the pot after hagalah in all ways; and sometimes when there is prohibition in vessel expulsion, since it is possible to kasher it — one does not refrain. Mateh Yehuda zl is stringent because it is possible to wait twenty-four hours — this is insufficient, for vessels he kasher on erev Pesach before prohibition time and he needs them for Pesach; but b'dieved if one used it on Pesach it is not forbidden even in a case where vessels were kashered in it, in my humble opinion. Women practice not to use a pot into which they pour boiling on the stone with which they kasher vessels, because chametz smell entered it; yet there is no smell or taste — no prohibition at all on Pesach.
