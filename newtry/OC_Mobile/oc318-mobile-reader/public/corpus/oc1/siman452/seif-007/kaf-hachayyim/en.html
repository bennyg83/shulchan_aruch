7) An incident occurred in which a piece of bread was found in the hagalah vessel in which utensils were being kashered before Pesach, and they asked whether the utensils required hagalah a second time. Beit David, siman 195, concludes that according to communities that do not apply chatichah na'asit neveilah, it is obvious that the utensils are not prohibited, because the flavor expelled from the bread into the water was nullified in 60. Even according to communities that do apply chatichah na'asit neveilah, a second hagalah is unnecessary, because we have not seen that they accepted this rule regarding mere flavor as opposed to tangible substance. Although they customarily kasher kneading utensils belonging to communities that are lenient, this is only an initial stringency. Since the matter rests on custom, we should not add another stringency. If, however, it is clearly established that their custom is to prohibit even after the fact because of flavor alone, then these utensils certainly require hagalah again. See Beit David there; Zechor LeAvraham, end of siman 451; and above, siman 447, letters 59 and 76.
