7) An incident occurred in which a piece of bread was found in the cauldron used to kasher utensils before Pesach. The question was whether the utensils had to be kashered a second time. Beis David, siman 195, concludes that according to communities that do not apply the rule that a piece becomes intrinsically prohibited, it is obvious that the utensils are not prohibited, because the flavor expelled from the bread into the water was nullified in sixty. Even according to communities that do apply that rule, a second kashering is unnecessary. Their acceptance of the rule was observed only regarding a tangible substance, not mere flavor. Although they customarily kasher kneading utensils belonging to lenient communities, and so forth, that is only an initial stringency. Since this is a custom, we should not add further stringency. If it is clearly established, however, that their custom is to prohibit even after the fact because of flavor alone, then these utensils must certainly be kashered again. See there.
