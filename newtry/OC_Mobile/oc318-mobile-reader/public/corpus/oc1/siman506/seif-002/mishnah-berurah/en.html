{5} One does not sift, etc. For all melachos before kneading the Sages forbade, because the way is to do them for a long time ahead; and according to several poskim they are forbidden according to the Torah, as was explained above in siman 495; and even if it was not possible for him to sift on erev Yom Tov.
{6} Even by means of a change. And as with all melachos forbidden on Yom Tov, that even with a change it is forbidden, as was explained above in siman 495 in the Mishnah Berurah.
{7} And a pebble fell into it, etc. That is, on Yom Tov; for if it was from erev Yom Tov he should have sifted yesterday, and it is permitted only by means of a change [Olat Shabbos].
{8} It is permitted. For his intent is not to sift in order to improve the flour, but rather to select thereby the pebbles and twigs that will remain in the sieve after the flour falls through the holes; and this the Sages did not forbid, for it fell on Yom Tov itself, as above. And it is unlike what is explained below in siman 510, se'if 2, that it is forbidden to select legumes from their waste with a sifter and sieve [and that is certainly forbidden even if he took them from a shopkeeper who recognizes him on Yom Tov itself and could not have selected them on erev Yom Tov]; for there the way of the melacha is to do it for a long time ahead, which is not so here, where the flour was already selected from the bran before dark, and it happened that a pebble or twig fell into it; it is apparent to all that it is for the momentary need to bake it on Yom Tov, and it is permitted.
{9} Even without a change. And some are stringent that even in this a change is required [Bach, and Eliyah Rabbah cited him; and see Chayei Adam, whose view is that a complete change is needed here, such as to sift with the back of the sieve or by means of a non-Jew]. And likewise one who crushes matzos on Yom Tov and needs to sift the flour milled from the crumbs should sift by means of a change [namely the back of the sieve according to the view of Chayei Adam].
{10} And some say it is permitted, etc. Meaning even this is permitted; and likewise it is permitted by means of sifting, according to the view of the Mechaber.
{11} But some are stringent. For since even on a weekday the way is to take a pebble by hand, it is like borer. And according to this view, likewise one should not take by hand the large crumbs from flour milled when crushing matzos on Yom Tov [Chayei Adam].
{12} And they forbid. That is, regarding taking a pebble by hand; but by means of sifting they too agree to the Mechaber's view that it is permitted; and see what we wrote in s.k. 9 regarding the view of the Bach. And it is proper to practice according to this view [Peri Chadash]. And likewise if flies fell into a cup, he should not remove them alone; rather he must take some of the liquid with them as well [Taz]. And likewise one who drinks beer and at its bottom there are dregs should leave a little beer upon the dregs [Peri Megadim].
{13} A slight change is needed. Even though it is ochel nefesh, behold it was possible for him to sift yesterday; and also this is interchangeable with a first sifting; which is not so when a pebble or twig fell, as above, for everyone knows that the sifting is only for the momentary need that the pebble and twig fall out, and also that otherwise it would not suffice and he would be withheld from the joy of Yom Tov [Chidushei HaRaah].
{14} For example, upon the table. For on a weekday the way is to sift upon a chest or kneading-trough; and if this person's way is to sift even on a weekday upon a table, he should make a different change; and all the more so if he sifts upon the back of the sieve from the other side, it is permitted. And the later authorities wrote that all this is if the second sieve does not have holes smaller than the first sieve, and whatever came out in the first will come out in the second; but if the second sieve has fine holes, it is certainly forbidden, for the coarse flour will remain in the sieve and the fine will come out, and it is like a first sifting. And Machatzit HaShekel wrote that according to this, even in our times when flours are sifted at the mill house, nevertheless one should not sift them again at home even by means of a change, for we see that sifting at the mill house does not remove all the waste, and the home sieve removes [more], and it is like a first sifting.
{15} If he changes, etc. Because there is one who says that even a Jew is permitted to sift by means of a change; even though most poskim disagree with him, nevertheless one may rely on his words to be lenient with a change by means of a non-Jew.
{16} A little. And the Peri Chadash is stringent even by means of a non-Jew; and it is possible that with a complete change, such as the back of a sieve, he agrees that it is permitted by means of a non-Jew [Peri Megadim].
