1) [Se'if 1] Flour may not be measured on Yom Tov, etc. The reason is that it could have been measured the preceding day. This is not something required for the pot, regarding which one might say that doing it beforehand would diminish the flavor or cause the cooked food to burn. Beis Yosef; Levush; Magen Avraham, subsection 1. Levush writes there that measuring is a primary category of melachah, but Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1, already questions this, because it is merely a weekday activity. See there. See above, siman 306, se'if 7, and our words there, subsection 61.
2) There. Flour may not be measured on Yom Tov, etc. It is a weekday activity and appears like selling. Even when measuring for oneself and selling nothing, it is forbidden. Even if one intends to perform a mitzvah, such as measuring the flour for his dough to determine the quantity requiring challah, he should not measure it but should take an estimated amount. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1. The law concerning measuring flour on Yom Tov of Pesach was explained above, at the end of siman 456; take it from there.
3) There. In order to knead. If one takes flour for a cooked accompaniment or a similar food that requires a measured quantity, it appears that measuring is permitted. Eshel Avraham, subsection 1; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 23, Likutei Rima, subsection 2; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 2.
4) There. Instead, he should take an estimated amount. If he takes the flour with a vessel designated for measuring, he must add or subtract a small amount. Bach; Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, subsection 2; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Pri Chadash; Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Chayei Adam, rule 85, subsection 1; Likutei Rima there, subsection 1; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 98, subsection 9; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 3.
5) There. Instead, he should take an estimated amount. There is no concern that he might take more than is needed for Yom Tov. Even if he takes more, there is no concern, because when the oven is full the bread bakes well. With our ovens, however, in which bread is not attached around the oven wall, it is explained below, siman 507, se'if 6, that one may not bake more than is needed for Yom Tov. Yeshuos Yaakov, subsection 1, therefore permits measuring flour on Yom Tov for this reason. Nevertheless, the unqualified wording of Tur, Shulchan Aruch, and the other later authorities implies that no distinction is made, and measuring is forbidden in every case. The reason appears to me to be that the statement that one may not bake more than needed in our ovens applies only when one knows with certainty that the additional amount is unnecessary. When one takes an estimated amount, however, it is only doubtful whether he will take a little more. This is not called baking more than necessary, because he might eat that small amount. Furthermore, it is impossible to precisely determine how much one will eat, for sometimes he eats little and sometimes more. This is clear.
6) There, in the gloss. It is permitted to take flour from the vessel, etc. This means that one need not pour the flour from the vessel into the kneading trough, but may take it from the vessel with his hands. This is permitted although he makes a depression in the flour remaining in the vessel. The prohibition against making a depression applies neither to flour nor to fruit. That prohibition is due only to building, and building applies only to the ground or something attached to the ground. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 23, subsection 2; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 4.
7) There, in the gloss. It is permitted to take flour, etc. This implies that making a depression applies to sand and earth when the material is hard, even if detached from the ground. Accordingly, those who on Yom Tov insert a wax candle into hard sand, or remove it from there, do not act properly, because they make a depression. Ran writes that it is forbidden to bury an egg in loose earth unless the earth is so loose that when the egg is placed upon it, the egg enters by itself. Tosafos write likewise. Shabbos 113a, however, implies that one may remove an inserted object even though this makes a depression. Magen Avraham, siman 498, subsection 32. See there. See above, siman 498, subsection 152, and below, siman 514, subsection 44.
7) There, in the gloss. Although he makes a depression in the flour. It is forbidden, however, to take ashes from a stove if doing so makes a depression. The words of Tosafos and Rosh imply that it is permitted, but one should not be lenient in this matter. Pri Chadash. See above, siman 498, se'if 15, and our words there, with Heaven's help.
