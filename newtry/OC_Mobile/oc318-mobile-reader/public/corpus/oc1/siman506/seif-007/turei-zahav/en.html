And it is permitted to bake the bread, etc. The Mordechai wrote that one should not bake it on Yom Tov, for he cannot eat it; however, if he rolled it and baked it on erev Yom Tov and did not separate challah, he may eat it, provided he leaves enough for challah, etc., end of his words. And Beit Yosef wrote on this: and one should wonder why he cannot eat it, for with challah of chutz la'aretz he eats and continues and afterward separates; and so appears the view of the poskim that he may bake it on Yom Tov, end of his words. And therefore the Rama ruled that it is permitted to bake, etc. And it appears to me to answer: what he said 'he cannot eat' means that he must leave the kohen's portion; fair enough with other dough one bakes the kohen's portion for the kohen, which is not so here, for today the kohen will not eat his portion, since it is forbidden to separate; if so he bakes one portion not for the need of Yom Tov. And it is difficult, for it is impossible to divide the challah from the dough on Yom Tov; it turns out that division is impossible; if so it is similar to an animal half of which belongs to a non-Jew, which is permitted in slaughter on Yom Tov because it is impossible [to get] a kezayis of meat without slaughter, as stated in siman 491, se'if 11; so too here. And that dough half of which belongs to a non-Jew, which is forbidden to bake on Yom Tov as stated in se'if 5, that is because it is possible to divide them in kneading; and here that does not apply. And one can say: since at the time of kneading it was a weekday and it was possible to separate it, and he did not separate, it turns out that the hindrance is from his side that he did not do properly, not from the side of the dough; we properly call it 'it was possible to divide them in kneading,' and it is forbidden, as it appears to me. His reason is correct; and it appears that he agrees that if he transgressed and baked, it is permitted to eat and leave over. And it is possible that the poskim who wrote that it is forbidden to separate challah in this and did not mention baking are discussing a case where he transgressed and baked; and although this is somewhat forced, since the Mordechai's view is explicitly so and we do not find an explicit [source] to contradict his words, it is proper to be concerned for his words since he has a correct reason; and so my father-in-law of blessed memory wrote.
