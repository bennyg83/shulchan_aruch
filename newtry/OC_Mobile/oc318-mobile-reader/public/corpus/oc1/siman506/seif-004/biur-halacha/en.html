"Rather, she sets it aside until evening and burns it." The Mishnah states that she sets it aside until evening and then burns it. Gra already notes the difficulty, because the accepted rule is that consecrated items are not burned at night. The case appears to concern challah or terumah that became definitely impure, as is evident from the sugya in Bameh Madlikin and from the related Talmudic discussions. The early authorities already addressed this wording. Ramban, Rashba, Ritva, Ran, and Maharam Chalava cite the Yerushalmi and discuss whether the phrase should be understood literally, whether this burning differs from the ordinary burning of sacred items, or whether the text reflects an opinion in the Yerushalmi that permits such burning at night. We do not generally rule in accordance with an opinion that allows burning sacred items at night. The wording of the Mishnah therefore requires interpretation in light of the explanations of those Rishonim. See their discussions in the cited passages.
