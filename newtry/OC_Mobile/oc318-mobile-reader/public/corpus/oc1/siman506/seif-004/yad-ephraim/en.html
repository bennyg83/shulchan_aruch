In Magen Avraham, s.k. 8: that bread has no remedy. And at first glance it appears that if he has matzos dipped for challah in hand, he may take one of them to join in a basket with chametz bread and leave the matzah to separate from it on Chol HaMoed, as the Peri Chadash wrote in siman 497, that by means of basket-joining it is permitted to eat and continue, etc.; and so wrote Mekor Chaim (except that he disagrees with the Peri Chadash and holds that regarding eating and continuing, basket-joining does not help; and I already wrote on this above). And it appears at first glance that even though the Peri Chadash there holds specifically to join them from erev Yom Tov, but basket-joining on Yom Tov is forbidden, for it is considered fixing: here, where both have the amount for challah, one needs no basket-joining, only adjacency before him suffices, and this is not fixing even according to the Peri Chadash. But this is not so, for the view of the later authorities is that also regarding being contiguous one needs basket-joining, as Sefer HaTerumah and Semag wrote; and also according to the Rosh it was said only when he wants to separate immediately while they are adjacent before him; which is not so in this, where he wants to eat and continue and separate after Yom Tov: it is obvious that at least without basket-joining it does not help, even according to the Peri Chadash's explanation of Beit Yosef's words; and so Darkei Moshe understood Beit Yosef's words, as I wrote above. And since it is not permitted without joining, again it is fixing and forbidden according to the Peri Chadash. And so too it is proven from the words of Tosafos and the Mordechai, who found no remedy in basket-joining; and necessarily either it does not help for eating and continuing, or it is considered fixing and forbidden on Yom Tov. And if so there is no place to do so with this bread to fix it by means of basket-joining; for even if we say it is not considered fixing, necessarily we must say it does not help for eating and continuing, and either way it is forbidden. But there is still room to say, according to what I wrote above in the name of the Taz, that if the obligation comes by means of joining, it is permitted to eat and continue; and if so, if he has matzos from dough kneaded with less than the measure, he may join it in a basket and eat and continue and leave the matzah; and although the Peri Chadash forbids because of fixing, that is not the implication of Tosafos and the poskim, as Mekor Chaim wrote there, for they wrote to knead a small dough and join, etc. However, in truth it appears one can resolve the Peri Chadash's view, that he holds as I wrote above that what they wrote to knead dough means with the measure; and if so one needs no basket-joining, only adjacency, for the discussion is where he separates immediately. And with adjacency this does not appear like fixing, what he places adjacent to him; and what the poskim used the language of joining is explained below in the Magen Avraham, who explains 'and he combines them together' as adjacency. But it appears more regarding the Peri Chadash's view that even if we explain the language of Tosafos and the poskim plainly, that they hold like Sefer HaTerumah and Semag that one needs basket-joining regarding being contiguous, nevertheless there is no proof from there that it is permitted to do so on Yom Tov and it is not considered fixing; for there it is certainly permitted, for there is one that permits which was kneaded today, and with permission he separates from it, and thereby he can properly join it and exempt also the others incidentally; for in doing the act of joining there is here no act of permission at all, for there is not even a thought that the others be permitted by means of this joining, since he does not want to eat and continue; and if so this joining does not cause a permission of fixing at all; only afterward when he separates from the obligated matzah, from which he separates with permission, the others are exempted automatically; and in such a case where the permission does not come immediately at the time of joining, the Peri Chadash also agrees that there is no fixing prohibition in this. And so too it is clear from the Mordechai's language who wrote: because one may permit even if he rolled from erev Yom Tov, for they do no act with them but only think to exempt the others incidentally with that obligated matzah, end of his words; and his intent is as I wrote, that even if we explain 'and it will exempt the others' as vessel-joining as above, nevertheless the joining does not do the fixing, only the separation; and the separation has in the others no act, only thought alone, that he thinks at the time he separates from this obligated one; and that is why he compares it to a permitted device of deception; which is not so in the Peri Chadash's case, where by means of basket-joining he wants to eat and continue, for there is here nothing permitted at all, and all the doughs are equal in that he will eat and continue, and each would need separation from it and in it; and if he did an act with them and joined them together with intent to fix thereby so that at the end we need separate only from one and the exemption comes to the rest immediately at the time of joining, the Peri Chadash properly said that it is fixing. And I found in Sefer Rosh Yosef on the laws of Pesach, letter 22, who wrote like the Peri Chadash's words and with more explanation, and because of the length I did not copy it here. And according to this, if we adopt the Peri Chadash's view, even to join a matzah from dough of less than the measure, the joining is forbidden because of fixing. But it appears that even if we permit from the side of joining and not like the Peri Chadash, nevertheless this joining does not help at all to permit eating and continuing, just as there is no permit with two doughs of the measure; and although the Taz permits, that is specifically if neither of the two doughs has the measure in either one, for since neither became obligated of itself at all, and only joining together brought them to obligation, this is as if he rolled them as one and they became obligated together; if so the mind grants that since they are considered as one we say he eats and continues; but if one has the measure and became obligated of itself, even though the second does not have the measure and became obligated only because of the joining, nevertheless he cannot eat the entire dough upon which the obligation rested to separate from it; for even if he leaves over from the second that was joined, this does not discharge the first of its obligation. And it follows simply: since he joined upon it the second that did not have the measure and it became obligated by this joining, it is as if he added upon it at the time the dough was there and they were attached, which helps for eating and continuing; for if so also when each had the measure and he joined them together in a basket we should say likewise that it is as if they were attached together at the time of kneading; and this is indeed the Peri Chadash's view, that basket-joining is like kneading them together; but I already wrote that Darkei Moshe and the later authorities imply they disagree in this; and so too it implies from the plain words of Tosafos and the Mordechai; and although one can distinguish somewhat, that if one is less than the measure it is more fitting to say as if he kneaded them, since the large dough caused its obligation, it does not appear to me to distinguish thus. But it appears that even if we say like the Peri Chadash that joining helps for eating and continuing, nevertheless this is no remedy at all to join from the matzos; and from the outset of the law it is broken: since he needs to leave the matzah to separate challah and leftovers from it, if so he necessarily needs that it be in a manner permitted on Pesach, and it is impossible for him that it be mixed with the chametz, namely that he return them to their flour and they mix together; and it is explained in Yoreh Deah that anything he is particular about regarding its mixture does not join at all. And even according to what the Shach wrote in Nekudos HaKesef, siman 326, that specifically regarding joining to become obligated in challah, a thing one is particular about does not join, which is not so regarding being contiguous, where we are not concerned: even if he is particular about its mixture, basket-joining helps; nevertheless it appears that it does not help here, for in Nekudos HaKesef there he explained the reason for the distinction: specifically regarding joining, for the main obligation is that we view them as if they are one dough joined together, and he is particular about them not to join them together; but regarding being contiguous, where he separates from one upon its fellows, one need not that they be joined together; see there. And according to this it appears that this is only when he comes to separate now from one upon another and both are before us; we say that for being contiguous one need not that they be considered one dough; but if we come to permit by means of basket-joining to eat and continue and afterward separate, there is no place to say so unless we say that what he joined them in a basket is as if he rolled them and kneaded them together as the Peri Chadash wrote; and it is obvious that in this the law returns to be like the law of joining to become obligated: since we must say they became one dough, joining does not help when he is particular about its mixture. And even according to what he wrote there afterward in Nekudos HaKesef, that specifically with tamei and tahor where he is particular about touching even without mixing, nevertheless it is clear from his words that regarding joining to become obligated, if he is particular about the mixture it does not help even touching and biting; and therefore even if one is coarse bread or has saffron in it, joining does not help to become obligated: that is because one cannot say they became as one dough since he is particular about their mixture, even though he is not particular about touching; and if so it is obvious that regarding this, where each has the measure and he will eat and continue and afterward separate, one must specifically not be particular, for then there is place to say basket-joining helps as if he kneaded them; but if he is particular, so that one cannot say it is as if he kneaded them since he is particular about their mixture, it is obvious that even touching and biting do not help, as written in Nekudos HaKesef there regarding joining to become obligated, that all agree on this; and the same law regarding joining to eat and continue; and all the more so basket-joining alone does not help in this. And see in Mekor Chaim there who wrote that if we say it helps when he joins from the matzah, or even if he already separated from the matzos, there is a remedy that he ask [to annul] the separation, for one may ask for the need of Shabbos; see there. And this requires further study whether one may rule so l'chatchila, for the view of the Taz in Yoreh Deah siman 323 is that specifically when he regrets from the side of the giving itself for some reason he has, then he uproots the essence of the vow; which is not so where he has nothing to regret about the essence of the matter, only because it became mixed and he will have a loss, or because of fear of punishment: then asking does not help; see there. And if so it is obvious that here he has no reason to regret the separation of challah from the matzos except in order to fix the chametz; it appears that asking does not help according to the Taz. And also according to the view of Semag that the Rama cited, and likewise in Nekudos HaKesef who rejected the Taz's words, nevertheless it appears that they said so only there: even though he asks about this separation because of the mixture, nevertheless he immediately returns and separates another in its place; for then there is one who regrets with a whole heart because he returns and fixes the dough immediately; but in this, where it is impossible to return and separate immediately, and he will eat without separation relying on separating after Yom Tov, it appears that this does not help to uproot the separation retrospectively, to say that had he known a cause would occur requiring him to join from these matzos he would not have separated. And all the more so according to what Bach and Magen Avraham wrote according to the Tur's view in Orach Chaim, that l'chatchila one should not act relying on eating and continuing, etc., only specifically when he forgot; and all the more so if he has many matzos and from doughs each of which had the measure, so that he would need to leave a piece from each matzah: one should not permit him to ask about the separation and eat and leave over from each matzah, lest he not leave over from each one and what he separates afterward not help him, as explained above. For the law there is no practical difference in this, for without that there is no place for the remedy of joining the matzah, as explained above. And see in the Magen Avraham who wrote that one should not say: let him give the leftover to a non-Jew as a gift, and the non-Jew will return it to him after Pesach and he will separate challah, for one should be concerned that the non-Jew will eat it and it turns out he ate tevel; this implies that were it not for the concern that he will eat it, he would be permitted to do so, to give to a non-Jew as a gift even though he ate relying on separating from it; he is permitted to give it as a gift so that he can separate when [the non-Jew] returns it to him. And according to this it appears that if he has a partition where he can hide it until after Pesach, there is a remedy: let him give it to a non-Jew as a gift, and the non-Jew will return and give it to the Jew as a deposit without any liability at all, even for negligence; for in such a case it is permitted, as explained in siman 440; and it implies that even l'chatchila it is permitted to accept such a deposit before Pesach, as explained in Chok Yaakov there, s.k. 9. And one should not say one should be concerned lest he come during Pesach and demand that he give it to him: this is not so, for he can stipulate with him that he need not trouble himself to give it to him until after Pesach; and it is proper to do so for the need of Shabbos, for on Shabbos he is obligated to eat bread; and even if he has rich matzah that he is permitted to eat before the tenth hour, nevertheless on Shabbos he is obligated to eat bread upon which one is liable for Birkas HaMazon; see siman 471, s.k. 5.
It further appears that even if we say like Pri Chadash, that joining helps for eating progressively, this is still no remedy by joining from the matzot. The law is flawed from the start: since he must leave the matzah to separate challah from it and from the remnants, it must be in a form permitted on Pesach, and it cannot be mixed with the chametz by returning them to flour and mixing them together. It is explained in Yoreh Deah that anything whose mixture one is particular to avoid does not combine at all. Even according to what the Shach wrote in Nekudot HaKesef, siman 326, that only regarding combination to become obligated in challah does concern about mixture prevent combination, but regarding mukaf we are not concerned and basket-combination helps even if one is particular about mixing, here it appears that it does not help. There in Nekudot HaKesef he explained the distinction: for combination to create the primary obligation, we view them as if they are one joined dough, and he is particular not to join them; but for mukaf, where one separates from one for the others, they need not be joined as one. According to this, that applies only when one now comes to separate from one for another and both are visibly before us. But if we seek to permit through basket-combination that he eat progressively and separate afterward, there is no room to say this unless we say the basket-combination is as though he rolled and mixed them together, as Pri Chadash wrote. In that case the law returns to the law of combination to become obligated, and since they must be treated as one dough, combination does not help where he is particular about their mixture. Even according to what Nekudot HaKesef later wrote, that specifically with impure and pure dough one is particular about contact even without mixture, nevertheless it is clear from his words that regarding combination to become obligated, if one is particular about the mixture, even contact and touching do not help. Therefore even where one is coarse bread or contains saffron, combination does not help to create obligation because one cannot say they became one dough when he is particular about their mixture. If so, for this matter too, where each has the required measure and he wants to eat progressively and separate afterward, it must specifically be where he is not particular; then there is room to say basket-combination works as though they were mixed. But where he is particular, it cannot be as though mixed, and even contact and touching do not help, as written in Nekudot HaKesef regarding combination to obligate, and all agree to this. The same applies to combination for eating progressively, and all the more so mere basket-combination does not help.
See Mekor Chaim there, who wrote that if we say it helps when he combines from the matzah, or even if he already separated from the matzot, there is a remedy: he can ask to annul the separation, for one may ask for the sake of Shabbat; see there. It requires study whether one may rule so initially. The Taz in Yoreh Deah siman 323 holds that this helps specifically when he regrets the giving itself because of some reason, thereby uprooting the original vow, but where he has no regret about the essence of the matter, only because it became mixed and he will suffer loss or because of fear of punishment, inquiry does not help; see there. If so, here he has no reason to regret separating challah from the matzot except to repair the chametz, and it appears that inquiry would not help according to the Taz. Even according to the Semag brought by the Rama, and although Nekudot HaKesef rejected the Taz, it seems they said so only there, where although he asks about this separation because of the mixture, he immediately returns and separates another in its place; then he regrets wholeheartedly because he immediately repairs the dough. But here, where he cannot immediately separate again and will eat without separation relying on separating after Yom Tov, it appears this does not help to uproot the separation retroactively and say that had he known this circumstance would occur and he would need to join from these matzot, he would not have separated. All the more so according to what the Bach and Magen Avraham wrote in the Tur's view in Orach Chaim, that initially one should not act relying on eating progressively, except where he forgot. All the more so if he has many matzot from doughs each with the required measure, and he would need to leave a piece from each matzah, one should not permit him to ask about the separation, eat, and leave from each matzah, lest he not leave from each one and what he separates afterward will not help, as explained above. In practice this has no legal difference, for in any event there is no place for the remedy of joining matzah, as explained above.
See the Magen Avraham, who wrote that one should not say he can give what remains to a non-Jew as a gift, and the non-Jew will return it to him after Pesach and he will separate challah, because there is concern the non-Jew will eat it, and he would be eating tevel. This implies that were it not for the concern that he will eat it, one may give it to a non-Jew as a gift even though he ate relying on separating from it, and may give it as a gift so that he can separate when it is returned to him. According to this, if he has a partition where he can hide it until after Pesach, there is a remedy: he should give it to the non-Jew as a gift, and the non-Jew should return it to the Jew as a deposit without any responsibility at all, even for negligence. In such a case it is permitted, as explained in siman 440, and it implies that even initially one may accept such a deposit before Pesach, as explained in Chok Yaakov there, seif katan 9. One should not object that perhaps the non-Jew will come during Pesach and demand it, for he can stipulate with him that he need not trouble himself to give it to him until after Pesach. It is proper to do this for Shabbat need, for on Shabbat one is obligated to eat bread.
