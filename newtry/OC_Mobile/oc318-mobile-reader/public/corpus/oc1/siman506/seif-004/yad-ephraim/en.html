In Magen Avraham seif katan 8: "that bread has no remedy." At first glance it seems that if he has matzot that are tevel for challah, he could take one of them and combine it in a basket with the chametz bread, leaving the matzah to separate from it on Chol HaMoed, as Pri Chadash wrote in siman 497 that through basket-combination one may eat progressively and then separate, etc.; so too in Mekor Chaim, though he disagrees with Pri Chadash and holds basket-combination does not help for eating progressively, as I wrote above. At first glance, even though Pri Chadash there holds that it must be joined before Yom Tov, and basket-combination on Yom Tov is forbidden as a form of fixing, here, where both have a challah-measure, basket-combination may not be needed; proximity before him should suffice, and this would not be fixing even according to Pri Chadash. But this is not so, for the later authorities hold that even for mukaf one needs basket-combination, as Sefer HaTerumah and Semag wrote. Even according to the Rosh, proximity helps only when one wants to separate immediately; here, where one wants to eat progressively and separate after Yom Tov, it is obvious that without basket-combination it does not help, even according to Pri Chadash's explanation of the Beit Yosef, and so Darkei Moshe understood the Beit Yosef, as I wrote above. Since it is not permitted without combination, it again becomes fixing and is forbidden according to Pri Chadash. This is also proven from Tosafot and Mordechai, who found no remedy through basket-combination; perforce either it does not help for eating progressively or it is considered fixing and forbidden on Yom Tov. Therefore there is no place to repair this bread through basket-combination: if one says it is not considered fixing, one must say it does not help for eating progressively, and either way it is forbidden. Still, there is room to argue based on what I wrote above in the name of the Taz, that if the obligation comes through combination, eating progressively is permitted. If so, if he has matzot from dough kneaded with less than the required measure, he could combine it in a basket, eat progressively, and leave the matzah. Although Pri Chadash forbids because of fixing, this is not the implication of Tosafot and the poskim, as Mekor Chaim wrote, for they wrote to knead a small dough and combine it, etc. In truth, however, Pri Chadash can be defended: he holds, as written above, that "to knead a dough" means a dough of the required measure, in which case no basket-combination is needed, only proximity, since it speaks of separating immediately; proximity suffices and does not look like fixing. More likely, even if Tosafot and the poskim are read simply, that they hold like Sefer HaTerumah and Semag that basket-combination is needed for mukaf, there is no proof from there that it may be done on Yom Tov and is not considered fixing. There, it is certainly permitted because there is one permitted dough kneaded that day, from which he may lawfully separate, and by joining it he can exempt the others along with it. The act of joining itself contains no act of permission at all, for he does not intend to eat progressively; the joining does not itself create the permission. Only afterward, when he separates from the obligated matzah, which he may lawfully separate from, the others are automatically exempted. Where permission does not come immediately at the time of joining, even Pri Chadash agrees there is no prohibition of fixing. This is explicit in Mordechai's language, that it is permitted even if he rolled it before Yom Tov because no action is done to them, only his thought is to exempt the others along with that obligated matzah. By contrast, in Pri Chadash's case, where by basket-combination he wants to eat progressively, there is no independently permitted item; all the doughs are equal, each would have required separation from itself, and by joining them with intent to fix that only one later separation be needed, the exemption for the others arrives immediately at the time of joining. This is what Pri Chadash properly calls fixing. I found in Rosh Yosef on Hilkhot Pesach, letter 22, that he wrote like Pri Chadash and explained it more, but because of length I did not copy it here. According to this, if we accept Pri Chadash, even joining matzah from dough below the required measure is forbidden as fixing. Yet it appears that even if we do not forbid because of joining, unlike Pri Chadash, it still does not help at all to permit eating progressively through this joining, just as there is no permission with two doughs each of the required measure. Although the Taz permits, that is specifically where neither of the two doughs has the required measure, so neither was obligated on its own and the joining created the obligation, as though they had been rolled together and obligated together. Then it is reasonable to say that since they are considered one, one may eat progressively. But if one dough has the required measure and became obligated on its own, even if the second lacks the measure and became obligated only through joining, one may not eat all of the dough that was already obligated to have separation taken from it; leaving from the joined second dough does not discharge the first. If you object that since he joined the second dough to it and it became obligated through this joining, it is as though he added to it at the time of kneading and attached it, so eating progressively should work, then the same would apply where each had the required measure and he joined them in a basket, that it should be as if they were joined during kneading. This is indeed Pri Chadash's view, that basket-combination is like mixing them together, but I have already written that Darkei Moshe and the later authorities imply they disagree, and so it is implied from the plain words of Tosafot and Mordechai. Although one could distinguish somewhat where one is below the measure, it does not appear to me to distinguish so.
It further appears that even if we say like Pri Chadash, that joining helps for eating progressively, this is still no remedy by joining from the matzot. The law is flawed from the start: since he must leave the matzah to separate challah from it and from the remnants, it must be in a form permitted on Pesach, and it cannot be mixed with the chametz by returning them to flour and mixing them together. It is explained in Yoreh Deah that anything whose mixture one is particular to avoid does not combine at all. Even according to what the Shach wrote in Nekudot HaKesef, siman 326, that only regarding combination to become obligated in challah does concern about mixture prevent combination, but regarding mukaf we are not concerned and basket-combination helps even if one is particular about mixing, here it appears that it does not help. There in Nekudot HaKesef he explained the distinction: for combination to create the primary obligation, we view them as if they are one joined dough, and he is particular not to join them; but for mukaf, where one separates from one for the others, they need not be joined as one. According to this, that applies only when one now comes to separate from one for another and both are visibly before us. But if we seek to permit through basket-combination that he eat progressively and separate afterward, there is no room to say this unless we say the basket-combination is as though he rolled and mixed them together, as Pri Chadash wrote. In that case the law returns to the law of combination to become obligated, and since they must be treated as one dough, combination does not help where he is particular about their mixture. Even according to what Nekudot HaKesef later wrote, that specifically with impure and pure dough one is particular about contact even without mixture, nevertheless it is clear from his words that regarding combination to become obligated, if one is particular about the mixture, even contact and touching do not help. Therefore even where one is coarse bread or contains saffron, combination does not help to create obligation because one cannot say they became one dough when he is particular about their mixture. If so, for this matter too, where each has the required measure and he wants to eat progressively and separate afterward, it must specifically be where he is not particular; then there is room to say basket-combination works as though they were mixed. But where he is particular, it cannot be as though mixed, and even contact and touching do not help, as written in Nekudot HaKesef regarding combination to obligate, and all agree to this. The same applies to combination for eating progressively, and all the more so mere basket-combination does not help.
See Mekor Chaim there, who wrote that if we say it helps when he combines from the matzah, or even if he already separated from the matzot, there is a remedy: he can ask to annul the separation, for one may ask for the sake of Shabbat; see there. It requires study whether one may rule so initially. The Taz in Yoreh Deah siman 323 holds that this helps specifically when he regrets the giving itself because of some reason, thereby uprooting the original vow, but where he has no regret about the essence of the matter, only because it became mixed and he will suffer loss or because of fear of punishment, inquiry does not help; see there. If so, here he has no reason to regret separating challah from the matzot except to repair the chametz, and it appears that inquiry would not help according to the Taz. Even according to the Semag brought by the Rama, and although Nekudot HaKesef rejected the Taz, it seems they said so only there, where although he asks about this separation because of the mixture, he immediately returns and separates another in its place; then he regrets wholeheartedly because he immediately repairs the dough. But here, where he cannot immediately separate again and will eat without separation relying on separating after Yom Tov, it appears this does not help to uproot the separation retroactively and say that had he known this circumstance would occur and he would need to join from these matzot, he would not have separated. All the more so according to what the Bach and Magen Avraham wrote in the Tur's view in Orach Chaim, that initially one should not act relying on eating progressively, except where he forgot. All the more so if he has many matzot from doughs each with the required measure, and he would need to leave a piece from each matzah, one should not permit him to ask about the separation, eat, and leave from each matzah, lest he not leave from each one and what he separates afterward will not help, as explained above. In practice this has no legal difference, for in any event there is no place for the remedy of joining matzah, as explained above.
See the Magen Avraham, who wrote that one should not say he can give what remains to a non-Jew as a gift, and the non-Jew will return it to him after Pesach and he will separate challah, because there is concern the non-Jew will eat it, and he would be eating tevel. This implies that were it not for the concern that he will eat it, one may give it to a non-Jew as a gift even though he ate relying on separating from it, and may give it as a gift so that he can separate when it is returned to him. According to this, if he has a partition where he can hide it until after Pesach, there is a remedy: he should give it to the non-Jew as a gift, and the non-Jew should return it to the Jew as a deposit without any responsibility at all, even for negligence. In such a case it is permitted, as explained in siman 440, and it implies that even initially one may accept such a deposit before Pesach, as explained in Chok Yaakov there, seif katan 9. One should not object that perhaps the non-Jew will come during Pesach and demand it, for he can stipulate with him that he need not trouble himself to give it to him until after Pesach. It is proper to do this for Shabbat need, for on Shabbat one is obligated to eat bread. Even if he has egg matzah that he may eat before the tenth hour, nevertheless on Shabbat he is obligated to eat bread over which one is obligated in birkat hamazon; see siman 471 seif katan 5.
