Challah outside Eretz Yisrael, which is challah consigned to the fire, must be burned even if the dough was kneaded with egg water or the like and was never susceptible to ritual impurity. According to the opinion that such dough is obligated in challah, it is burned because no ritually pure kohen is available nowadays. Since the custom not to burn it does not apply, the same law should apply even where the challah itself is ritually pure: one should wait until nightfall. See Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilchos Yom Tov, halachah 8, regarding this. One must be careful not to burn it on Yom Tov, but to leave it until evening. On other festivals this is what is done. Baking it, however, appears forbidden according to all opinions, because one could have divided the dough before kneading. See seif katan 7. On Pesach, where leaving it unbaked is impossible, it should be baked; see siman 457. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained further in Magen Avraham, seif katan 10.
