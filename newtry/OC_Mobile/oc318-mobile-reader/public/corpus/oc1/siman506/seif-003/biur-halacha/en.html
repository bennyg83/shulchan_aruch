And to bring it to the kohen — R' Yeshash wrote that just as Tosafos proved for Beit Shammai that one does not bring gifts to the kohen, likewise if he comes to his house it is forbidden to give them to him unless he is regularly accustomed to eat there frequently; if so, likewise even for Beit Hillel who permit bringing everywhere, nevertheless where Beit Hillel too agree that one does not bring — such as when an alley or karmelit interrupts — likewise it should be forbidden to give him even when he comes to his house unless he is regularly accustomed there frequently; and this is according to what he explained in Tosafos' words there s.v. ein molichin. And therefore his explanation is also in Tosafos' rulings; and Taz wondered at him: if an alley or karmelit interrupts, what of it — behold it is ochel nefesh of a person; and Ateret Tzvi reconciles this, see there, and his words are not understood at all. The main point is that Yeshash refers to Shabbat, and likewise in Mordechai's article, and this is simple; however regarding the essential law see Maharsha, Maharam, Maharam Schiff, and Pri Chadash, who brought Rosh's words in chapter 4 of Demai who wrote explicitly that the mishnah refers per the one who forbids terumah for everyone; but according to what we rule like R' Yosi in the baraita, for Beit Hillel even terumah one brings to the kohen's house and one is not concerned that perhaps he separated today [and truly Maharsha and Maharam did not grasp Rosh's intent and wondered at him, which caused them not to see Yeshash who explains his words where one does not bring for everyone]. And if so, whence do we divide all these distinctions; and therefore even if he may not lead, at any rate he can come to his house and eat; and likewise Mordechai agreed to be lenient; and also the reasoning that they assume he separated on Yom Tov does not apply to challah, for all permit raising where there are three on Yom Tov. Truly Ateret Tzvi wrote that from Rambam's words who copied this law in chapter 23 of Shabbat it is proved he holds the mishnah is per the halachah and not like R' Shimson, see there; but one who examines well will see that in his composition he did not explain this mishnah at all per the Yerushalmi, and as he copied in explaining the mishnah he shall not take them is like he shall not give them; and proof that in his composition he copied the mishnah's language he shall not take them, and it appears he explained the mishnah according to its plain sense that he shall not take them, meaning he is not permitted to move them from their place; and this ruling is specifically where he did not separate from erev Yom Tov but only called it by name, and therefore the Sages decreed that he himself should not move it, as one who raises terumot on Shabbat which is forbidden — but priests come and take and eat, for for them there is no prohibition, for in truth it is as separated from erev Yom Tov, see there in his composition which proves as we explained. And therefore where he separated by hand we do not find at all that he may not take them; and afterward I found in the Gra's explanation there that he explains explicitly that for our Talmud the explanation is not like the Yerushalmi but that he shall not separate, as we wrote. And according to what we wrote it would seem to explain in Beitzah 37 in Rav Yosef's answer that it was needed only to give it to the kohen that same day (see Rashi's explanation there — very forced); and were it not for his words it would appear in my humble opinion that Rav Yosef answers the difficulty that he asks it is obvious, meaning it is already fixed, and we already heard in Shabbat 33 that even in doubtful twilight one does not tithe on Shabbat, and the same on Yom Tov, and all the more on Yom Tov itself; and on this he answers that the mishnah speaks where he already called it by name, and automatically the homeowner may eat by this — he sets his eyes on one side and eats on the other; and his raising is only in order to give it to the kohen that day; and one might have thought that in this it is permitted, for behold the grain is already fixed — it teaches us it is forbidden. And likewise in the mishnah of Demai that the rabbis made as if still tevel and he shall not separate; and that which the Gemara says and this is when they were immersed from yesterday means it was tevel from yesterday and is called tevel because it was truly tevel yesterday (and only he called it by name) and he could have separated from it; and to exclude if it became tevel today, such as dough whose kneading was on Yom Tov itself — it is permitted to separate.

And tomorrow he separates — see Mishna Berurah what he wrote regarding erev Pesach that falls on Shabbat, that he cannot use this advice; and this is from Magen Avraham who wrote, and these are his words: for he will need to burn the chametz, and if so he ends up eating tevel retroactively; and Magen Avraham concludes that he has no remedy. In Mishna Berurah we copied in the name of Korban Netanel and other later authorities to rely in a time of distress on the poskim who rule like Rava that challah of chutz la'aretz, even if kneaded from erev Yom Tov, may be separated on Yom Tov and given to a minor or adult kohen who immersed for his emission; and in Korban Netanel he further concludes that if he has no minor kohen he may separate less than an olive-size, for he does not transgress thereby in Beit Yosef, and impose a vessel on it until after Yom Tov. It is difficult, for behold it becomes forbidden in benefit when the time of prohibition arrives — how can he separate challah from items forbidden in benefit; and likewise it plainly implies in Pesachim daf 33, end of section you shall give. And likewise it is explained in Tzelach there that with chametz on Pesach, for us who hold it is forbidden in benefit, no sanctity applies to the terumah; and that which we distinguish whether there was a time of fitness or not is only according to R' Yosi HaGelili who holds it is not forbidden in benefit, see there [and likewise in the glosses of the Ra'em printed in the Vilna Shas, and see also in the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger there]; and therefore I did not copy these words of his in Mishna Berurah. And that which Magen Avraham hung his words because he must burn — lest otherwise even though forbidden in benefit on Pesach he would enjoy it until after Pesach and again it would not be forbidden from the Torah — Korban Netanel's words cannot be reconciled with this, for even less than an olive-size one must burn at least rabbinically in chutz la'aretz; and if so it is forbidden to leave it until after Pesach, and during Pesach behold it is forbidden in benefit as above, and no challah applies to it; and also in Korban Netanel itself he wrote that one leaves it only until after Yom Tov, as the law of chametz found on Pesach, see there — and that is until chutz la'aretz.
