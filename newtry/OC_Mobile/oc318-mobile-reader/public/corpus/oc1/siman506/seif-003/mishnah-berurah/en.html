{17} He may separate, etc. Even though one does not raise terumos, etc., on Yom Tov because it appears like fixing or like dedicating, when one kneads on Yom Tov they did not decree; and even when there is no kohen before him, or in a place where the challah is burned; for since it is permitted to knead l'chatchila on Yom Tov because hot bread is preferable, they also permitted him to fix his dough and remove it from tevel status.
{18} Even if he separated it yesterday. And we do not say he should have brought it yesterday. And even if he needs to carry it through a public domain it is also permitted, for carrying out is permitted on Yom Tov. And know that also on Shabbos it is permitted for him to bring it to the kohen in a place where there is an eruv, and in a manner if it was baked yesterday, for raw dough is fit for nothing on Shabbos; and in a place without an eruv, whether the kohen may come to the Jew's house on Shabbos and eat there, see Biur Halacha.
{19} That was kneaded on erev Yom Tov. And the discussion is where he completed all the kneading on erev Yom Tov, for he was already obligated in separating challah; but if he still had to engage on Yom Tov in completing its kneading, then from erev Yom Tov he was not yet obligated in challah, and it is like one who began to knead on Yom Tov.
{20} It is forbidden to separate, etc. For one does not raise terumos and maasros on Shabbos and Yom Tov where they were tevel from yesterday, as above in s.k. 17; and likewise if it was baked yesterday it is forbidden to separate today. And even with challah of chutz la'aretz the law is so; and even if he did not separate because of forgetfulness, it is also forbidden for him to separate it. And if he transgressed and separated: if deliberately, it is forbidden for him and for others until Motzei Shabbos; and if unwittingly, it is permitted immediately, as above in siman 339 in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 25.
{21} Rather he eats, etc. That is, according to us in chutz la'aretz, where its obligation is not according to the Torah; and with challah of Eretz Yisrael there is no remedy unless he kneads another dough, as at the end of the se'if.
{22} And he leaves over a little. That is, more than the amount he wishes to separate, so that he will separate challah from that leftover; for we require that its leftover be recognizable; and further, if he leaves only the amount of challah, it is like one who separates on Yom Tov.
{23} And tomorrow he separates, etc. And on erev Pesach that falls on Shabbos, when he baked chametz bread for Shabbos and it is impossible to eat and leave over and set aside until tomorrow, if he did not separate challah on erev Shabbos, several later authorities agreed that after the fact we rely on those poskim that with challah of chutz la'aretz it is permitted to separate even on Shabbos and Yom Tov; and he should separate and give it to a minor kohen less than nine years old, or even to an adult who immersed. And although there are later authorities who hold that this bread has no remedy for eating, nevertheless in a pressured situation where he has no other bread for Shabbos one may rely to be lenient.
{24} And it is permitted to bake the bread by means of eating, etc. And even though he is also engaged for the challah portion within it, and that portion will not be eaten on Yom Tov by a kohen, and in a similar case we forbid below in se'if 6: this case is different, for it is impossible to divide the challah from the dough, since it is forbidden for him to separate; and further, the challah portion nowadays is a small thing and there is no added effort for it. And some are stringent and say that since he kneaded yesterday, and yesterday it was possible to divide it and separate the challah portion, we do not permit him on Yom Tov to bake the whole dough because of the challah portion within it that is baked not for the need of Yom Tov; and one should be concerned for this l'chatchila not to bake the whole dough, and only leave a little dough not to bake until after Yom Tov, and from it he will separate challah also for the first dough. However, if he transgressed and baked, it is certainly permitted for him to eat and leave a little from which he will separate challah on Motzei Yom Tov.
{25} And if he wishes he may, etc. And the discussion is where he needs it, or wishes to eat hot bread; and therefore we do not care if incidentally the first dough is also fixed; and even with challah of Eretz Yisrael he may use this counsel.
{26} Another dough. And specifically when it has the amount for challah; but if it does not have the amount, except that he joins it with the first that has it, it is forbidden, for it is like one separating challah from dough kneaded on erev Yom Tov [Machatzit HaShekel, Peri Megadim, and Yad Ephraim]. And some are lenient even if it does not have the amount, and by means of joining in a basket; and in a pressured situation one should be lenient.
{27} And he combines them together. According to what the Mechaber ruled in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 325: two doughs that each have the amount for challah need neither vessel-joining nor touching; rather he places both before him and separates from this upon that; and here too likewise if each had the amount.
