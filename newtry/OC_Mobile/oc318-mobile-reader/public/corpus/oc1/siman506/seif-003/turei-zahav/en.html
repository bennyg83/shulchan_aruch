And a pebble fell into it, etc. In the Gemara it is stated: Our Rabbis taught: one does not do a second sifting of flour on Yom Tov (that was sifted yesterday, and one comes to sift it again in a sieve to improve it: one does not do a second sifting, for it was possible for him yesterday). R' Papus and R' Yehuda ben Beteira said: one does a second sifting (for there is no [significant] effort, and it is an apparent matter that this is a second time, and it is not like sifting to select). And they agree that if a pebble or twig fell into it, one does a second sifting. A Tanna taught before Ravina: one does not do a second sifting of flour on Yom Tov; but if a pebble or twig fell, he selects it by hand (but doing a second sifting is forbidden even in this). He said to him: all the more so it is forbidden, for it is like borer, and borer is an av melacha like sifting; but one who does a second sifting is not meraked, for everything comes out and there is no bran here. The wife of Rav Yosef sifted flour over the back of a sieve; he said to her: see that I want fine bread. (You need not do a second sifting on Yom Tov, for one does a second sifting of flour on Yom Tov.) The wife of Rav Ashi sifted flour upon the back of a table (because of a change; meaning the underside of the table, and there is a cavity there). And Tosafos explained that even upon the table it is a change. Said Rav Ashi: this [wife] of ours is the daughter of Rami bar Chama, and he is master of the incident. And the Rosh, the Tur, and the Ran ruled like the one who says one does not do a second sifting for beauty unless by means of some change, like the wife of Rav Ashi; and one need not do as the wife of Rav Yosef. And Rav Yosef said to his wife that you need not a change as great as this, for the flour is not fine thereby; rather a small change suffices, namely upon the table. But if a pebble fell there, no change at all is needed; rather he does a second sifting as is. And they did not write at all that ruling of Ravina who forbids taking the pebble or twig by hand. And Beit Yosef wrote the reason: Ravina said so only according to what that Tanna said before him, to be more stringent regarding doing a second sifting than regarding taking by hand, for he holds one does not do a second sifting at all even by means of a change; but according to us, who hold one does a second sifting by means of a change, Ravina agrees that the hand is no better than doing a second sifting; and if so, according to the truth, also by hand it is permitted. Further Beit Yosef answered that certainly it is forbidden to take a pebble by hand, and the poskim did not write it regarding sifting flour because it is not a matter of sifting, and they relied on what is written in the law of borer; and this appears more [correct], end of his words. And in my humble opinion the first answer is more correct, for nothing of this is mentioned at all in the law of borer that there should be a prohibition to take a pebble or twig from a food item because of borer; and the Rashba explicitly permitted to take by hand, as Beit Yosef wrote; rather certainly according to the first explanation. And the Raavad in the Hasagot wrote in chapter 4, and this is his language: my master says the law follows the words of R' Papus and R' Yehuda who say one does a second sifting; and if a pebble or twig fell, it is forbidden; and if he changed, it is permitted, end of his words. At first glance it implies that what he wrote 'forbidden' means forbidden to do a second sifting, namely a second sifting, excluding the beginning; and upon this he said: and if he changed, it is permitted, meaning by means of a change a second sifting is permitted even if a pebble fell. And so the words of Kol Bo that Beit Yosef cites imply, and this is his language: the Raavad wrote: a second time one sifts it on Yom Tov if a pebble did not fall into it; but if a pebble fell into it, one does not do a second sifting to sift it, because it appears like borer; and if he changed, it is permitted, end of his words. And it is surprising upon him, for we explicitly say that they agree regarding a fallen pebble that doing a second sifting is permitted, which implies that a fallen pebble is better than doing a second sifting; and since he ruled like the one who says one does a second sifting even without a pebble, all the more so with a pebble. And further it is difficult: why should a change be forbidden because of borer, when borer is permitted on Yom Tov like cooking, as explained in siman 510 in the words of Maggid Mishneh; and Maggid Mishneh explained Rashi regarding what Ravina said: all the more so by hand it is forbidden because of borer, but doing a second sifting is not an av melacha; that is, it is preferable to do what is not an av melacha; but here where it is entirely forbidden, it is certainly difficult; and see what I wrote there. Therefore one must explain the Raavad's words as Maggid Mishneh wrote, and this is his language: the explanation of his words is that it is forbidden to remove the pebble by hand, but he may do a second sifting of the flour; but the Rashba explained that it is permitted to remove it by hand; and this appears to be the view of our Rabbi the Rambam, who did not write a prohibition of taking by hand, end of his words. If so, what the Raavad wrote 'and if he changed, it is permitted' means a second sifting in its usual way without a change; for he explains what Ravina said 'all the more so by hand it is forbidden' thus: if you come to be stringent regarding doing a second sifting and you rule like the first Tanna that one does not do a second sifting, all the more so you should forbid by hand, which is more similar to borer; and this is forbidden even according to the one who says one does a second sifting; and why do you permit by hand? And according to me he holds like the one who says one does a second sifting, and even so by hand it is forbidden since it is possible without an av melacha, as it appears to me according to the Raavad. And he holds that the fact that Rav Ashi's wife made a change upon the table was an excessive stringency, for no change at all is needed; rather one does a second sifting in the usual way; and therefore R' Yehuda said to his wife: see that I want fine bread, therefore do not make any change at all; and so the Ran wrote on this. And if so, by hand it is forbidden according to the conclusion to take a pebble or twig from the flour, for it is borer according to the Raavad, for it is like taking waste from within the food, which is forbidden in siman 319; which is not so when he does a second sifting of the flour, namely he sifts it again, then he takes the food from within the waste. And if so, when flies fall into a cup on Shabbos, it is forbidden according to the Raavad to remove the flies alone from there; rather he must take some of the drink with them as well; and this is the view of those who are stringent that the Rama mentioned in se'if 2. And my father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote another explanation in the words of the Rosh and the Raavad; and what appears to me clear and simple I have written.
