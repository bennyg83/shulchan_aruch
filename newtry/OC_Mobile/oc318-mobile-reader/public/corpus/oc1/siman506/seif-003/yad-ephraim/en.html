(In Shulchan Aruch, se'if 3, in the gloss) Rather he eats and leaves a little, and tomorrow he separates challah from the leftover. See above siman 457 in the Tur who wrote: and even if he forgot and ate a little, he separates upon the remainder, for we hold challah of chutz la'aretz, etc. And the Bach and all the later authorities asked upon this: for even l'chatchila it is permitted, so why did he use the language 'he forgot and ate'? And what the Taz wrote, that one might have thought that since less than the measure remains one must combine dough, it teaches us that one need not: this was puzzling, for why need 'he forgot and ate' as a prohibited act; let him say that if some was lost and at the time of separating less than the measure remained. And also what he wrote that in Eretz Yisrael one indeed must combine is puzzling in my humble opinion, for it appears that also in Eretz Yisrael one need not have a full measure at the time of separating, but rather that there was the measure at the time of rolling, even if some was eaten or lost, provided that what remains is enough to separate and leave over so that they are recognizable; he properly separates from it and in it, and needs no other dough at all. And afterward I saw that also Mekor Chaim wrote so. And what the Peri Chadash wrote, that the Tur discusses challah of Eretz Yisrael and therefore specifically 'he forgot and ate', and it is also fine that he separates only upon the remainder and not upon what he ate, for he already transgressed and has no remedy to separate upon it, and he brings an example from challah of chutz la'aretz; see there: this is puzzling, for how does he bring an example from the opposite, for in chutz la'aretz there is separation upon what was eaten, and the matter is simple that one cannot reconcile it at all with the Tur's words. And the Bach initially meant that the Tur's view is that l'chatchila one should not eat, only after the fact; and even so it was difficult for him why he says 'upon the remainder', for that also helps for what was already eaten; therefore he wrote that he comes to teach us that one must leave more than enough for challah. And the one who looks will see that this is not the Tur's manner of language. And also in Yoreh Deah siman 323 he wrote: and it does not become tevel, for it is permitted to eat from the bread and separate it afterward; it is clear that even l'chatchila it is permitted. And also it is surprising that above he discusses erev Yom Tov, for on Yom Tov one does not separate challah while it is still dough, as is explained afterward regarding the law of one who kneads on Yom Tov of Pesach; and if so how did he write 'he forgot and ate', namely on Yom Tov which is the time of eating; if so it is forbidden to separate now the dough that became obligated on erev Yom Tov; and it is forced to say his intent is that he separates upon the remainder, namely after Yom Tov, or that we explain he forgot and ate on erev Yom Tov, for it is obvious that is not so, for what difference is there between erev Yom Tov and a weekday in this, and it is obvious that if he wants to eat the remainder he must separate from it. And also this of challah of chutz la'aretz is not relevant to this; and if to say that the separation helps even for what he already ate, what practical difference is there for the law in this. And with force one can say his intent is that even if he does not want to eat from the remainder he should separate from it so that it will help to exempt what he will acquire; and this too is not so, for if so what is 'upon the remainder' that he says; he should have said 'he should separate from the remainder'. And my heart tells me that through a scribal error that fell in the writing, upon which the hands of copy-editors took hold until it became much corrupted, and the main reading appears that one should say: even if he forgot, he eats and leaves a little and separates upon it tomorrow; and that is exactly the language the Rama wrote here, except that he added the words 'from the leftover', from which we do not learn that one must leave more than enough for challah as the Magen Avraham wrote; and the Tur hinted this in what he wrote 'and he leaves a little and separates upon it tomorrow', which implies he does not separate the entire leftover. And the intent of the words is: certainly that which they permit l'chatchila to eat and continue is where there is some need to do so; but obviously the choicest mitzvah is to separate from the dough, and if he did not separate he should separate immediately after baking before eating; and even if we say he can rely on separation after eating, and even that there is no choicest mitzvah here, that is where it is in his hand to separate immediately after eating; but if he cannot separate immediately and must leave the rest until tomorrow when he will separate from it, the mind does not grant that it be permitted l'chatchila to do so, to eat some and leave some until tomorrow; l'chatchila he should separate immediately while it is still dough, or at least after baking and joining. And therefore he first wrote the law of separation l'chatchila that it should be before eating; and upon this he said 'and if he forgot', meaning and if he forgot to separate on erev Yom Tov, and on Yom Tov it is impossible to separate; upon this he wrote 'he eats it' (and it was printed in error and they flipped the vav to the head of the word and it became from it the word 'and ate') a little, to separate upon it tomorrow, meaning after Yom Tov (and so is the language of Tosafos and also in the Rama's language here); and that is what he wrote 'for challah of chutz la'aretz he eats, etc.'; and therefore it is permitted to eat l'chatchila on Yom Tov, for separation from some that remains after Yom Tov permits the eating he ate on Yom Tov; and the same law that the poskim wrote here in the laws of Yom Tov if he forgot to separate on erev Yom Tov, and as is also explained in the Tur siman 506; see there.
(There) And if he wishes he may knead another dough, etc. It appears he means with the amount for challah; for even though Tosafos in Beitzah and the Mordechai in Pesachim used in their words 'one matzah', that is with the measure, as explained there that the discussion is of kneading matzos each of which has the measure; see there. And with less than the measure it appears forbidden, since he could have eaten it without separating, except that he causes separation by hand because he joins it intentionally, and no challah obligation came upon him at all today since he does not need hot bread of the measure; it is forbidden for him to join it intentionally and obligate it in challah; and it is possible that this is worse than rolling on erev Yom Tov. And so it implies explicitly from the end of the Mordechai's words, who wrote: incidentally with that obligated matzah, which implies it became obligated of itself. And so too is clear from the words of the Magen Avraham, s.k. 12, who wrote 'and he combines them together', meaning that they should be adjacent together, as written in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 325. And necessarily he means because in this dough that he bakes today there is also the measure; for it is explained there that whenever both together have the measure, one needs only that they be adjacent, according to the view of the Rosh. And what the Shach wrote there in s.k. 6, that even if one has the measure and one does not have the measure, one needs only adjacency and does not need joining of a basket and covering: that is, that also the one that does not have the measure does not need joining in order to become obligated, for it comes from a large dough that had the measure and became obligated in challah, as he brings upon it from that of siman 324, se'if 11, where it is explained regarding one who took seor upon dough from which challah was not yet raised, etc.; and see there in the Shach, s.k. 19, if it has flour, etc.; and therefore adjacency alone suffices, etc. And so too it is explained explicitly in siman 325 and siman 324, from which the Shach copied, and he need not have written this. Only because in Mekor Chaim, siman 457, he wrote that there is a scribal error in the Shach as above, that one needs only vessel-joining; and with due respect, in the rush of his sharpness and fluency he thought the discussion is of dough kneaded from the outset with less than the measure, for which it is obvious that it becomes obligated only by means of basket-joining; and therefore he emended like the Shach. And in truth this is not so, as I wrote: that it has no measure means that now it has no measure, but nevertheless a challah obligation already rests upon it, for at the time of rolling there was the measure in the dough. And I found in the gloss of the Rav of Prague on Semak, laws of rest on Yom Tov, folio 81: and if he forgot, etc., he returns and kneads on Yom Tov the amount for challah and joins them in a vessel with that kneaded on erev Yom Tov and separates; and he brought an equal wording in Kol Bo, laws of Yom Tov; see there. And I saw in Sefer Mekor Chaim, siman 457, regarding Tosafos in the first chapter of Beitzah that Beit Yosef cited, who wrote that there are two remedies, etc., that he knead and bake of a small dough less than the measure on Yom Tov, etc.; and in my humble opinion this is not so, for a small dough is not mentioned in this; rather Rashi and other poskim wrote a small cake regarding challah on Pesach: he should make a small cake; but here this is not mentioned, and he needs to knead the amount for challah and join them with what he has from erev Yom Tov. And adjacency alone suffices, and he separates from the obligated one and exempts the other also in his thought. However, what the Magen Avraham wrote that adjacency alone suffices requires examination, and will be explained nearby.
