And to bring it to the kohen. Even on Shabbos (Yam Shel Shlomo). And it appears to me specifically when it is baked; and when it is raw it is fit for nothing on Shabbos.
He eats and leaves over. That is, with challah of chutz la'aretz; but for challah of Eretz Yisrael, see Yoreh Deah siman 323.
And tomorrow he separates. And automatically, on erev Pesach that falls on Shabbos, if one forgot to separate challah on erev Shabbos from chametz bread that he baked for Shabbos, it is forbidden to eat from it, for he will need to dispose of the chametz, and if so it turns out he ate tevel retroactively, as we say in the Gemara: perhaps the skin bottle will burst and it turns out he drank tevel retrospectively. And one should not say: let him give the leftover to a non-Jew as a gift, and the non-Jew will return it to him after Pesach and he will separate challah, for it is forbidden to give on condition; and alternatively, perhaps the non-Jew will eat it and it turns out he ate tevel. And one should not say: since it is impossible to eat from it without separating, it is permitted to separate on Shabbos, for challah of Eretz Yisrael is also forbidden to eat from without separating, and even so it is forbidden to separate it on Yom Tov; therefore it appears to me that they have no remedy.
From the leftover, challah. But it is forbidden to leave over only enough for challah alone (Beit Yosef).
And it is permitted to bake. And the Bach ruled like the Mordechai that it is forbidden to bake, like dough half of which belongs to a non-Jew (as written in se'if 6), because it is possible to divide the dough, except that a prohibition rests upon it, that one does not raise challah on Yom Tov, end of his words. And it appears to me a clear proof that it is permitted from the Mishnah in Pesachim chapter 3, as I wrote in siman 457, se'if 2, for there too it is permitted to separate it, only that he does so because of the chametz prohibition. And in a parchment Mordechai there is another reason: if you permit to bake and leave over, then the Sages accomplished nothing with their enactment by which they fined him since he did not separate on erev Yom Tov. Nevertheless it appears to me to be lenient; see siman 512, se'if 1-2. And according to this, since the reason is a fine, it is possible that if he forgot to separate on erev Yom Tov it is permitted to separate on Yom Tov; but from the terse formulation of the poskim it implies there is no distinction.
