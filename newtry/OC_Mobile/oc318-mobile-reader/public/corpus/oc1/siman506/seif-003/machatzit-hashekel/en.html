(s.k. 3) Some are stringent, etc. But one who does a second sifting, etc., for it is apparent, etc. And therefore the Mechaber permitted; and also those who are stringent agree that if a pebble fell into it, it is permitted to sift it a second time. For specifically if he takes it by hand there is a manner of selecting; but by means of doing a second sifting it is not a manner of selecting. And also regarding sifting there is none, for everything comes out, since there is no bran there; and even though the pebble remains in the sieve, since there is no bran this is not sifting, and it is permitted even without a change. Even though if there is no pebble in it and he sifts it a second time, at least a change is needed, for so Beit Yosef wrote that a change is needed. One must say that when there is no pebble in it, even though there is no bran, nevertheless since he intended the sifting of the flour it appears like sifting and a change is needed; which is not so when there is a pebble in it and his intent is only to remove the pebble, it does not appear like sifting.
And if so with our flour, etc. Meaning: for this reason, that a second sifting is permitted since it was already sifted and there is no bran there, if so according to us, where it was already sifted at the mill (meaning by the tool called a beitel), even a first time is permitted, for even a first time is called a second time. And in any case a change is needed, as written in Shulchan Aruch if there is no pebble in it. However, according to what the Bach wrote, that for the second reason sifting was permitted only with the first sieve, but with another sieve that extracts the flour and retains the semolina it is forbidden, for it is like a first sifting: what difference is there between a first sifting that extracts the bran and a second sifting that extracts the flour (and if so, likewise if the holes of the second sieve are smaller than the holes of the first sieve, and thereby he also extracts and separates the fine bran, it is also forbidden); and the Peri Chadash wrote that it is proper to be stringent like his words. If so, also with our flour, even though it was already sifted once at the mill, nevertheless it is forbidden to sift it, for the sieve extracts more waste than the mill extracted, as the senses testify. And see what the Taz wrote here and in siman 319 how one should act if a fly fell into liquids.
