One who kneads. See Taz. The word “rather” in the Taz’s discussion of the ruling of Mahari Abuhav appears to be a scribal error. It should read that Mahari Abuhav did not write his ruling concerning this case, namely bringing challah to a kohen, for he mentioned only the separation of challah. See Beit Yosef. The Bach, however, explains that Mahari Abuhav also refers to bringing it to a kohen: if one rolled dough before Yom Tov in Eretz Yisrael, where it is forbidden to separate challah on Yom Tov, and he transgressed and separated it on Yom Tov, he may not bring it to a kohen on Yom Tov. See there. See Rambam, chapter 23 of Hilchos Shabbos, halachah 15, and Beitzah 17b, which imply that even on Yom Tov, if one separated terumah and the like, it may not be eaten that day. It is therefore obvious that one may not bring it to a kohen that day. Possibly, even if it was separated inadvertently, one may not bring it to a kohen. This requires study. What the Taz writes concerning Maharshal refers to a public domain. Alternatively, it is permitted because we rule that even terumah separated before Yom Tov may be brought to a kohen on Yom Tov. There is no practical difference when the kohen is in his house. According to the Bach, however, there is a practical difference where one inadvertently separated challah on Yom Tov: even in his own house he may not transfer it to a kohen. One may say that this applies when the kohen is not accustomed to receive gifts from him. See Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 9. Some people take a small piece, attach it to a larger loaf, and then designate the small piece as challah. This is neither visibly separated nor set apart, and where the dough was kneaded before Yom Tov it is forbidden. This is obvious. See Magen Avraham; with Hashem’s help, the matter will be explained further there.
