Even if he takes more, there is no concern, because when the oven is full the bread bakes well. With our ovens, however, in which bread is not attached around the oven wall, it is explained below, siman 507, se'if 6, that one may not bake more than is needed for Yom Tov. Yeshuos Yaakov, subsection 1, therefore permits measuring flour on Yom Tov for this reason. Nevertheless, the unqualified wording of Tur, Shulchan Aruch, and the other later authorities implies that no distinction is made, and measuring is forbidden in every case. The reason appears to me to be that the statement that one may not bake more than needed in our ovens applies only when one knows with certainty that the additional amount is unnecessary. When one takes an estimated amount, however, it is only doubtful whether he will take a little more. This is not called baking more than necessary, because he might eat that small amount. Furthermore, it is impossible to precisely determine how much one will eat, for sometimes he eats little and sometimes more. This is clear.
