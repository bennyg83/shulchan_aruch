One kneads dough, etc. In the first chapter of Beitzah daf 12 we hold like R' Yosi who said: Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel did not disagree about the gifts of the foreleg, jaw, and maw that one brings to the kohen on Yom Tov; they disagreed only about terumah, for Beit Shammai say one does not bring, meaning specifically gifts one brings, for he is entitled to raise them, meaning he is permitted to raise them on Yom Tov; but terumah it is forbidden to raise on Yom Tov, as stated in chapter Mashilin: one does not raise terumot and ma'asrot on Yom Tov; therefore what he raised of terumah on erev Yom Tov one does not bring. In Beit Hillel, just as one brings the gifts, so one brings terumah; and it is simple that challah's law is like the gifts, and all separate it on Yom Tov if kneaded on Yom Tov — automatically its law is like the gifts and one brings it to the kohen even for Beit Shammai. And Beit Yosef's words here are not in precision, for he wrote: and it is known the halachah follows Beit Hillel, and it implies that even terumah raised last night Beit Hillel permit to bring — for in the mishnah, etc., why should he hang the halachah follows Beit Hillel regarding challah, which is like the gifts and Beit Shammai agree to it, and even if separated yesterday, since he can also separate on Yom Tov like the gifts for R' Yosi whom we hold like. Further in what he wrote: even terumah raised last night — what does even mean, for it is impossible to separate terumah on Yom Tov but only from erev Yom Tov; rather in the flow of his words he did not precisely distinguish, comparing terumah to challah in this. Tur wrote: one kneads dough on Yom Tov, may separate challah from it and bring it to the kohen; and likewise priestly gifts one may bring to the kohen even if raised last night; and Beit Yosef wrote that this even if raised last night applies also to challah; but I found the Gaon Mahari Weil wrote that there is a difference between gifts and dough: with gifts, even if slaughtered last night, he may separate them, not so with dough; and the reason is because with gifts, since they are not tevel (meaning there is no tevel prohibition if one eats the meat before separating the gifts), and moreover as explained and standing they are like blood and recognizable before separation as after separation, for he separates now — he does nothing; not so with dough, end of his words. And Beit Yosef wrote on this: therefore our Rabbi's language is explained simply: do not ask even if raised last night except regarding gifts; but regarding challah, specifically if raised today one is permitted to bring it, but if raised last night not; however it is difficult, for in the Gemara it does not imply so, as stated; therefore one must say that even if raised that our Rabbi wrote applies also to challah, and not like the rabbi's words, end of Beit Yosef. And I say: although our Rabbi Yosef is ruler in all the world in his explanations and rulings, nevertheless here an oversight came before the ruler, for he attributed empty words, God forbid, to the Gaon Mahari Weil — to say it is forbidden to bring to the kohen what was raised of challah on erev Yom Tov; such should not be in Israel, for even Beit Shammai agree to it, for Beit Shammai's reason regarding gifts is since he is entitled to raise it today, and challah is like gifts regarding this that he is permitted to raise it on Yom Tov; and it is obvious its law is truly like gifts that Beit Shammai permit even if separated from erev Yom Tov, since today he has right to separate it; likewise with challah there is no distinction except terumah that cannot be separated today, as stated; and the Gaon Mahari Weil did not write his words except on this law of bringing to the kohen — for he did not mention this at all, only from separation he mentioned, and he refers to the law afterward, namely if one takes dough from erev Yom Tov whether he may separate challah on Yom Tov — Tur wrote Rif and Rashi forbade this, and on this Mahari Weil gave the difference between gifts and challah that with gifts it is permitted to separate today what was slaughtered last night, not so with challah; and the reason is those two reasons he mentioned, not so with challah; and it appears simple that the copyist who copied Mahari Weil's words erred in place in Tur's words — Mahari Weil wrote his words on the law of dough of erev Yom Tov after this, and the copyist copied his words here regarding bringing to the kohen, and this caused Beit Yosef's error; and nevertheless it is surprising that he did not notice that Mahari Weil's words never mention bringing to the kohen at all, and he was not precise in this. And at the end of the seif Beit Yosef wrote again Mahari Weil's words in the name of the Rashba, for there is truly their place; and Tosafos wrote in the first chapter of Beitzah there on the mishnah: Beit Shammai say one does not bring to the kohen challah and gifts, etc. — likewise in his house it is forbidden to give him unless he is regularly accustomed with him; and proof from the Yerushalmi; and Rashi Lamed brought from this at length and concludes that even for Beit Hillel who permit bringing to the kohen, if it is a matter forbidden to bring because an alley or karmelit interrupts, nevertheless it is forbidden to give him in his house unless he is regularly accustomed with him; and I did not know the source for his words, for certainly according to us we do not hold like the mishnah but like R' Yosi, and for Beit Hillel one brings even terumah — you will not find prohibition of giving in the house, for this is no worse than bringing; and further there is no prohibition in bringing via an alley, since it is ochel nefesh for the kohen, and there is no ochel nefesh issue of hotzaah on Yom Tov; and although for an Israelite there is no ochel nefesh here, nevertheless he brings it for the kohen to eat, for otherwise it would be forbidden even to move in his house since it is not fit for him; therefore the poskim did not mention this at all.
