(There) Seif 4 — One may pray in any language, etc. [n.b.] In the Gemara they gave a reason that ministering angels do not attend to Aramaic; the Tosafot challenged this, etc. And in my humble opinion it appears to explain the intent; and it is also understood from what is written "for You hear the prayer of every mouth" — what is the greatness in this? Is there before Him, blessed be He, a distinction between person and person? Rather the intent is: He, blessed be He, divided the nations into seventy nations and likewise divided seventy languages; and therefore, just as the nation below speaks the language allotted to it from Him, blessed be He, so its minister above speaks that language — this one in this and this one in that; and when the minister comes to seek mercy before Him, blessed be He, for his nation, he too speaks before Him, blessed be He, in the language of that nation. Therefore He said: "Hearer of prayer, every mouth" — every minister and minister in his language, and the Holy One, blessed be He, hears him. And behold for Israel the holy language was allotted; therefore our minister — Michael the great minister — also speaks only the holy language; and it is also possible he knows only the holy language; therefore an individual is not permitted to pray except in the language our minister speaks above; therefore the obligation for an individual to pray only in the holy language. And therefore regarding other ministering angels it is possible they know all languages; nevertheless our minister understands only the holy language. And in particular if they pray in another language not designated for us, this causes accusation: the ministers above each speak in the tongue the Holy One, blessed be He, allotted him, while we ask in another language not allotted to us. Only in congregation there is no accusation, for one may say the minister is only one and we are many; but an individual may not pray in another language — it is accusation from the ministers above that we pray in a language not allotted to us; therefore "may their spirit shrink" and "cursed be their portion" who enacted prayer in another language, may the the Master remember — and may the spirit of the Creator be aroused speedily, amen, so may it be His will. (There) in the same seif: And some say even an individual, etc. [n.b.] Their dispute depends on the explanation of the Gemara in chapter 1 of Shabbat: what we say that ministering angels do not attend to Aramaic — if the intent is they do not understand the language at all, or they understand but it is repugnant in their eyes. And the view that they understand everything but it is repugnant in their eyes is very difficult from the Gemara in Sotah (33a), which challenges: ministering angels do not understand Aramaic; and a baraita teaches: Yochanan the High Priest heard a bath kol, etc., and it was in Aramaic; and they answer: a bath kol is different, for it is to make heard; or alternatively Gabriel, as the master said: Gabriel came and taught him seventy languages. This is very difficult: per those who hold they do not know the language at all, it is well answered that it was Gabriel who prepared everything; and proof: the master said Gabriel came and taught seventy languages — proven he recognizes all. But per the view that they recognize all but it is repugnant — if so, perforce the challenge from the baraita that he would speak in Aramaic means: since it is repugnant in their eyes, he should not speak in that language; if so, what is answered "a bath kol is different, to make heard" — meaning even though repugnant, when there is need to make heard one must speak in Aramaic; if so, what is meant afterward "or alternatively Gabriel," as the master said Gabriel came and taught seventy languages — per that view one must explain the answer: Gabriel — Aramaic is not repugnant in his eyes; if so, what proof from "Gabriel came and taught seventy languages"? There too there was necessity for Yosef; and what proof for when there is no necessity that it not be repugnant — there too perforce there was necessity; otherwise why did they not learn until now; also the Holy One, blessed be He, would not perform miracles to deceive; perforce it was a need of the hour. If so, what proof is this for the general case? And apparently this proves perforce as those who hold they do not recognize the language at all; and those who hold they recognize but it is repugnant may say they explain the sugya: this is one answer — its intent is thus: "or alternatively a bath kol is different, to make heard; and when there is necessity, therefore it is not repugnant in their eyes." And if you say even when there is necessity it is still repugnant — then one must say it was Gabriel, for it is not repugnant in his eyes, as the master said Gabriel came and taught seventy languages; if so, in any case we see Gabriel spoke Aramaic. If we say he spoke because of need of the hour, the first answer remains — a bath kol is different, to make heard. If we say even when there is need of the hour it is repugnant — again one must say for Gabriel it is not repugnant; and perforce, as in my humble opinion, it is correct; examine well.
