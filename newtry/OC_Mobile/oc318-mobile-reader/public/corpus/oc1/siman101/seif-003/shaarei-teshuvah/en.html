To his ears. See Baer Heitev and Yad Ephraim's discussion based on Bechor Shor in Berachos. Ra'aya Mehemna, Parashas Pinchas, states: 'In prayer each person prays quietly, so that his voice in prayer is not heard by his fellow, etc.' This implies that even the person standing beside him should not hear. Zohar, Parashas Vayigash, says more: 'Therefore a person need not hear his voice in prayer,' implying that even he should not hear it with his own ears. R' Menachem di Lonzano is cited as saying that if he himself hears, he is not punished; if he causes another person to hear, he is punished even when the other hears only his voice and not the words. He supports this from the wording 'that his voice be heard,' rather than 'one who hears a voice.' This inference has no force: Why would the Zohar discuss the hearer, who has done nothing? It discusses only the person producing the sound. Nor is there proof from Zohar, Vayigash, for it may mean only that he should not make his voice audible to others. The word can be vocalized accordingly, or perhaps one letter was substituted for another. Nevertheless, it is also written in Arizal's name that he should not make the prayer audible to his own ears, and who can come after the king? Machazik Berachah writes that this standard is only for those who refine their conduct. For the public it is enough not to make oneself audible to another. Leave Israel to their practice, for these measures were stated only for exceptionally private people; see there. The essential rule depends on one's honest assessment. Some people, unless they articulate the words clearly enough for a slight sound to leave their lips, will fail amid their many thoughts even to move their lips, or will be overcome by sleep. When no other method works, one may at least produce an indistinct sound without making the words themselves audible. Even conscientious people who refine their conduct have done so when they sensed that their minds were not sufficiently settled to concentrate fully during silent prayer.
