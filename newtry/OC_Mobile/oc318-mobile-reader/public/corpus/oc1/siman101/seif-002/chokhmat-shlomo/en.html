My son, the sharp and incisive scholar Rabbi Avraham Binyamin, may he live, showed me Responsa MeAhavah, siman 13, where the author was asked about Rashi on I Samuel, who explains the verse "Eli thought her drunk" to mean that people were not accustomed to pray quietly. The questioner noted that Chazal taught that one must pray quietly; see there, where the author struggled to resolve this. In my humble opinion, it is readily explained according to Yalkut Shimoni on Parashas Vaeschanan, on the verse, "For what great nation has Hashem so close to it?" It states: Come and see, the ministering angels call out in a loud voice, as it is written, "The doorposts shook from the voice of the caller." Why? Because Hashem is distant from them. A Jew, however, stands and prays quietly, and Hashem hears him. Why? Because Hashem is close to him, as it is written, "For He stands at the right hand of the destitute." Thus, one to whom Hashem is close may pray quietly, whereas one from whom Hashem is distant must pray aloud. Hashem stands only near a male and not near a female, because it is not respectful for Him to do so, as stated by the Midrash and Rashi in Parashas Toldos on the verse, "Hashem said to her," describing how many indirect measures He took in order not to converse with a woman. Accordingly, it would not be respectful for Him to stand near her, and women therefore prayed aloud. Rashi's statement that "they were not accustomed" means that women were not accustomed to pray quietly. Eli therefore thought Channah drunk when she prayed quietly. She answered, however, "I am a woman of sorrowful spirit." Scripture states, "Hashem is close to the brokenhearted." This must mean that even one who is otherwise unworthy of closeness becomes close through a broken heart. For regarding ordinary people it already states, "Hashem is close to all who call upon Him." Thus, even regarding women, He is close when the woman is brokenhearted. She therefore said, "No, my master, I am a woman of sorrowful spirit," meaning that she was confident that Hashem was close to her, and she therefore prayed quietly. This is also the precise meaning of "I poured out my soul before Hashem," because He stood near her, and for that reason she prayed quietly. This is well explained, with Hashem's help; examine it carefully.
