Jerusalem Talmud, chapter 2: it was taught — one who prayed and did not make it audible to his ears has fulfilled his obligation. For whom was this necessary? For R’ Yosi. And R’ Yosi [is the one regarding whom it was taught] “one who reads Krias Shema and did not make it audible to his ears has fulfilled his obligation” — R’ Yosi says he did not fulfill his obligation. And so also the Mishnah, as the Gemara states that “all who make their voice heard” — meaning, to others. And so it is in the Zohar, Parshas Vayakhel. And this is unlike what is written in the Tosefta, chapter 3, cited by the Rashba and the Tur — “one might think he should make it audible to his ears; this has already been explained by Channah.” But this is a scribal error. And so it is in Tanna D’vei Eliyahu, beginning of chapter 28. And possibly also there it is a scribal error, since in the Gemara they cited the Tosefta and did not say “to his ears.” And so in our Tosefta. And so in the Jerusalem Talmud it is stated: “one might think he should raise his voice” — and see the Tur.

There, daf 24b.

So wrote the Rosh there, even though the Gemara only said [this regarding] making [one’s voice] audible. But in the Jerusalem Talmud, chapter 4, it is stated: R’ Yonah, when he prayed in the synagogue, would pray in a whisper; when he prayed at home, he would pray aloud until the members of his household learned prayer from him. The Rabbeinu Yonah and the Rosh explained this to mean: he would pray aloud to arouse intention to such an extent, until the members of his household would hear and learn the prayer. But the Rashba and the Tur explained that his intent in making himself heard was solely so they would learn the prayer from him — and so it is in the Mordechai: “so that they would learn from him,” etc. — as the Rama wrote: “and if,” etc.

Gemara, there.
