Magen Avraham s.k. 5. He wrote in Yad Maamarot, as stated; and what he wrote that it is better to pray, etc. — nevertheless it is plain one fulfills in the holy language even if he does not understand, as explained in Magen Avraham s.k. 51 and in siman 62 s.k. 1; and there it is explained in Rambam and Shulchan Aruch that he should beware of words of shame in that language and be precise in it like the holy language, etc. Therefore in our time, when no one knows the holy language thoroughly on its own, the lenient position for himself is that he fulfills in a foreign tongue; they rebuke him with reproof that he not depart from the ways of the congregation practiced in all the diasporas of Israel from antiquity — to say the text of prayer in the holy language, through which he fulfills even if he does not understand. One who comes to be concerned for what Shulchan Aruch wrote in Kitzur can easily learn the explanation of the matter at least; and since he understands the explanation, behold he knows what he requests and prays; and even if it is hard for him to accept the explanation of every word individually, there is no harm; and he should not abandon a living spring to dig broken cisterns. And also, if he does not wish to trouble himself even to learn the explanation, he has sufficient option to pray with the congregation in the holy language, and afterward who prevents his hand from reading the prayer afterward in a foreign tongue as well, like women who are accustomed to pray with the congregation and return and read to themselves the printed translation in a foreign tongue — I elaborated on this in a responsum, with God's help. And what is written in the name of Shulchan Aruch, siman 581 and 585, as stated.
