In the Jerusalem Talmud it is stated that R' Yonah, when he prayed in the synagogue, would pray in a whisper, but when he prayed at home he would pray aloud until the members of his household learned prayer from him. The Tur explains that "until they learned" is equivalent to "so that they would learn" — for otherwise, if he did not intend this and they happened to learn from him incidentally, it would be problematic, since there is a prohibition against raising one's voice. And one cannot say that his reason was that he could not concentrate while whispering, as that would be a disgrace to R' Yonah — implying that whenever he prayed in the synagogue he prayed without intention, since he required an audible voice to concentrate. Rather, his reason was necessarily so that the members of his household would learn. And so is the view of the Mordechai, who cites this Jerusalem Talmud with the language "so that they would learn from him," as the Beit Yosef wrote. And the Rosh wrote, and these are his words: he would pray thus to arouse the spirit of intent until the members of his household heard and learned prayer from him — and this applies to an individual, etc. — end quote. It appears he holds that even if one can concentrate in a whisper, nevertheless if one cannot concentrate as much as with a voice, it is called inability to concentrate and he may pray aloud as an individual; and if so, what was difficult for the Tur is resolved as we mentioned; and so we rule, as above.
