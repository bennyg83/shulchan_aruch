(s.k. 2) And not, etc. — siman 62 and siman 185, as stated. And this is regarding Birkat HaMazon: if he blessed Birkat HaMazon in his heart alone, he has not fulfilled his obligation.

For if not so, they should have enacted, etc. — for we hold that when Ezra's enactments were in force, a baal keri was forbidden in prayer; nevertheless, when the time for Keriat Shema arrived, they said (daf 20): he would contemplate in his heart, since Keriat Shema is d'oraisa; and even though thought is not like speech and does not help for Keriat Shema, nevertheless Chazal said he should contemplate so that the whole world would not be engaged in accepting the yoke of Heaven while he sits idle — which is not so regarding prayer: even thought they did not permit. And per Magen Avraham's view, on the contrary, for prayer — even though it is rabbinic — it would have been more fitting for Chazal to obligate him from Keriat Shema; for since thought is not like speech, he does not violate a prohibition if he contemplates and fulfills his obligation of prayer thereby. This deals with one who articulates from his lips; and what is written "in his heart" is not precise.

For it is a general rule. Meaning: that which we say regarding Keriat Shema, that we require one to articulate with his lips — it is not precise to Keriat Shema alone; rather, the same law applies to all matters, even prayer. And if you say regarding prayer, as it is written "and to serve Him with all your heart," thought suffices, as stated above — if so, since it is not a general rule, perhaps also for Birkat HaMazon thought suffices on another account; and as Magen Avraham wrote, for it is written "and you shall bless" — also with the heart is called a blessing. Perforce the Rosh holds that what is stated regarding Keriat Shema, that speech is required, is a general rule for all matters, both Birkat HaMazon and prayer. Or what Magen Avraham wrote regarding Keriat Shema: the reason is as written "and you shall teach them" — in the Gemara they said, as it is written "Hear, O Israel" — make heard to your ears what you utter with your mouth.
