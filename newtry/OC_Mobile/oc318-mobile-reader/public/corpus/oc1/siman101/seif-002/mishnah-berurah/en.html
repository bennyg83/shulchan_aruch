(5) And making [it] audible — some wrote that one should be concerned for the words of the Zohar, that even l’chatchila one should not make [the prayer] audible to his ears. But the Magen Avraham wrote there is no proof from the Zohar, and so wrote the Biur HaGra — that even the Zohar’s view is like the Shulchan Aruch. And so wrote the other acharonim, that it is better l’chatchila to make [the prayer] audible to his ears. And b’dieved, according to all opinions, if he said it so quietly that even his own ears did not hear, he has fulfilled his obligation, since he articulated [the words] with his lips.

(6) And he should not make [his voice] heard — as it is written regarding the prayer of Channah: “only her lips were moving and her voice was not heard.” And anyone who makes his voice heard in his prayer shows a deficiency in faith, as if he does not believe that the Holy One, blessed be He, hears a whispered prayer. And anyone who raises his voice in his prayer is [behaving] like the false prophets, whose practice was to cry out aloud to their idol. And see in Birkei Yosef and in Chayei Adam that l'chatchila one must pray quietly enough that even one's companion standing nearby cannot hear. Nevertheless, if he is unable to concentrate in this manner, he should do whatever enables him to concentrate, but take care not to disturb others — as stated below.

(7) His voice — and even during Pesukei D'zimra it is good not to raise one's voice, since the Holy One, blessed be He, hears a whisper — not like those who raise their voices excessively. However, on Shabbos when it is customary for one person to say Pesukei D'zimra aloud, it is perfectly fine [Peri Megadim].

(8) While whispering — the Taz wrote that even if one can concentrate while whispering, but not as well as when praying aloud, this is considered being unable to concentrate, and he may pray alone in an audible voice. See in the Biur Halacha, where we wrote that one should not be lenient in this.

(9) To raise his voice — and this refers to a case where even if he makes his voice audible to some degree he still will not be able to concentrate; otherwise it is forbidden.

(10) When praying with the congregation it is forbidden — even to make his voice audible to some degree, and certainly to raise his voice.
