In his heart — b'dieved, if he prayed in his heart, the Magen Avraham leans toward ruling that he has not fulfilled his obligation. And so wrote [the author of the book] Nefesh HaChayyim, chapter 5.

And if he cannot concentrate while whispering — see in the Mishna Berurah in the name of the Taz. And according to what is written in the Zohar, that if one raises his voice in prayer to the extent that his companion can hear, his prayer is not heard above — it appears to me that one should not rely on the leniency of the Taz. And regarding the Taz’s difficulty against the Rosh, see the Prisha and it will be resolved for you. And in particular, based on what is implied in the Biur HaGra in this seif and in seif 3 in the Rama — that per the views of the Rashba and the Tur, there is no source in the Gemara to permit raising one’s voice in a situation where one cannot concentrate — in any case with regard to the Taz’s ruling, one should certainly be stringent. But the Gra’s words require some further study, since it is proven explicitly in the Tur that it is permitted to raise one’s voice, and perhaps one can say that his intent is that per the Tur’s approach there is no Talmudic source for this ruling.

Since it disturbs the congregation — rather, if he cannot concentrate while whispering, he should go and pray at home aloud [Prisha]. And the case of seif 1 must therefore refer to one who, even at home and even aloud, cannot concentrate due to his excessive preoccupations. Nevertheless, nowadays we should not permit praying alone to arouse intention so that others will learn from him — unless he is among the great men of the generation, renowned for the fact that all his actions are for the sake of Heaven — so wrote the Peri Megadim.
