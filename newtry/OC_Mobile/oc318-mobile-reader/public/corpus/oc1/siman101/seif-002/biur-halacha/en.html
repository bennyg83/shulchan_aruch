In his heart. If, after the fact, he prayed only in his heart, Magen Avraham tends to rule that he did not fulfill his obligation. Nefesh HaChaim, chapter 5, rules likewise.
If he cannot concentrate while praying quietly. See Mishnah Berurah in the name of the Taz. Since the Zohar states that if one raises his voice in prayer so much that another can hear him, his prayer is not heard Above, it appears to me that one should not rely on the Taz's leniency. Regarding the Taz's question against the Rosh, see Perishah and the matter will be resolved. This is especially so because Biur HaGra here and in seif 3 in the gloss implies that according to Rashba and Tur, the Gemara provides no source permitting one to raise his voice when he cannot concentrate. In any event, one should certainly be stringent regarding the Taz's ruling. The Gra's words require some study, however, because Tur explicitly proves that raising one's voice is permitted. Perhaps the Gra means that according to Tur this ruling has no source in the Gemara.
Because he will disturb the congregation. Rather, if he cannot concentrate quietly, he should go home and pray aloud there, as stated by Perishah. The case in seif 1 must therefore concern one who cannot concentrate even at home and aloud because of his many distractions. Nevertheless, nowadays we should not issue a ruling permitting one to pray alone in order to arouse concentration, lest others learn from him, unless he is the leading authority of the generation and it is widely known that all his deeds are for the sake of Heaven. So writes Peri Megadim.
