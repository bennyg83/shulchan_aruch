(1) [Seif 1] One who prays must have intent in all the blessings; and if he cannot have intent in all of them, at least he should have intent in Avot, etc. — Tur wrote in the name of the Rosh that a person should accustom himself to have intent at least in the conclusion of every blessing, in which there are one hundred thirteen words, as in the prayer of Chana; and one hundred thirteen times "heart" in the Chumash, to say that intent of the heart is required in them — until here. And Bach, Levush, and Ohr Torah, section 1; and Rabbi Akiva Eiger, section 2; and Atzei Zahav, section 1; Rabbi Zalman, section 1; prayer leader of Kiryat Sefer 2. The reason Bach wrote: because the conclusion of the blessing is recounting His praise, blessed be He, and it is fitting that one direct his heart more in recounting His praise than in his request; and he wrote that Semak wrote thus at the beginning of siman 11 — see there; and the prayer leader brought it there. And it further appears to me the reason one must have more intent in the conclusion of every blessing: because it contains mention of the Name; and anyone who does not have intent in mention of the Name is punished, as explained in our words above, siman 5, section 5 — see there.
