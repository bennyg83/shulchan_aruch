Seif 1 — One who prays must, etc. [n.b.] It is explained in the books that a person must imagine himself as a poor person at the time of prayer. And their reason is simple, as is written: "for not with this and not with that, but with the poor is the afflicted spirit," and likewise it is written "a prayer for the poor when he wraps himself," and likewise the prayer of David: "Incline, O Lord, Your ear, for I am poor and needy," etc. But the reason of the matter and the benefit in this is: it is known from the verse "Let not the downtrodden return ashamed," and the Holy One, blessed be He, fulfills the Torah first; and since a person places himself as poor, therefore He, blessed be He, fulfills the Torah and does not return him empty. And this is what Scripture says: "Let not the downtrodden return ashamed" — "let the poor and needy praise Your name," meaning: through "let not the downtrodden return ashamed," it is fitting that poor and needy praise Your name, for they will be answered through this attribute of "let not the downtrodden return ashamed." And it comes out well and is clear. May it be His will that this be fulfilled in us, and "let not he return" — He, blessed be He — "my prayer empty," "for I am poor and needy," as stated. Therefore it appears fitting to say before "Elokei Nitzor" or afterward this verse "Let not the downtrodden return ashamed," etc. But one should be careful that he too do so; then one may be confident that He, blessed be He, will repay him measure for measure, and He, blessed be He, will also fulfill thus.
