(1) One who prays, etc. — He should train himself to direct his intention at least at the conclusion of each blessing.

(2) That he should direct his intention — this means understanding the meaning of the words. Therefore it is very appropriate for the masses to learn the meaning of the words of the entire prayer, and certainly of the blessing of Avos; and with regard to Modim, he is certainly obligated to know its explanation.

(3) During Avos — for it is the setting forth of the praises of the Omnipresent. Therefore it is not proper that at that moment he should turn his heart to other matters. Some poskim hold that Modim is also considered like Avos with regard to l'chatchila. And if he estimates that even during Avos he will not be able to concentrate, he should not pray at all until his mind settles enough that he will be able to concentrate at least during Avos.

(4) And nowadays, etc. — The Chayei Adam wrote: it appears to me that since, from the strict law, he must return and pray, in any case if he remembered before saying “Baruch Atah” at the end of the blessing, he should return and say from “Elokei Avraham,” etc.
