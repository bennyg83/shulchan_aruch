See the Taz. The Tur explained it per the Jerusalem Talmud as equivalent to "so that they would learn" — meaning that if one acts so that others will learn from him, he may raise his voice. However, raising one's voice excessively is forbidden. And therefore the cantors who raise their voices excessively — when repeating the prayer so that the congregation may respond Yehei Shemei Rabbah and Amen — this is a grave transgression [see Bach there]. And regarding what the Taz wrote, that otherwise it would be a disgrace to R' Yonah — this is proven from the Tur, as he wrote — see there. And see further in the Prisha: when his mind was clear he would pray in the synagogue, and when his mind was not clear he would pray at home. Nevertheless, nowadays we should not permit praying alone to arouse intention so that others will learn from him — unless he is one of the great men of the generation, renowned for the fact that all his actions are for the sake of Heaven.
