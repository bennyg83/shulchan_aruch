So that. See Taz. Tur explains this in accordance with the Yerushalmi, "so that they will learn," meaning that if one acts so that others will learn from him, he may raise his voice. Raising it excessively, however, is forbidden. Therefore, chazzanim who raise their voices excessively during the repetition in order that the congregation answer Yehei Shemei Rabbah and amen commit a severe transgression, as Bach writes; see there. Taz writes that otherwise the passage would imply criticism of Rabbi Yochanan, and Tur's wording proves this; see there. See also Perishah: when his mind was clear he prayed in the beis midrash, and when his mind was not clear he prayed at home. Nevertheless, nowadays we should not rule that one may pray alone to arouse concentration, lest others learn from him, as stated in siman 90, seif 18, unless he is the leading authority of the generation and it is publicly known that all his deeds are for the sake of Heaven.
