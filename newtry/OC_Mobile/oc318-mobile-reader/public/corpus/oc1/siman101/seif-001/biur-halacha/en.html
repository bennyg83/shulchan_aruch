One who prays, etc. — This applies even per the view stated above in siman 60 seif 4, that mitzvos do not require intention — since there the intent is to fulfill the obligation [of the mitzvah]. This case is different, as the concern here is that he should not be directing his thoughts toward other matters during prayer. And it is implied from the language of the Rashba cited in the Beit Yosef above in siman 63 s.v. "katav" that one must take care during Avos not to let his heart turn toward other matters in the middle of the blessing — and even if he returns and concentrates when he subsequently recites the words of the blessing, this is [still problematic], as it appears at first glance from the plain meaning of his language there, though there is some room to counter this. And this requires further study with regard to b’dieved. But in any case, l’chatchila one must be very careful in this regard.

And if he is not able, etc. — This language implies that even l'chatchila it is permitted to stand and pray, since he is acting under compulsion, being unable to settle his mind. And so is implied by the language of the Baraisa. And it is remarkable that this is not mentioned in the Rambam, who writes this ruling only in the language of b'dieved — that if he directed his intention during the first blessing he has fulfilled his obligation. Perhaps when one is unable, the Rambam also agrees that it is better than not praying at all. In any case, it appears obvious that if he can wait and settle his mind from his preoccupations without thereby causing the time of Krias Shema or prayer to pass, he must settle his mind [before praying].

He should direct his intention during Avos — see above in siman 53 seif 5 in the Rama, which implies there that b’dieved it is effective if the prayer leader prayed even though he does not understand what he is saying. Perhaps there the discussion is of one who understands at least the blessing of Avos; or perhaps the prayer leader is better since “one who listens is like one who answers” — as if the listener himself recited the blessing of Avos and he understands its meaning. And according to what the Peri Megadim wrote in siman 690, in his Aleph Aleph note 9, it is impossible to fulfill [the obligation] in that manner — and so [the resolution] is according to our first answer.

And nowadays we do not repeat, etc. — At first glance, the intent is that if he finished the Shemoneh Esrei and did not concentrate during Avos [he does not repeat]. But if he is standing at “Atah Gibor” and remembers that he did not concentrate during Avos — since strictly speaking he has not fulfilled his obligation, how can we tell him to [continue and] recite additional blessings that he has not fulfilled? Having missed the blessing of Avos, should we tell him to recite additional blessings that he certainly did not fulfill? This is what appeared to me. But from the words of the Chayei Adam, rule 24, law 2, it is implied that he does not repeat even if he is standing at “Atah Gibor.” It appears preferable to me to suggest another approach in this situation: he should not continue at all, and should wait for the prayer leader to reach the blessing of Avos and direct his intention to fulfill his obligation through it. When the prayer leader reaches the blessing “Atah Gibor,” he should begin himself — for we rule regarding prayer that [the prayer leader] can only fulfill the obligation for one who is not proficient in the prayer, which is not the case for Avos, since he is unable to recite it himself.
