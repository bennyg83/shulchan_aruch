One who prays, etc. This applies even according to the opinion above in siman 60, seif 4, that mitzvos do not require intent. There, the issue is intent to fulfill the mitzvah, whereas here the requirement is that one not direct his thoughts to other matters during prayer. The wording of the Rashba cited by Beit Yosef above in siman 63, s.v. "he wrote," implies that during Avos one must take care that his mind not turn to other matters in the middle of the blessing, even if he later returns to concentration when reciting the words of the blessing. This appears from the plain meaning of his words, although it may be somewhat rebutted. The after-the-fact ruling requires study, but initially one must certainly be very careful about this.
If he is unable, etc. This wording implies that even initially he may stand and pray, since circumstances beyond his control prevent him from settling his mind. The wording of the baraisa likewise implies this. It is surprising that Rambam did not mention this, for he states the law only after the fact, that if one concentrated during the first blessing he fulfilled his obligation. Perhaps Rambam agrees that when one is unable to concentrate, this is preferable to not praying at all. In any event, it is clear that if he can wait and settle his mind from his distractions without thereby missing the times for Krias Shema and prayer, he must first settle his mind.
He should concentrate during Avos. See above, siman 53, seif 5 in the gloss, which implies that after the fact it is effective when a chazzan prayed even though he did not understand what he was saying. Perhaps that case concerns one who at least understands the blessing of Avos. Alternatively, a chazzan may be preferable because hearing is equivalent to responding, so it is as though the listener himself recited the blessing of Avos, and the listener understands its meaning. According to what Peri Megadim writes in siman 690, Eshel Avraham, seif katan 9, one cannot discharge another's obligation in this manner. We must therefore adopt our first answer.
Nowadays we do not repeat, etc. Seemingly, this refers to one who completed the Amidah without concentrating during Avos. If, however, he is standing at Atah Gibor and remembers that he did not concentrate during Avos, then according to the strict law he has not fulfilled his obligation. How can we tell him to recite further blessings through which he cannot fulfill his obligation, since the blessing of Avos is missing? Merely because he will probably fail to concentrate again, should we tell him to recite further blessings through which he certainly cannot fulfill his obligation? This seemed correct to me. However, Chayei Adam, rule 24, law 2, implies that he does not return even while standing at Atah Gibor. A better solution appears to me: he should say nothing further at that point, but wait for the chazzan to recite Avos and intend to fulfill his obligation through him. When the chazzan reaches Atah Gibor, he should begin praying on his own. Although we rule that in prayer one can discharge only the obligation of someone who is not proficient, Avos is different here because he is unable to recite it himself.
