[1] He should concentrate during Avos, etc. Abudraham explains that Avos is more important than the other blessings because it contains the greatness of Hashem, the merit of the early righteous, and the redemption of the later generations. Kol Bo writes that it contains 42 words, corresponding to the Divine Name of 42 letters. Beit Yosef cites Semak: If one cannot concentrate during all the blessings of the Amidah, he should concentrate during Avos and Modim; if he cannot concentrate during both, he should concentrate during Avos. Tur, siman 113, adds that this is why the blessing begins with the letter beis and concludes with mem. Beit Yosef writes that it requires study how Semak knew that Modim is preferable to the other blessings, and therefore Shulchan Aruch did not mention Modim. I found, however, in Agudah at the end of the chapter Ein Omdin: "He should concentrate during one of them. One said during Avos, and one said during thanksgiving." This shows that such was the text of the Gemara. It appears that initially Semak was concerned for both views, while after the fact he regarded the opinion requiring Avos as primary. It is surprising that Beit Yosef did not see Agudah. I likewise found in Sefer Chassidim, siman 158, that one should concentrate during Avos or during thanksgiving, and Kol Bo and Maharil also rule like Semak. Bach also gives a reason for Semak: Avos begins the first three blessings, which arrange Hashem's praise, while Modim begins the final three, and therefore one bows at the beginning and end of Avos and thanksgiving. This is difficult, since Modim is the second blessing of the final three. One must say that because it begins the blessings of praise, it requires concentration, whereas Retzeh is in the nature of a request for one's needs. Still, the expression "the beginning of the final three" is puzzling. Furthermore, the reason for bowing at Modim is to humble oneself through thanksgiving, as explained below in siman 121. Indeed, for this reason it appears that concentration is required.
