[1] He should direct his intention during Avos, etc. — The Abudraham explained that it is more important than the other blessings because they contain the greatness of Hashem’s Name, the merit of the forefathers, and the redemption of the latter generations — end of his words. And the Kol Bo wrote that it is because it contains 42 words, corresponding to the 42-letter Name. The Beit Yosef wrote that the Sefer Mitzvos Katan wrote: if one cannot direct his intention in all eighteen blessings, he should direct his intention in Avos and Modim; if he cannot direct his intention in both, he should direct his intention in Avos — end of his words. And in the Tur, siman 113, he adds that therefore the blessing begins with the letter beis and ends with the letter mem. And the Beit Yosef wrote: one must study from where he derives that Modim is preferable to the other blessings — end of his language. Therefore [the Mechaber] did not mention Modim in the Shulchan Aruch. But I found in the Agudah, end of chapter Ain Omdin: “he should direct his heart in one of them — one said in Avos and one said in Hodaah [Modim]” — end of his words. This implies that this was indeed the Talmudic reading. And it appears that l’chatchila the Sefer Mitzvos Katan was concerned for both, while b’dieved he holds the primary ruling follows the one who says Avos. And this is remarkable concerning the Beit Yosef, that he did not see the words of the Agudah. And so I found in Sefer Chassidim siman 158: “he should direct his intention in Avos or in Hodaah.” And so the Kol Bo and Maharil ruled like the Sefer Mitzvos Katan. Also the Bach wrote a reason for the words of the Sefer Mitzvos Katan — that the blessing of Avos was the beginning of the first three [blessings], which enumerate His praises, and Modim was the beginning of the last three; therefore he bows at Avos and at Hodaah — first and last. End of his words. But there is a difficulty with him, since Modim is the second blessing of the last three. And one must say that nevertheless, since it is the beginning of the blessings of praise, it requires intention — the blessing of Retzeh is a kind of petition for one’s needs, but the formulation “beginning of the last three” is problematic. Furthermore, the reason for bowing at Modim is in order to submit oneself through the act of thanks, as stated below in siman 121. And indeed, for this reason it appears that it requires intention.
