[4] He shall not make his voice audible, etc. One who does so is among those of little faith, and this was also learned from Channah's prayer. See Eliyah Zuta on this. I further wrote in the name of Malbushei Yom Tov that all this applies to the quiet Amidah. In all these lands, however, people customarily raise their voices and cry aloud during piyyutim and similar passages, to the point that non-Jews mock this. All my life I was distressed to know the source of this custom, since Jewish custom has the force of Torah, until Hashem enlightened my eyes and I saw that Ramban at the end of Parashas Bo writes: "The purpose of raising voices in prayer," etc., as Chazal said on the verse, "They called out to Hashem with strength," from here you learn that prayer requires a voice. See above, siman 95, seif 3.
