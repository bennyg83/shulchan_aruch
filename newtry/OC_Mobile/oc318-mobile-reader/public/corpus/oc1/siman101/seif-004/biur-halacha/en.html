One may pray at all, etc. — See the Mishna Berurah. And the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Sefer Chassidim that it is better to pray in a language one understands if one does not understand the holy tongue. And it is proven in Sefer Chassidim siman 588 that this applies specifically when he is G-d-fearing and his sole intent is to pray with concentration. But if he is not in that situation, he should pray in the holy tongue — for the holy tongue possesses many special qualities above all other languages; it is the language in which the Holy One, blessed be He, speaks with His prophets, as the Ramban wrote in Parshas Tissa. And our Sages said that the world was created with the holy tongue, as it is written “to this one shall be called woman (ishah) for she was taken from man (ish).” Also, when the members of the Knesset HaGedolah composed the text of the prayer, they were 120 elders, among them several prophets, and they deliberated over each blessing, over each of its words and the combinations of its letters, incorporating many concealed and sublime secrets. When we recite these words as the Knesset HaGedolah formulated them, even if we do not know how to direct our intentions, our prayer nonetheless ascends properly — for the words themselves activate their holiness above. This is not the case when one prays in a vernacular language. See the Biur Halacha.
