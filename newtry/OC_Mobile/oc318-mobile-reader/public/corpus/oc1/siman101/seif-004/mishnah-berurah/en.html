(13) In any language. The choicest fulfillment of the mitzvah is specifically in Lashon HaKodesh. See siman 62, seif 2, and what we wrote there in Mishnah Berurah in the name of the later authorities. See also Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chaim, simanim 84 and 86, who proves at length that the permission to pray in any language applies only occasionally. To establish another language as a permanent practice, appoint a chazzan to use it, and thereby cause Lashon HaKodesh to be entirely forgotten is impossible under any circumstances. For many additional sound reasons, all the leading authorities of the time wrote at length in the work Divrei HaBris and agreed that doing so is absolutely forbidden. This excludes the new sects that arose abroad and translated the entire prayer text into the vernacular. One sin leads to another, and they omitted the blessing for the ingathering of the exiles and the blessing "And to Jerusalem Your city." Just as they wish to erase the memory of Jerusalem, they wish to erase Lashon HaKodesh from Israel, lest Israel be redeemed in the merit of not having changed their language. May Hashem protect us from such heretical opinions. See Biur Halachah.
(14) That he desires. This applies only when he thoroughly understands that language. In Lashon HaKodesh, however, he fulfills his obligation even if he does not understand the language.
(15) Only in Lashon HaKodesh. For the ministering angels do not attend to Aramaic, and the same applies to other languages besides Lashon HaKodesh. The community, however, requires no intermediary, because Hashem Himself accepts their prayer.
(16) For an ill person. Meaning when praying not in the patient's presence. In the patient's presence, one may pray in any language, because the Shechinah is present there, as stated in Shabbos 12b.
(17) The established prayer. Since the prayer is established for the community, Hashem Himself turns to it even when it is not recited at the time the congregation is praying.
(18) Even an individual. This opinion holds that the angels understand every language, but do not attend to Aramaic because it is contemptible in their eyes. Therefore, women are permitted to pray in other languages.
(19) Aramaic. Communal prayer may be recited even in Aramaic. This explains why Yekum Purkan, Berich Shemei, and similar passages are recited publicly, as stated by Peri Megadim. Accordingly, one praying at home may not recite any Yekum Purkan. This is likewise evident from the Or Zarua HaGadol, Hilchos Shabbos, siman 50.
