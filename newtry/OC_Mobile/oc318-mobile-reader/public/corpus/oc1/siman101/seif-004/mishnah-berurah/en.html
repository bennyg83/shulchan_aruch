(13) In any language — and the most preferred fulfillment of the mitzvah is specifically in the holy tongue. See siman 62 seif 2 and in the Mishna Berurah there what we wrote in the name of the acharonim regarding this. And also see the responsa of the Chatam Sofer, Orach Chayyim siman 84 and 86, where he elaborates with many proofs that what was permitted — to pray in any language — refers specifically to occasional instances. But to establish this as a permanent fixed practice, to appoint a cantor [who prays in another language], and to cause the holy tongue to be completely forgotten — this is absolutely impossible under any circumstances. See there. Furthermore, for many weighty reasons, all the gedolim of the generation elaborated in the book Divrei HaBrit and agreed that it is a complete prohibition to do so — and this is to exclude the new [reform] movements that have broken out outside the country in this regard, who translated the entire text of the prayer into the language of the nations. One transgression leads to another, as they omitted the blessing of the Ingathering of the Exiles and the blessing of “and to Jerusalem Your city.” And just as they wish to erase the remembrance of Jerusalem, so they wish to cause the holy tongue to be forgotten from Israel, lest [Israel] be redeemed by virtue of not having changed their language. May the Holy One, blessed be He, protect us from such heretical views. See the Biur Halacha.

(14) That he wishes — provided that he understands that language fully. But in the holy tongue, one fulfills his obligation even if he does not understand the language.

(15) Only in the holy tongue — because the ministering angels do not attend to other languages, and the same applies to all other languages except the holy tongue. But a congregation does not need an intermediary, since the Holy One, blessed be He, Himself accepts their prayer.

(16) For a sick person — meaning, not in the sick person's presence. But in the presence of the sick person, it is permitted in any language, since the Divine Presence is there [Shabbos daf 12b].

(17) The fixed [liturgy] — since this prayer is fixed for the congregation, the Holy One, blessed be He, Himself attends to it even outside the times when the congregation prays.

(18) Even an individual — for he holds that the angels recognize every language, only they do not attend to Aramaic since it is unseemly in their eyes. And therefore women are permitted to pray in other languages.

(19) Aramaic — but when praying with a congregation, even Aramaic is permitted. And this is why it is proper that we say Yekum Purkan and Brich Shemei when praying with a congregation, and similar [prayers] [Peri Megadim]. And accordingly, if one prays at home, he may not recite any Yekum Purkan. And so it is proven in the Or Zarua HaGadol in the Laws of Shabbos, siman 50.
