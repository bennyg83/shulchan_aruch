(א) An individual who spent Shabbat in a field — The same applies even in a settled place, permitted up to two se'ah, as below siman 362 seif 5; and 'field' teaches that even in a field where he cannot make excellent partitions, the Sages were stringent and forbade if he holds more than two se'ah.
(ב) Such as lechayim, etc. — meaning he planted reeds in the ground less than three tefachim apart; and it refers when they are stuck in the ground firmly so wind does not move them — otherwise it is not considered a partition at all, as siman 362 seif 1.
(ג) Or lavud without lechayim — such as he surrounded with several ropes one above another with less than three between rope and rope; see Sefer Gaon Yaakov who brought Rashba and Ritva that standing more than the breach also counts as inferior partition like lechayim without lavud; other poskim did not mention this; Yerushalmi does not imply so — see there.
(ד) Up to two se'ah — and including exactly two; more than two se'ah, not even for his needs.
(ה) From two se'ah onward — meaning the enclosure held more than bet se'ah; the Sages forbade carrying in it like other karmelit; nevertheless it is not considered full karmelit to permit taking from it to another karmelit, for it is truly complete reshut hayachid through lavud — only the Sages were stringent l'chatchila because it is an inferior partition. With complete partitions one may carry throughout even holding much more than bet se'ah, for they enclosed for residence since they enclosed to dwell there on Shabbat, even for one Shabbat only [later authorities].
(ו) And the same if they are two — meaning here too we do not give both together more than bet se'ah.
(ז) Throughout the enclosure — for we consider the entire partition as if it does not exist.
(ח) When they enclosed more than six, etc. — six se'ah is for three people, each bet se'ah; they enclosed more, e.g. seven, and needed only five — two se'ah remain empty and therefore forbidden. But if they did not enclose more than six, since six is the measure for three, each bet se'ah — we are not stringent even if bet se'ah remains empty; see Taz and R' Avraham; and Sefer Gaon Yaakov that all early authorities dispute this and hold: even with less than six, whenever two se'ah are empty it is forbidden.
