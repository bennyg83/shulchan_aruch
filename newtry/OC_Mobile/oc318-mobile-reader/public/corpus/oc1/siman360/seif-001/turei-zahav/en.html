(א) And the same law if they are two — Beit Yosef was uncertain: does it mean that for two we also give each a bet se'ah together since they spent Shabbat together, or that we give each only bet se'ah? In chapter Kol Gagot regarding three karpeifot 93a it implies each gets bet se'ah — it says the middle one has walls, etc., we give them only bet shisha. Perhaps one may distinguish: there each spent Shabbat in his own karpeif, but here where two spent Shabbat in one karpeif perhaps we give them only one karpeif. Until here. In my humble opinion, since we say in the first chapter R' Nachman taught: one person — bet se'ah; two — bet se'ah; three — become a caravan and we give them six — and if we thought two get four se'ah, what is the superiority of three getting six? Why do we need the status of caravan if we give proportionally? Rather necessarily until three we do not give proportionally; only with three specifically is there superiority that it is proportional — and the conclusion is all their needs. So Rambam, Tur, and R' Yonasan: two se'ah for three individuals.

(ב) But if they did not enclose more than six, etc. — So Beit Yosef in the name of Mordechai. One may ask: Rashi explained explicitly that even though they are three, if they need only two se'ah and enclosed four se'ah the partitions are void — and we find no one disputes Rashi; at minimum he should have brought Rashi's words and revealed a dispute. What Beit Yosef brought as proof from Rav Gidal — they needed only five and enclosed seven, even four are forbidden — is not proof; for if we say there is no superiority to six over more than six, there is no novelty at all. In all cases if more than bet se'ah remains empty they are forbidden — Tur and other poskim did not distinguish this.
