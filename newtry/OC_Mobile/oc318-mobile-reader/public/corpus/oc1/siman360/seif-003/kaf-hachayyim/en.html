And he surrounded it with inferior partitions, etc. With complete partitions, meaning a lattice of both vertical and horizontal members, one may carry even within an area measuring a kor or two kor, since the enclosure was made for residence there on that Shabbos. Beis Yosef in the name of Rashi; Olas Shabbos, subsection 1; Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 2; Eshel Avraham, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there. However, the words of Rambam in chapter 16, halachos 12 and 18, imply that there is no distinction, and that even with a complete partition one may not carry in an area larger than beis sasayim. See there. Chemed Moshe, subsection 1. Nesiv Chaim writes likewise in the name of Rabbeinu Yehonasan, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and Ritva. See there. Nevertheless, the words of the authorities cited above imply that we rule in accordance with Rashi, that with complete partitions one may carry even in an area larger than beis sasayim. Shiltei HaGibborim and Or Zarua likewise write that with complete partitions even an individual may carry throughout all the space required, even if an area of beis sasayim or more remains empty of utensils. See there. Pischei Olam, subsection 1. A complete partition need not consist entirely of both vertical and horizontal members. If it consists of vertical members and two or three horizontal members were added to reinforce it, it is called complete. Similarly, if it consists of horizontal members and two or three vertical members were added, it is called complete. Mishbetzos Zahav, siman 363, subsection 1, writes likewise. See there. Although he writes that the case requires examination, this refers to adding one member; with two or three, it is properly called a complete partition.
