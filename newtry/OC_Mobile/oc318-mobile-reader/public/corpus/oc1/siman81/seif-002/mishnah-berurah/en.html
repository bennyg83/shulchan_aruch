(7) He was reciting. The same applies if he was praying.
(8) And saw excrement. The same applies to urine, as stated below in siman 90, seif 27. Regarding where he resumes afterward, however, all authorities agree that in the case of urine he returns only to the place where he stopped, as explained above at the end of siman 76.
(9) Opposite him. The case is one in which the excrement was more than four amos from the point at which its odor ceased. Had it been within four amos, all authorities would agree that he must return to the beginning, because he was negligent in failing to check beforehand, as explained above at the end of siman 76.
(10) That he should throw it. The same applies if he turns his face toward the west, since he is not standing in the Amidah.
(11) Four amos. From the point at which the odor ceases.
(12) A river was before him. The case is one in which he cannot turn his face toward the west for some reason. Otherwise, he should turn his face away. See Magen Avraham.
(13) To the place where he stopped. This view holds that since the excrement was not within his four amos, we do not say that he should have checked. The Torah did not trouble him to inspect the entire range of his sight. The latter view holds that since this was a place in which there was reason for doubt, he was negligent in reciting Krias Shema or praying before clarifying the matter. See Eliyah Rabbah, who writes that the Rosh and the Rokeach also agree with Rabbeinu Yonah. Nevertheless, as a matter of practical halachah, it appears that one should not be stringent after the fact, since the Derech HaChaim, Shulchan Shlomo, and other later authorities of our era all cite only the first opinion as the ruling. If he paused long enough to complete the entire recitation, see above in siman 65 and the Mishnah Berurah there.
