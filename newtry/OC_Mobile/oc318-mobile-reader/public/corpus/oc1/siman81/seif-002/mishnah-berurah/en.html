(7) If he was reciting [Shema]—and the same applies if he was praying.

(8) And he saw feces—the same applies regarding urine; and as below in siman 90, se'if 127. But regarding returning afterward, for all opinions regarding urine he returns only to the place where he stopped, as stated at the end of siman 76.

(9) Opposite him—this refers when it is more than four amot from where the smell ended; for if it were within four, for all opinions he would need to return to the beginning, for he was negligent—he should have checked from the start—and as stated at the end of siman 76.

(10) That it be cast behind him—and the same applies that he can turn his face toward the west side, since he is not standing in the Shemoneh Esrei prayer.

(11) Four amot—from the place where the smell ended.

(12) Before him a river—and it refers when he cannot turn his face to the west for some reason; for otherwise he would turn his face and stand four amot.

(13) To the place where he stopped—the first view holds: since it was not within his four amot, we do not say he should have checked—for the Torah did not trouble him to check in plain sight. The latter view holds: since it was a place where one should be concerned, he was negligent that he should not recite and pray until it was clarified for him. See Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote that Rosh and Rokeach also hold like Rabbeinu Yonah; nevertheless for practical law it seems we need not be stringent bedi'eved, for Darchei HaChaim and Shulchan Aruch Shelomo and other acharonim of our time all cited only the first opinion for practical law. And if he waited long enough to complete all of it, see above siman 65 and Mishna Berurah there.
