A kezayit. See Turei Zahav. Rashi follows his own view that the child must specifically have eaten these foods, because feces produced by these kinds of food have a foul odor. He therefore correctly explains that one should not think the law applies specifically to bread; it applies equally to porridge. However, it was demonstrated that even according to Rashi this is not exact: the phrase “from when he ate” serves only to establish the measure, and the same law applies if he never tasted it. Even so, one should not think the child must specifically be able to eat bread. The ruling teaches that even if he has reached only the ability to eat porridge, one must distance oneself. The measure is based on grain, because he may be able to eat other foods but not grain; the Sages specifically established grain as the measure for this law. See Magen Avraham.
