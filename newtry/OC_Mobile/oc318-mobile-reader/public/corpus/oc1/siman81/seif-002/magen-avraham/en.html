And if this is impossible, for example, etc. This is difficult. He should turn his face away so that the excrement is behind him. The Gemara's case is understandable regarding the Amidah, because one must face Jerusalem, but here, regarding Krias Shema, the ruling is difficult. It appears to me that the case is one in which excrement lies on both sides of him and a river is in front of him. One might ask that he should walk backward until he passes the excrement and then turn his face away from it. The answer is that the Sages did not trouble him to walk backward when he can place it to his sides.
