To eat. From this wording it implies that even if he cannot eat—for example, he is weak by nature—nevertheless, since others who are older in years like him can eat, he must distance. It is puzzling, for we do not find this in any posek; therefore it seems the meaning is: if another like him can eat, presumably he too can eat. Magen Avraham there. Bach wrote that for a minor one need not distance if we know this feces does not come from grain—for example, he has not eaten grain for several days; and if we know he never ate an olive-sized amount of grain, even a child of several years need not distance—see there. Taz disagrees with him—see there. See Pri Chadash and Sedei Yehoshua in the chapter "One Whose Dead Relative Is Before Him."

Grain. All five species are called grain and their feces putrefies. Also in food made for a baby called "pappa" we estimate according to what an adult can eat—a peras. Magen Avraham. The measure of a peras—see siman 612. The measure of a kezayit—see siman 486. In Yoreh De'ah siman 265 se'if 8, in Hagahot Maimoniyot it implies that it is good and proper to distance from a minor's feces even an eight-day-old—see there. Maharshal wrote it is not proper to recite Shema beside an infant, for ordinarily an infant is diapered in refuse—end quote. Magen Avraham wrote, and it seems to me for another reason, that they do not wipe themselves and feces in the anal orifice—even if covered—is forbidden—end quote.
