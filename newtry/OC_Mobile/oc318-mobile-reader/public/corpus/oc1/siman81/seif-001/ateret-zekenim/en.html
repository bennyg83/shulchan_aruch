If one was reciting and saw feces in front of him, he should walk far enough to position it behind him, etc. The same law applies if he remains where he is, turns his face, leaves the feces behind him, and distances himself four cubits from it. The Gemara uses the language of walking only in connection with prayer, where it discusses one walking eastward and he cannot act otherwise, because in any event he must turn his face back toward the east. This is the implication of Rabbeinu Yonah. Alternatively, the Gemara mentions the remedy of walking because it is easier. If so, if he wishes to stand still and delay his journey in order to turn his face westward, thereby leaving the feces four cubits behind him, it is obvious that he may do so, and this is preferable. Eshel Avraham Zuta.
