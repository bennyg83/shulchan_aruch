If he was reciting and saw feces opposite him, he should go so that he throws it behind him, etc. The same applies if he remains in his place and turns his face and places the feces behind him and distances four amot—only that the Gemara's wording is thus regarding prayer, and there it discusses one who walks toward the east and cannot do otherwise in this matter, because in any case he must turn his face toward the east (R' Yehuda's meaning). Or one may say that the enactment chose the wording of "going" because it is lenient; and if so, if he wishes to stand and delay himself from his way so that he turns his face toward the west in order to place feces there behind him, four amot—it is obvious that he may do so, and it is better (Ateret Zekenim).
