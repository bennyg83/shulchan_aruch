(1) [Se'if 1] A minor who has reached the category of one like him, etc.—and even though he has never eaten grain at all, we distance from his feces and his urine, for once he has reached this category his feces putrefies—Beit Yosef, Levush, Ohr Torah letter 1, Taz seder katan 1; and he concludes that Bach's words do not appear correct at all, and Shulchan Aruch's ruling is clear and established—see there. Likewise Sedei Chemed in Mekor Chayyim letter 1 and 2. Likewise Ohr Torah there that one should be stringent per the author. And Rav David Arameh in his commentary on Rambam concludes per Maran—see there; Maamer Mordechai brought it letter 2. Likewise Kaf HaChayyim letter 1, Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 5 letter 5. However Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Beit Yosef letter 2 brought Bach's words, that if he did not eat an olive of grain, or even if he ate and it is known this feces is not from grain food, we do not distance—and it seems he agrees to his words. And likewise the opinion of Eliyah Rabbah letter 4, and wrote that Nachalat Tzvi agreed many times. Pri Chadash wrote for practical halacha that lekhatchilah one should be concerned for the author's words, that any minor who reached the category of one like him who can eat grain must distance from his feces; and bedieved if he read or prayed he has fulfilled his obligation and need not return to read—see there. Machatzis HaBashekel agreed in letter 2 and Seder Beit Yaakov brought it letter 29 (however what Beit Yaakov wrote there in letter 30, Bach's words, is astonishing—for how after he brought Maran's words and Pri Chadash in letter 29 did he return in letter 30 to bring Bach's words, which oppose Maran and Pri Chadash—requires study). Shulchan Aruch HaTev letter 1, likewise Kaf HaChayyim there. See Yoreh De'ah siman 265 se'if 8 in the gloss, which implies it is good and proper to distance from a minor's feces even an eight-day-old; Magen Avraham brought this in this siman seder katan 1. Chayyei Adam general 3 letter 8, likewise Kaf HaChayyim there, Kitzur Shulchan Aruch there. "Eight days" is not precise, but even for a one-day-old one should be careful, as R' Zalman wrote letter 2.
