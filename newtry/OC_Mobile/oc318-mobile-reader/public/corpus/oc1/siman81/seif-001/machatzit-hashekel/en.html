It appears to me for another reason, etc. Excrement at the anal opening is forbidden even when covered. In Shulchan Aruch Baer Heitev it is written that this is an error, because excrement at the anal opening is forbidden only to the person whose anal opening contains the excrement, but not to others. In the sefer Chok Yaakov I saw that he brings proof for the Baer Heitev from Rashi in Yoma 30a. Regarding Rav Pappa's statement that excrement in its place, meaning at the anal opening, forbids reciting Krias Shema, Rashi explains: 'It is forbidden for this person to recite Krias Shema.' This implies that the prohibition applies specifically to that person. He discusses this at length; see there. In my humble opinion, however, the words of the Magen Avraham follow the Gemara's conclusion. The Gemara establishes Rav Pappa's statement as referring to a case in which the excrement is visible when the person sits but not visible when he stands. It asks why this differs from excrement on one's flesh, regarding which Rav Huna permits reciting Krias Shema and Rav Chisda forbids it. The Gemara answers that in its place the filth is greater. Rashi explains that it has not emerged into the air and its odor has not dispersed. Thus, although covered excrement on one's flesh is permitted even for that person, just as it is permitted for another person, excrement at the anal opening is nevertheless forbidden because the filth is greater. For that same reason it should also be forbidden to another person, since we find no distinction between the affected person and another person. Rashi's phrase, 'It is forbidden for this person to recite Krias Shema,' reflects the Gemara's initial assumption, before it concluded that excrement at the anal opening is worse than excrement elsewhere. The Gemara then asks from excrement on one's flesh. Rashi was compelled to explain why the Gemara challenged Rav Pappa specifically from excrement on one's flesh, rather than asking simply: Since the excrement is covered, why should it be forbidden? After all, even excrement enclosed in glass is permitted, as Rava states in Berachos 25, because the Torah made the matter depend on covering, as it says, 'You shall cover your excrement,' and here it is covered. Necessarily, the initial assumption was that Rav Pappa's statement that excrement in its place is forbidden meant that it is forbidden only to that person, for whom covering does not help. The Gemara therefore asks from excrement on one's flesh, proving that covering helps even for the affected person. According to the conclusion, however, that the filth is greater, what basis is there to distinguish between that person and another, when nowhere do we find such a distinction regarding excrement and there is no reason to make one?
