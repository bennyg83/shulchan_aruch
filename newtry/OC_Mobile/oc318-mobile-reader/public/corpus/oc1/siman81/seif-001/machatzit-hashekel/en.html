And in Yoreh De'ah siman 26 — and Rama wrote there: one should clean the infant (meaning, when they circumcise him) from his excrement before he blesses. And Shach wrote: even though we do not distance from a child's excrement until, etc., and as written in Orach Chayyim siman 81 — nevertheless it is better to clean him before the blessing — end quote:
And it seems to me for another reason, etc. And excrement at the anus, even if covered, is forbidden; and in Shulchan Aruch Baer Heitev wrote it is an error — for excrement at the anus is forbidden only for that person whose excrement is at his anus, but not for others. And in Sefer Chokhmat Moshe I saw that he brought proof for Baer Heitev's words from Rashi in Yoma page 30b, who wrote on the statement of R' Pinchas who said: excrement in its place (meaning, at the anus) — forbidden to recite Shema; and Rashi explained: this one is forbidden to recite Shema — end quote, implying specifically that person. And he elaborated — see there. And in my humble opinion, Magen Avraham's words are according to the conclusion that is established there to R' Pinchas, that it deals when he sits and it appears standing and is not seen; and they challenge: why is this different from excrement on his flesh, where Rav Huna said it is permitted to recite Shema and R' Chiya said forbidden, etc.; and they answer: in its place the foulness is greater (Rashi: that it did not go out to the air and its odor did not spread). And since even for that person, covered excrement on his flesh is permitted as for another, nevertheless excrement at the anus is forbidden because the foulness is greater — if so, for the same reason likewise for another it should be forbidden, for we find no distinction between that person and another. And what Rashi wrote "this one is forbidden to recite Shema" — is according to the presumption that it did not conclude that at the anus is worse than elsewhere; and for this they challenged from excrement on his flesh. And it is a difficulty for Rashi why he needed to challenge from excrement on his flesh — he should have challenged: since it is covered, why is it forbidden?
