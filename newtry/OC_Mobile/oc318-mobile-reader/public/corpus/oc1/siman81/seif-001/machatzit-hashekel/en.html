(Seder katan 1) To the category, etc.—it implies that even if he himself cannot, etc., since the verse gave the sign in another and not in himself if he can eat, etc.

In the Gemara: and for an adult, even though he cannot eat, etc.—and this is because other adults can eat.

It implies that for a minor, etc., it is permitted—for otherwise what difference is there between a minor and an adult, since in all of them we estimate by others of their age; and in the Gemara it implies from its saying "and for an adult," etc., that their laws are not equal.

He can eat; and if it is known that he cannot eat—for example, he is weak, etc.—as should be read.

And in Yoreh De'ah siman 26, and Rama wrote there, and these are his words: one should clean the minor (meaning when he is circumcised) of his feces before he blesses. Shach wrote, and these are his words: and even though we do not distance from a minor's feces until, etc., and as stated in Orach Chayyim siman 81 se'if 1—nevertheless it is better to clean him before the blessing—end quote.

And it seems to me for another reason, etc. Feces in the anal orifice—even if covered—is forbidden; and in Shulchan Aruch, Baer Heitev wrote that it is an error, for feces in the anal orifice is not forbidden except for that person whose feces is in his anal orifice, but not for others. In Chokhmas Adam I saw that Beit Yosef brought proof for Baer Heitev's words from Rashi Yoma 44b, who wrote on the statement of Rabbeinu Peretz, who said feces in its place (meaning in the anal orifice) is forbidden to recite Shema; and Rashi explained, and these are his words: this one is forbidden to recite Shema—end quote; it implies specifically that person, and he elaborated there. And in my humble opinion, Magen Avraham's words accord with the conclusion established there to Rabbeinu Peretz's view, which discusses one who is sitting and it appears standing and is not visible; and it asks from what is different about feces on his flesh, for Rav Huna said it is permitted to recite Shema and Rav Chiya said forbidden, etc.; and it answers: in its place the filth is greater (Rashi explained: it did not go out to the air and its smell did not spread). And since even for that person feces on his flesh when covered is permitted like for another, nevertheless feces in the anal orifice is forbidden because the filth is greater; if so, from what reason should it likewise be forbidden for another, for we do not find a distinction between that person and another? And what Rashi said "this one is forbidden to recite Shema" means per the initial thought that did not conclude that in the anal orifice is worse than another place; and therefore it asked from feces on his flesh. And there is a difficulty for Rashi: why did he need to ask from feces on his flesh—he should have asked: since it is covered, why is it forbidden? And behold, even feces in a potty is permitted to recite, as R' Yehuda said in Berachos 25a, for the Merciful One made feces dependent on covering, as it is written "and you shall cover your excrement"—and it is covered; nevertheless it must be said that the initial thought was possible to explain what Rabbeinu Peretz said, "feces in its place is forbidden," as meaning for that person, for whom covering does not help; and therefore it asked from feces on his flesh, etc., which proves that even for that person covering helps. But per what it concludes, that the filth is greater—from what reason should we distinguish between that person and another, since we do not find anywhere regarding feces a distinction between that person and another, and there is no reason to distinguish.
