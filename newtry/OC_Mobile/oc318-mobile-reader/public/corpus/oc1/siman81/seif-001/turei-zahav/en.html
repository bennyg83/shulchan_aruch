A child who has reached [this stage], etc. In the Gemara it states in this wording: a child who can eat an olive of grain—we distance from his feces, etc. R' Chiya said: and that he can eat it within kedei achilat peras. R' Chiya son of Rav of Yevah: for an adult, even though he cannot eat it within kedei achilat peras—as it is written, "He increases knowledge, he increases pain"—and Rashi explained: grain—all five species are called grain; we distance from his feces from when he ate an olive of these in any manner of eating, for feces of these species putrefies—end quote. Beit Yosef wrote that it seems from his words that if this child has not yet eaten an olive of grain, one does not distance from his feces even if he is several years old; and it also seems from his words at first glance that even if he ate an olive of grain, if many days passed until one cannot say this feces is from that grain he ate, one does not distance from it, etc. But it does not seem so from the words of the poskim; rather their intent is that he has reached the ability to eat an olive of grain within kedei achilat peras—even though he has not yet eaten grain at all—one distances from his feces and urine, for whoever has reached this category, his feces putrefies—end quote. And such are his words here in Shulchan Aruch. One may question: from where does he infer from the wording of the poskim that it is not like Rashi? Yet the words of Tur and Rambam were literally the Gemara's words. One must say his intent is that the poskim are accustomed to explain their wording more than the Talmud's way, whose words are brief; and if so, the poskim should not have written "can eat," which implies "but did not actually eat"—yet certainly the truth is so. But I wondered greatly at what the rabbi understood from Rashi's view—that whoever ate an olive of grain many days ago need not now distance from his feces; and this cannot be said at all. For if so, what the Gemara said "and for an adult even though he cannot eat within kedei achilat peras" would likewise be his law—that if he did not eat grain types for many days he would not need to distance from his feces. Yet an adult is not stricter than a child except in the measure of kedei achilat peras, but not in the matter of eating itself that they said an adult is stricter than a child in this; rather certainly they are truly equal in this. For just as with an adult who has not eaten grain types for many days—as one finds such people growing among the mountains whose main sustenance is milk and non-grain foods—would they not need to distance from their feces? Certainly words of madness would one who says so. So too a child who has reached the category of kedei achilat peras certainly must distance from his feces even though he has not eaten grain types for many days, for they share one law exactly—and no one will deny this. And this itself is also Rashi's intent, who wrote "from when he ate an olive of these," and did not say "if he ate an olive of these"; rather it is clear as the sun that Rashi came only to give a boundary and limit—from when does distancing from the child's feces begin, namely from when we see he ate once of these types within kedei achilat peras; then afterward his feces putrefies and his law is like an adult's, and even if he did not afterward eat grain types he already entered the category of distancing and his bowels putrefy for any food. We find that Rashi's view too is like all the poskim, and Shulchan Aruch ruled well: "whoever has reached the category that another like him," etc. Rashi refers where it is known to us regarding this child from his eating that we saw; and in truth if we did not see from him any eating of grain types for a reason they did not give it to him—but if it is clear by people's sense of the infant's clear nature that he could eat grain at the measure of kedei achilat peras, it is as if we saw he ate so, and we distance from his feces. The rabbi Beit Yosef needlessly made a dispute between Rashi and the poskim here. And more than this I wonder at Machatzit HaShekel who disputed the ruling of Shulchan Aruch and Beit Yosef on the grounds that all poskim hold like Rashi that whoever never ate grain or ate many days ago need not distance from his feces—and this does not seem at all, for an adult proves as I mentioned; and the ruling of Shulchan Aruch is clear and established.

An olive of grain—meaning even in types of cooked dish and porridge, as Rashi wrote: in any manner of eating.

In the time that an adult can [eat a peras], etc. But if this child must pause longer than this, it is like one who eats now half an olive and tomorrow half an olive—for such is the halakhah le-Moshe mi-Sinai that eating does not combine for a pause longer than this—end quote Rashi.
