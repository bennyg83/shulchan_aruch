Child. Taz: behold in Sukkah 42b he distinguishes between child and adult (adult in years — already six and seven and the like; there is no reason to say adult in stature). The explanation must be as Magen Avraham 1: adult in years — even if weak by nature and never tasted a kezayit as an adult — nevertheless we distance from his excrement, for his intestines putrefy all kinds of food since he is grown; one cannot say he cannot eat now and ate a long time — it is plain that many people eat many days and years only light foods and the like, and certainly they must distance from his excrement. And if so, since the gemara distinguished only in this law — adult in years even though he cannot eat — meaning he never tasted grain as an adult and his nature is weak and he cannot inherently — a child who can, meaning likewise can and never tasted; and if so, Shulchan Aruch ruled well regarding a child, and Rashi's view too is thus. And what he wrote "from when he ate" — meaning certainly he already ate once; for otherwise let us say: if he ate and this excrement proves, etc. And this that he wondered on Bach in this — and since this is not so, that too is not so, that we should say specifically that he ate once; Rashi holds "from when he ate" — no need to estimate. And in truth, if it is clear to us anyway that one like him can eat as an adult even though he never ate this — we distance, comparable to an adult who cannot — meaning always weak by nature, comparable to one who can alone even though he never tasted. And Peri Chadash challenged: if so, an adult is obvious — one like him can eat, and a child too likewise.
