A minor. See Turei Zahav. In Sukkah 42b, the Gemara distinguishes between a minor and an older child. “Older” means older in years; already six or seven years old, or the like; there is no reason to interpret it as thirteen years old. The explanation must therefore be as Magen Avraham writes in se'if katan 1: one who is older in years, even if he is naturally weak and has never eaten a kezayit within kedei achilat pras, must nevertheless have people distance themselves from his feces, because once he is older, his intestines cause every kind of food to produce a foul odor. It cannot mean that he is unable to eat now but ate long ago, for it is obvious that many people eat only relishes and similar foods for many days or years, and one must certainly distance oneself from their feces. If so, since the Gemara distinguishes only in this law and says that one older in years is included even though he cannot eat, it means that he never ate grain within kedei achilat pras, is naturally weak, and is unable to do so. Correspondingly, a minor “who is able” means one who is able to eat that amount even if he has never actually eaten it. Shulchan Aruch therefore ruled correctly regarding a minor, and Rashi also holds this way. Rashi's expression, “from when he ate,” might appear to mean that he certainly already ate it once, for otherwise it should have said, “if he ate,” and the character of this feces would demonstrate the matter. That inference is certainly incorrect. This is the point of the challenge to Bach: just as that inference is not valid, neither is the claim that the law applies specifically when he actually ate it once. Rashi used the expression “from when he ate” to teach that no further assessment is needed. In truth, if it is otherwise clear to us that another child like him can eat a kezayit within kedei achilat pras, we distance ourselves from this child's feces even though he himself never ate it. This resembles the case of an older child who cannot eat it; meaning that he has always been naturally weak. So too here, the ability alone is sufficient even if he never tasted it. See Pri Chadash, who challenges that if so the law of an older child is obvious, since another like him can eat, and the same would be true of a minor. Magen Avraham answers that the older child is naturally weak and has never been capable of eating it; otherwise, even regarding a minor in such a case one must distance oneself. Regarding a minor, we require that he be capable of eating it on one occasion. Nevertheless, it remains difficult, because Rashi's statement that feces produced by these kinds of food have a foul odor implies that he must at least once have eaten grain. This requires study.
