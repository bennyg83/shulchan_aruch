Child—Taz: In Sukkah 42b it distinguishes between a child and an adult ("adult" in years—already six, seven, and the like; there is no reason to say "adult" means learned in knowledge); necessarily the explanation is as Magen Avraham 1—that "adult" in years, even if weak by nature and never tasted an olive within kedei achilat peras, nevertheless we distance from his feces, for his bowels putrefy for any food since he is grown—not to say he cannot eat now and ate long ago; obviously many adults eat for many days and years only types of non-grain foods and the like, and certainly they must distance from his feces. And therefore since the Gemara divided only regarding this law of an adult in years even if he cannot eat, it means one who never tasted grain within kedei achilat peras and his nature is weak and he cannot; while "child" means he also can and never tasted. So Shulchan Aruch ruled well regarding a child, and Rashi's view is likewise. And what Rashi wrote "from when he ate" means certainly he ate once already—for otherwise one might say "if he ate and this feces proves it, it is certainly not from that grain"—and this is what puzzled Bach here. And since that is not so, this is also not so—that one should say only if he ate once; rather Rashi takes "from when he ate"—one need not estimate. And in truth, if it is clear to us in any event that another like him can eat within kedei achilat peras even though this one never ate, we distance, similar to an adult who cannot—meaning one who was always weak by nature; similar to such a one who could alone even if he never tasted. Eliyah Rabbah challenged: if so, an adult is obvious—for others like him can eat, and a child too is likewise; and Magen Avraham answered this—that an adult weak by nature and unable to eat means he could never; for otherwise even such a child we would distance, and for a child we require that he could eat once. Nevertheless it is difficult what Rashi wrote that feces of these species putrefies—it implies at least once he ate grain, and it is unresolved. And see Magen Avraham, that from his saying "adult" even if he cannot eat an olive—see there.
