To the stage of one like him. From this language it implies even if he cannot eat — such as if he is weak by nature — nevertheless, since others who are his age in years can eat, he must distance. And it requires study, for we do not find this in any posek. And further, the gemara says: and in an adult, even though he cannot eat a kezayit in the time of eating a peras, we distance from his excrement — implying that in a child, if he cannot eat, it is permitted. Therefore it seems the intent is: if one like him can eat, presumably he too can eat; and if he cannot eat — such as if he is weak — even if he is three or four years old, he need not distance. And in an adult, even though he cannot eat, he must distance. And Bach wrote that in a child one need not distance if we know this excrement does not come from grain — such as if he did not eat grain for several days, as Rashi wrote; and likewise the poskim. And if we know he never ate a kezayit of grain, even if he is many years old, he need not distance — end quote. And Rashi wrote: all five species are called grain and their excrement putrefies. And in Yoreh De'ah siman 265 seif 8 it implies it is good and proper to distance from a child's excrement even an eight-day-old — see there. Rashal wrote: it is not proper to recite Shema next to an infant, for an ordinary infant soils in the garbage heap — end quote.
