To the stage at which another child like him can eat. This wording implies that even if this particular child cannot eat, for example because he is naturally weak, one must nevertheless distance oneself so long as other children of his age can eat. This requires study, for we find no authority who states this. Moreover, the Gemara says that in the case of an adult, even if he cannot eat an olive-sized portion within the time required to eat a peras, one must distance oneself from his excrement. This implies that in the case of a minor who cannot eat that amount, it is permitted. It therefore appears to me that the Mechaber means as follows: If another child like him can eat, we presume that this child can also eat. But if he cannot eat, for example because he is weak, then even if he is three or four years old one need not distance oneself. In the case of an adult, however, one must distance oneself even if he cannot eat. The Bach writes that in the case of a minor one need not distance oneself if it is known that this excrement did not result from grain, for example because the child has not eaten grain for several days, as Rashi writes and as the authorities rule. If it is known that he has never eaten an olive-sized portion of grain, then even if he is several years old one need not distance oneself. Rashi writes that all five species are called grain, and their excrement has a foul odor. Yoreh Deah, siman 265, seif 8, implies that it is proper and upright to distance oneself from the excrement of a minor even at eight days old; see there. The Maharshal writes that it is improper to recite Krias Shema near an infant, since an infant ordinarily handles refuse. It appears to me that there is another reason: Infants do not clean themselves, and excrement at the anal opening is forbidden even when covered, as stated in siman 76, seif 4.
An olive-sized portion of grain. The gloss to Semak writes that the law regarding the food made for an infant called papa requires study. It appears to me that with papa we measure according to the amount of papa that an adult can eat within the time required to eat a peras. For the measure of a peras, see siman 612, seif 4, and for the measure of an olive-sized portion, see siman 486.
