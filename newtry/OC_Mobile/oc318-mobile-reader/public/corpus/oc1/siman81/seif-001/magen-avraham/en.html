To the category of one like him. From this wording it implies that even if he cannot eat—for example, he is weak by nature—nevertheless, since others who are older in years like him can eat, he must distance. It is puzzling, for we do not find this in any posek; and furthermore, for it says in the Gemara: and for an adult, even though he cannot eat an olive within the time to eat a peras, we distance from his feces—it implies that for a minor, if he cannot eat, it is permitted; therefore it seems the meaning is: if another like him can eat, presumably he too can eat; and if he cannot eat—for example, he is weak—even if he is three or four years old, he need not distance; and for an adult, even though he cannot eat, he must distance. Bach wrote that for a minor one need not distance if we know this feces does not come from grain—for example, he has not eaten grain for several days, as Rashi wrote, and likewise the poskim; and if we know he never ate an olive of grain, even a child of several years need not distance—end quote. Rashi wrote: all five species are called grain and their feces putrefies. In Yoreh De'ah siman 265 se'if 8 it implies it is good and proper to distance from a minor's feces even an eight-day-old—see there. Maharshal wrote it is not proper to recite Shema beside an infant, for ordinarily an infant is diapered in refuse—end quote. And it seems to me for another reason, that they do not wipe themselves, and feces in the anal orifice—even if covered—is forbidden, as stated siman 76 se'if 4.

An olive of grain. Semak's gloss wrote: also in food made for an infant that they call "pappa"—it is uncertain; and in my opinion, for pappa we measure according to how much an adult can eat a peras of pappa; and the measure of a peras is discussed in siman 382 se'if 4; and the measure of an olive in siman 486.
