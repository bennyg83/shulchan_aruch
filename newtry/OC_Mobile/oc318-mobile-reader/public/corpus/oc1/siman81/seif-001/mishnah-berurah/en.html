(1) A child who has reached [this stage]—specifically a child; but an adult—even if he cannot eat grain at all—we distance from his feces and urine. And "adult" for this matter means one who is already six, already seven [Peri Megadim].

(2) To the category of "another like him"—that is, even though we did not see this one eat, since another like him can eat, presumably he too can eat; and even if he can only eat through cooking, it is called eating. But if the child is weak and we know he cannot eat an olive within the time to eat a peras, one need not distance from his feces and urine even if he is three or four years old—and this is when from his birth he could not eat an olive within the time to eat a peras. But if he could eat once and afterward became weak and cannot, and even if he has not eaten this grain for many days—for certainly this feces does not come from grain—nevertheless his law is like an adult who must always distance from his feces and urine even if he cannot eat any grain at all. And more than this: even if the child in fact never ate grain at all, nevertheless if by nature he could eat an olive of grain within the time to eat a peras, his bowels putrefy for any food he eats, similar to an adult; and some are lenient for a child if it is known he has not eaten this grain for many days, and all the more if he never ate. But lekhat'chilah one certainly should be stringent and distance even from such a child's feces, for it is a doubt of a Torah prohibition; and bedi'eved if he prayed facing it he need not return, for one may rely on those who are lenient—unless we saw this child himself also ate, for then for all opinions his law is like an adult and even bedi'eved he should return and pray.

(3) Grain—all five species are called grain and their feces putrefies. And for cooked food made for an infant we measure according to how much an adult can eat of that type within the time of a peras. The measure of a peras: some say three eggs and some say four eggs. All this is from the strict law; but it is good and proper to distance from a minor's feces even an eight-day-old. Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Maharshal that it is not proper to recite Shema opposite infants, for ordinarily they are diapered in refuse where there is feces, and their clothes and shoes are soiled.

(4) Adult—this means an average person, for some by nature eat quickly and some shorten their eating. And if this child must pause in eating his olive longer than this measure, he is like one who eats half an olive today and half an olive tomorrow—for such is the halakhah le-Moshe mi-Sinai in general, that eating does not combine for such a long pause.

(5) We distance—for whoever has reached this category, his feces putrefies.

(6) Or from his urine—it appears that since he has reached this measure, even regarding what issues from his organ it is forbidden by Torah like any other adult.
