(1) A minor who has reached. This applies specifically to a minor. Regarding an adult, even if he cannot eat grain at all, one must distance oneself from his excrement and urine. For this purpose, one is considered an adult when he is already six or seven years old, according to the Peri Megadim.
(2) To the stage at which another child. Meaning, even if we have not seen this child eat, since another child like him can eat, we presume that he too can eat. Even if he can eat only when the grain is cooked, this is considered eating. If the child is weak and we know that he cannot eat an olive-sized portion within the time required to eat a peras, one need not distance oneself from his excrement or urine even if he is three or four years old. This applies only if he has never been able to eat an olive-sized portion within that time. If he was once able to eat it and later became weak and can no longer do so, then even if he has not eaten grain for many days and this excrement certainly did not result from grain, his law is like that of an adult, from whose excrement and urine one must always distance oneself even if he cannot eat grain at all. More than this, even if the child has never actually eaten grain, if by his nature he was capable of eating an olive-sized portion of grain within the time required to eat a peras, his intestines cause every food he eats to produce foul-smelling excrement, just as with an adult. Some authorities are lenient regarding a minor when it is known that he has not eaten grain for many days, and all the more so if he has never eaten it. Initially, however, one should certainly be stringent and distance oneself even from the excrement of such a minor, since the matter involves a possible Torah prohibition. After the fact, if one prayed facing it, one need not repeat the prayer, because one may rely on the lenient authorities, unless we saw this child himself eat grain. In that case, all authorities agree that his law is like that of an adult, and even after the fact one must repeat the prayer.
(3) Grain. All five species are called grain, and their excrement has a foul odor. Regarding a cooked food made for an infant, the measure is based on the amount of that food an adult can eat within the time required to eat a peras. Some say a peras is three egg-volumes, and others say four egg-volumes. This is the strict law, but it is proper and upright to distance oneself from the excrement of a minor even at eight days old. The Magen Avraham writes in the name of the Maharshal that it is improper to recite Krias Shema facing infants, since infants ordinarily handle refuse where excrement is found, and their clothing and shoes are soiled.
(4) An adult. 'An adult' here means an average person, since some people naturally eat quickly while others eat slowly. If the minor must take longer than this measure to eat an olive-sized portion, it is as though he ate half an olive-sized portion today and half tomorrow. It is a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai that eating does not combine when separated by such a lengthy interval.
(5) One must distance oneself. For once the child has reached this stage, his excrement has a foul odor.
(6) Or from his urine. It appears that once he has reached this stage, standing opposite the stream of his urine is also forbidden by Torah law, as with any other adult.
