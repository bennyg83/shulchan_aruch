In Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1: One must say that when he puts them on for the mitzvah, etc. It appears that his intent is to explain that when he puts them on without mitzvah intent, there is no prohibition of degrading Shabbos, and therefore no blessing applies upon removing them. If one were obligated to remove tefillin because the verse says, 'You shall observe this statute, etc.,' as the scholars of Eretz Yisrael maintain, it would be appropriate to recite a blessing: Removing them would itself be a mitzvah, fulfilled through that act. Here, however, he is not obligated to remove the tefillin and their removal is no mitzvah. He is merely forbidden to intend that the tefillin on his arm and head serve the mitzvah, lest he appear to demean Shabbos. Plainly, one does not recite a blessing over refraining from performing a mitzvah. Beit Yosef writes that one may not put them on lest he go out into the public domain, while Shaarei Teshuvah says there is independently no prohibition. This is no difficulty, for the poskim dispute whether that reason creates a prohibition; see Magen Avraham, siman 308, se'if katan 11, citing Tosafos in Menachos. In my humble opinion, even if one holds that putting them on for the mitzvah demeans Shabbos, no blessing applies. A blessing is appropriate only when the mitzvah arises from the tefillin themselves. Here the act is required only because otherwise one appears to demean Shabbos. See Beit Yosef, who writes that even under Rambam's view that nighttime is not a time for tefillin, no blessing is recited upon removal because one is not obligated to remove them. The same applies here according to Magen Avraham's statement that when they are not worn for the mitzvah there is no degradation.
