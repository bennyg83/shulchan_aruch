In Magen Avraham siman katan 1—and one must say that when laying them for the sake of a mitzvah, etc. It appears his intent is that he comes to answer: that if one lays not for the sake of a mitzvah there is no prohibition due to belittling, etc.; and therefore a blessing is not relevant—for granted, when one is obligated to remove tefillin because it is written "and you shall observe the statute," etc., per the view of the people of the West, they would properly bless on this, for it is a mitzvah to remove tefillin and one fulfills the mitzvah through this removal—therefore one blesses on it. But if one is not obligated to remove tefillin and there is no mitzvah in it, only that it is forbidden to intend regarding these tefillin resting on his arm and head for the sake of a mitzvah lest one appear belittling—it is obvious one should not bless on this, for it is forbidden to fulfill mitzvos. And what Beit Yosef wrote, that it is forbidden to lay lest one go out to the public domain, and in responsa he wrote there is no prohibition at all—this in any case is difficult, for in truth the poskim dispute this whether it is forbidden for this reason—see siman 308 in Magen Avraham siman katan 11 in the name of Tosafos in Menachos. And indeed it seems to me, even if you say that also when laying for the sake of a mitzvah one belittles—nevertheless a blessing is not relevant here, for specifically when the mitzvah is due to the tefillin themselves; but when there is no mitzvah except because through this one appears belittling on Shabbos—a blessing is not relevant for this. And see Beit Yosef, who wrote that according to Rambam, who holds night is not time for tefillin—nevertheless a blessing is not relevant since one is not obligated to remove them; and likewise here according to what Magen Avraham said, that when not for the sake of a mitzvah there is no belittling, etc.
