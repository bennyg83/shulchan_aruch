Seif 1: One does not recite a blessing, etc. Although Niddah 51b and the end of the sixth chapter of Berachos state that the people of the West would recite a blessing, Rabbeinu Tam explains that they did so specifically when removing the tefillin close to sunset. This follows the opinion that night is not the time for tefillin, so that one who leaves them on violates a prohibition, as stated on Menachos 36b. We, however, rule like the opinion that night is the time for tefillin, as stated there: “The halachah follows this view, but it is not publicly taught.” We also follow Rabbi Akiva, who maintains that the verse “You shall observe this statute” refers to the Pesach offering rather than to tefillin.
Even when he removes them, etc. Meaning, although Shabbos is certainly not the time for tefillin, since we do not derive this from the verse concerning the statute, we do not recite a blessing. The blessing was recited specifically over tefillin because of the verse “You shall observe this statute.” According to our view, which derives the exemption of Shabbos from “It shall be a sign for you,” no blessing is recited. This is what the Yerushalmi in the second chapter of Berachos states: “When he removes them, what does he say? Blessed are You, etc., Who commanded us to observe His statutes.” This follows the opinion that the verse concerning the statute refers to tefillin. According to the opinion that it refers to the Pesach offering, no such blessing is recited. Tosafos on Niddah explain that since we derive the law from “You shall observe,” one is obligated to remove them. See Beit Yosef and Magen Avraham, and see Tosafos in Niddah, Berachos, Menachos there, and Eruvin 96a.
