One should not bless, etc. The Tur began with removal in general and concluded "when removing them even at night"—it appears his words mean: for seemingly one might say that what the people of the West blessed le-shomer chukav was thanksgiving to His blessed Name for fulfillment of the mitzvah they had already fulfilled, like Birkat HaMazon on a meal—and if so we too should bless thus when removing after prayer. Nevertheless he writes it is not so, for the people of the West held night is not time, etc., and their blessing was near sunset—therefore we should not bless even at night.

Regarding the blessing. Meaning: to say le-shomer chukav that the people of the West blessed—because they held night is not time for tefillin and it is written "and you shall observe His statutes"—it is obvious that we do not bless, since as we rule night is time for tefillin; only even when removing on erev Shabbos bein hashmashos, when there is obligation even according to us to remove by force of "and it shall be for you as a sign," and Shabbos and Yom Tov need no sign for they are themselves a sign—one might have thought one blesses on this another blessing; it comes to teach that one does not. And the reason our master wrote: anything from which removal of tefillin is not learned from "and you shall observe" needs no blessing. And Beit Yosef raised difficulty: what do we care from which verse we learn—nevertheless let one bless similar to the verse "and it shall be for you as a sign." And he gave two answers: First, that by force of "and they shall be for you" there is no prohibition here but only no need to lay then—and it is clear that in truth there is prohibition here, for Rosh wrote in Hilchot Ketzuvot in the name of the Barcelonian that it is forbidden to lay tefillin on Shabbos and Yom Tov due to "and you shall observe," and he disagreed and wrote that the reason is from "and they shall be for you as a sign"—from which we learn that regarding the law of prohibition there is no dispute. And further answer: that we bless only what we find in the Talmud—namely le-shomer chukav—and since this was rejected we have no blessing at all. And it is clear: why did the sages of the Talmud not truly enact another blessing? And it appears to me that we give thanks to God only for what He sanctified us with mitzvos He gave us and warned us explicitly regarding—namely "and you shall observe" per the view of the people of the West; but the prohibition according to us is because the sanctity of Shabbos has come upon us and the sanctity of tefillin has departed—and behold in any case we bless on the sanctity of Shabbos, and automatically another sanctity, tefillin, has departed.
