One does not recite a blessing. See Taz. Bach asks why the text says “even at night,” implying that the people of Eretz Yisrael would recite a blessing even when removing them during the day. This is incorrect, for Rabbeinu Tam explains in Berachos 44b and Menachos 35b that they recited the blessing only when removing them at twilight. Taz adds another difficulty. Tur begins, “One does not recite a blessing when removing them,” which implies even during the day. The answer to one difficulty resolves the other. Tur comes to exclude the thought that the people of Eretz Yisrael recited a blessing even during the day as an expression of gratitude for the mitzvos we merited to fulfill, comparable to Birkat HaMazon, which gives thanks for the past. One might then think that according to our view as well, even though the verse concerning the statute refers to the Pesach offering, another blessing should have been instituted, as Beit Yosef asks regarding one who removes tefillin at twilight on Erev Shabbos. This approach does not accept Beit Yosef's answer there, but rather the explanation I wrote in letter 2, that the sign of Shabbos itself warrants a blessing. Here that consideration does not apply. Tur therefore teaches that this is not so. Even the people of Eretz Yisrael recited a blessing only at twilight. Accordingly, for us, even when removing them at night, and certainly during the day, no blessing is recited. Although night is prohibited D'Rabbanan, according to the Torah night is the time for tefillin. The word “even” may also be explained as follows. According to the opinion that night is not the time for tefillin by Torah law, twilight is forbidden because it may already be night, and the people of Eretz Yisrael removed them close to twilight and recited a blessing. Even if one argues that no blessing is recited over a doubt, it was nevertheless close to night. For us, however, tefillin may be worn during twilight because the doubt concerns only a D'Rabbanan prohibition, as will be explained, with Hashem's help, in siman 30, Magen Avraham, seif katan 2. Tur therefore says “even at night”: although night is forbidden D'Rabbanan, no blessing is recited, unlike other D'Rabbanan obligations, because there is no requirement to remove them, etc.
