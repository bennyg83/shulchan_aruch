One should not bless. The Taz—the Bach raised difficulty: what does "even at night" imply—that the people of the West would bless even when removing by day? And this is as Rabbeinu Tam wrote [Berachos 44b, Menachos 35b] that the people of the West would bless only when removing during twilight. And the Taz added to raise difficulty: the Tur began "one does not bless" when removing them—implying by day. And for this one answer resolves the other: the Tur comes to say that it is not that the people of the West would bless even by day—a thanksgiving for mitzvos we fulfilled, like Birkat HaMazon, thanksgiving for the past—and if so for us too, since the verse is pressed to refer to Passover, we should enact another blessing, as Beit Yosef's difficulty when removing on erev Shabbos during twilight; and he did not accept Beit Yosef's answer—see there—but as stated in note 2 of the note on Shabbos one blesses, and here there is no such thing; therefore the Tur said it is not so, but the people of the West themselves would not bless except during twilight, etc. Therefore for us, even when removing at night—not like by day, for they too, even at night—for we rule night is time for tefillin—nevertheless. And apparently the language "even" can be said: as held by the one who says night nevertheless is not time for tefillin, during twilight it is forbidden due to doubt of night, and the people of the West would remove near twilight and bless (and if you say they do not bless on a doubt, nevertheless it was close to night). And according to us it is permitted to wear them during twilight—a rabbinic doubt—as we need to say below, with God's help, in siman 30 in Magen Avraham note 2, who said even at night rabbinically it is forbidden—nevertheless one does not bless, like other rabbinic matters where one need not remove them, etc.
