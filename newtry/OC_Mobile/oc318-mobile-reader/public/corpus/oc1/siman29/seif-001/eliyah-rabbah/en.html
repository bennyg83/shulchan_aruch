[1] [Levush] What the Levush wrote—that from the verse "and they shall be for you as a sign" it implies only that the tefillin themselves shall be a sign; that if we do not have one sign beyond the matter itself so that there be two witnesses—and thus on Shabbos and Yom Tov, which are themselves a sign, tefillin are not needed—but that there should be a prohibition needing three witnesses, we have not heard. And I found in the Rokeach siman 30; this is his language: And you might ask, what is the reason one is obligated on a weekday to wear them—behold, there is the sign of circumcision? And one could say that the sign of circumcision is not called a "sign," for we require a sign of the Exodus from Egypt, and Shabbos and tefillin are a sign of the Exodus from Egypt—until here his language. Thus it is explained that one sign suffices, and the reason appears to be that a sign is preferable to testimony—but the sign of circumcision is not called a sign for this purpose—and this is the question on the Levush. Again I found in Abudraham page 19 like the words of the Levush—see there; it requires study. And behold, Magen Avraham raised a difficulty from siman 31 that there is disrespect; and he answered that when one lays them for the sake of a mitzvah it is considered disrespect, but when not for the sake of a mitzvah there is no prohibition to lay them. And the Taz answered that no blessing is needed, for in any case we bless on the sanctity of Shabbos. And in the Levush siman 31 he also wrote an answer on this—see there.
