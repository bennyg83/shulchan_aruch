[1] Levush writes that the verse “and they shall be for you as a sign” implies only that tefillin are a sign if we do not already possess another sign in addition to circumcision, so that there will be two witnesses. Accordingly, on Shabbos and Yom Tov, which themselves are a sign, tefillin are unnecessary. But we have not heard that three witnesses are forbidden. I found in Rokeach, siman 30: “It is difficult. Why is one obligated during the week to bear tefillin, when he already has the sign of circumcision? One may answer that circumcision is not called a sign in this context, for we require a sign connected to the Exodus from Egypt. Shabbos and tefillin are both signs of the Exodus.” This clearly shows that a single sign suffices. The reason appears to be that a sign is more powerful than ordinary testimony. The sign of circumcision is simply not called the relevant sign here. This is difficult for Levush. I later found in Abudraham, page 19, support for Levush's words. See there; the matter requires study. Magen Avraham asks from siman 31, where wearing tefillin on Shabbos is described as degrading the King's seal. He answers that if one puts them on for the sake of the mitzvah, it is degrading, but if he puts them on not for the sake of the mitzvah, there is no prohibition. Taz answers that no blessing is needed, because one already recites blessings over the sanctity of Shabbos. Levush in siman 31 likewise gives an answer to this; see there.
