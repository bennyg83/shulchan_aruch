(Siman katan 1) Erev Shabbos bein hashmashos. In Beit Yosef the reason, etc.—as we say in the Gemara: In the West, every erev Shabbos when they would remove the tefillin they would bless le-shomer chukav, for they held like the tanna who expounded the verse written in parashat Tefillin, "And you shall observe this statute for its appointed time from days to days"—the word "days" excludes nights, for night is not time for tefillin; and from "days" they expounded "and not all days," to exclude Shabbosos and Yom Tov, during which one may not lay tefillin.

And the amoraim dispute: since it is written in this verse "and you shall observe"—whether it is a positive command or a negative command not to lay tefillin at night and on Shabbos and Yom Tov—therefore when they would remove the tefillin in the evening or close to Shabbos and Yom Tov they would bless le-shomer chukav, in the language of the verse "and you shall observe the statute," etc. But according to us, as we rule like the one who says night is not time for tefillin as explained in the next siman, and this verse "and you shall observe the statute" does not refer to tefillin but only to the Passover offering—therefore even though rabbinically one may not lay tefillin at night as stated in the next siman, nevertheless since by Torah law it is time for tefillin, one does not bless when removing them in the evening, even on erev Shabbos and Yom Tov.

For in this we agree that Shabbos and Yom Tov are not time for tefillin, and not from the verse "and you shall observe," etc., but rather from what is written "and it shall be for you as a sign," etc., to exclude Shabbos and Yom Tov which are themselves a sign—as explained in siman 31; if so, since from the Torah one must remove tefillin on erev Shabbos and Yom Tov, one should bless—

However, that one does not bless le-shomer chukav—since the verse "and you shall observe the statute" does not refer to tefillin according to us—nevertheless one should bless with another formula; as Rabbenu wrote, that by Torah law even on Shabbos and Yom Tov there is no prohibition to wear them—the Torah did not say so, but rather that on Shabbos and Yom Tov they are themselves a sign and tefillin-sign is not needed; but if one wished to wear them on Shabbos and Yom Tov there is no prohibition except rabbinically it is forbidden lest one go out with them, etc.; and from the Torah why does one not bless even when removing on erev Shabbos and Yom Tov—

And also contradicts, etc. Meaning: what is stated in siman 31 contradicts what is stated in siman 308.
