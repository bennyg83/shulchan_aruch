(Seif katan 1) See Be'er HaGolah. Beit Yosef gives the reason, etc. The Gemara states that in Eretz Yisrael, every Erev Shabbos, when they removed their tefillin, they recited the blessing “Who commanded us to observe His statutes.” They followed the Tanna who expounds the verse written in the passage of tefillin, “You shall observe this statute at its appointed time, from days to days.” The word “days” excludes nights, teaching that night is not the time for tefillin. The word “from days,” and not all days, excludes Shabbos and Yom Tov, on which tefillin are not worn.
The Amoraim disagree whether the word “observe” in this verse creates a positive commandment or a prohibition against wearing tefillin at night and on Shabbos and Yom Tov. Therefore, when they removed the tefillin in the evening or close to Shabbos and Yom Tov, they recited a blessing using the wording of the verse, “You shall observe the statute.” We, however, rule like the opinion that night is the time for tefillin, as explained in the next siman. We expound the verse “You shall observe this statute” not as referring to tefillin, but to the Pesach offering. Therefore, although D'Rabbanan one should not wear tefillin at night, as explained in the next siman, since according to the Torah night is the time for tefillin, no blessing is recited when removing them in the evening, and even not on Erev Shabbos or Erev Yom Tov.
In this matter we nevertheless agree that Shabbos and Yom Tov are not times for tefillin, not because of the verse “You shall observe,” but because the verse says, “It shall be for you as a sign,” excluding Shabbos and Yom Tov, which themselves are signs. If so, since according to the Torah one must remove the tefillin before Shabbos and Yom Tov, he should seemingly recite a blessing.
Although one should not recite the formula “Who commanded us to observe His statutes,” since according to our view that verse does not refer to tefillin, he should nevertheless recite another form of blessing. Beit Yosef therefore answers that according to the Torah there is no prohibition against wearing tefillin even on Shabbos and Yom Tov. The Torah says only that on Shabbos and Yom Tov they themselves are signs and there is no need for the sign of tefillin. If a person nevertheless wishes to put them on during Shabbos and Yom Tov, there is no Torah prohibition. It is forbidden only D'Rabbanan, lest he go out wearing them into the public domain. For this reason, no blessing is recited even when removing them on Erev Shabbos or Erev Yom Tov.
