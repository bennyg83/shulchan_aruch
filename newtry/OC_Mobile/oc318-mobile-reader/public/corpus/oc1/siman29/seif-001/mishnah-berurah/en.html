(1) One does not recite a blessing. This excludes the practice mentioned in Niddah 51b, that the people of Eretz Yisrael would recite, after removing tefillin at night, “Who has sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to observe His statutes.” They maintained that night is not the time for tefillin and derived this from the verse, “You shall observe this statute at its appointed time, from days to days”: days, and not nights. We, however, maintain that this verse refers to another matter, as stated in the Gemara, and we rule that night is the time for tefillin. Initially one should not put them on then because the Sages decreed lest one sleep in them and pass wind. Therefore, no blessing is recited upon removing them.
(2) Twilight. Although we rule that Shabbos and Yom Tov are not times for tefillin, and one who deliberately puts them on then for the sake of the mitzvah violates bal tosif, if he puts them on without intention for the mitzvah there is no Torah prohibition. There is only a D'Rabbanan prohibition because of the decree lest he go out wearing them into the public domain. Some dispute even this, as will be explained below in siman 31. Therefore, no blessing is relevant, because removing the tefillin is not itself a mitzvah. He removes them only because of the decree.
