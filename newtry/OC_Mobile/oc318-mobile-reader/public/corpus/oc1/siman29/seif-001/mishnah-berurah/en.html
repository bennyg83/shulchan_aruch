(1) One should not bless—Explanation: to exclude that which we say [Niddah 61a, 71b] that the people of the West would bless "asher kid'shanu b'mitzvosav v'tzivanu le-shomer chukav" after removing tefillin at night—for they held that night is not time for tefillin, and they derived this from the verse "and you shall observe this statute for its appointed time from days to days"—"days" and not nights. But according to us, who hold that this verse comes for another matter as stated in the Gemara, and night—as we rule—is time for tefillin, only one should not lay them ab initio, for they decreed lest one sleep in them and emit an odor—therefore one should not bless.

(2) Bein hashmashos—Explanation: even though as we rule Shabbos and Yom Tov are not time for tefillin, and when one wishes to lay them then for the sake of a mitzvah one transgresses bal tosif—nevertheless, since if one lays them without intention for the sake of a mitzvah there is no Torah prohibition in this but only rabbinically, due to a decree lest one go out with them to the public domain, and some also dispute this, as will be explained below in siman 31—therefore a blessing is not relevant for this, since in removing the tefillin itself there is no mitzvah, and one removes them only due to a decree.
