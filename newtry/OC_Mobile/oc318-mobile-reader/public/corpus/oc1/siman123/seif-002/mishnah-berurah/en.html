(6) He should stand — and he should position his feet as in prayer when saying oseh shalom, because he inclines himself to the side of the Shechinah.
(7) And he should not return — that if he returns immediately it resembles a student taking leave of his master who stepped backward and returns immediately, whose end proves his beginning that he did not step backward in order to take leave from him, and it is disgraceful; but when he waits for Kedushah or at least for the beginning of the prayer leader's prayer, it appears to all that he returns for the sake of Kedushah and should have intention for what the prayer leader will say.
(8) To his place — meaning if he wishes to return to his place he is not permitted until, etc.; but if he wishes he stands there and does not return to his place at all [Kesef Mishnah]; and Magen Avraham wrote that according to what he brought in Beit Yosef, some say we require six steps — that is, three behind him and three in his return to his place — if so necessarily he must return to his place in three steps forward; and for this reason some are strict that a person should not pass before them while they stand in the place where they stepped so as not to interrupt between their six steps; but nevertheless through their strictness they err when they see someone who wishes to pass before them — they hurry to return to their place before the prayer leader begins the prayer, which is from the fundamental law.
(9) For Kedushah — and if he prolonged his prayer and at the time he stepped the prayer leader reached Kedushah, he may return immediately to his place to say Kedushah; and even though Maharil practiced that when he finished Sh'moneh Esrei and the prayer leader fell on his face, then he would remain in the place where he stepped there and would also fall on his face with the congregation equally and would not return immediately to his place — Kedushah is different because it is better to say it in his place like the rest of the congregation. And see below siman 131 seif 2 regarding falling on one's face what is written there in Mishna Berurah.
(10) And at least, etc. — see in Bach who agreed that ideally it is proper to be strict until Kedushah unless the place is narrow and pressing so as not to cause dispute; and so wrote in Derech Chaim; however at a time when they say piyyutim certainly there is room to be lenient and return immediately when the prayer leader begins Sh'moneh Esrei.
(11) And similarly for one praying alone — and Bach disagrees and rules that there is no distinction between one who prays alone at home and one who prays with the congregation; rather in every case one must wait for the measure until the prayer leader reaches Kedushah, or at least until the prayer leader begins aloud; and likewise Magen Avraham — unless he must return and pray again for make-up prayers, for then according to all opinions it is enough [to wait] the time to walk four cubits, for everyone knows his return is to pray again; nevertheless in a case of pressing need there is room to be lenient that even in this [waiting] four cubits is enough.
(12) It is forbidden to turn [one's face] — that is, even though he still stands in the place where his steps were concluded, he should not turn his face toward the congregation — that is, toward the west — for as long as the prayer leader has not finished, because this will cause annulment of intention for the worshippers, and also they will suspect him that he skipped; and regarding if he wants to return to his place, this is already explained that ideally he must wait until the prayer leader reaches Kedushah, or at least until the prayer leader begins.
