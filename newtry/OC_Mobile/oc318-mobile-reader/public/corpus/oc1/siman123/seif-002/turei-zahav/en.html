And he should not return to his place. In the Gemara we say on this that if he did not do so, he is like a dog that returns to its vomit. It appears the explanation is: since he returns immediately, it implies that what he prayed does not please him and he wants to pray again what he already prayed; if so, he disgraces his prayer. And they gave a measure for waiting until the prayer of the prayer leader, so that it appears to everyone that he returns in order to concentrate on what the prayer leader will say; and the prayer leader himself—his measure is the time to walk four cubits. And similarly the individual praying, as Rama wrote afterward. And through this he shows everyone that he is not returning, and there is no disgrace to the prayer he prayed. And it appears to me that even if he does not return immediately to his place, he should not say Tachanun immediately after lifting his foot but should wait the time to walk four cubits:

And similarly one praying alone. Beit Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yeruham wrote on this; and it likewise appears from what we say at the end of Terumat HaDeshen that one should wait between one prayer and another so that one's mind be settled upon it; and the Yerushalmi explains that this means the time to walk four cubits—until here. Explanation: just as there we find that this was the measure of waiting regarding the matter between one prayer and another, likewise this is called the measure of waiting if he returns to the place where he prayed:

It is forbidden to turn one's face, etc. Explanation: even though he still stands in the place where his steps ended, he should not turn his face to see those standing behind him, for he will cause nullification of concentration for those still praying if he looks at them.
