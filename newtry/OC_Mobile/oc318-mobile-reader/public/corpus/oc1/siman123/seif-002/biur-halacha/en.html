(א) “shall stand” — see in Mishna Berurah in what he wrote, “when he says,” etc.; for behold the essential law of this standing: Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Radbaz and Rashba, and it is not found in Rashba; and as he wrote in Eliyah Rabbah; and also in Radbaz I did not find, for he deals there only with Kedushah. Nevertheless it seems one should rule thus, at least regarding “oseh shalom.”

(ב) “and shall not return to his place,” etc. — see in Taz, who wrote that even if he does not return immediately to his place, he should not say Tachanun immediately after uprooting his feet, but should wait the amount of time to walk four cubits. And see in Be'er Heitev siman 95 note 5, who is uncertain about his ruling; and likewise in Peri Megadim siman 131 note 4, in Mishbetzos Zahav he wrote that he does not know from where this [comes]; and from the words of Maharil it does not imply thus — see there.

(ג) “and the chazzan shall stand,” etc. — see in Magen Avraham, who wrote in the name of Rashba that by law he may return immediately, for it is known that his return is to discharge the many from their obligation, but they practiced thus. And see in Peri Megadim. However, from the words of the Gra it implies that by law he must wait the amount of time to walk four cubits, for this is included in what he ruled in siman 105, that between prayer and prayer one must wait the amount of time to walk four cubits — see there. And likewise ruled in the book Magen Gibborim in the name of the Aruch.

(ד) “forbidden,” etc., until the chazzan finishes — this implies that once he has finished it is enough, even if most of the congregation are still standing. And behold, by the first reason that I copied in Mishna Berurah, which is from Taz, it seems one should be stringent in this; and by the second reason it is permitted, and likewise Peri Megadim implies. Practically, one should look into this.
