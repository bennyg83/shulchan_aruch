He should stand. He is obligated to position his feet as in prayer [Rav David HaKochavi, Beit 27 and see there, and in the book Beit Tzvi siman 187].

And he should not return. And when they say piyyutim he returns when the prayer leader begins Sh'moneh Esrei (Levush): Kesef Mishnah wrote that if he wishes he stands there and does not return to his place; but per the view of some authorities the reason is that we require six steps as explained in Beit Yosef, therefore he must also step three steps forward; and for this reason some are strict that a person should not pass before them while they are standing there, so as not to interrupt between the six steps (Levush); see siman 95 seif 1 and what is written siman 124 and siman 131 seif 2.

And the prayer leader should stand. But by law he may return immediately, for with an individual it appears as though he wishes to return and confess, and he is like a dog, etc., and see siman 427 seif 4; but with a prayer leader it is known that he does so in order to lead the many in Sh'moneh Esrei (there in Rashba).

This measure. And Bach ruled as the Radakh said in the name of Ra'avad that he must wait the measure until the prayer leader reaches Kedushah; and what is written in siman 105 (25) "another opinion" is because he must return and pray; but otherwise it is like a dog, etc.; and so is clear from the responsum of Rashba; and also what Rabbenu Yerucham wrote "he should wait a little" — one could say this means this measure, and so appears to one who examines there; and what is written in Beit Yosef and so it appears, etc., are from the words of Beit Yosef.

It is forbidden to turn [back]. So they should not suspect him that he skipped (Levush); and when the prayer leader begins he is permitted to turn his face, but to return to his place is forbidden until he reaches Kedushah as stated above, not like Bach — see there.
