To his place. And he must direct his feet as in prayer. Radbaz, Magen Avraham.

To Kedushah. And when they say piyutim, he turns back when the shaliach tzibbur begins Yishtabach. Levush, Magen Avraham, Knesset HaGedolah, and Maharil z"l—if he had finished Yishtabach immediately when the shaliach tzibbur sits for Tachanun; and similarly in Mussaf, if the shaliach tzibbur opened VeYihi Noam and he immediately finished his steps—for after Yishtabach he would remain in the place where he stepped and cover his face and say Tachanun, or in Mussaf VeYihi Noam before he returned to his place, see there. And from the words of Taz, seif katan 3, it does not seem so, for he wrote: "And similarly the individual praying—even if he does not return immediately to his place, he should not say Tachanun immediately after lifting his foot but should wait the time to walk four cubits," see there. And perhaps there is a distinction between one praying alone or with the congregation; and from the words of Maharil it appears one need not be strict that falling on one's face be seated, and Rivash likewise wrote; and see siman 131, seif 2, as written there. Kesef Mishneh wrote that if he wishes he stands there and does not return to his place. Magen Avraham wrote: in the view of some, the reason we require six steps—for it is written "and their feet were a straight foot," and it is written "and the sole of their feet like the sole of a calf's foot"—behold six. And therefore one must also step three steps before him, meaning another three; see Beit Yosef. And some are strict that a person should not pass before them while they stand there, so as not to interrupt between the six steps. But what people do when they see someone who wants to pass before them—to hurry to return to their place before waiting the specified measure—is an error. Levush and Knesset HaGedolah.

Four cubits. And from the law one may return immediately when the shaliach tzibbur [begins]. Rashba, Magen Avraham (see in Eliyah Rabbah at length on this from Rashba's responsum).

At this measure. And they should not say Tachanun immediately but should wait the time to walk four cubits—Taz; and see seif katan 5 in the name of Maharil, as cited there. Bach ruled there is no distinction for one praying with the congregation, and he must remain for the measure until the shaliach tzibbur reaches Kedushah, and Magen Avraham likewise.
