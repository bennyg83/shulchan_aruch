[5] At least, etc. This means that ideally one should remain until Kedushah. Where space is narrow, conflict may arise, and there is pressure, one may be lenient and return when the chazzan begins, as Bach rules. Lechem Chamudos distinguishes that when piyutim are recited, one may return when the chazzan begins. The Gemara says that one who immediately returns to his place resembles a dog returning to its vomit. See Beit Yosef and Levush for their explanation. Rashba, responsa 436 and 675, and Binyamin Ze’ev 167 explain that because he immediately returns to his place, it appears as though he wishes to confess a second time, similar to the law below in Hilchos Yom Kippur that one who confesses again on a later Yom Kippur resembles a dog returning to its vomit. See Likkutei Maharil.
