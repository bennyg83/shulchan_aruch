With one bow, etc. Rabbi Yeshayah, of blessed memory, explains that with a bow he took one step backward, meaning a step equal to the measure of the bent legs. So writes Shibolei HaLeket there, though this requires study. In Tzeidah LaDerech, folio 44, it appears that this bow must be deep enough for the vertebrae to protrude, like the bows in Avot and Modim explained in siman 113.
And after he has stepped backward, etc. This excludes the practice of those who say Oseh Shalom while taking the steps. Rather, it should be said after the steps. The same law applies to Oseh Shalom in Kaddish and to the chazzan. So writes Terumat HaDeshen, siman 13.
He should bow before Him, etc. He should say 'upon all Israel.' Regarding the fact that even an individual says 'and say Amen,' Mateh Moshe writes that he heard in the name of Rabbi Yehudah HaChasid that this is like saying to an individual, 'Bless the One who sits,' because the words are directed toward the angels who accompany a person each day.
