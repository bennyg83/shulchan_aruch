Three steps. Per Beit Yosef, who wrote many reasons; and I also heard a reason that through the merit of three steps Nevuchadnezzar merited to destroy the Beit HaMikdash, and therefore we step three steps and pray that the Beit HaMikdash be rebuilt; and in the Gemara there are four steps mentioned, and in Rabba Shabbos 4a there are three steps, and likewise in Zohar Parshat Mikeitz page 297; and the simple meaning is that one steps backwards like a servant taking leave of his master.

And after he stepped. Not like those who say "oseh shalom" while still stepping, for it is not proper to do so.

To the side of his left. For that is the right side of the Shechinah.

"Shalom aleinu." Not like those who say "upon us and upon all Israel."
