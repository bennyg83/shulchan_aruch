1) [Seif 1] He bows and steps back three steps, etc. And if he did not do so, it would have been fitting for him not to have prayed. Gemara, Yoma folio 53b; Tur and Beis Yosef; Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 1.
2) There: Three steps, etc. As a reason for these three steps, Mahari Abuhav, of blessed memory, wrote in the name of the Orchos Chaim that they correspond to the three mil that Israel distanced themselves from Mount Sinai at the time of the giving of the Torah. Further, it is found in an aggadah that Moshe Rabbeinu, peace be upon him, entered through darkness, cloud, and thick fog before the Holy One, blessed be He, and when he left, he exited from these three; and therefore we take three steps. Until here. And the Shibolei HaLeket wrote in the name of the Geonim a reason for the three steps: because when a person stands in tefillah he stands in a place of holiness, and the Shechinah is above his head; and once he takes leave of his tefillah, he requires three steps backward in order to leave the place of holiness and stand in a mundane place. And a proof to the matter that it is so: once they step backward three steps, they extend greetings of peace to one another, as if to say, until now we were in a holy place, and we have gone out to a mundane place. End of his language. And in the name of Rabbeinu Hai [Gaon] it is found written that the reason for the three steps is that the tefillos were enacted corresponding to the daily temidim, and when the kohen would ascend the altar with the limbs of the tamid, he would ascend on the right side, circle around, and descend on the left side; and between the ramp and the altar there were three rows of stone, and he would descend them with three steps, heel to toe; and we do as they used to do. End of his language. Beis Yosef; Levush. And the Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, wrote that he heard a reason: that in the merit of three steps [which Nevuchadnetzar took for the honor of Heaven] he merited to destroy the Beis HaMikdash; and therefore we step three steps and pray that the Beis HaMikdash be rebuilt; see there. And in Shaar HaKavanos, at the end of the sixth discourse on the Amidah, he wrote the reason: because during the Amidah prayer he was in the aspect of the world of Atzilus, and now he distances himself from it with three steps, corresponding to the three worlds Beriah, Yetzirah, and Asiyah; see there. And the plain sense of the matter is that he steps backward like a servant who takes leave of his master. Magen Avraham there; and so wrote the Levush there. And see further above, siman 95, ot 7.
3) There: With one bow. And this bow must be until the vertebrae of the spine protrude, like the bows of Avos and Modim explained in siman 113. Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1, in the name of the sefer Tzeidah LaDerech, folio 44; Mishnah Berurah, ot 1.
4) There: And after he has stepped, etc. And not like those who say 'Oseh Shalom' while they are still stepping, for it is not proper to do so. Magen Avraham, s.k. 2; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 2; Soles Belulah, ot 2; Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 135a; Beis Oved, ot 2; Kaf HaChaim [of R' Chaim Palagi], siman 15, ot 40; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 1; Mishnah Berurah, ot 3. And see what the Maamar Mordechai wrote, ot 1, and what the Machazik Berachah wrote upon him, ot 1, and the Yafeh LaLev, ot 1; see there.
5) There: And after he has stepped, etc. And the same law applies to the 'Oseh Shalom' of Kaddish. Terumas HaDeshen, siman 13, and the Beis Yosef cited him at the end of siman 56; Eliyah Rabbah there; Mishnah Berurah there. And see our words on siman 56, ot 36.
6) There: He turns his face to his left side, which is the right of the Holy One, blessed be He; for one who prays sees himself as though the Shechinah is before his face, as it is stated, 'I have set Hashem before me always.' Beis Yosef in the name of Rashi, of blessed memory; Levush; Taz, s.k. 2.
