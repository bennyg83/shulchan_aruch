(13) First — for presumably a person normally lifts his right foot first [when walking], therefore here he lifts the left, as he shows by himself as though it was burdensome for him to leave the place; and see in Biur Halacha. And behold the order of these three steps is: first he steps with the left foot a small step, and then he steps with the right foot a large step, and then he steps with the left so that his feet are equal.
(14) In order that he place [heel beside big toe], etc. — and like the steps of the kohanim at the time of service who would walk heel beside big toe. And Magen Avraham wrote that with less than this measure it is not called a step at all, and one may not be lenient even if the place is narrow and pressing; and there are those who are lenient in a case of pressing need. And specifically if the person standing behind him is not praying; but when he is praying, in every case he may not step within his four cubits, as explained above in siman 102.
(15) And ideally — the wording of Rama is not precise, for he should have written in the language of 'some say', for he disagrees with the Mechaber who holds 'at the least', and it implies coarse steps are preferable.
(16) He should not step, etc. — for prayers were instituted corresponding to the daily offerings and we require similarity to the kohanim in their service; therefore he should not take coarser steps; and further because it appears like running before the king. And so ruled Bach and Dagul MeRevavah.
