First. Even one whose dominant foot is the left must first step with the ordinary left foot. See Magen Avraham and Taz. Some first move the right foot next to the left and then step with the left. Lechem Chamudos. Bach writes that if the synagogue is crowded, one may take steps shorter than heel-to-toe and rely on the Rashba, responsum 381, who holds that these steps have no fixed measure. This is the universal practice because of crowding. Where there is no crowding, however, one must carefully take heel-to-toe steps. Magen Avraham writes that the Rashba only means that one need not take an average step of one amah, but less than heel-to-toe is not considered a step at all. The present practice is that when one finishes the Amidah and another person praying behind him prevents him from taking three steps, he waits until the other finishes and then takes the three steps. Knesses HaGedolah.
