The left foot. I think the reason is because presumably a person normally lifts his right foot first; therefore with the left it appears as though it was burdensome for him to take leave from before the Omnipresent; and see siman 97; and for this reason a left-handed person lifts his left, which is the right of the world, first; and it is also possible to say the reason is that one must step to the right of the Shechinah first, and if so one must step with the left first; and some practice stepping the right foot toward the left and then stepping with the left (Levush): Bach wrote that if there is pressing need in the synagogue one may [step] at least less than the measure of big toe beside heel and rely on Rashba who wrote in responsum siman 381 that there is no measure for these steps, end quote; and I hold Rashba wrote only that one need not take a medium step which is an amah, see there, but less than heel beside big toe is not called a step at all; and in Darkei Moshe he wrote the reason that prayers were instituted corresponding to the tamidim and we require similarity to the kohanim in their service, therefore one should not take coarser steps; and further because it appears like running before the king, etc.; and so is clear in Tur siman 95, as cited there from Yerushalmi.
