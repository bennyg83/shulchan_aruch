When taking the steps backward, one first moves the left foot. This is the view of Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 5 of Hilchos Tefillah, Shibolei HaLeket, and Midrash Shocher Tov, and this is the accepted practice.
Regarding the measure of the steps, it appears that this applies specifically where possible. In a synagogue crowded with many people, where there is insufficient space to take three heel-to-toe steps and remain there, one may rely in a pressing circumstance on the Rashba, who writes in responsum 381 that these steps have no fixed measure. See there. An individual who prayed with the congregation may not turn around until the chazzan has begun his repetition. One should preferably be stringent until the chazzan reaches Kedushah, and this is the practice where possible. Where space is constricted and quarrels may result, he may turn around as soon as the chazzan begins aloud, but not before then.
