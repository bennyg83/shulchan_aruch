When stepping, he lifts the left foot first—thus is the view of Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 5 of Hilchot Tefillah, and likewise in Shibbolei HaLeket and in Midrash Shochar Tov, and so is the custom.

And the measure of the steps, etc. It appears that this is specifically where it is possible; but in the synagogue, where many enter and space is cramped so that one cannot step three times with the thumb alongside the heel and stand there—it is fitting to rely on Rashba in a time of need, who wrote in his responsa, siman 581, to step without measure, see there. And one who prayed with the congregation may not turn his face until the shaliach tzibbur began his prayer; and one should be stringent until the shaliach tzibbur reaches Kedushah, and so is the custom lekhatchilah where possible. But where there is pressure and it will lead to quarreling, he may turn back immediately when the shaliach tzibbur opened in a loud voice—but not before that.
