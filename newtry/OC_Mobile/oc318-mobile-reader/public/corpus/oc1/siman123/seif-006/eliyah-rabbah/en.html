[6] The time needed, etc. This requires extensive study. Beit Yosef quotes the Rashba: “You asked whether a chazzan may return immediately to his place when he wishes to repeat the Amidah aloud. I have not seen any of the great authorities object, although every chazzan does so. On the contrary, it appears necessary. I heard from great Ashkenazic authorities that their chazzanim do so, except that they wait there briefly, the time needed to walk four steps.” On this basis Rama and Levush write that the chazzan should wait. However, in responsum Rashba 436 the wording is: “On the contrary, it appears that they are obligated to do so, for when he prayed privately he was required to take three steps, and if he did not, they said that it is as though he did not pray. I heard from one of the great Ashkenazic authorities that their chazzanim do so, waiting in place the time needed to walk four steps. The distinction between an individual and a chazzan is that it is known that the chazzan returns to discharge others, so he does not resemble a dog returning to its vomit.” This distinction implies that in the Rashba’s view he may return immediately and need not wait the time of four steps. The words “I heard that their chazzanim do so” apparently refer upward, meaning that they return immediately. Beit Yosef therefore added the words “except” and “briefly,” although those words are not in the responsum. It is also difficult to understand why this should be considered brief or why any delay is necessary if the concern of returning like a dog does not apply. Rather, the statement that their chazzanim “do so” refers downward, meaning that what they do is wait the time needed to walk four steps. They therefore hold that a chazzan too must wait like an individual, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of Rambam. Malbushei Yom Tov likewise writes in the name of Derashas Maharash that he should remain until the congregation reaches Atah Kadosh. Nevertheless, that Ashkenazic authority apparently permitted the shorter delay of four steps because of burdening the congregation. According to the Rashba’s distinction, however, the concern does not apply to a chazzan and he may return immediately. Furthermore, the Rashba’s words as printed earlier are themselves unclear: “On the contrary, they are obligated, for when he prayed privately he had to take three steps.” How does this prove that he must return immediately? The responsum must contain an omission or error. I also found this responsum reproduced verbatim in Rashba 675, but the entire passage from “On the contrary” through “there is a distinction” is absent, and there it is clear that he may return immediately. Beit Yosef also writes this in the name of Ohel Moed. It appears that the passage in the first responsum is an interpolation and not the Rashba’s own words. I found the same in responsum Binyamin Ze’ev 164, and I am astonished that the great later authorities did not mention it.
