(6) The amount [of time], etc. — great examination is needed, for so wrote Beit Yosef from Rashba, may his memory be blessed: they asked whether the chazzan may return to his place immediately when he wants to return and pray aloud. The answer: I have not seen any of the gedolim who object to this, although every chazzan does so; on the contrary it seems one must do so. And I heard from the mouth of great Ashkenazi [authorities] that their chazzan does so, but waits there a little — the amount of walking four cubits — until here is his wording. According to this Rama and Levush wrote that the chazzan should wait; but I examined the responsum of Rashba himself siman 376 — Rashba's wording is not thus, but rather: on the contrary it seems they are obligated to do so, for when he prayed for himself he must make three steps, and if he did not, they said he is as though he did not pray. And I heard from one of the great Ashkenazim that their chazzan does so — waits in his place the amount to walk four cubits. And the difference between an individual and a chazzan is that with a chazzan it is known he returns to discharge others from their obligation, so he does not look like a dog, etc., until here. And behold, from the difference he wrote it implies that he thinks one may return immediately and need not wait four cubits. And what he wrote, “I heard that their chazzan does so,” seemingly refers above — that they would do so, returning immediately; and from this Beit Yosef added in his wording the words “but” and “a little.” But you see it is not there thus; also it is difficult — for what would this “little” be, and why is it needed at all, since he is not like a dog returning, etc.? Rather it seems that what he wrote, “their chazzan does so,” refers below — to their waiting; meaning, what do they do thus? They wait the amount to walk, etc. And we would explain that a chazzan too must wait like an individual, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rambam. And likewise Malbushim Yom Tov wrote in the name of a derashah of Maharash that he should stand the amount until “Atah kadosh.” However it seems that one of the great Ashkenazim would be lenient because of trouble to the congregation, that the amount to walk four cubits suffices. But according to Rashba there is a difference — a chazzan is not like a dog returning; if so he may return immediately. And furthermore, the words of Rashba mentioned above are not understood as Mordechai wrote — “on the contrary it seems they are obligated,” etc., “when he prayed,” etc. — what proof is this that one is obligated to return immediately? It needs examination. Perforce there is omission and error in this responsum. And I further found in Rashba's responsum siman 475 they copied this responsum letter for letter and did not write there at all “on the contrary,” etc., until “and there is a difference”; and it is clear in the responsum that one may return immediately; and Beit Yosef wrote likewise in the name of Ohel Moed. And it seems some addition was made in the first responsum and is not Rashba's words. And I found likewise in the responsum of Binyamin Ze'ev siman 564. And I wonder that the great acharonim did not mention this.
