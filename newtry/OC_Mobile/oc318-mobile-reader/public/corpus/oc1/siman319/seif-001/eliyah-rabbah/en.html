[1] [Levush] And the primary form, etc. Meaning that with a sieve and sifter, the primary form of selecting is removing food from refuse. Regarding other forms of selection, see seif 4.
[2] In a tray. Meaning a flat board in the vernacular. A tamchui is a large bowl into which the entire pot is poured, and from there portions are transferred to individual bowls.
[3] [Levush] Or close to it, etc. Beit Yosef in the name of Rabbenu Yerucham implies that the selection need not be immediately before eating, but it suffices that he eats it during the first meal, excluding leaving it for a second meal. Yet Beit Yosef's citation of Mordechai's concluding words supports Levush. See Responsa Rabbi Yom Tov Tzahalon, siman 203. This requires study.
[4] [Levush] And if he left some, etc. This does not appear correct to me, because if at the time of selection the act was permitted and lawful, he should not become liable retroactively (Malbushei Yom Tov). See Beit Yosef.
[5] From the leaves, etc. But moldy leaves, even if edible with difficulty, may not be selected away from good food, as stated at the end of siman 611 (Magen Avraham). There he cites Maggid Mishneh, but this is no proof, because the prohibition may be that he is preparing for a weekday or not selecting for immediate use. See Shiltei Gibborim, chapter Elu Kesharim, who records the practice of sorting them before Shabbos. Malbushei Yom Tov writes that one who is stringent is praiseworthy.
[6] He selects it, etc. But the reverse, selecting what he does not wish to eat immediately, is prohibited because it is like removing refuse from food.
