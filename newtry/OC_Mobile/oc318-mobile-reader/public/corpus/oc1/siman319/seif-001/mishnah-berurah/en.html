Behold, selecting is one of the thirty-nine primary labors of Shabbat, and one is liable for it also to bring a chattat when done unwittingly and death when done intentionally, like the other labors. Because of our many sins, many people stumble in the prohibition of selecting; therefore I will explain it, with God's help, in all its details, and I will preface this siman with a brief introduction: The Torah prohibition of selecting applies only in one of three ways, for then it is the weekday manner of selecting. (a) If one selects refuse from food, even if he selects by hand and intends to eat immediately, he is liable, for this is the weekday manner of selecting, to remove the refuse from the food.
(b) If he selects with a utensil normally used for selecting, such as a sieve or sifter, he is liable even if he selects the food from the refuse and intends to eat immediately, for this is the way one selects with a utensil: sometimes he selects the food from the refuse, such as when the holes of the sifter are fine and the food is coarse, and sometimes the reverse. If he selects with a utensil not normally used for selecting, he is exempt but it is forbidden, as below.
(c) Even if he selects food from refuse by hand, but intends to eat it later, he is liable. It is permitted to select only if one is careful in all three ways: he selects the food from the refuse, the selecting is by hand and not with a utensil, and he intends to eat it immediately. Then it is not the manner of selecting but the manner of eating. These are the principal rules in this siman, and the remaining details we will explain, God willing, in the following siman.
