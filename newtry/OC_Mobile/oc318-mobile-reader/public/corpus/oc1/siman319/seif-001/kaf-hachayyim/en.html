1) [Seif 1] One who selects food, etc. Selecting is one of the thirty-nine primary labors. One who transgresses deliberately after warning is liable to stoning, and one who transgresses inadvertently must bring a fixed sin offering. The author explains here which form of selecting incurs the death penalty, which leaves one exempt although the act is forbidden, and which is permitted initially. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1. Whether the selected item may be eaten that day, the distinction between inadvertent and deliberate performance, and selecting for an ill person were explained above in siman 318, seifim 1 and 2, regarding cooking. The same applies to selecting, since there is no distinction between the labors, as stated there. Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 1, has already challenged Eshel Avraham's distinction between removing refuse from food and selecting between two kinds; see there. See also below, paragraphs 36 and 37.
2) There: With a sieve or sifter, he is liable. This is the usual manner of selecting. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Even when selecting for immediate consumption, one is liable if he uses a sieve or sifter. Beis Yosef; Bach; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1.
3) There: With a winnowing tray or large dish, etc. A winnowing tray is a wooden utensil shaped like a channel, wide at the rear and narrow at the front, made by metalworkers. One who selects legumes with it places them in the wide section and shakes it. Because the legumes are round, they roll and descend through the narrow opening while the refuse remains in the utensil. Rashi, Shabbos 74a. A large dish is a broad bowl into which the entire pot is poured and from which portions are transferred to smaller bowls. Bach; Perishah, paragraph 3; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2. See below, seif 7.
4) There: He is exempt, but it is forbidden. It is not permitted initially because it resembles selecting. He is not liable to bring a sin offering because it is performed unusually, since the principal manner of selecting is with a sieve or sifter. Selecting by hand does not resemble the prohibited labor at all. Rashi there. Rashi's words that selecting by hand does not resemble selecting mean that it is permitted initially, as stated in the Shulchan Aruch.
5) There: He is exempt, but it is forbidden. Even for immediate consumption, selecting with a winnowing tray or large dish leaves one exempt but the act is forbidden. Beis Yosef; Bach. Know that when selecting with such a utensil, even selecting less than the volume of a dried fig is forbidden as half the prescribed measure because of a sieve or sifter. Half the prescribed measure on Shabbos is forbidden by the Torah, not rabbinically, so this is not a decree added to another decree. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1.
6) There: If he selected by hand in order to eat, etc. If he selected by hand to place it in storage, he is liable. Tur. See below, seif 2.
7) There: If he selected by hand in order to eat, etc. It is permitted even if he afterward places it in another utensil. This is obvious, as Beis Yosef writes. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3; Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 3.
8) There: It is permitted for immediate use. This applies specifically when removing food from refuse. Maggid Mishneh; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. Even if the food is the greater quantity and selecting it requires more effort, he must nevertheless specifically remove the food, as stated below in seif 4 in the gloss; see there. If he selected for immediate use but afterward changed his mind and kept it for the late-afternoon meal, it is improper to do so. He does not retroactively become liable to bring a sin offering, because the Torah forbade only purposeful labor. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 2. See below, paragraph 36.
9) There: It is permitted for immediate use. 'Immediate' does not mean literally at once, but during that meal. Taz, paragraph 1, proves this from the Gemara; see there. Although Rema states this in the gloss, Levush Serad writes that his intent is to say that the Shulchan Aruch also agrees with Rema. The Gemara's expression 'selects and eats' means immediately, while 'selects and sets aside' means shortly before the meal. These are his words.
10) There: It is permitted for immediate use. Similarly, one may select by hand to feed an animal immediately. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 5. Specifically selecting by hand is permitted for immediate consumption because this is the manner of eating. This dispensation does not apply to other labors. Magen Avraham, siman 320, paragraph 7; see there.
11) There, gloss: Anything selected for that meal, etc. This means selecting before a meal for the needs of that meal. Beis Yosef writes in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham that when one selects after a meal, anything selected for another meal that same day is considered immediate. When selecting during a meal, however, he may select only for that meal. Similarly, when selecting before a meal, the selected items must be eaten during the first meal. If some remain until after he rises from the meal, it is not considered immediate and he is liable. Beis Yosef then cites Mordechai, who writes that it is permitted only when one begins eating after selecting, while eating an hour later is like selecting for storage and is forbidden. Beis Yosef writes that Rambam also appears to follow Mordechai; see there. This is likewise his ruling here in the Shulchan Aruch, as stated below in paragraph 35. Levush, seif 2, likewise writes that one should select only during a meal or shortly before eating. Magen Avraham, siman 321, paragraph 15; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3; Chayei Adam, klal 16, paragraphs 2 and 3. Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes that whenever one selects shortly before a meal, he may select everything needed throughout that meal, even if the meal continues for some time. If some selected food remains after the meal, this is immaterial because he selected permissibly, provided he did not use a stratagem. These are his words. Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Beshalach, paragraph 1, similarly writes that one may select for a meal beginning in less than an hour, and this remains permitted even if the meal continues for many hours.
12) There, gloss: Even if others are eating with him, it is permitted. One person may select on behalf of everyone. Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there.
13) There, gloss: Even if others are eating with him, etc. This is not exact. It is permitted even if he selects everything for others and does not eat with them. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, paragraph 1, however, writes that this matter remains disputed, because some require specifically that he eat with them; see there. It therefore appears proper to be stringent and eat some of the item that he selects for others.
14) There, gloss: Even if others are eating with him, it is permitted. Although Maran did not include this ruling in the Shulchan Aruch, it appears that all the halachic authorities agree and omitted it because they considered it obvious. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2.
15) There, gloss: Therefore, it is permitted to select vegetables, etc. This applies specifically when removing food from refuse. Even if the leaves in question are not completely inedible and can be eaten with difficulty, one may not remove them as refuse. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 6; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4; Chayei Adam, klal 16, paragraph 4. The practice of cutting away a spoiled portion, however, is certainly like peeling apples or removing fine pin bones from fish, which is permitted shortly before eating. For this reason, some permit tearing away paper stuck to pastries while eating. Meorei Or, section Be'er Sheva, page 89a; Orach Chaim, paragraph 2. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 3, writes the same. See below, paragraph 19.
16) There, gloss: Therefore, it is permitted to select vegetables, etc. One must take care not to begin selecting until after leaving the synagogue so that it is truly close to the meal. He may then select everything needed for that meal. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there.
17) There, gloss: Therefore, it is permitted to select vegetables, etc. See Shiltei Gibborim in the chapter Eilu Kesharim, who practiced selecting them before Shabbos and Yom Tov. One who is stringent will receive a blessing. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5.
