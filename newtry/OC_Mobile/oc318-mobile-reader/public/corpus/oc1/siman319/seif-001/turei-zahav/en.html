To eat immediately, etc. Not specifically immediately, but in that meal — for in the Gemara it is stated: The Rabbis taught — if two species of food were before him, he may select and eat, select and leave; he may not select; and if he selected — liable to a chatas; and there are many resolutions to explain this baraita, and the conclusion — Abbaye said: this is what it means — he may select and eat immediately, select and leave immediately; for his intent that day he may not select; and if he selected — he is considered like one who selects for storage and is liable to a chatas. And incidentally I will mention what was difficult to me: why does Abbaye strain to explain the baraita in a forced manner, and why did he not explain that three clauses are taught — immediately permitted l'chatchila to eat and to leave; for his intent that day exempt but forbidden; and for storage liable to a chatas — as R' Yehuda explained according to his resolution? Granted for Ulla, R' Chiya, and R' Hamnuna we have only two distinctions — therefore we are compelled to explain on two matters, namely permitted l'chatchila and liability to a chatas; unlike here where we have three distinctions — namely immediately, that day, and leaving for tomorrow — why not explain the baraita thus as well? And it appears to me that this permission of immediately is obvious, for otherwise how could anyone eat — he certainly would not take all the food at once into his mouth, but takes one by one according to what his eyes see; what he takes first he takes — if so, it is plainly permitted. And also what it says "select and leave immediately" is difficult — it is obvious, since permitted to eat the selected, likewise permitted to leave the left for eating immediately in that meal; therefore he had to explain that what it says afterward "for his intent that day he may not select" involves liability to a chatas; if so, it would follow that even to leave to eat immediately is forbidden, lest we permit him to leave for that meal and perhaps he will leave for his intent that day and be liable to a chatas; unlike if there were no liability to a chatas but only exempt yet forbidden — there is no reason to err and decree for him in leaving for that meal; therefore Abbaye was compelled to explain the Mishnah on two paths — this appears correct to me:
