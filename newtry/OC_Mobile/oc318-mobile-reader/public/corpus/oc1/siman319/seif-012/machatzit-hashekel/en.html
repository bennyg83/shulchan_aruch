(א) (s.k. 12) And for Rambam, etc. And requires further study, for in cloudy even according to view 1 it is forbidden — meaning if so what is this that Rav Beit Yosef wrote "and for Rambam even in cloths," etc., "but not in cloudy" — it appears Rambam is stringent in this more than view 1, and this at first glance is not so:

(ב) And one must say for view 1, etc., and as Rav Hai Gaon wrote in name of Ran, etc., that taught daf 139b "they strain wine in cloths and in an Egyptian basket"; and Ran challenged since it teaches in cloths it appears but in a filter forbidden; and perforce one must say it deals when there are lees in it and impossible to drink without straining — behold in such a case even in cloths forbidden, for it is full borer; except perforce it deals with clear, if so why particularly cloths, even in a filter also permitted, as Ze'eiri said there one puts clear wine and clear water into the filter on Shabbat; and he answered the Mishnah deals when it is somewhat cloudy and possible to drink it; nevertheless since most people do not drink it without straining — therefore in a filter forbidden, except in cloths where there is change; and Ze'eiri deals with clear that most people drink without straining and therefore permitted even in a filter; and Rav Hai Gaon wrote Rashi's view is also thus; therefore Rashi explained on the Mishnah that taught "they strain wine in cloths" because of the flour, etc., see there. (And from this understand what is written above in s.k. 10 that they are found in error to permit on account of flour even in a filter in name of Rashi.) But Rambam's view is a filter even clear ones forbidden; and what Ze'eiri said one puts clear wine, etc., into the filter — meaning into cloths, and he calls it filter since his intent is to strain and filter in it, thus Rav Hai Gaon; behold according to him in cloths they require truly clear ones, but somewhat cloudy forbidden, for such is Ze'eiri's language, and also from the Torah to distinguish between somewhat cloudy and fully cloudy; since Ran did not write to distinguish thus except from the necessity of his difficulty from the Mishnah and Ze'eiri that contradict each other — and for Rambam requires further study:
