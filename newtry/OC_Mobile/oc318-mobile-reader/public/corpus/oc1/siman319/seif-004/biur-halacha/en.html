One who selects waste, etc. This is the Rambam's language, meaning even for immediate use, for if it is for later, even selecting food from waste is liable, as stated in Beit Yosef. One may doubt whether the term borer applies only when one first selects and prepares in order to eat immediately, but if during the act of eating itself, while holding the food and intending to eat, one removes the waste and throws it away, perhaps this is not borer even though it is done before eating, since this is the manner of eating. Or perhaps there is no distinction, and he must throw it away only after eating, or throw some food with it. I found in Birkei Yosef that Mahari Abulafia and Maharit Tzahalon dispute this: Mahari Abulafia is lenient, while Maharit Tzahalon forbids, and for this reason forbids removing a fly from a cup unless one pours out some liquid with it. Birkei Yosef writes that from the silence of the poskim, who did not distinguish, it appears that selecting waste from food is liable even in this way. On examining the books I found that the Rishonim disagree about this reasoning. Ramban's novellae imply like Mahari Abulafia, for in his notes to Shabbat 74 he writes that this is not the manner of selecting, but like one eating who finds waste in the piece he is eating and separates the waste from the food, which is permitted. Ritva copies this language. However, from the responsum of the Rosh, quoted below in seif 16, it appears that he holds like Maharit Tzahalon, for he permits filtering water containing worms into one's mouth through a cloth only because holding back the waste at the moment of drinking so it not enter his mouth is not like a melakhah; he did not simply say that borer does not apply during drinking itself. Thus he evidently does not distinguish.
From food, etc. So wrote the Ran in Klal Gadol in the name of Rabbenu Chananel, and the Maggid Mishneh writes that the Ramban agreed and that this appears from the Halakhot; I do not know his source, for perhaps the Rif cited lupines only where one is liable because it is for later, while selecting food from waste is permitted even for later. Tosafot appear to explain that way. The Semag, Semak, Rosh, Ritva, and Meiri also write this, as cited in Beit Yosef. Ma'amar Mordekhai writes that from here it is clear that at large meals one must be careful not to tear a fish from its back and remove the central spine on Shabbat, for that is selecting waste from food, for which one is liable even for immediate use; rather, he should leave the spine in place. Likewise one should not throw bones out from meat except in the manner of eating: a bone with some meat on it should be held, eaten from, and then thrown from his hand; a bone with no meat should not be touched, but the meat should be eaten and the bones left in the bowl until afterward. The world is not careful in this, even before eating when people prepare food for the table and remove bones from meat, which would involve a chatat liability. I searched for merit for them: if the bones are soft and fit for eating, and it is common that after separating them one eats them, they are certainly not waste. Even if they are hard, since the bones and meat are attached, this resembles the Magen Avraham's citation in siman 510 from Yam Shel Shlomo regarding nuts and peanuts that were broken but remain in their shells: for borer they are one type, and preparing the food from its covering is merely food preparation, not melakhah. Although the Magen Avraham challenged this from the Yerushalmi that peeling garlic and onions is liable as borer, his challenge was only against permitting even for later. For immediate eating, the Magen Avraham agrees that it is not borer, as the Beit Yosef himself states regarding peeling garlic and onions. Thus here too, when intending immediate eating, it is merely preparing food, since the bones are still attached and not separate waste. One should not object to those who are lenient during eating itself, and even before eating for immediate use there are grounds to rely on the Rama and Maharshal, together with some Rishonim cited by Birkei Yosef who permit even waste from food for immediate use. Nevertheless, preparing this for later, as is done at large meals, must be avoided very carefully, like peeling garlic and onions for later. All this concerns bones with meat on them. Bones with no meat at all must not be selected out and thrown from the bowl before eating, for that is waste from food; rather, take the meat and leave the bones, as Ma'amar Mordekhai wrote. If it is hard to be exact, one should suck each bone a little before throwing it away, and it will no longer be called waste.
Liable. See Mishnah Berurah: this applies even for immediate use, for for later even food from waste is liable, as the poskim write. Many people prepare a Shabbat morning dish, made from animal feet, to leave for seudah shelishit, and some thereby stumble in several prohibitions. I must therefore explain them so they know how to act permissibly.
1. Their practice is to peel garlic and onions, cut them very finely, and place them into it. This involves two prohibitions. First, peeling for later is liable as borer, as stated in the Yerushalmi, cited in Beit Yosef at the end of siman 321 and in Rama's gloss there; therefore one who wants to add garlic or onions for later should not peel them. Second, cutting them very finely involves tochen, grinding, as below in siman 321:12; for later, all agree one is liable for tochen. Many are stringent even for immediate use, as explained there in Mishnah Berurah, so even those preparing the food to eat immediately must be careful not to cut onions very fine. 2. If the gravy is hot at the level of yad soledet bo when the onions and garlic are put in, there is also bishul if it is a kli rishon, and even in a kli sheni some are stringent, especially if the hand would be scalded by it; one must be very careful, as written above in siman 318:5, Mishnah Berurah s.k. 48. This is very common among those who eat this food hot in the morning and mix in chametz and dry bread crumbs; they must be careful even in a kli sheni when the gravy is hot enough to scald, and it is proper to be careful even at yad soledet bo not to put anything into it then. 3. They select the bones from the meat and throw them away. This must be considered borer according to all views, since it is for later, unless one sucks each bone a little in his mouth before discarding it, in which case it is no longer borer. Perhaps a bone containing marrow is considered one kind with the meat and is not subject to borer, but this is not reasonable, for it is no better than roasted and cooked meat, which we wrote above are considered two kinds. Therefore the proper practice is as we wrote.
