(א) If two species were before him, etc. — Behold Peri Megadim is uncertain: if he selected one species from the other and his intent is to leave both for later — whether selecting applies here, that one is food and one refuse — see there; and in my humble opinion it appears plain from Rambam's language that the selecting when he selects one species from the other and thereby each species is separate — this is the essence of the melacha; but if his intent is to eat immediately and it is in his hand — it is the manner of eating; if so, a fortiori; and where he left one species in place, selecting applies — all the more when he took each species and selected for himself — liable. And behold Peri Megadim in Mishbetzot Zahav s.k. 12 on that which is stated there that permitted to strain the yolk from the albumen through a strainer [which is like nafah and sieve] even though it is called two species; and the reason because he does not strain the yolk in order to eat but to beautify the appearance of mustard — and from this he proved there that likewise permitted to select two species of food one from the other even through nafah and sieve where he does not wish to eat them now; and in my humble opinion this is not comparable at all, for there both are not standing for eating ever — the albumen was mixed in the refuse of mustard and he does not wish to eat it, and the yolk descends to the bottom for color and not for eating; therefore selecting does not apply there, for he does not repair them through his selecting for eating ever; but when he selects two species of food each from the other in order to eat each species by itself later — behold he properly repairs both through his selecting, and it is full selecting — as we wrote:

(ב) Two species of food — This seif is superfluous, for he already wrote this law in seif 1; he mentioned it only because of the end — if he selected and left for later — this law was not yet mentioned, for in seif 2 it deals only with food and refuse and not two species of food:

(ג) And leaves the second in order, etc. — See Mishna Berurah; and behold the Mechaber's language is copied from Rambam chapter 8 of the laws of Shabbat halacha 13 — "and leaves for others or for himself"; and the Rav added the word "the second" in order and changed the intent of the Mechaber and Rambam z"l to inform us that that which he does not wish now is like refuse and he must select only what he wishes to eat — as Terumat HaDeshen wrote [Peri Megadim]; and if Rambam's view is also like Rama — not clear, for there are great ones who do not hold thus — see in Sefer Ma'amad that he brought in the name of R' David Aramah in his commentary on Rambam who wrote that in two species of food, even if he did not eat what he selected but left what was in his hand for his intent that day or the next day and ate the remaining species — permitted, for we forbid this only in selecting absolute refuse from food; unlike here where there are two species of food, and whatever he selects in order to eat immediately is permitted, end of Ma'amad; and so too in Sefer Megillat Sefer according to Rambam; nevertheless in practice one should not depart from Terumat HaDeshen and Rama, whose source is Rosh's words [and Tosafos' words in the passage "if two species," etc., that he selects what he is not desirous of — one may be uncertain whether their intent is immediately or even for later, and they disagree with Rambam, Semag, and other poskim who hold refuse from food — immediately liable] — and this touches a d'oraisa prohibition:

(ד) To eat immediately — Behold in Sefer Yeshuot Yaakov he challenged the essence of the melacha of selecting: even in selecting refuse from food, why is he liable — behold it is melacha she'ein tzricha l'gufah, for he does not need the refuse at all but selected it in order to remove harm from himself, and behold it is like one who finds a corpse to bury; and he answered that the matter of melacha of selecting is that the refuse is not fit for eating and also the food is not so fit for eating with the refuse inside — therefore he separates refuse from food; if so, the melacha is not called upon selecting the refuse but upon repairing the food that it be fit for eating — and this is melacha needed for the body of the food, for he makes it complete food; therefore specifically refuse from food that is not fit for eating at all from the outset and he makes it food through this selecting; but in two species of food, when he separates the second food because he does not wish to eat it — considered melacha she'ein tzricha l'gufah, since this food he wishes to eat now is fit for eating even if the second food was not separated, and his separation is only because now his appetite is not for it — melacha she'ein tzricha l'gufah, end of his words; if so, according to his words Rambam's words cited in Shulchan Aruch seif 1 would be according to his view that melacha she'ein tzricha l'gufah — liable; but for us who rule melacha she'ein tzricha l'gufah — exempt — he would not be liable; therefore in selecting what he is not desirous to eat, even in a vessel — only if he selects what he is desirous to eat immediately, in a vessel or by hand, and for later — difficult; for according to his words, why did Rosh [and other poskim] copy that they hold melacha she'ein tzricha l'gufah exempt this law that what he is not desirous to eat is called refuse; and one cannot say he copied this to be lenient, as is proven from his words there; therefore one must say they hold that also in two species of food mixed such that each species is mixed with the other and the species is repaired through selecting its fellow from it — therefore called melacha needed for the body only when the species are mixed together each with the other and the species is repaired through selecting its fellow from it; unlike when one species is placed in a bowl on a species below and he removes the upper species to reach the species below — no repair applies that it be called melacha needed for the body; and more it appears that even a rabbinic prohibition does not apply here; and even for one who says melacha she'ein tzricha l'gufah is liable — also permitted here, as Rambam's and Shulchan Aruch's language implies: "if two species were before him, etc., mixed" — implies; but if they are not mixed, selecting does not apply in removing one species from another; and even if you wish to strain and say that when not arranged each species separately it is included in mixed and selecting applies — nevertheless it appears one should not be stringent except when he removes from above and his intent in selecting is to leave it for later, for then if it is included in selecting we can say it is called melacha and also melacha needed for the body, since in removing it from the bowl for later it makes no difference whether he prepares it for this day or another day; but if he does not think about it at all and only wishes to remove it to reach the species below — this is not included in selecting at all, and comparable to what Shulchan Aruch ruled above in siman 316 seif 7 regarding trapping a snake — if he is preoccupied such that he does not intend to trap it, permitted; and this even for Rambam who holds melacha she'ein tzricha l'gufah is liable, as the commentators wrote the reason: since he does not want trapping per se but only to separate it from himself — not included in melacha d'oraisa; and the same in our matter — he only wishes to remove it to reach the species below; and regarding refuse from food it is different, for there he repairs the food through his selecting, unlike here where he does not repair the lower species thereby and only due to the essence of selecting that he selects each species from the other — this does not apply here, as we wrote proof from trapping a snake; and even though there are several poskim who disagree there with Rambam and hold the essence of the reason is melacha she'ein tzricha l'gufah exempt — likewise we can say here, for at minimum melacha she'ein tzricha l'gufah, since he does not repair the lower species thereby and also does not want the selecting of the upper species per se but only to remove it and reach the species below; and even though there is at least rabbinic prohibition and only because in a place of damage they did not decree they permitted — nevertheless it appears in our matter one should not be stringent, for Terumat HaDeshen himself initially tends to be lenient even when mixed well and he wishes to select in order to leave for later, and because selecting does not apply where each species is recognizable separately he was not stringent at the end except due to concern of liability, see there; and since we explained in our matter that there is no liability in any case — it appears one should not be stringent in the matter:

(ה) That he selected in the morning — See Mishna Berurah; so too in Sefer Tosafot Shabbat and Sefer Ma'aseh Rokeach and other Acharonim; and there are Acharonim including Magen Avraham who copied that regarding two species of food the law changed from selecting food from refuse, and "for his intent that day" is not said but from meal to meal — for after he ate the meal and prepares for the other meal, even though it is still ample time for it — nevertheless included in immediately.
