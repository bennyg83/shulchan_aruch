[7] But whenever it is, etc. Terumat HaDeshen and Rama write this. Taz disagrees and holds that even within one species there can be selecting, and one should not select large pieces from small ones except for what he wishes to eat now. Alternatively, he may take pieces indiscriminately as they come to hand for a later meal, but not in a selective manner. Maharshal, Beitzah 1:42, likewise implies this. This requires study because Maharshal there writes regarding broken nuts that it is permitted since they are one species, as Magen Avraham cites in siman 510, seif katan 4. It is strained to reconcile. Nevertheless, I find proof in the Yerushalmi, Beitzah chapter 1 and chapter Kelal Gadol, which states according to Chizkiyah that even pomegranates from among pomegranates can involve selection. See Taz, seif katan 13. His proof is not compelling, as Magen Avraham explains. The primary rule is that even within one species selection can be prohibited, as Shulchan Aruch, Bach, and Maharshal at the end of Beitzah chapter 1 rule, and the present passage likewise implies.
[8] [Levush] And when the refuse, etc. Bach infers from Shulchan Aruch's general statement that one may select food from refuse that this applies specifically when there is much food, but where the food is little one should be stringent like Rosh and not move the mixture at all. In my humble opinion this is incorrect. On the contrary, Shulchan Aruch's unqualified wording implies permission even when the food is little. Proof is that he copied Rambam's wording, from which Binyamin Ze'ev inferred leniency. Rama also writes there, "even when the food is more abundant, one should not select the refuse," implying that food may be selected regardless. I am also surprised because Shulchan Aruch in siman 510, seif 2, explicitly permits selecting the food. One cannot distinguish Shabbos from Yom Tov on grounds of muktzeh, for although the rules of selecting as melachah differ, regarding muktzeh Yom Tov is in fact stricter than Shabbos, as stated in siman 495. The primary source is Beitzah 14, and the authorities derive the present rule from there. Bach himself in siman 510 implies Shabbos and Yom Tov are equal here. The primary ruling is lenient, for Rabbenu Yerucham, Avodat HaKodesh, Shiltei Gibborim in the name of Riaz on Beitzah chapter 1, and Maharshal there all agree. The main stringent basis is Rashi's interpretation, yet Ran's treatment of Rashi there indicates he does not accept it. It is permitted to remove wasps, mosquitoes, gnats, dust, or other refuse from wine because the melachah of selecting does not apply to liquid in this manner (Responsa Rabbi Yom Tov Tzahalon there).
[9] [Levush] And according to Tur, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov writes that Levush made a serious error here, for Tur does not disagree with Rambam regarding lupines. The Gemara explicitly rules that lupines themselves are considered refuse, and therefore even Rosh and Tur prohibit selecting them. Tur permits our kind only because they do not resemble the lupines of the Gemara and are not considered refuse. Yet even Tur's position remains difficult from the Gemara's lupines. From these words it is apparent that he did not see Rabbenu Yerucham, p. 76, who writes that Rif cites the lupine law and that he wonders why they are worse than the nuts discussed earlier, though our beans are certainly permitted because they do not spoil if their peel is not removed. Levush may mean that everyone agrees the Gemara's lupines are prohibited, and the dispute concerns ours. According to Rosh and Tur, whose reason is that the Gemara's lupines spoil inside their shells, our lupines are permitted, as Rabbenu Yerucham writes, because ours do not spoil. According to Rambam, however, whose reason is that the refuse sweetens them, perhaps the same concern applies to ours. This requires study.
