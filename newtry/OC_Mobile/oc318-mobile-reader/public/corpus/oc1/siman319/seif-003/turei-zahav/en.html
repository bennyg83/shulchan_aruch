Large from small. For in Terumat HaDeshen siman 57 he wrote that the prohibition of selecting applies only in two species — for in the Gemara it states "two species were before him," and specifically two species; for wherever we can say "specifically" we do not say "not specifically"; and it is astonishing in my humble opinion, for if it appears for a leniency that it mentions two species — in one species it is obvious that selecting applies in what he wishes to leave — but even two species that are already recognizable and each distinct from the other, where mixing does not apply — nevertheless he may not select; and further, in food mixed with refuse it is one species, and the same if there is no refuse but he wishes to leave some of it — that remainder is called refuse, as Tur wrote nearby regarding two species, and the same law here; and so it appears from R' Chiya's words that Tosafos bring in the chapter Kol HaGadol — R' Chiya explained that in food from food selecting applies, that he selects what he is not desirous to eat from what he wishes to eat, for that which he is not desirous of is considered refuse — it implies even in one species it is so, that what he does not wish to eat now is called refuse; and he selects it to leave for another meal — forbidden. And further, it is astonishing that Rama copied that large from small is not called selecting — for in Terumat HaDeshen it implies that in one species there is no selecting at all, even when equal, but in two species he wrote that one takes from both species to leave them for another meal — in this it is like one species, etc.; and we already rejected this, for for a leniency it mentions two species — all the more so one species; and so Tur's words imply, who began "one who selects is liable," etc., and afterward wrote "therefore if food with their refuse were before him," etc., and afterward wrote "if two species of food were before him," etc. — it implies he speaks first of one species; and Terumat HaDeshen wrote there that one of the great ones was careful to select only what he wishes to eat now, even in one species, and he disagrees with him; and in Maharil he wrote in the laws of Pesach regarding matzah on Pesach Yom Tov — permitted, for we rule there is no grinding after grinding; but he should be careful not to select large crumbs from small, for there is a prohibition of selecting in foods, end of his words; and Rashal in the first chapter of Beitza in his book siman 42 brings him, only he wondered on him — behold selecting is permitted on Yom Tov, only l'chatchila one should minimize exertion, end of his words — it implies that on Shabbat Rashal is stringent that there is selecting here; and since there is concern of a d'oraisa prohibition, one should be stringent even in one species and not select even in one species, and even large from small — only what he wishes to eat now, or that he take indiscriminately what comes to his hand to leave for another meal — not in the manner of selecting, as appears to me:
