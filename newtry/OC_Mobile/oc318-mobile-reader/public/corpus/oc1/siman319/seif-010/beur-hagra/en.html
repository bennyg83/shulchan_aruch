(ב) "But," etc. According to his view, and as stated in siman 302 and siman 334 that even if there is no soiling on it there is a concern of laundering, and Magen Avraham's words appear primary — that his reason is because of squeezing, and what is stated because of libun — it does not appear so, for this is not libun as Tosafos wrote in Shabbos 111a s.v. "this," and in Kesubos 6a s.v. "this." And these in chapter Tolin, etc., that specifically wine in a sudar is permitted because there is no concern of squeezing, and this is the view of the Tur; nevertheless one can say that the Tur mentioned libun because even a chatas obligation applies, unlike because of squeezing which is only a rabbinic decree; but the primary reason is as Tosafos wrote above in end of chapter 14 of Shabbos and first chapter of Kesubos and Mordechai end of parsha because of lest he squeeze, and as stated regarding one who walks and encounters a pool of water, etc., and regarding an aluntit, and regarding those women who do not change a spread sudar, etc.; but because of libun they already wrote there that in a thing in which there is no soiling, or that is a manner of soiling, there is no concern of libun, and as stated above siman 302, and he wrote there "and that which they did not decree in that of one who walks to greet his rabbi," etc., and a niddah who acts deceptively and immerses — because in a place of mitzvah they do not decree lest he squeeze, and likewise in Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniot and Shulchan Aruch; and it must be that it is not like Rav Yosef in chapter 2 of Beitza who decreed regarding immersing vessels lest he squeeze, and likewise he challenges Rav Yosef there from that of a niddah who acts deceptively, etc., and they answer there with another change — rather like Rabba or Rava there:

(ג) "And wine," etc. per the view of Rabbeinu Tam that there are two types of squeezing — in water because of laundering, and in wine and other liquids because of m'farek; and where it goes to waste there is nothing to decree regarding wine, for m'farek applies only when he needs to drink; and Peri Megadim in that which is not desirable to him s.v. "exempt," as stated 103a, and all the more so that it is not because of libun; and one can say even for those who dispute Rabbeinu Tam and the Aruch and forbid Peri Megadim that is not desirable to him — nevertheless one can say that we do not decree, since it is only shvus; and see in Tosafos in Kesubos there the view of Rabbeinu Tam and R' Yehuda who dispute regarding other liquids whether one who squeezes is liable because of m'farek or because of laundering; and even if you would say because of laundering, Peri Megadim that is not desirable to us — if it is permitted or forbidden according to his view; nevertheless even for R' Yehuda we do not decree lest he squeeze in other liquids, even though squeezing is forbidden for the sake of a mitzvah; even in water it is permitted as stated above; but another version that wrote that when he intends there is no decree lest he squeeze — this does not appear to me, for if so, what does it say "let it rest," etc., and in many places; and also regarding the matter of libun they dispute as above, and see in end of chapter 14 of Shabbos and as stated in siman 302:

(ד) "And if they are," and for Rambam in meshameret. From that it says in the mishnah "in sudarim," and therefore clear, as stated in the gemara; and nevertheless specifically in sudarim, and this is what is stated "and even in sudarim," etc.; and he holds that the meshameret they spoke of in the gemara is not meshameret; rather one spreads, and this is what is stated "and wine," etc., into the sudar; and their view — the Tur holds that therefore it is taught in the mishnah "in sudarim" for a leniency, for in it water is forbidden, and the meshameret of the gemara is meshameret strictly speaking; and for Rambam sudarim is for a stringency, for meshameret is forbidden in all cases, and the meshameret of the gemara is not meshameret; and the wine of the mishnah is also not meshameret, and it follows his view that he holds that all squeezing is because of libun; and if so, in wine too libun applies; and Ran brought proof from that which we say in chapter Lulav "the stolen," and chapter "one who steals for eating and not for laundering"; and it must be as Semag wrote — since he does not intend laundering, his rinsing is his laundering; and Magen Avraham in the name of Maggid Mishneh in siman 318 s.k. 36; and the Tur follows his view that he holds that in wine there is no concern of laundering, and he holds that even without intent it is laundering, and as Tosafos wrote and as above.
