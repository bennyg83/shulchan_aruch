[15] Because they are clear, etc. Meaning that even when there are some dregs, if most people would drink the wine in its present somewhat cloudy state and only a minority are particular, it is called clear and may be strained (Beit Yosef in the name of Ran). Bach holds that "clear" means completely clear and that straining is only to remove a few splinters or bits of straw or to improve clarity further. He disagrees with Ran. The later authorities cite only Ran's view. I see some proof from Shabbos 109 for Bach, where the Gemara says, "since it is drunk this way," implying that everyone drinks it thus, just as it says, "since these onions are eaten without vegetables." Rambam, moreover, prohibits entirely, as will be explained.
[16] [Levush] Made for that purpose, etc. This requires study. Since we say that soaking a garment constitutes laundering, why should it matter whether the cloth was made for filtering or not? Semag, etc. Magen Avraham answers that Tur holds soaking is not laundering where there is no dirt. This is incorrect, because in siman 302 Tur clearly follows the stringent view in seif 9 there, as also appears in the Remezim at the beginning of chapter 12. Derishah likewise writes that Tur holds so, and Semag and Sefer HaTerumah provide no proof otherwise, because they may follow the first view there. Taz asks from Beit Yosef in the name of Ran at the end of siman 326 that wine whitens fabric, so why is filtering wine through a cloth permitted? This is surprising, because the Mishnah explicitly permits filtering wine, and Ran himself at the end of chapter Shemonah Sheratzim implies that this is not the normal manner of laundering.
[17] If they are cloudy, etc. Meaning completely cloudy. But if they can be drunk even though most people are particular, it is permitted according to Ran and the later authorities. According to Bach, it appears that whenever most people are particular it is prohibited even through a cloth.
