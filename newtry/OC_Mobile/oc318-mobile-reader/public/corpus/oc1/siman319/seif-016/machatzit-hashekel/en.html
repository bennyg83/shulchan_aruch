(א) (s.k. 16) Remains, etc. But, etc., to eat, etc., forbidden. They were precise from Tur's language that he does not put the yolk into it except for appearance — it appears for eating it is forbidden:

(ב) It is called refuse, and since he does not want to eat the albumen and the albumen remains above — he is separating refuse from food, for the albumen that he does not want to eat is called refuse; and in such a case forbidden; but since he does not put the yolk into the mustard except for appearance and his intent is not to eat the yolk — if so albumen and yolk are equal since he does not want to eat either, and there is no borer concern:

(ג) But, etc., it appears this is called one type — Magen Avraham s.k. 5 brought in name of Maggid Mishneh who challenged what is written regarding separating withered leaves that are not food except through exertion — if he separated them from the vegetable there is only rabbinic concern; and churning and straining that separate curd from cheese or butter, or straining that separates wine from lees — is Torah prohibition, and behold also curd and lees are food through exertion; and what is the difficulty? Granted leaves with vegetable are one type, but curd with cheese or lees with wine are two types; perforce he holds that also curd with cheese and lees with wine are one type since attached together from the start; if so the same albumen with yolk — why are they called one type and there is no separating concern? And he wrote on this in Sefer Tosafos Shabbos thus: I do not understand his words, for behold he wrote there in name of Maggid Mishneh that the reason churning and straining are forbidden even though one type, since they are moist things; if so for the same reason it should be forbidden here regarding albumen and yolk; and possible, see there; and in my opinion requires further study, for I already wrote above s.k. 5 the reasoning to distinguish between moist and dry things, that moist things blend with each other and he holds it is full separating since they blend; unlike dry things that even if mixed, since they do not blend — separating does not apply, thus; if so in albumen and yolk too, since they are moist nevertheless they do not blend and each stands alone and they have the law of dry things; therefore it appears the explanation is since even if he wants to eat the albumen according to Magen Avraham it is permitted — if so what is this that Tur wrote "and they do not put it except to improve appearance," for even if he wants to eat it — permitted:

(ד) That is, the albumen is called refuse since he does not want to eat it thus; behold also the yolk is not fit to eat except through mixture that they mix it in mustard — if so the albumen that remains without mixture is not fit for eating and is refuse and there is borer concern, thus Tur that they do not put the yolk except for appearance; but it is fit to eat without mustard mixture, and the same the albumen alone is fit to eat and is food:
