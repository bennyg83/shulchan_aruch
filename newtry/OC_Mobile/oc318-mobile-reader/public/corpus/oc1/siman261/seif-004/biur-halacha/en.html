One may not establish an eruv. This means an eruv chatzeiros, as I explained in the Mishna Berurah. It is likewise explicit in the Mordechai at the end of the chapter Bameh Madlikin, from which this ruling is derived. It is surprising that the Peri Megadim wishes to say that the Mechaber is discussing an eruv techumin here. See the Mordechai there, where Rabbeinu Yoel disagrees and permits it. The Mechaber rules here without qualification in accordance with those who are stringent, and the Rama likewise rules without qualification to be stringent in siman 253, se'if 2, in his gloss. Now, in Shulchan Aruch, siman 393, se'if 2, the Mechaber cites a dispute concerning this matter, and it appears there that he inclines more toward the permissive view. They maintain that the permission granted during bein hashmashos applies even when one has already accepted Shabbos upon himself. To avoid a contradiction between the rulings, many later authorities, namely the Eliyah Rabbah in one of his resolutions, as cited by the Peri Megadim, the Machazik Berachah cited in Shaarei Teshuvah, and the Shulchan Atzei Shittim, distinguish between the cases. They explain that there the case concerns an individual who accepted Shabbos upon himself, whereas here it concerns the communal acceptance of Shabbos through Barechu, which is more stringent. See the Taz in siman 601, who rules that one may establish an eruv even after accepting Shabbos. The Derech HaChaim likewise inclines to rule that any shevus performed for the sake of a mitzvah is permitted even after the community has accepted Shabbos, and even during the half hour before nightfall. His reason is that this period is no more stringent than bein hashmashos, and the community certainly did not intend to accept an addition to Shabbos that would be more stringent than bein hashmashos itself; see there. Nevertheless, the Tosefta from which the poskim derive proof for the stringent ruling is not resolved by his reasoning; see the Mordechai. See also the Biur HaGra in siman 393. It appears from his words that he inclines more toward the prohibitive view, and he notes that this is also the Rama's view in siman 253, se'if 2, in the gloss. Nevertheless, it appears to me that when an additional uncertainty is present, one may rule leniently in this matter. An example is where it is two hours before nightfall, which according to many poskim is before plag haminchah, so that his acceptance of Shabbos has no effect. Although Mahariv above in the gloss mentions one or two hours, it is possible that he referred only to the Torah-prohibited melacha of kindling, regarding which the acceptance takes effect and the act is permitted only through a non-Jew. Regarding a shevus performed for the sake of a mitzvah, however, one may be lenient even to perform it himself when it is two hours before nightfall.
