(א) Answering Barchu — because it is the start of the Maariv prayer of Shabbat, therefore everyone refrains then from melacha, as below siman 263 seif 10, that it is like one who explicitly accepted kedushat Shabbat upon himself and is then forbidden in all the safeguards and fences the Sages fenced for Shabbat, such as entering a bathhouse to sweat in general, and all the more so bathing in hot water, and similar in all shevutin; and the Mechaber came to inform us that even those things they permitted during bein hashemashot, such as eruvei chatzerot and hatmanah that are certainly for Shabbat need, and likewise every shevut for mitzvah need — nevertheless here it is forbidden; and the reason is since he explicitly accepted Shabbat upon himself, even though it is a long time before bein hashemashot it is more stringent than bein hashemashot without acceptance [and therefore the permission of bein hashemashot mentioned above regarding eruv chatzerot and hatmanah and similar is only if he is in a place where there is no community, for otherwise certainly most of the community already accepted Shabbat during bein hashemashot and the minority are drawn after them perforce, as written siman 263 seif 12 and 13 — see there]; and see Biur Halacha in the name of many Acharonim who wrote that specifically via Barchu which is community acceptance of Shabbat; but if an individual accepted Shabbat upon himself from while it is still day it is not more stringent than bein hashemashot, and likewise is implied from Magen Avraham s.k. 7:

(ד) Like answering Barchu — that presumably since he mentions Shabbat it is like acceptance. Magen Avraham wrote: and now the custom is to say Mizmor Shir, etc., and nevertheless they do all melacha until Barchu [and this is when the time is before bein hashemashot] and the reason is that originally they accepted thus and do not intend acceptance thereby. All this in Magen Avraham's time; but in our places the custom today is immediately when they say Mizmor Shir the community accepts Shabbat upon themselves and it is forbidden in all melacha even though the day is still great [Peri Megadim and Derech Chaim]; and he wrote in Sefer Derech Chachmah that likewise in places where they practice saying Lecha Dodi and conclude Bo'i Kallah it is actual acceptance of Shabbat; and see Tosafot Shabbat:
