(s.k. 4) And one may not establish an eiruv, etc. — for it is acquisition of a house — meaning every person may walk two thousand amah in every direction from his house and from the place where he sleeps. And when he wishes to walk to the east of his house four thousand amah, he places his eiruv at the end of two thousand amah from his house. And it is as if he acquired at that place of his eiruv a dwelling, and there is his house. It is found he walks two thousand amah from the place of his eiruv in every direction — if so, from his city toward the east side he walks four thousand. If so, this resembles one who acquired a house in the place where he places the eiruv, and this is forbidden on bein hashemashos. Not so eruv chatzerot, for they enacted it only because houses belong only to an individual and the courtyard is shared among all the courtyard owners. It is found the courtyard facing the houses is similar to reshut haRabbim facing reshut hayachid; and if they take from the houses to the courtyard they will err to take also from reshut hayachid to reshut haRabbim. Therefore they enacted eruv chatzerot — namely, each house in that courtyard gives bread in the house of another in that courtyard, and through this it is as if all of them dwell in that house; and it is not on account of acquisition as if he acquired that house, but on account of dwelling — as if all of them dwell there. And if so, as the courtyard belongs to all of them, so too the house, for all of them dwell together; and so the other houses are considered as one, as explained below siman 366; and therefore this shevut of eruv chatzerot is lighter than eiruv techumim, as written siman 398. However, so it is in Beitzah daf 17 — one does not place eiruv techumim from the first yom tov to the second, for to acquire a dwelling on yom tov is forbidden. And Tosafot wrote: but eruv chatzerot is permitted, for it is not acquisition on erev Shabbat. But here in chapter BeMeh Madlikin on this law written here, Rashi and Tosafot wrote the reason of eiruv techumim — that the Sages relied it on a verse, and is stricter; not so eruv chatzerot where there is no verse — lighter on erev Shabbat:
