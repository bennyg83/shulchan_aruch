(s.k. 4) One may not establish an eiruv, etc. Because this constitutes acquiring a residence. Meaning, every person may walk two thousand amos in every direction from his house and place of residence. When he wishes to walk four thousand amos east of his house, he places his eiruv at the end of two thousand amos from his house. It is then as though he acquired residence there, at the place of his eiruv, and that is considered his home. He may therefore walk two thousand amos in every direction from the place of his eiruv, and thus four thousand amos eastward from his city. This resembles acquiring a house at the place where he deposits the eiruv, which is forbidden during bein hashmashos. An eiruv chatzeiros is different. It was enacted because each house belongs to an individual, while the courtyard is jointly owned by all the residents of the courtyard. The courtyard relative to the houses therefore resembles a public domain relative to a private domain. Were they to carry from the houses into the courtyard, they might mistakenly carry from a private domain into a public domain. The Sages therefore enacted an eiruv chatzeiros, whereby every household in that courtyard contributes bread to one house in the courtyard. Through this, it is considered as though they all reside in that house. This is not based on acquisition, as though each person acquired that house, but on residence, as though they all live there. Thus, just as the courtyard belongs to all of them, the house also belongs to all of them, since they all reside together, and all the other houses are likewise regarded as one, as explained below in siman 366. The shevus of eiruv chatzeiros is therefore more lenient than eiruv techumin, as written in siman 528. This is indeed stated in Beitzah, folio 17, that one may not establish an eiruv techumin from the first day of Yom Tov for the second, because acquiring a place of residence on Yom Tov is forbidden. Tosafos wrote, however, that an eiruv chatzeiros is permitted because it is not an acquisition; see there. Here, however, in the chapter Bameh Madlikin, concerning the law stated here, Rashi and Tosafos wrote that eiruv techumin is more stringent because the Sages supported it with a Scriptural verse, whereas eiruv chatzeiros, which has no such Scriptural support, is more lenient; see there.
