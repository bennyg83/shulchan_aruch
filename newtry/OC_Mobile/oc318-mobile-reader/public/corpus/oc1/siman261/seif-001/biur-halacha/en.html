(2) And one may not immerse [vessels], etc.; see in Mishna Berurah what I wrote that if he needs [the vessel] for Shabbos, etc.; and behold in responsum Sha'agas Aryeh siman 56 he inclines to be stringent regarding metal vessels even in bein hashemashot, and his words there are not compelling; and likewise all the latter poskim did not divide on this:
(3) The vessels; see in Mishna Berurah; and furthermore it is possible to say that it deals specifically regarding impure vessels alone, and it comes to teach us through this that today as well the rabbinic decree applies that it is forbidden to immerse impure vessels to purify them, even though today it does not appear as a repair since tumah and taharah are not practiced now and what repair immersion accomplishes; nevertheless it is forbidden because a matter [established] by majority requires another majority to permit it; see Beitzah 5a and it is resolved through this the difficulty of Peri To'ar, see there:
(4) And one may not kindle; see in Mishna Berurah in what is written in the name of Peri Megadim, and he disagrees with Tosafos who was uncertain regarding a positive commandment at the time of tosefet Shabbos or during bein hashemashot, and he brought proof to be stringent regarding bein hashemashot and regarding tosefet Shabbos he sides to be lenient; and behold the time of bein hashemashot itself is known to have differing opinions among the poskim, and see below in seif 2 and in the name of the latter [poskim] as written [poskim] how one should act in practice to be careful to light before sunset, for otherwise according to several poskim it is a safek d'oraisa; but b'dieved if he transgressed and lit or performed other melacha after sunset at a time when there are differing opinions among the poskim, one may be lenient b'dieved to benefit from it, and similarly Magen Avraham wrote in siman 323 seif katan 11 regarding immersing new vessels that if one transgressed and immersed, it is permitted to use them since there are those who permit l'chatchila:
(5) And one may make an eruv, etc.; and the same applies that he may place eruv tavshilin then [Acharonim in the name of Mordechai]:
(6) Eruvei chatzerot; this is even for a matter of reshus, as is proven from Levush. And the reason is that all eruvei chatzerot is included in a mitzvah, as is written at the end of siman 260 and in siman 366 seif 13 that it is a mitzvah to seek out eruvei chatzerot, and they did not decree during bein hashemashot; and Korban Minchah wrote that nevertheless it is not called on account of this a mitzvah except that they permitted it because the reason is that he is preoccupied and hurries because of it [Tosafos Shabbos]. And it is proven from the words of Magen Avraham in siman 280 seif katan 3 that he seeks to find reasons for why one must hurry regarding eruvei chatzerot before bein hashemashot, for it is clear to him that during bein hashemashot one may also bless when placing the eruv chatzerot, and it is not like other matters where we hold safek berachot l'hakel; and the reason must be that regarding eruv chatzerot which is lenient they made the matter as if it were a certain day. And if he is uncertain about the time itself whether it is bein hashemashot or it is already night, he should be careful not to bless; and regarding the eruv itself there would appear to be proof to be stringent as well, that we do not say in this safek d'rabbanan l'kula from what Bach wrote and Magen Avraham brought in seif katan 1 that a safek bein hashemashot [namely either it is day or it is bein hashemashot] is judged as bein hashemashot, and the latter poskim wrote the reason that safek heser yeda (doubt due to lack of knowledge) does not enter at all into the category of safek, and therefore it is not a sefeik sefeika and is judged as if it were certainly bein hashemashot; and likewise in our matter the opposite; and one should examine, and see below in what we wrote in the general rules of time measurements how one should act in practice:
