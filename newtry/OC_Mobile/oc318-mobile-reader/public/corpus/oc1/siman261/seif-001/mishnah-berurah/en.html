(1) Bein hashemashos — and if he is unsure whether the time for bein hashemashos has arrived, it is likewise forbidden:

(2) After sunset of the sun — that is, after the end of sunset; for according to Tosafot and Shulchan Aruch it extends from the beginning of sunset until its end more than three mil, as below in seif 2; and afterward the time of bein hashemashos begins. And see below in Mishna Berurah s.k. 20:

(3) One may not tithe — even tithe of fruits which is d'rabbanan:

(4) What is certain — for it is like repairing a vessel, and the same applies that it is forbidden to separate challah even in chutz la'aretz. And challah of chutz la'aretz is more stringent than tithe — for regarding tithe, if he has nothing to eat on Shabbat it is for the sake of a mitzvah, and we hold they did not decree shevut on bein hashemashos for the sake of a mitzvah, as he ruled below; but regarding challah one should be stringent in every manner, for challah of chutz la'aretz one may eat without separating challah and leave a little until after Shabbat and separate from what remains; and therefore when Shabbat falls on erev Pesach and he did not separate challah from the matzah on erev Pesach while it was still day — it is permitted to separate even on erev Pesach bein hashemashos, for it is impossible to eat and leave a piece from every matzah. And the same applies if he did not separate challah while it was still day from the chametz bread he will eat in the morning on Shabbat:

(5) And one may not immerse, etc. — the Mechaber copied the language of the mishnah [Shabbat 34] even though it is not practiced now, for the mishnah deals with immersing vessels from their impurity; nevertheless there is a practical difference from this for us regarding immersing new vessels acquired from a gentile — according to those who forbid immersing them on Shabbat, as below siman 323 seif 7, that it is likewise forbidden for them to immerse on bein hashemashos; and the reason is because it appears like repairing the vessel — but through immersion it is permitted to use it; however if he needs it for Shabbat and has no other, he may immerse on bein hashemashos and bless as required:

(6) And one may not kindle — in this it is forbidden even for the sake of a mitzvah, lest the Shabbat meal be nullified thereby; and such is a safek d'oraisa. And even b'dieved, if he kindled or did any other melacha on bein hashemashos — it is forbidden to benefit from it, as if he had done it on Shabbat itself, which is forbidden even if he did it b'shogeg, as explained siman 318 [Peri Megadim and see Biur Halacha]:

(7) And one may not establish eiruv techumim — for in the Gemara there is a verse support for eiruv techumim, and therefore it is stricter, that even for a matter of mitzvah it is forbidden; so wrote Shulchan Aruch and Levush is lenient for a matter of mitzvah [and these two views are found in Sefer Meiri on erev Shabbat, who writes that per the lenient view the Gemara forbids establishing an eiruv specifically for a matter of optional use, and with an eiruv at his feet in general it is permitted even for optional use (see siman 415 s.k. 2 in Magen Avraham) — for with bread we hold that even from while it is still day one may not establish an eiruv except for a matter of mitzvah (as written siman 415 seif 1) — end of Meiri's words]:

(8) And see siman 415 — meaning it is explained there that b'dieved his eiruv is an eiruv; and see Magen Avraham:

(9) The demai — for most amei ha'aretz tithe, and it is not called repairing:

(10) And one may insulate — in something that does not add steam, as written at the beginning of siman 257 — see there where all details of the law are explained with their reasons. And for other things forbidden d'rabbanan, aside from those listed here, the law of bein hashemashos is like Shabbat itself, unless it is for a matter of mitzvah or other pressing need, as explained below siman 342 — see there:

(11) And one may establish — and he may also bless; and see Shulchan Aruch HaRav and what we wrote there. Nevertheless l'chatchila he should beware not to delay eruv chatzerot until bein hashemashos [Magen Avraham siman 205 s.k. 3 — see there]:

(12) Siman 393 — that is, shared alleyways have the law of eruv chatzerot, and other legal details — see there:

(13) To tell a gentile — for speech to a gentile is included in shevut, and they did not decree shevut on bein hashemashos for the sake of a mitzvah, as below siman 342 — see there:

(14) Bein hashemashos — the Mechaber follows his view that it appears to him below siman 307 seif 5 that on Shabbat itself it is forbidden through a gentile in melacha d'oraisa even if it is for the sake of Shabbat; and see below siman 276 seif 2 in Hagahah:

(15) For the sake of a mitzvah — even if it is not for the sake of Shabbat, as below siman 342 — see there and in Mishna Berurah:

(ע) And he is concerned about it — Rashal wrote: meaning any matter that if it is not done he would suffer distress on Shabbat, it is permitted to do via a non-Jew during bein hashemashot; but in my heart it strikes me to be stringent if he has no need for them at all on Shabbat but only for weekday need; however if it is great financial loss or great need it is permitted as shevut during bein hashemashot, and in particular shevut of amirah to a non-Jew that has no action; and therefore it is permitted to tell a non-Jew during bein hashemashot to kindle a necessary candle, since the world is careful about it we consider it as great need — end of his words in brief:

(פ) An hour or two — specifically an hour or two; but half an hour close to dark, when then possibly in all places of Israel they accepted Shabbat upon themselves, is more stringent and forbidden even to tell a non-Jew to do melacha; however for need of mitzvah it is permitted. And even according to what the Mechaber rules below seif 4 that after accepting Shabbat it is forbidden to transgress even shevut in a place of mitzvah — nevertheless regarding amirah to a non-Jew we are not more stringent than bein hashemashot:

(צ) To tell a non-Jew to kindle — even not in a place of mitzvah, since the day is still great, certainly there are places that have not yet accepted Shabbat upon themselves and do melacha themselves; it suffices for us that via acceptance we rest ourselves from melacha. And know that in our matter we speak where the community in his city accepted Shabbat upon themselves and kedushat Shabbat falls perforce even if one does not wish to accept; therefore he wrote to tell a non-Jew, etc., for the above reason; but an individual who accepted Shabbat upon himself is permitted even to tell his fellow Israelite to do melacha for him, as below siman 263 seif 17 — see there the reason in Mishna Berurah:
