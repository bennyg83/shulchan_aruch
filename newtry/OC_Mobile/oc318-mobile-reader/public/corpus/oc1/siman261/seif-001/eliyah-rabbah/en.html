One may not immerse, etc., unnecessarily, etc. Taz is stringent even where immersion is needed, because one can transfer ownership of the utensil to a non-Jew, as below in siman 323 seif 7. Olat Shabbat similarly writes that one may not separate challah even for an immediate need because he can leave a small amount over, as below in siman 506. They also cite Bach that one may immerse through a subterfuge, such as drawing water in the vessel. Below it will be explained that even on Shabbos this is permitted. Perhaps Bach means that because such a subterfuge is possible, one may not immerse it openly during bein hashmashot even where transferring it to a non-Jew is impossible.
An eruv techumin for a discretionary purpose, etc. This is difficult, because below in siman 415 even before Shabbos one may not make an eruv techumin for a discretionary purpose. One may answer that here the case is an eruv made by walking to the place, which Beit Yosef there permits before Shabbos. The Levush there, however, does not appear to distinguish. Perhaps his intent is that for a discretionary purpose it is invalid even after the fact. Yet in seif 2 there he rules that after the fact the eruv is valid, though perhaps that is only for a mitzvah purpose. This is strained. Moreover, according to this, initially even an eruv techumin for a mitzvah would be prohibited during bein hashmashot, which does not appear from the Levush.
[Levush] For a matter of, etc. With fire, however, it is prohibited even for a mitzvah. The implied reason is that kindling is a complete act of repair. But this is insufficient, because separating maaser is also a complete act of repair. Rather, since maaser is not full-fledged melachah but only a shvut, the Sages did not decree it during bein hashmashot. Yet this raises a difficulty: the Gemara in Shabbos 46 could have established the entire Mishnah as referring to eruv techumin, with the first clause concerning a non-mitzvah purpose. One can answer.
