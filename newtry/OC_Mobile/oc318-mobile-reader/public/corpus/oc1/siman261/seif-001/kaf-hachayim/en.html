(1) [Seif 1] Safek chashichah and it is bein hashemashot, etc. — regarding the measure of bein hashemashot the early and later [authorities] struggled over it, and the Gra"z in responsa 21 wrote that the world practiced in all the provinces of Israel like Sefer HaGeonim (and they are Rabbeinu Sherira Gaon and Rav Hai Gaon and Rabbeinu Nissim Gaon as is written in responsa Maharam Alshakar siman 96), and in what is written "within the time of two or three milin" they kindle. And Maharal ibn Chaviv chapter 2 of Kiddush HaChodesh attributed this view to the Rif and Rambam and the Rash, and also the words of the Nagid Rabbeinu Avraham son of Rambam like this reasoning as Maharam Alshakar wrote in responsa siman 96. Birkei Yosef note 1, and he wrote and it is implied that we hold leniently like Sefer HaGeonim, and even if you say the dispute is balanced we do not have in our hands to nullify the custom — end quote. And likewise the Rav Gaht Mekushar in his commentary to Tosafot of the Ra'avan end of Hilchot Kippur daf 92b that the custom of the world is according to Sefer HaGeonim that immediately after sunset the time of bein hashemashot begins, which is three-quarters of a mil, and this reasoning is close to the view, and the practice of Israel is Torah and they have upon what to rely; and one who wishes to be stringent like Rabbenu Tam (namely in what is written) may, but one should not protest against those who are lenient, etc., see there. And Machber brought note 7, and likewise in responsa Batei Kohanim vol. 2 siman 4, and Machber in K"A note 2 brought, see there. And likewise the Gra extended and brought several proofs to this that bein hashemashot begins from the beginning of sunset — namely immediately after the sun is not visible on the earth, see there. And the custom now among us is specifically like Sefer HaGeonim to be stringent — namely for kabbalat Shabbat and Yom Kippur and the like — namely after the beginning of sunset of the sun when the sun is no longer visible on the earth they consider it bein hashemashot, and after three-quarters of a mil to this time they consider it night; but regarding Motzei Shabbat and Yom Kippur and eating matzah on Pesach night and the like, for everything that requires definite night they do not consider it definite night except until after the emergence of three medium-sized stars — namely everywhere according to its place; and in those places such as Eretz Yisrael and Syria and Aram Naharaim its time is like two-thirds of an hour after the sun is no longer visible on the earth.
