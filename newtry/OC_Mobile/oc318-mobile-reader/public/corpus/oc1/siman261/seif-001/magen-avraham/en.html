Bein hashemashos. And if he is unsure whether it is bein hashemashos, it is forbidden (Bach):

One may not tithe. For it is like repairing a vessel, and the same applies that it is forbidden to separate challah — as Magen Avraham wrote siman 205 and siman 306:

And one may not immerse. For it is repairing a vessel:

And one may not establish an eiruv. For it is acquisition of a house — as written siman 398:

And one may insulate. In something that does not add steam — as written siman 257; but for other things the law of bein hashemashos is like Shabbat itself:

And it is permitted to speak. But to tithe and to immerse are not a mitzvah, and eruv chatzerot is a mitzvah (nevertheless in many places he wrote that eruv chatzerot is not a mitzvah but rather he is occupied and hurried about it; therefore if he has nothing to eat on Shabbat or has no vessels — it is permitted to immerse on bein hashemashos). However there are shevutot that are forbidden also on bein hashemashos as written siman 409 seif 3 and siman 314 seif 3; and Rashal wrote in a responsum siman 46 — everything that if it did not come to his hand he would grieve over it — it is found he would grieve on Shabbat — for this they permitted. And further, my heart strikes me to be stringent: if it is a complete business for weekday needs that he does not need at all on Shabbat — forbidden even if he would grieve over it; but for a great need or with substantial loss — shevut is permitted; and in particular shevut of speaking to a gentile. And likewise it is permitted to say to a gentile on bein hashemashos to light a candle on the day his father died, since the world is careful about it — we consider it as a great need, for there is no distinction between a great need and substantial loss — end of his words:

To light a candle. Even not in a place of a mitzvah — for since the day is still great, certainly there are places that have not yet accepted Shabbat upon themselves; and it is comparable to that which we say — they eat, we eat; to us, darkening is not darkening (there). And if you will say — but without this it is permitted, for if he wishes he need not accept Shabbat upon himself, as written siman 263. And one could say that case is different, for it deals with an individual who accepts Shabbat upon himself; but a congregation that accepted Shabbat upon themselves — Shabbat has befallen them perforce and is worse than bein hashemashos, as written seif 4. Therefore Mahari'v needed a different reason; and it implies from his words that if it is half an hour near darkening — for then it is possible all places in Israel have accepted Shabbat upon themselves — it is forbidden to tell a gentile.
