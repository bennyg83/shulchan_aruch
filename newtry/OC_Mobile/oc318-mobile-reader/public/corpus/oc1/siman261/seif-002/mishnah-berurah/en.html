(19) That one must add (both at entry and at exit, as stated below siman 293 seif 2) see there. And upon this time there is neither lav nor karet, only a positive mitzvah from the Torah; and we derive [Gemara Rosh Hashanah 9] from what is written on Yom Kippur: "and you shall afflict your souls on the ninth of the month in the evening from evening until evening you shall rest your rest"; and we say: perhaps on the ninth they fast? Scripture says "in the evening"; if in the evening, perhaps from when it darkens? Scripture says "on the ninth"; how so? He begins and fasts from while it is still day in order to add from weekday onto the sacred; and also at exit he adds, from "from evening to evening" and from "you shall rest your rest" we derive that wherever cessation is stated, as regarding Shabbos and Yom Tov, one must also add and rest from melacha. And the time of addition is perforce before bein hashemashot, for during bein hashemashot it is safek perhaps night and one is liable an asham talui, and no verse is needed to add:
(20) From the start of sunset; this is Rabbeinu Tam's view and his supporters, who hold there are two sunsets: at first the sun is hidden from our eyes and sets, and this is called the start of sunset; it waits the measure of three mil and a quarter mil and still it is day; and from then on begins the second sunset when the light begins to set completely, and this is called the end of sunset; its duration lasts the measure of walking three-quarters of a mil, which is 1,500 amos, and that interval is bein hashmashos; and afterward come out three medium stars that are the sign of night; and it turns out according to his view from the start of sunset until the emergence of the stars is four mil; and see what we wrote nearby that many poskim disagree on this and hold that immediately when the sun was hidden from our eyes it is bein hashemashot, which is safek day safek night:
(21) If he wished to do it, etc.; meaning via speech that he accepts it upon himself for the sake of tosefet Shabbos, or via saying Barchu as below seif 4. And whether acceptance in the heart helps for this; see siman 608, se'if 3, in the Rama's gloss and Mishna Berurah there. And see siman 263 seif 10 in Rama's gloss that our custom is that the woman kindling Shabbos candles accepts Shabbos with this kindling:
(22) What time; and addition alone is not enough, rather one needs a little more. And the measure of addition together with bein hashemashot amounts to almost half an hour; and see Biur Halacha:
(23) Three-quarters of a mil; and this is approximately a quarter of an hour. And behold Shulchan Aruch mentioned in this seif Rabbeinu Tam's view and his supporters; but many of the Rishonim hold, and also the Gra agreed to their method, that bein hashemashot begins immediately after the start of sunset, namely from when the sun was hidden from our eyes, and its duration lasts three-quarters of a mil; and afterward near it come out the three medium stars and it is night from the Torah for all matters. And therefore one must be very careful not to do melacha after the sun was hidden from our eyes, and even melacha of mitzvah such as kindling candles for the Shabbos meal; also be very careful to finish their kindling before the sun sets, for afterward it is included in bein hashemashot; and see above s.k. 6. And l'chatchila one should not wait until the last moment, rather advance their kindling from when the sun is at the tops of the trees as below; and one who is stringent upon himself and refrains from melacha half an hour or at least a third of an hour before sunset; fortunate is he, for he fulfills thereby the method of all the Rishonim. And regarding Motzei Shabbos see Biur Halacha where we explained in the name of poskim that for everyone the measure of three-quarters of a mil changes according to time and place, and this was not stated in the Gemara except for the horizon of Babylonia and in the time of Nisan and Tishrei when days and nights are equal; and in our places that lean toward the north of the world it lasts much longer; therefore one must be very careful not to do melacha on Motzei Shabbos even if much time passed after sunset until small stars are seen, as below siman 293 seif 2; see there in Mishna Berurah and Biur Halacha:
(24) Before night; night is called from the Torah for all matters from when three stars are seen; and see Biur Halacha:
(25) He has the option; and he is forbidden in doing melacha; but if he accepted Shabbos upon himself before plag hamincha his acceptance has no effect; and plag hamincha is called an hour and a quarter before evening; see above siman 233 that these are seasonal hours, meaning whether the day is long or short it is divided into 12 parts, and a part and a quarter before evening is plag hamincha. We also wrote there that there are opinions among poskim whether this hour and a quarter is before sunset or before the emergence of the stars; therefore in our matter b'dieved if he accepted upon himself for tosefet Shabbos until an hour and a quarter before sunset he should be stringent not to do melacha:
