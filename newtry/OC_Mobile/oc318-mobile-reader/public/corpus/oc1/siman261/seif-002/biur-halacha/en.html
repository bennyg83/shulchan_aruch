(1) Some say one must, etc. — they used the language "some say" because Rambam disagrees on this as is explained in Beit Yosef; and see in Biur HaGra who rules like Rambam [and it requires further study in his words that he wrote that the conclusion of Tosafot is also like Rambam and it is not so clear in Rosh Hashanah there, and in Moed Katan daf 4a in Tosafot s.v. mah l'halan it is proven explicitly that they hold that tosefet Shabbat is d'oraisa; and perhaps he had a different text in Tosafot Rosh Hashanah; and also his seeing that he brought from what they said on Shabbat daf 18 that we permit with the sun is not the only [proof] according to his view who rules like the view of the Geonim that immediately when the sun sets from our eyes it is included in bein hashemashot, which is not so according to Shulchan Aruch — it is not proof at all]; and in Acharonim he wrote that for practical law we follow this "some say" because all the Rishonim hold thus [from the sugya in Rosh Hashanah 9 and Yoma chapter 1]; and likewise in Shulchan Aruch below in siman 393 seif 2 he rules anonymously like this "some say." And know that regarding the time of erev Shabbat there is not so much difference, for for both of them the prohibition of melacha begins after sunset and not before; and on the contrary, for the Gra according to his view it is more stringent, for according to him who rules like the view of the Geonim, immediately when the sun sets — namely from the hour when the sun is hidden from our eyes — it is included in bein hashemashot, and in this there is safek issur skilah, whereas tosefet Shabbat contains nothing except a mere positive commandment, and also it depends on his will during the time of 3 milin after sunset as is explained in Shulchan Aruch. And know further that d'rabbanan even for Rambam it is a mitzvah to add a little from the weekday onto the holy [day], as the plain meaning of the wording of Rabbeinu HaMagid which is brought in Beit Yosef implies. And what Bach challenged — if so Rambam should not have omitted this law of addition, for in truth he already included it in what he brought the law of the tekios which they would blow on erev Shabbat in chapter 5 [Keneset HaGedolah in the name of Ra'anach]; and likewise in Biur HaGra that even for Rambam it is a mitzvah to add from the weekday onto the holy from this that the aforesaid tekios [and ostensibly it appears that also on Motzei Shabbat it is a mitzvah for all to add from the weekday onto the holy a little, for departing Shabbat is not inferior to entering — except that for Rambam this would be d'rabbanan and according to Shulchan Aruch d'oraisa]. And accordingly for the Gra it is more stringent also in this matter, for according to him it is a mitzvah to add at least d'rabbanan a little before sunset; whereas according to Shulchan Aruch we do not obligate at all before sunset. And in truth per the view of the Geonim who stand in the view of the Gra regarding bein hashemashot, and as we will write below if they hold like the view of the Rif and the other Rishonim that tosefet is required — that which we learned "we permit with the sun" — therefore it is not specifically with the sun literally but a little before because of tosefet, and per them there would be a concern of d'oraisa:

(2) From the beginning of sunset — the reason for all this is that in Pesachim 94 it states that from sunset until tzeit hakochavim is the measure of 4 milin; and in Shabbat [34-35] it states that from sunset bein hashemashot begins [for Rabba according to R. Yehudah whom we rule like]; and it says there that bein hashemashot lasts three-quarters of a mil and afterward it becomes night; and we say there that three medium-sized stars constitute night. If so, there is a contradiction, and perforce Rabbenu Tam and his camp answer to divide between sunset in Pesachim and sunset in Shabbat there, that the one in Pesachim is the beginning of sunset — from when the body of the sun is hidden when it sets from our eyes — and from then until tzeit hakochavim is 4 milin; and when the sun sets in Shabbat there it is the end of sunset, which is when the light of the sun also sets from most of the sky toward the west alone; and from then bein hashemashot begins, which is three-quarters of a mil before tzeit hakochavim. And from the beginning of sunset, which is the sunset of the body of the sun, until the end of the aforesaid sunset is day, and its duration is 3 milin and a quarter; and then is the time of tosefet Shabbat, as is written here in Shulchan Aruch; and among all of them they are 4 milin, and until 4 milin from the beginning of sunset it is not certainly night — this is the view of Rabbenu Tam and his camp; and see below in siman 331 seif 5 in Magen Avraham, it appears there that his view for halachah is like this view; but the Gra z"l disagrees on this view and extended with several proofs that sunset in Shabbat regarding bein hashemashot is also the beginning of sunset like sunset in Pesachim there, and immediately after sunset of the body of the sun bein hashemashot begins according to R. Yehudah whom R. Yochanan ruled like stringently on Shabbat there; and the duration of bein hashemashot is three-quarters of a mil; and the aforesaid difficulty he answers to divide between tzeit hakochavim of Shabbat and tzeit hakochavim of Pesachim, that tzeit hakochavim of Shabbat is the time of night which is three medium-sized stars, and tzeit hakochavim of Pesachim is the emergence of all the stars visible at night, which is a much later time, and is 4 milin after the beginning of sunset. And even though it appears to the eyes that the duration of bein hashemashot until tzeit hakochavim is much more than three-quarters of a mil, it is as the Gra wrote in his Biur that the measure of the Gemara is stated only regarding the horizon of Babylonia or Eretz Yisrael; and in our countries which are more inclined toward the north it lengthens more; and therefore regarding the end of bein hashemashot we do not have a fixed duration from the Gemara on our horizon, and it depends only according to sight when it is three medium-sized stars, but not large ones; and because we are not expert which are medium, one must wait until small ones as is written siman 293, and see there what we wrote further on this matter. And per the aforesaid view of the Gra, immediately after sunset of the body of the sun over the earth it is complete bein hashemashot, as stated. And in truth this view of his is not new, for Maharam Alshakar already agreed thus in responsum siman 96 that immediately after sunset of the body of the sun it is complete bein hashemashot, and he brought there that thus explicitly wrote the early Geonim in their responsa — they are Rabbeinu Sherira Gaon and Rav Hai Gaon and Rabbeinu Nissim Gaon; also R' Avraham HaChasid son of Rambam z"l also wrote there that thus is implied from the Rif and Rambam and Rashi, see there; and Shach brought it for halachah in Yoreh De'ah siman 266 seif katan 11 regarding the matter of bein hashemashot; and see also in the commentary of Maharalbach on chapter 2 of Hilchot Kiddush HaChodesh of Rambam s.v. klal ha'oleh that he also wrote that from the Rif and Rambam it is implied that he holds that from the beginning of sunset [namely when the sun is hidden from our eyes] it is bein hashemashot; and also Maharal of Prague in his chiddushim on Shabbat explained exactly like the aforesaid view of the Gra; and also Ra'avan holds thus that immediately when the body of the sun sets and is hidden from our eyes the time of bein hashemashot begins, see Hagahot Mordechai chapter 2 of Shabbat; and also in Sefer Seder Zmanim he extended and ruled for practical law like the Gra z"l that immediately after the beginning of sunset it is bein hashemashot. And behold for practical law one must certainly be careful per the view of the Gra and many of the Rishonim and poskim mentioned above that immediately after the beginning of sunset — namely from the hour when the sun is hidden from our eyes — it is a complete prohibition not to perform melacha, and Heaven forbid to be lenient in this, for it is safek issur skilah per the view of all these great authorities [and especially in our days when the world is not expert in the time of bein hashemashot; and in this manner for all there is to be stringent to refrain from melacha from the beginning of sunset as is stated in the Gemara and brought in Shulchan Aruch seif 3]; and it is not specifically regarding Shabbat, for the same applies to every matter in the Torah where there is a difference between day and night — we go to be stringent that immediately at the beginning of sunset bein hashemashot begins and the time of bein hashemashot continues until the emergence of three medium-sized stars, as stated. And know that in Sefer Yere'im of Rabbeinu Eliezer MiMitz was even more stringent regarding the beginning of bein hashemashot, and he holds that bein hashemashot begins three-quarters of a mil before the beginning of sunset, and his words are brought in Agudah and in Mordechai chapter 2 of Shabbat; and see in Bach who extended on this, and his view is that one should be careful l'chatchila to practice like the view of the Yere'im, see there, and he brought that thus was the custom of the communities from ancient times; and see in Magen Avraham who also brought the words of Bach, and it is implied from him that stringently he was concerned for the words of Bach [even though in the fundamental law he holds like Magen Avraham like the view of Rabbenu Tam as is proven below siman 331 seif 5 in what is written there regarding milah on erev Shabbat]; however the Gra rejected the view of the Ra'avan mentioned above, and see in Sefer Seder Zmanim what he wrote explicitly on the words of the Ra'avan; and nevertheless l'chatchila it is certainly good to be concerned for the words of the Ra'avan and to advance a little more in kindling the candles in order to satisfy his view as well, as I wrote in Mishna Berurah seif katan 23, and as Bach wrote that thus was the custom of the communities from ancient times:

(3) That it is 3 milin and a quarter — see in Peri Megadim that the 4 milin are equidistant hours and not temporal hours; if so accordingly even during the period of Tammuz this measure applies as well; however in Sefer Minchat Cohen his view for halachah is that even per the view of Rabbenu Tam the four milin are temporal, and in summer days it lengthens more, and the words of the Gemara were stated only during the time of Nissan and Tishrei when days and nights are equal, whereas on other days the matter changes per the time [but for practical law he concludes there that one should not rely on this reasoning except to be stringent, such as on Motzei Shabbat and not to be lenient]; and likewise it is implied from what Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Mahari"l that regarding accepting Shabbat it is obvious that we follow each day after its hours [meaning that the time from alot hashachar until tzeit hakochavim of Shabbat, within which is also included the time of 4 milin after its hours]; and likewise is also the view of the Gra in his Biur that all the measures of the Gemara — whether the 4 milin which is until the emergence of all the stars according to his view, or the three-quarters of a mil which is the time of bein hashemashot — all are in the time of Nissan and Tishrei, whereas at another time it changes per the time, and in summer days bein hashemashot lengthens much more; and likewise the Gra wrote further that the measures of the Gemara were stated only on the horizon of Babylonia, but in our countries which incline toward the north bein hashemashot always lengthens more; and likewise wrote Sefer Minchat Cohen; and who in our time can precisely determine the time — therefore on Motzei Shabbat one must be very careful even per the view of the Geonim and the Gra not to perform melacha until three medium-sized stars are seen, for this is the clear sign of night stated in the Gemara in several places [Berachot 2a and Pesachim 2a and Megillah 20b], and before then it is not certainly night; and this sign applies in every place and at every time, but because we are not expert in medium ones we need small ones as below, and see in siman 293 what we wrote regarding the upper redness equaling the lower, which also applies regarding Motzei Shabbat. And per the view of Rabbenu Tam one must wait on Motzei Shabbat from performing melacha until the time of 4 milin, which is at least the measure of an hour and a fifth from the time of the beginning of sunset, which according to his view is then the time of emergence of three medium-sized stars; and it is proper l'chatchila to follow the view of Rabbenu Tam and all these great authorities who hold his view not to perform melacha on Motzei Shabbat until the measure of 4 milin is completed; and see what we wrote below in siman 293 in Biur Halacha s.v. ad at the end of the passage there. And regarding temporal hours on long days it appears that if one sees the upper redness equaling the lower — namely the red glow sank from the entire arch of the sky on the western side — and there are also three stars, one need not be stringent to wait for temporal hours on long days even per the view of Rabbenu Tam [for behold these two signs are also stated in the Gemara [Shabbat 35] regarding the time of night, and it is also stated in the Gemara there that Abaye would look at the sign of the redness on erev Shabbat in the Gemara, and it is implied that with this sign alone he was satisfied; and although we are not as expert as they were, at least when the sign of the stars is also joined certainly we need not be more stringent; and better for us to say that we are not expert in the calculation of the horizons which change the sun in its marches according to place and time, and therefore we do not know well the calculation of the 4 milin which is only one sign, and we have what to rely on from the saying that we err in these two signs]:

(4) What time — see in Mishna Berurah what we wrote that it is not sufficient, etc.; likewise the Rash in Beitzah chapter HaMeivi and it is brought in Beit Yosef, and the poskim wrote that its measure was not clarified from the Gemara; and it appears that in any case it is less than the time of three-quarters of a mil [for otherwise the difficulty of Tosafot in Rosh Hashanah daf 9a s.v. v'Rebbi Akiva would remain, in what they challenged from that which is stated in Shabbat safek chashichah, see there]; and therefore I ruled anonymously in Mishna Berurah like the first view:

(5) And the measure of time of bein hashemashot, etc. — behold in Shabbat 35 we say the halachah is like R. Yehudah regarding Shabbat and the halachah is like R. Yosi regarding terumah, and the same applies regarding Motzei Shabbat as the poskim wrote; and ostensibly one should examine, since the reason of R. Yochanan who ruled thus is because of safek as is proven in the Gemara there, it is possible that we are stringent only regarding d'oraisa whose safek is l'chumra; but regarding all the shevutin, even if we hold that they decreed upon them during bein hashemashot, it is only during the bein hashemashot of R. Yosi; however from the closing wording of Shulchan Aruch it is implied that it applies also to the view that deals with shevutin which are d'rabbanan, and likewise is proven from Rama in the gloss in seif 1; and similarly it is implied in 35b in Tosafot s.v. hikshah, etc., see there at the end of their words; and likewise is proven from Rambam who wrote in chapter 5 of Hilchot Shabbat halachah 4 that from when the sun sets is the time called bein hashemashot everywhere; and in chapter 24 halachah 10 regarding the shevutin he wrote anonymously "during bein hashemashot" — behold it is explicit that from sunset which is the bein hashemashot of R. Yehudah the shevutin mentioned in bein hashemashot are forbidden. And indeed regarding a matter where we go leniently during bein hashemashot and we rely that it is night, such as regarding an individual fast [see in the Rash chapter 2 of Bava Metzia seif 24 and in siman 562 seif 1 in Biur HaGra there], it is implied from the words of the Rash, and likewise in the chiddushim of Rashba in the name of Rabbenu Yonah, that it applies even during the bein hashemashot of R. Yehudah; and accordingly regarding counting the omer according to those who are lenient during bein hashemashot in siman 489 it would be permitted even during the bein hashemashot of R. Yehudah; and likewise regarding shevut during bein hashemashot for the need of a mitzvah, where we hold in siman 302 that they did not decree upon it — per the view of the latter authorities who hold there that it applies even on Motzei Shabbat, it would likewise be permitted during the bein hashemashot of R. Yehudah, even though for R. Yosi it is still certainly day and not bein hashemashot. However one should examine according to what the Gra wrote in his Biur, and likewise in Sefer Korban Netanel — both of them were astonished at the Rash in what he was lenient regarding fasting because we hold stringently that the halachah is like R. Yosi, and if so according to him there is not even safek lailah before then, and we do not apply two leniencies to say that safek is permitted and the halachah is like R. Yehudah — for this too is safek; if so it is possible in our matter as well regarding shevut on Motzei Shabbat or regarding counting as well we do not apply leniency because of safek d'rabbanan, since for R. Yosi even safek does not exist:

(6) Before night — night is called from the Torah for every matter from when three medium-sized stars are seen; and because we are not expert between large and medium, therefore one must refrain from melacha even per the view of Rabbenu Tam three-quarters of a mil before any stars are seen; and likewise Minchat Cohen, and it is straightforward. And know that as is known the measure of 4 milin changed per the horizon and time as is written above, and in our countries it is customary for stars to appear before the completion of the measure of 4 milin — one cannot perform melacha on erev Shabbat until the completion of the time explained in Shulchan Aruch even per the view of Rabbenu Tam, for the day has already darkened at that time and it is night or at least safek lailah; rather he should refrain at least half an hour [which includes the time of bein hashemashot and tosefet Shabbat, as is written in Sefer Chayei Adam] before the time when it is customary for stars to appear in that place. And all this is per the view of Rabbenu Tam; but we already wrote above that per the view of the Geonim one must refrain from melacha immediately after sunset when bein hashemashot begins:

(7) To advance, etc. — behold according to what the Mechaber wrote below in siman 459 seif 2 it is proven that he holds that 12 seasonal hours of the day are counted from alot hashachar until tzeit hakochavim, and plag haminchah is an hour and a quarter before tzeit hakochavim; if so there is nothing between the words of Rama and the earlier [view] except one-twentieth of the hour, for 4 milin are an hour and a fifth per the view of the Mechaber there, and from plag haminchah until night is an hour and a quarter; and it is known that a quarter exceeds a fifth only in twentieths. Afterward I found in Levush in siman 267 who raised this in any case, and see there that his actual view is that plag haminchah is an hour and a quarter before sunset; and see in Mishna Berurah:
