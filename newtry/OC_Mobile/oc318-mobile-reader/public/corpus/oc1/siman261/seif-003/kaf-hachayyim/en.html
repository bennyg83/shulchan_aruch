Although we maintain the opinion that rabbinic prohibitions were not imposed during twilight, as stated in siman 342, Beis Yosef writes that this applies only for the sake of a mitzvah or when one is preoccupied and hurried. He therefore writes that during twilight one may tell a non-Jew to perform any labor needed for a mitzvah or about which one is preoccupied and hurried; see there. Taz, paragraph 1, cites this and writes that accordingly, if one needs a utensil on Shabbos and has no other, he may immerse it during twilight. There is, however, another permitted course: giving it to a non-Jew as a gift, as stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 120, seif 16. We should therefore not permit the rabbinic prohibition even during twilight. These are his words. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, writes the same. He may likewise immerse it through a subterfuge, such as drawing water with it for drinking or another need, as stated below in siman 323, seif 7; see there. If it is a dirty knife, he may immerse it while washing the knife, which is also a permitted subterfuge. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 3. See below, paragraph 10.
