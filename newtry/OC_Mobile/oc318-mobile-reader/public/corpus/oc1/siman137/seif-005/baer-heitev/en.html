The Sefer Torah. If they have not yet rolled the scroll closed, he returns and reads the remaining verses and recites the concluding blessing, but not the opening blessing, as stated in seif katan 5 in the name of Taz.
The parashah. Peri Chadash disagrees and rules that they should begin from the place where they stopped.
And afterward. If one verse was omitted from Parashas Parah, they need not return and read it, since that parashah had already been read. Olas Tamid and Magen Avraham. Maharam of Lublin, siman 85, writes concerning a chazzan who on the first day of Chol HaMoed Sukkos read for maftir the reading of the second day, rolled the Sefer Torah closed, and did not read the passage of the third day. He ruled that they should reopen the Sefer Torah and read aloud from “And on the third day” until the end, without blessings before or after. He concludes that one who instructs them to recite blessings has not lost. See there. Magen Avraham, Peri Chadash, and Yad Aharon disagree and rule unequivocally that blessings must be recited before and after. See above, seif katan 4.
