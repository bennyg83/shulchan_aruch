[5] He returns and reads, etc. — with two verses with him, etc. — so Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot. And Maharam of Lublin wrote in responsum siman 88, and this is his language: since it is not explained in Hagahot Maimoniyot that he returns and blesses, it could be he returns and reads without a blessing — and so the wording implies, until here his language. Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah and Olat Tamid brought this to decide halachah; but Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah wrote: I do not know what is different about Parashat Parah at the end of this siman, until here. And in Olat Tamid he forced a distinction; and in Eliyahu Zuta I wondered that it escaped all the great ones mentioned above — Massekhet Soferim chapter 21 halachah 4, this is its language: he returns, opens, blesses, and reads — he and two others, until here — behold it is explicit that he blesses. And it is more astonishing that they only saw Beit Yosef and did not examine Hagahot Maimoniyot itself, which wrote explicitly chapter 12 of Laws of Prayer that he blesses, and Beit Yosef copied briefly. And it appears to me he truly relied on Parashat Parah at the end of this siman that one blesses; and now what Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah wondered in note 1 — this is its language: wonder on Hagahot Maimoniyot, from where that he reads with two others? In Massekhet Soferim this is not mentioned, until here his language — meaning he also only saw chapter 1 of Massekhet Soferim and not chapter 21, where it teaches explicitly with two others; and this is a novel matter on a great collection like him. Also what Olat Tamid left in "requires study for practice" regarding skipping three verses and whether one blesses — see there; he is certainly mistaken, for even for one verse one blesses; and it is obvious that even if seven men read and each three verses without the skipped verse, he returns and reads.
