[5] He returns and reads, etc. Meaning, the omitted verse together with two other verses, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos. Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 88, states: “Since Hagahos Maimoniyos does not explicitly say that he returns and recites the blessings, it is possible that he returns and reads without a blessing. The wording also implies this.” Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah and Olas Tamid cite this as practical halachah. Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah adds: “I do not know why this differs from Parashas Parah at the end of this siman.” Olas Tamid strains to distinguish the cases. In Eliyahu Zuta I expressed astonishment that all these great authorities overlooked Maseches Soferim, chapter 21, halachah 4, which states explicitly: “He returns, opens the Sefer Torah, recites the blessing, and reads, he and two others.” It is explicit that he recites a blessing. It is even more astonishing that they looked only in Beit Yosef and did not examine Hagahos Maimoniyos itself, which explicitly states in chapter 12 of Hilchos Tefillah that he recites a blessing. Beit Yosef merely cited it in abbreviated form. It appears to me that he indeed relied on the law of Parashas Parah at the end of this siman, where it is stated that he recites a blessing. Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah, note 1, writes: “It is astonishing that Hagahos Maimoniyos requires him to read with two others. What is his source, when Maseches Soferim does not mention this?” He too apparently saw only chapter 1 of Maseches Soferim and not chapter 21, where it explicitly teaches that he reads with two others. This is remarkable for such a great collector of sources. Olas Tamid likewise leaves the practical law requiring study where three verses were omitted and whether he recites a blessing. This is certainly an error, for even when one verse was omitted he recites a blessing. It is also obvious that even if seven men read and each read three verses without the omitted verse, he must return and read.
