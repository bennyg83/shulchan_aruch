[1] And whichever of them reads four, etc. The Perishah writes: This applies specifically in their time, when each reader read for himself. Nowadays, when the chazzan reads, he should give four verses either to the first or to the last reader, but not to a middle reader, so as not to embarrass the others. These are the words of Maharshal. I have cited his words to exclude the later authorities who did not quote Maharshal accurately. Bach writes: I do not know Maharshal’s source for making this distinction. It appears to me, however, from Rashi’s commentary to Megillah 21a, where he writes: “If the first did not do so and the second did, or if the second also did not and the third did, he is praiseworthy.” This implies that ideally the first should read four. Accordingly, nowadays, when the chazzan reads, he should not embarrass the first reader. Likewise, if he did not give four to the first, he should give them to the second and not to the third, because the second has the same distinguished status as the first. Know also that Rashi explains there that if the passage is long enough and they read four verses for each reader, they are all praiseworthy. Perishah writes that this proves that when two parashiyos are joined, the kohen should also read from both parashiyos. Those who say that one should never alter the first parashah are mistaken, for why should the first reader, the kohen, receive a diminished portion? Maharam Mintz, siman 85, writes that the custom is for one reader to read the end of the first parashah and the beginning of the second together when two parashiyos are read, so that it appears as one continuous parashah.
[2] And he read, etc., ten verses, etc. Olas Tamid writes that ten is not exact, for the same law applies if he read only nine, since he has fulfilled his obligation as stated in seif 3. It is possible to distinguish that reading with omissions is worse, and here ten verses are required. This is also implied by Magen Avraham.
