Such as Parashas Amalek. Beit Yosef cites Orchos Chaim: They did not add verses to it from above or below because the Holy One, blessed is He, said: Amalek caused My Name and My throne to be incomplete, so too his parashah is incomplete. The Yerushalmi says: He committed a truncated act; therefore his parashah is truncated. One may ask why they did not answer simply that the subject concludes there and the passage contains only nine verses, as Tosafos writes. The answer is that these reasons explain why they did not add verses from above or below. Tosafos, however, asks that even if there is a sound reason, this still contradicts the Mishnah’s rule that fewer than ten verses are not read, which implies in every case. If so, the Mishnah should have said, “except for Parashas Amalek.” Tosafos therefore answers that the Mishnah’s rule applies only where the subject has not concluded. Parashas Amalek is not included in the rule, because the subject concludes there, and it was therefore unnecessary to state an exception. Although regarding the haftarah Tosafos writes there that when the subject concludes one may read fewer than twenty-one verses, and there one does not add even though the Yerushalmi’s reason does not apply, one must say that haftarah is different. Its blessing relates only to the subject of that parashah, so how could one read it together with verses that concern another subject? This does not apply to Torah reading.
