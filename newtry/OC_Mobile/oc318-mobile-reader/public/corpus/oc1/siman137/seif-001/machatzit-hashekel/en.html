Note 1: And one reads, etc. — so as not to embarrass the first and last — such is the text in Bach in the name of Rashal; but Bach challenged him: from where? But Rashal said so from reasoning: granted if the chazzan reads four for the first or last, there is no embarrassment for others, for it is known to all that the first aliyah has superiority from its being first, as Magen Avraham wrote in the small seif after this; therefore reading four for the first is not honor for himself but for his aliyah which is important; and similarly no embarrassment for others. And when reading four for the last, the last's superiority is also known to all, as Magen Avraham wrote afterward; therefore no honor and no embarrassment for the oleh, as above. But the middle one's superiority is as we find in the Menorah, as Magen Avraham wrote in the note after — this is not so known to the world, and they will suspect the chazzan who called the second for four because of the oleh's own honor, and there is embarrassment for others; not so in Talmudic times when the oleh himself read — if he read two [aliyot of] four verses, there is no embarrassment for the last that the second made honor for himself by reading four verses.
