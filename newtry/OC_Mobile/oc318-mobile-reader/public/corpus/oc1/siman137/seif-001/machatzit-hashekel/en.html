Seif katan 1, "and one reads," etc. "So as not to shame the first and the last" is the text in Bach in the name of Maharshal, although Bach questioned its source. It appears that Maharshal said this as a matter of reasoning. If the chazzan reads 4 verses for the first or the last reader, there is no embarrassment to the others, because everyone knows that the first aliyah possesses importance by virtue of being first, as Magen Avraham writes in the following seif katan. Thus, reading 4 verses for the first is understood as honor for the aliyah itself, not personal honor for the reader. The same is true of the last aliyah, whose distinction is also generally known. The distinction of the middle reader, however, which Magen Avraham relates to the arrangement of the menorah, is not as widely known. People may therefore suspect that the chazzan gave the second reader 4 verses because of that reader's personal honor, which could embarrass the others. In Talmudic times, when the oleh himself read, this concern did not arise in the same way: if the second reader chose to read 4 verses, the last reader was not embarrassed merely because the second person honored himself by reading more.
