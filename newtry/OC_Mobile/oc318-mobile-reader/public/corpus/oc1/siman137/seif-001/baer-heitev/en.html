Shelah writes the pairing of day and night in Torah or prayer, morning and evening. Immediately upon awakening from sleep one washes hands and thereby avoids [ruining] hand-washing after bathroom. Certainly if he does not wish to sleep even while still lying down (Shelah). Tololas Yaakov cites Zohar: one who walks four cubits in the morning without washing hands is liable for death—so rule decisively. BaCH wondered at Rabbeinu Beis Yosef for not writing this; apparently most cannot be careful in this and better they err unknowingly than knowingly, therefore he did not write it; Kenesses HaGedolah likewise for netilas yadayim; see Yad Aharon siman 4 in Hagahos Tur.

He should picture the four-letter Name constantly before his eyes as Y-H-V-H—this is the inner meaning of “I have set the Lord before me,” and it yields great benefit for awe (Arizal).

Nevertheless he should not quarrel with them (Beis Yosef).

Not so; rather pause briefly and do not stand up suddenly (Gitin 70a). Seder HaYom writes: upon rising say Modeh Ani before You, living and enduring King, who has mercifully restored my soul—hand-washing is not required for this even if his hands are dirty, for he mentions neither the Name nor an appellation; nevertheless for learning Torah it appears forbidden (Eliyahu Zuta). After washing hands he says the forty-two-letter name Ana Bechoach.
