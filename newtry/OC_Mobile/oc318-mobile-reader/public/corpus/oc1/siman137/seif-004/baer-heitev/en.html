And to read. Taz concludes that if they remembered after the concluding blessing and the chazzan has not yet called another reader, the one who omitted one verse should read one additional verse. He should not recite the opening blessing, but should complete that verse and then recite the concluding blessing again. If they did not remember until after another person had already read, the first reader withdraws and the second reader begins with the opening blessing. He begins again from the place where the previous reader began and adds as many verses as he wishes. It follows that all the verses had blessings recited before and after them, except that the first reader’s reading of two verses counts for nothing. Eliyah Rabbah disagrees with Taz; see there. Magen Avraham writes that on a weekday the first reader should read three verses again, because one may not add to the prescribed number of readers. On Shabbos, however, another person should read three verses from that point onward. The same applies on Yom Tov, since some authorities permit adding readers, and therefore the first person should not read again. Responsa Panim Meiros, siman 94, agrees.
And to read. If the second reader repeated one verse from the earlier passage, he has fulfilled his obligation according to Magen Avraham. Olas Tamid does not write this. See there.
