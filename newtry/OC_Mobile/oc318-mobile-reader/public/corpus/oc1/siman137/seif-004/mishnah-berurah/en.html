(12) Two verses. Even if ten verses were read altogether, such as when the two preceding readers each read four, he must nevertheless return and read, because a reading of fewer than three verses is not significant. Taz writes that the same applies at Shacharis on Shabbos, even after seven readers have read as required. If one person read only two verses, he must return for the same reason.
(13) He must return and read. Regarding the opening blessing, the law is as follows. If he has not yet recited the concluding blessing, then even if the Sefer Torah was already rolled and he intended to recite the blessing, but they remembered that he read only two verses, he returns and reads without another opening blessing. If he already recited the concluding blessing, the first reading has completely ended. He must therefore recite the opening blessing again and begin from the place where he began the previous reading, because those two verses did not count at all, and read one additional verse to complete three. If the two preceding readers each read only three verses, he must read four verses so that the total reaches ten. Even if the next person was already called and ascended to the bimah, that person should remain there while the first recites the blessing again, reads the three verses as explained, and recites the concluding blessing; afterward the next person reads. If the second person already began the blessing, or completed the entire blessing, before they remembered, he should begin from where the previous person began, including the same two earlier verses, complete one additional verse, and recite the concluding blessing. Some say that in addition to the two earlier verses he should read three more. The first person should remain there until the second finishes. Afterward, the first person who had read two verses recites the blessing and continues from where the second stopped. If the first already descended from the bimah before they remembered, another person may be called to complete the three readers. This applies when there was no kohen in the beis midrash and Yisraelim were called. But if this happened to a kohen who read only two verses, and then the levi recited his blessing, and they remembered that the kohen erred, the levi should begin only from where the kohen stopped and read three verses from there onward. He should not repeat the first two verses read by the kohen, because on a weekday the reading is valid even if the beginning of the portion is omitted, and repeating from the beginning would cast aspersion on the kohanim present. After the levi, two Yisraelim should be called to complete the prescribed number, because the first kohen who erred cannot be called after the levi, since a kohen may not read after a levi. The reading of only two verses is considered nonexistent. If this occurred at Shacharis on Shabbos or Yom Tov, where one of the readers read two verses and recited the concluding blessing before the mistake was noticed, some say it is preferable that he not read again himself but that another person be called. On a weekday, since one may not add to the prescribed number of readers, calling another person in his place would appear as though four people were called. On Shabbos, where additions are permitted, it is better to call another person than for the same person to read twice from one Sefer Torah, which should not initially be done, as explained in siman 282. The new reader begins from where the first began, as above, and they complete seven readers besides the one who read only two verses. Others say that even on Shabbos there is no need to call another person.
(14) Parashas Amalek. The entire portion contains only nine verses. Since nine are read there initially, on other days nine verses at least suffice after the fact, and ten are required only for the preferred fulfillment. Biur HaGra and Responsa Panim Meiros disagree and hold that ten verses are indispensable; if fewer were read, he returns, recites a blessing, and reads. It appears that even according to the first opinion, it is proper for the congregation to read another three verses without a blessing.
(15) Fewer than nine. Meaning, each person read three verses, but the total was only eight, such as when the third repeated one verse already read by the second. Even so, they did not fulfill the obligation. If the third had read only two verses, the beginning of the seif already taught that they did not fulfill it. Some say that they did fulfill the obligation after the fact, since each person read at least three verses.
(16) They must return and read. Meaning, the person who read improperly. The plural word “they must” is not exact.
