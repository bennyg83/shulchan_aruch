[4] One verse he returns, etc. — it implies that if he did not skip an entire verse he need not return, and specifically if he already finished reading; but if the Torah scroll is still before him, he should return and read that verse — so wrote Olat Tamid below end of siman 282, and it requires study for practical law. Also some proof from Keriat Shema above siman 64 seif 2, that the law of skipping one word is like skipping an entire verse, as Levush wrote; and although in Tur and Shulchan Aruch there only an entire verse is mentioned, perforce one must say one word is the same, as Tosafot and Rosh wrote in Berachot 15a. Also it appears to me to learn from there: if he skipped a word he need not return and read from the start of the verse but from that word, as Levush wrote there and Lechem Chamudot 14a, until here; and the same applies when an error is found in a Torah scroll; see Beit Yosef siman 104; and so the wording implies in siman 143 seif 4: they begin from the place where the error was found, etc.
