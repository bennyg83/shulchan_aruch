Or — the Taz in Beit Yosef proved from ma'amad people: at the beginning five verses were skipped and they did not enact concern for those entering and leaving to read the first all and second all; rather where possible it is good to read at least twice anew, and we are not concerned for those entering and leaving. The same applies to Rosh Chodesh — he spoke eight verses; Levi reads twice anew, for it is impossible he read thrice anew — two aliyot remain at the end of the portion, and concern of those leaving we are more concerned about than those entering; therefore the Mechaber wrote where it is not possible, two suffice. Behold where it is not possible, it suffices with what the first read entirely — like the fruit of the festival; and the first part is unnecessary except that for the fruit of the festival it is not possible on the fourth, which is in honor of the festival, and requires specifically safeik yoma (not below on the fifth day — see letter 4) — impossible entirely; not so Rosh Chodesh, where it is somewhat possible, and therefore he reads twice anew; and it teaches that on Rosh Chodesh one does not read exactly what the first read, etc. — Magen Avraham 12.
