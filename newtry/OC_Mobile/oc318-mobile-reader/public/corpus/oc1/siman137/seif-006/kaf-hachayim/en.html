(6) There: and he read ten verses, etc. — it appears ten is not precise; similarly if he read only nine verses he has fulfilled, as in seif 4; only here it mentions ten because of the end: on Shabbat even if he read twenty-one verses he does not fulfill if he skipped one verse — Orach Torah note 5; and so Peri Chadash note 3; and so Magen Avraham note 4, except Magen Avraham returned and wrote: possibly when he skipped in the middle, fewer than ten does not suffice; and so Sefer A"R note 2; and so Levush in seif 5. Therefore, since there is a dispute and it is difficult, one may be lenient even against Mechaber; and there is another dispute: from Mechaber's wording it implies this return when he skipped and did not read ten verses means he returns and reads the skipped verse with two verses — as if he skipped on Shabbat; and per Rambam chapter 11 halachah 6 he must return and read three in order, as Mishnat Hasidim note 3; one may rely on the lenient authorities who wrote they fulfill with this reading — sitting and standing is preferable.
