6) There: And he read ten verses, etc. It appears that 'ten' is not exact, for the same applies if he read only nine verses, that he too fulfilled, as stated in seif 4; rather, he mentioned here ten verses on account of the latter clause, that on Shabbos, even if he read twenty-one verses, he did not fulfill if he skipped one verse. Olas Tamid, ot 5; and so appears the view of the Pri Chadash, ot 3; and so wrote the Magen Avraham, s.k. 4, only that the Magen Avraham then returned and wrote: possibly when he skipped in the middle, fewer than ten do not suffice. And so wrote the Eliyah Rabbah, ot 2; and so wrote the Levush in seif 5. And therefore, since there is a dispute in this, and the rule is 'safek berachos lehakel' even against Maran, of blessed memory; and further, there is another dispute, for from the plain reading of the words of Maran, of blessed memory, it is implied that this returning, when he skipped and did not read ten verses, means that he returns and reads the skipped verse with two verses, just as when he skipped on Shabbos; and according to Maseches Soferim, chapter 11, halachah 6, one must return and read three olim in order, as the Mishbetzos Zahav wrote, ot 3: one may rely on those who are lenient, who wrote that they fulfilled with this reading, for 'sit and do nothing' is preferable.
