(19) The first. This applies only after the fact. Initially, he should not read backward from verses previously read, even if he intends to add many verses afterward. Nevertheless, some have the custom in Parashas Bechukosai that the person called for the tochachah begins three verses earlier, so as to begin with a positive matter, even though the third reader already read those verses.
(20) Three verses. Where possible.
(21) Or even two. Meaning, he adds two verses to one verse that he repeats from the previous reader, as we do on Rosh Chodesh, where the levi begins with a verse read by the kohen and adds only two verses, because no other arrangement is possible, as will be explained in the laws of Rosh Chodesh.
(22) Where it is impossible. Meaning, it is impossible to read three, but only two, because the subject ends. If no repetition at all were possible, it would resemble the case of the Sukkos offerings.
(23) And if not. Meaning, he did not read three verses where it was possible to read three, or did not read two where it was at least possible to read two.
(24) It does not count toward the prescribed number. This wording implies that there is no prohibition against repeating what the previous reader read, but it does not count toward the prescribed number. This follows the Mechaber's view in siman 282, seif 2. See the Rama there, who disagrees.
(25) From the Sukkos offerings. On Chol HaMoed Sukkos, the fourth reader repeats what the kohen and levi read because it is impossible to read beforehand from material unrelated to that day.
