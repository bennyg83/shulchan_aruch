It does not. This implies that it is permitted, but does not count toward the prescribed number. See siman 282, seif 2. This requires study, for when Parashas Shekalim falls in Parashas Tetzaveh, the Gemara states that six people read from “And you shall command” through “And you shall make a laver,” and the maftir repeats from “When you take” through “And you shall make.” Nevertheless, the maftir counts toward the prescribed number, and so writes the Tur in siman 685. One cannot answer that there too it is because no alternative is possible, for it is possible to add another reader so that the maftir is not included in the prescribed number. It is also possible for the earlier reader to continue three verses beyond “When you take,” and for the seventh reader to repeat from “When you take” and add beyond what the first read. One may answer that the Parashas Shekalim reading must stand alone and not include anything beyond it. Nevertheless, it remains difficult: the sixth reader could read until three verses after “When you take,” and the seventh could repeat from “When you take” and add beyond what the first read. One may answer that if so, people would say the weekly portion ends at “for your souls.” But now, since the maftir adds nothing and such repetition is ordinarily prohibited, it is evident that it is done for Parashas Shekalim. Perhaps for this reason he need not add. This requires study.
