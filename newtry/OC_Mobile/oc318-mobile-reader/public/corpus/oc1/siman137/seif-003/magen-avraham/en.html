Return and read. It is written in the Old Testament, that the Holy One is called a man without a single verse, and it is written that he should be blessed as a stranger and read:

(4) And two with him — because they do not read Torah with fewer than three aliyot. Rabbi Meir Lublin siman 85 wrote: the shatz read on Shabbat day 1 of chol hamoed Sukkot and called maftir; day 2 he rolled the scroll and did not read; day 3 he had already blessed after it, and I instructed to return and open the Torah scroll and the shatz read aloud on day 3 until the end without blessing before or after, since on day 3 it is only because of doubt they do not bless, as we find in Sukkah in Shulchan Aruch — blessings we do not bless; and even though for forgetting parshat haParah they bless — parshat haParah is unique as d'oraisa (see siman 685); and even though we are expert in calendar fixation, nevertheless they were not stringent on festival readings as Turei Zahav siman 24 wrote; and even if Turei Zahav only meant other festival portions, additional offerings are the day's obligation — he holds one returns if skipped; nevertheless since he wrote anonymously it implies he does not distinguish; and on Shabbat where Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote one returns if skipped, it may be he reads without blessing — from all these reasons I instructed to read without blessing; nevertheless one who teaches to bless did not lose. The Rabbi did not see in Yerushalmi end that if one skipped one verse on Shabbat he returns, opens, blesses, and reads — see there; but on additional festival portions there is room to question. Since Turei Zahav wrote anonymously it implies no distinction — as if he only saw Beit Yosef and not the body of the law in Turei Zahav that deals with what is written in several places about Shirat HaYam and the Ten Commandments with the shatz when they do not hear from the shatz, etc., and discussed this; and on this he wrote but on seven days of Pesach and Shavuot there is not such stringency since they already read the portions on their Shabbats — like Monday and Thursday; if so Turei Zahav did not come to teach the law of festivals but incidentally, and we find nothing to infer from his anonymity. Tosafot Megillah 23 wrote: if on Yom Tov one read the whole minyan and forgot to read the day's obligation, he returns to the scroll and another reads the day's obligation, and the last who read before is as if he does not exist. It appears to me that since he wrote anonymously he holds on all festivals one returns — not only on the festival where the Gemara's law is to read festival offerings, but even on other festivals where R' Akiva's enactment is to read the day's offerings as Tosafot and Rosh wrote there, and so Beit Yosef siman 282 in the name of Shlah — still they must be read, for what harm if read immediately or later — it does not cause another blessing; but if one skipped one verse he does not return, for he is no worse than the festival portion itself mentioned in the Gemara which nevertheless does not require return — all the more so additional offerings. And now in our case where he skipped, on day 3 it is as if he did not read at all, lest today be day 3; especially for us who are expert in calendar fixation that it is certain. And what he wrote that because of doubt we do not bless — requires further study, for every second day of Yom Tov is doubtful and we bless on kiddush and havdalah many blessings since the Sages enacted — see Tosafot Sukkah 44 and Shabbat 23. And what we do not bless in Shulchan Aruch on Sukkah is as Maharil wrote — lest violate bal tosif, or lest belittle Yom Tov as Tosafot on lulav — therefore it seems simple he should bless as Tosafot, see Levush.
