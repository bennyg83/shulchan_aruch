[3] And if not, etc. Maseches Soferim, at the end of chapter 11, states: “He must return and read ten verses properly, in their order, without skipping.” Although if he read ten verses even with omissions it suffices after the fact, whenever he did not read ten verses he must return and read them in order. Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah writes likewise. This now resolves seif 3, which states that if they read fewer than nine verses they must return. Since he must return and read those two verses whose following blessing was not recited because of the omission discussed above, they therefore read all ten verses. Magen Avraham strained to explain that the phrase “they must return” refers to the one who read fewer than three verses, but he did not arrive at this explanation. Perhaps this is precisely what the poskim came to teach. See seif katan 3 [2] and examine carefully. If, however, the third reader read only two verses, he should read another two verses from the place where they began.
[4] He must return because of one verse, etc. This implies that if he did not omit an entire verse, he need not return. This applies specifically if he already finished reading. If the Sefer Torah is still before him, he should return and read that verse. Olas Tamid writes this below at the end of siman 282, but it requires study as practical halachah. There is also some proof from Krias Shema above, siman 64, seif 2, where the law of omitting one word is like that of omitting an entire verse, as Levush writes. Although the Tur and Shulchan Aruch there mention only one verse, one must necessarily say that the same applies to one word, as Tosafos and Rosh write in Berachos 15a. It also appears to me from there that if he omitted one word, he need not return to the beginning of the verse, but only to that word, as Levush writes there and Lechem Chamudos, folio 14. The same applies when an error is found in a Sefer Torah. See Beit Yosef, siman 104. This is likewise implied by the wording in siman 143, seif 4: “They begin from the place where the error was found,” etc.
[5] He returns and reads, etc. Meaning, the omitted verse together with two other verses, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos. Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 88, states: “Since Hagahos Maimoniyos does not explicitly say that he returns and recites the blessings, it is possible that he returns and reads without a blessing. The wording also implies this.” Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah and Olas Tamid cite this as practical halachah. Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah adds: “I do not know why this differs from Parashas Parah at the end of this siman.” Olas Tamid strains to distinguish the cases. In Eliyahu Zuta I expressed astonishment that all these great authorities overlooked Maseches Soferim, chapter 21, halachah 4, which states explicitly: “He returns, opens the Sefer Torah, recites the blessing, and reads, he and two others.” It is explicit that he recites a blessing. It is even more astonishing that they looked only in Beit Yosef and did not examine Hagahos Maimoniyos itself, which explicitly states in chapter 12 of Hilchos Tefillah that he recites a blessing. Beit Yosef merely cited it in abbreviated form. It appears to me that he indeed relied on the law of Parashas Parah at the end of this siman, where it is stated that he recites a blessing. Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah, note 1, writes: “It is astonishing that Hagahos Maimoniyos requires him to read with two others. What is his source, when Maseches Soferim does not mention this?” He too apparently saw only chapter 1 of Maseches Soferim and not chapter 21, where it explicitly teaches that he reads with two others. This is remarkable for such a great collector of sources. Olas Tamid likewise leaves the practical law requiring study where three verses were omitted and whether he recites a blessing. This is certainly an error, for even when one verse was omitted he recites a blessing. It is also obvious that even if seven men read and each read three verses without the omitted verse, he must return and read.
