Ten verses. This is not exact, for the same law applies if they read only nine verses; they have likewise fulfilled their obligation, as the Mechaber writes in seif 4. It is possible, however, that where a verse was omitted in the middle, fewer than ten do not suffice. Magen Avraham.
One verse. Even if twenty-one verses were read for the seven readers without the omitted verse, they must nevertheless return and read it. See Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah and Peri Chadash.
And he reads. He recites the Torah blessings. For this reason he reads the omitted verse together with two additional verses, because a reading over which blessings are recited must contain three verses. Magen Avraham and Taz. Peri Chadash likewise rules that he must recite the blessings before and after, unlike Maharam of Lublin. See Magen Avraham and seif katan 9. Olas Tamid agrees with Maharam of Lublin that he returns and reads without a blessing before or after. See there. See also Yad Aharon, Bnei Chiya, and Meil Shmuel, siman 2. Eliyah Rabbah likewise rules that he returns and recites the blessings, unlike Maharam of Lublin. See there.
