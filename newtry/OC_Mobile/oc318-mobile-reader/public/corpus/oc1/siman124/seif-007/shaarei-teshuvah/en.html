To be silent — Baer Heitev. See in the responsa of the Rosh: those who speak along with the prayer leader in the middle of a blessing — even though there is no blessing in vain here, since they do not open and close with “Baruch,” and there are also nine who listen — nevertheless it appears like irreverence (kalut rosh). And it was written in Shulchan Arukh, in the name of a statement of Mordechai, that what is written in Shulchan Arukh that one should be silent means that he should not speak idle words or things that are not part of the eighteen [blessings]. Also, there is no prohibition of irreverence except for those who raise their voices; but in a whisper it seems preferable, for one concentrates more on what the cantor said. From the words of Bach and Eliyahu Rabba it appears that even in a whisper there is cause for concern — and he does not hold thus; see there. It seems proper to conduct according to Bach and Eliyahu Rabba, that one should be concerned that in the flow of speech one may also come to recite the blessing and it will be a blessing in vain. Some conduct on Musaf of Rosh Hashanah, in the verses of Malchuyot, Zichronot, and Shofarot, to say the verses in a whisper along with the prayer leader, so as to arouse themselves in intention of the heart — and one should not protest them. What was written in the name of Ram"a of Fano in Shulchan Arukh regarding the first generations about this, and especially one who knows the secrets of the matter, etc. The people who approach God conducted like the custom of the early pious ones, to have the siddur open before them during repetition of the prayer and to intend each word; see there. So too in Shelah.
