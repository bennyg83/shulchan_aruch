(39) In any matter. This wording implies that even if he omitted Mashiv Haruach, which belongs to the first three blessings, this rule applies. So is evident from Or Zarua, siman 460. See above, siman 114, Shaarei Teshuvah seif katan 8, and below, siman 126, seif 4, together with the Mishnah Berurah there in the name of Eliyah Rabbah and the Gra.
(40) He should concentrate. Although the established law is that the chazzan discharges only one who is not proficient, this case is different because the person did pray but merely forgot an insertion. The chazzan therefore discharges him even though he is proficient. The later authorities write that it is preferable for him to repeat the Amidah himself, because nowadays not everyone can concentrate on hearing the chazzan from beginning to end. His attention may wander and he may fail to hear words indispensable to the Amidah. Bach and Eliyah Rabbah write that if the chazzan habitually swallows words, one may not rely on him as a matter of law and must pray independently.
(41) From beginning to end. Birkei Yosef concludes that he should not recite Modim D’Rabbanan then, but should concentrate on hearing the Modim recited by the chazzan. Nowadays, since some chazzanim recite the beginning of Modim quietly, one cannot fulfill his Amidah through the chazzan. I do not know the basis of this custom. Although the congregation recites Modim D’Rabbanan then, the repetition was instituted to discharge one who is not proficient, and the chazzan must at least recite it loudly enough for ten people standing around him to hear.
