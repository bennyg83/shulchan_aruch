(11) "and before the response of amen," etc. — see in Mishna Berurah in what he wrote in the name of Taz; and his reasoning is that in every blesser the respondent must wait until the blesser finishes, and here the response of amen of those who heard the blessing is like an extended blessing, and properly he may answer amen immediately after they finish; also amen. And in the novellae of Ra'ah he wrote on this, and this is his language: and it appears to me that there is no difference—that the author also holds that if one heard the blessing and did not answer amen until the entire congregation finished responding, one may answer amen; only in the author's law it deals with one who is still engaged in his prayer when the chazan finished the blessing, and it is not reasonable to answer amen, for he interrupted with speech between the end of the blessing and the response of amen; and from this Teshuvat haD learned this from the passage in Berachos that if one finished his prayer before the response of amen from the majority of the congregation, one may answer, since the congregation's response of amen is included in the end of the blessing, as if the prayer leader now finished the blessing and did not interrupt with speech; not so because of the minority who prolong, for that is not called the end of the blessing, and automatically there is an interruption in what he interrupted in his prayer—end of his words. And it is difficult for me according to him: at least if one finished his prayer together with the majority of the congregation, it should also be permitted to answer amen immediately, for again there was no interruption between the congregation's response of amen, which is the end of the blessing, and his amen; and why do we require that he finish his prayer before the majority of the congregation finishes responding amen? Perhaps because it is impossible to be precise and it is strained. And in the book Magen Gibborim he wrote another answer: because the prayer leader presumably does not wait except until the majority of the congregation responds amen, and not for those who prolong excessively, as above; and afterward he begins the next blessing, and automatically he can no longer answer amen on the previous blessing, for it has already passed; and with this my above difficulty is also resolved. And behold, per the Gra and Magen Gibborim, also Kaddish and Kedushah and Barchu—if one entered the synagogue and found the congregation saying Kaddish and Kedushah and Barchu, even though the majority of the congregation already said it and only the minority are finishing saying it, nevertheless one may say it with them; and with this the difficulty of Peri Megadim above in siman 56 seif 1 in the gloss is removed, where he wrote there that one who comes to the synagogue and hears the congregation responding Kaddish, etc., and does not distinguish there any difference between whether one heard the majority of the congregation or only the minority; while here the Rama was silent toward the author, it implies that he agrees with his law; but according to their reasons it is resolved; and practically it requires further study; and therefore in Mishna Berurah note 46 I wrote that whenever the majority of the congregation has not finished, etc.
