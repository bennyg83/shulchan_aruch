From the mouth of the majority of the congregation, etc. In Piskei Maharai there, siman 119, he concluded, and these are his words: that answering amen is also part of the blessing, as it is found in chapter 3 of Berachot, where the Talmud says what is written “from the mouth of the majority of those answering,” etc., end quote — and I did not understand his words, except that in chapter 3 of Berachot, page 46, it is found thus regarding one who cuts the bread of hamotzi: he is not permitted to cut the bread until the blessing is finished first, as it is found in chapter 15, and Rashi brings it there — therefore Rava commanded his son there that he should not cut until the majority finish amen, because answering amen is also part of the blessing. Thus, if the majority finished, the minority is as one who does not exist, since the blessing has already finished. Not so regarding answering amen for one who did not hear the blessing but only heard amen — what do we care about the majority? Even if all of them finished, why should he not say amen after them? For in every blesser, the one answering must wait until the blesser finishes — and here, the answering of amen of those who heard the blessing is like an extended blessing; he may properly say amen after their conclusion and answer amen. This is clear from the words of Achai Shelot that Beit Yosef and Rama brought afterward, and these are his words: one who hears many of Israel saying amen — their presumption is that they only answer amen after they heard the blessing, and one is not concerned for orphaned amen, etc.; but if he hears an individual who said amen and did not hear the blessing, there is concern for orphaned amen and he is not permitted to answer amen with him, end quote. Thus, even after an individual answering amen, if not for concern of orphaned amen he would answer with him — and here, where he hears from the congregation, even if the majority finished, nevertheless he may answer amen, whether after he heard the conclusion from the majority or after the remaining minority, since there is no concern here for orphaned amen; for also those who began with the rest of the congregation to answer amen may properly answer amen after he hears the word amen from them, as answering after the blessing, per my humble opinion.
