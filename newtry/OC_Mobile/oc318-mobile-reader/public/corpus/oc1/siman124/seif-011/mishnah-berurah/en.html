(מב) "and before it finished" — that is, although it was explained in seif 8 in the gloss that one must answer amen immediately when the blessing finished, for otherwise it is like answering amen without a blessing at all; nevertheless here, if the majority of the congregation has not yet finished amen, it is fine, because answering amen is also part of the blessing, and when they answer amen it is as though they are now finishing the essence of the blessing. And Taz disagrees and ruled that even if the whole congregation finished answering, it is also permitted to answer if it was immediately after them; and in the chiddushim of R' Akiva Eiger and in the book Magen Giborim they agreed with Shulchan Aruch — and see in Biur Halacha.

(מג) "he finished," etc. — that is, after "may they be acceptable"; and he already said the supplications he is accustomed to in them, and as above in siman 122 and in Mishna Berurah skh"א.

(מד) "and even," etc. — this is also per the author, and he only innovated in what he wrote "and knows which blessing he is going to" — this is his view above in seif 8 in the gloss, that if he does not know which blessing it refers to, he is in the category of amen yetomah.

(מה) "one who hears the congregation" — the same law if he heard an individual answering amen and knows which blessing it refers to — he answers with him. And he cited congregation because the author deals regarding congregation; and although the author permits only before the majority finished amen, but the minority does not help — that is because minority relative to majority is as though it does not exist, or for other reasons as I wrote in Biur Halacha; but if there is no congregation at all but only an individual answers with him.

(מו) "and likewise in Kaddish," etc. — that is, that whoever has not finished, the majority of the congregation to answer — he answers with them even if he heard nothing from the mouth of the prayer leader. And see in siman 56 in Mishna Berurah skh"ט and in siman 57 skh"א, and see in Biur Halacha on the words "and before" at its end.
