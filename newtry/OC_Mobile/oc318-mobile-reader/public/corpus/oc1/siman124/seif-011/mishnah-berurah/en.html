(42) Before it has ended. This means that although seif 8 explains that amen must be answered immediately when the blessing ends, for otherwise it resembles answering amen without a blessing, here it is acceptable so long as most of the congregation has not yet finished answering amen. Answering amen is itself considered part of the blessing; when they answer, it is as though they are now completing the blessing itself. Taz disagrees and rules that even if the entire congregation has finished answering, one may still answer immediately after them. Rabbi Akiva Eiger and Magen Giborim agree with the Shulchan Aruch. See Biur Halachah.
(43) He finished, etc. This means after Yihyu Leratzon, and after he has recited the supplications he customarily says, as explained above in siman 122 and Mishnah Berurah seif katan 1.
(44) And even, etc. This too follows the Mechaber. Rama adds only the requirement that he know which blessing was recited, following his position above in seif 8 that if he does not know which blessing is being answered, it is an orphaned amen.
(45) He hears the congregation. The same applies if he hears an individual answer amen and knows which blessing is being answered; he answers with him. The Mechaber mentions a congregation because that is the case under discussion. Although the Mechaber permits answering only before most of the congregation has finished, and a minority is insufficient, that is because a minority relative to the majority is considered nonexistent, or for other reasons explained in Biur Halachah. If no congregation is present and only an individual answers, he may answer with him.
(46) Likewise for Kaddish, etc. So long as most of the congregation has not finished responding, he responds with them even if he heard nothing from the chazzan. See siman 56, Mishnah Berurah seif katan 9; siman 57, seif katan 1; and the conclusion of Biur Halachah, beginning “Before.”
