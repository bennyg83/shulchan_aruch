(ה) "There should be one," etc. — so wrote the Rama; and Olas Tamid wrote: it seems that what Shulchan Aruch wrote in seif 5, that if there are not nine intending it is close to being his blessing in vain, disagrees with this and it seems to him one is not enough; and one may wonder why the Rama did not write in the language of "some say." And Levush's words that one is enough when children answer amen does not seem correct, for we require specifically adults, as one should not be lenient regarding a blessing in vain, which is without teshuvah — so far. And according to this one wonders more on Levush, who wrote himself in seif 1 that we require nine as Shulchan Aruch wrote, and here wrote the Rama's words. In my humble opinion it is not difficult at all, for a blessing in vain does not apply here since the shaliach tzibur has not yet prayed quietly — and that they wrote "it is good," etc., implies by law it is not impeded even with one; and it also resolves what Levush wrote that children suffice — namely if there is no adult. And further, as I wrote above, they do not speak of an actual blessing in vain but that it is close. But it is difficult why Levush wrote here like the Rama from Tur that there should be one, etc., and below in siman 233 wrote that children answer and did not write it here. It seems to me according to what Maharil wrote — this is his wording: he commanded one of them who had not yet prayed, in order that he answer amen after the shaliach tzibur, or call one of the city's sons who already prayed — so far his wording. Perforce it deals when time is not so pressed that prayer time would pass if he does not begin now — for if so the other would also pray; therefore here Levush cited such as when they fear it will pass, etc., if so perhaps another will not pray. But in siman 233 he wrote: and if sometimes the hour is pressed, that it is very close to evening, etc., he wrote that children answer because all need to pray immediately; also there is no leisure to return after one already prayed.
