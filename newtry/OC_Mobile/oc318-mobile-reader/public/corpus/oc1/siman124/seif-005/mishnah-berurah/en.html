(21) He says, etc. — and if he is standing in a place where he may not interrupt, such as in Pesukei DeZimra, and all the more in the blessing of Kriat Shema — even between the paragraphs it is forbidden to say it. And likewise if he heard a blessing in which he is obligated and he intends to fulfill through the one blessing, such as the blessing on the shofar, megillah, kiddush, and the like — he should not answer Baruch Hu and Baruch Shem, for hearing is like answering, and it is as if he said the blessing himself, and it is an interruption in a blessing. See in Chayei Adam; a doubt remains regarding after the fact; and it appears that after the fact one should not be stringent about this — see in Sefer Magen Giborim what he wrote in the name of Maaseh Rokeach.
(22) Baruch Hu and Baruch Shem — and Scripture hints at this: "for the Name of Heaven I proclaim; give greatness to our Heaven" (Devarim 32:3). And further, if when they mention a righteous person of flesh and blood one must bless him, as it says "the memory of the righteous is for a blessing" — how much more so the Righteous One of the worlds. And blessings whose closing is short, only about two words, like "Who gives sight to the blind" in the morning blessing — and the like in the Shemoneh Esreh — for all blessings it is proper for the shaliach tzibur to beware then not to hurry so much to conclude the blessing immediately, but to pause a little so all who answer will hear which blessing he concludes afterward; and also they will be able to answer amen immediately — for if not, sometimes because of the voice of answering Baruch Hu and Baruch Shem many of those answering do not know which blessing he concludes, and also their answering is not immediately after the blessing finishes — and all this has concern of orphan amen, as below in seif 8 in the gloss.
