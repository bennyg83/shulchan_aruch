(5) And it is good, etc. — meaning when there is remaining time in the day; and although he wrote in note 4 that it refers to when they will not be able to finish, etc. — one must say that that one who answers after the chazzan has already prayed. And what Magen Avraham wrote — meaning when there is remaining time: the intention is not that that one who answers will have time to pray, for he has already prayed; rather meaning that what he wrote "and it is good that there be one," etc. implies it is only on the side of what is better and is not obligatory; and on this he wrote only when there is remaining time to invite one who has already prayed to call and answer amen. But if the hour is so pressed that in the time it would take to invite one who prayed to answer amen the time will pass, one need not do so but should pray even though there is no one to answer amen. And in Levush, chapter Tefillat HaShachar seif 16, and this is his language: but in these countries they do so — that the chazzan begins in a loud voice and says until HaEl HaKadosh and finishes all eighteen, and the congregation, when they answered amen to HaEl HaKadosh, pray afterward each one in a whisper.

And it appears to me that if there is no one who will answer amen on the chazzan's blessings, he should not finish his prayer in a loud voice but should finish it in a whisper. And again I found that Rama wrote in siman 124 in the name of Maharil that in a time of urgency, etc., and the congregation pray with him word for word, etc., and it is good that there be one, etc.; nevertheless it appears to me that what he wrote that the congregation should not begin until after HaEl HaKadosh — and perhaps Maharil also did not say so except when the hour is so pressed that if they do not begin immediately with the chazzan they will not be able to finish before all the time passes; and perhaps Magen Avraham hinted to this in note 4, where he wrote in the name of Levush "but without this it is better not to begin until after HaEl HaKadosh," etc., end of his words. And in Sefer A"R he wrote on this that it is impossible to explain Maharil's words thus, for he wrote and this is his language: he commanded one of them who had not yet prayed, in order that he answer amen after the chazzan, or call one of the townspeople who had already prayed, end of his words. And since he wrote first that he commanded one who had not yet prayed, therefore it does not refer to when the hour is so pressed — for otherwise at what time will that individual who answers pray? And he wrote: as it seems they practice not according to halachah, see there. And regarding his difficulty from Maharil's book — there is no Maharil book extant; and perhaps there it refers to when the hour was pressed from shortness of time, but those people had to do after prayer something very necessary with great loss (see above siman 108 seif 8), and thereby the time was so short that even to wait until after HaEl HaKadosh was not possible. And even if Maharil's intention is not as Levush's opinion, nevertheless it appears from Levush's words that he is inclined not like Maharil and Rama but for the advantage of what is written; it is possible Maharil also concedes thus, see there.
