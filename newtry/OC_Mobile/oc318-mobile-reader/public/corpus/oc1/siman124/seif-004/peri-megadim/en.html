**Because** (Taz) there is a deep matter here, and the ways of wisdom are hidden from me, I said I would speak and gain relief. Here in Berachos 47a they said amen ye'utoma (orphan amen) is forbidden; and in Sukkah 51b it implies that they would wave with sudarim — on this there are three resolutions. (1) The resolution of Rabbeinu Yonah in the Tur (see there — Rashi explains it; Bach: Rashi, author of Tosafos; but Beit Yosef and Taz and all explain Rabbeinu Yonah of blessed memory): to fulfill through it one must hear the whole berachah; if one word is missing he does not fulfill — and this is what they call amen ye'utoma. The prohibition is not from answering amen per se, but from the prohibition of one who wants to fulfill through answering amen more than the one blessing — therefore they said one does not fulfill this way, and he has a curse from Chazal if he relies on this; but from the side of answering amen itself there is no prohibition if afterward he wants to bless for himself. And since Chazal said in Berachos 53b, "If he did not hear the berachah, how does he fulfill?" — it is clear that Rabbeinu Yonah's ruling applies. Or: anyone who wants to fulfill must hear the entire berachah from beginning to end, for in the explanation they said there: Israel, even though he did not hear the whole berachah, answers — and if he did not hear, how does he fulfill? It implies he heard part of it, yet he does not fulfill — and similarly the Rambam of blessed memory explained explicitly in chapter 1 of Hilchot Berachot, brought in Shulchan Aruch 213:3 (and Elya Rabbah; and in my humble opinion none dispute Rabbeinu Yonah on this). (2) The distinction the Tur wrote: between knowing and not knowing which berachah one stands in. (3) Elya Rabbah in the name of Rabbeinu Hai, and as Magen Avraham understood sign 16: to distinguish between an individual — and that case of the flute: the congregation would hear and answer, and those standing at a distance who answer with the congregation — it is not amen ye'utoma.

**But** in the Tur's explanation that brought the words of R. Yitzchak (Rabbeinu Yonah) — the words of Rav Kohen Tzedek — and what he wrote: however, there are three explanations in it. The first explanation: the words of Mahari"a; and as it appears, thus is Rabbeinu Yonah — specifically when one wants to fulfill, one does not fulfill in such a case; if so, he fulfills even in the berachah of the eighteen that the shaliach tzibur repeats, for every person is obligated to hear and answer amen (as in seif 4, see there) — he answers amen even though he did not hear, and it is not amen ye'utoma, even though ab initio he is obligated to hear the whole prayer from the shaliach tzibur; nevertheless, if he did not hear, he is permitted to answer amen — and in this the case in Sukkah is discussed. We find for Rabbeinu Yonah several voices: whoever is not fulfilling is permitted to answer even if he does not know which berachah; but Rav Kohen Tzedek holds that a berachah one is obligated in, like chazarat hatfilah, is amen ye'utoma even if he knows which berachah; and in Sukkah, in the berachah of the Torah that one is not obligated to hear — all the more so one who enjoys [the meal] is permitted even if he does not know which berachah. And the Tur disputes this and says: however, one could say in another matter in Sukkah — if he knows which berachah it is permitted; and that in Berachos is when he does not know, and it is forbidden even for one who enjoys. And one could say from the explanations: they mean Rav Kohen Tzedek and the Tur — leniency and stringency for Rav Kohen Tzedek: in chazarat hatfilah, even if he knows — forbidden; leniency in Torah reading and one who enjoys — even if he does not know, permitted. And for the Tur also leniency and stringency. But in Beit Yosef he understood only two disputes of Rabbeinu Yonah, as in explanation (1) — and Rav Kohen Tzedek and Tur (1) are one, and there is no distinction between chazarat hatfilah, Torah reading, and one who enjoys: all who do not know — forbidden; and who knows — permitted. But a berachah one wants to fulfill through — all agree to Rabbeinu Yonah that one must hear from beginning to end, from Berachos 53b and as siman 213:3. But the Taz explained a third explanation of Rabbeinu Yonah with Rav Kohen Tzedek and Tur (2) — dispute as Beit Yosef; and in this way for one who enjoys all agree Rav Kohen Tzedek and Tur that even if he does not know it is permitted to answer amen; and for fulfilling all agree to Rabbeinu Yonah that even if he knows he does not fulfill (and it is compelled from Berachos 53b), for they dispute in chazarat hatfilah that one is obligated to hear — for Rabbeinu Yonah it is not amen ye'utoma even if he does not know which berachah; but there is a little prohibition, for certainly ab initio one must hear the whole prayer as in seif 10; and in Sukkah otherwise, and in a time of need ab initio it is permitted. If not so, aside from what he did improperly by not hearing the berachah, there is a prohibition that he answered amen without hearing the berachah. Rav Kohen Tzedek and the Tur hold that chazarat hatfilah is called obligated, and it is amen ye'utoma; and even through need it is forbidden to answer in such a case all who do not know which berachah; and if he knows — permitted even ab initio. And Rama adopted the stringency of both — see there in Taz. And therefore what the Tur did not write that case in Sukkah in Rabbeinu Yonah's words — one can resolve: (1) on the contrary, from there it is a bit difficult, for there is in any case a prohibition, and it must be said it is a time of need that is different; and (2) Rabbeinu Yonah from the Yerushalmi brought his words: amen ye'utoma — all who are obligated — its plain meaning is like Rabbeinu Yonah for fulfilling, etc.; and for Rav Kohen Tzedek one must force the obligation of chazarat hatfilah. And also: Rav Kohen Tzedek would not dispute the Yerushalmi; but according to Beit Yosef's explanation, Rav Kohen Tzedek and Tur dispute the Yerushalmi that even one who enjoys is amen ye'utoma, and one is not obligated at all to hear.

**And behold,** according to this the author adopted Rabbeinu Yonah's opinion and is obligated — that is, to fulfill — and it is difficult: for we wrote in 213:3 that one must hear the whole berachah literally; and why did he change here, that it implies only from the reason of amen ye'utoma alone? And on this Magen Avraham in chapter 1 of Hilchot Berachot signs 11, 13, and 14 challenged the Rambam thus, and answered that he rejected Rabbeinu Hai's opinion to distinguish between individual and congregation (and Levush holds that for Rabbeinu Hai even for actual fulfilling, whenever the congregation answers amen and heard the berachah, and an individual did not hear but answers with them — he fulfills; and for this the Rambam in sign 14 rejected even such a case — he does not fulfill). And behold, in the author it cannot be explained thus, for it should have said he does not answer amen at all among those answering, as the Rambam of blessed memory wrote. And if we say according to Mahari"a — Rav Kohen Tzedek — one could say the Rambam's opinion is thus: that he did not hear the berachah he is obligated in (not to fulfill), and according to Rav Kohen Tzedek it is amen ye'utoma; and to resolve Levush's difficulty — this he already said in sign 11 there: for fulfilling, etc., he does not fulfill; and here from the prohibition of answering amen — all who are obligated to hear, etc., as stated. And thus the author's words are explained, who omitted the word "to fulfill," and we say this is Rav Kohen Tzedek's opinion; and similarly the Rambam of blessed memory and Rabbeinu Nissim (Eliyah Rabbah wrote the Rambam's and Rabbeinu Nissim's and Rashi's opinions — one should examine what Rabbeinu Nissim and Rabbeinu Yonah wrote) — and as the Taz wrote; and to explain the Yerushalmi (and the word "another" is a bit difficult); and Mishna Berurah emended the Tur's opinion and adopted the stringency of both explanations — see Levush: it appears he adopted the stringency of both explanations like Mahari"a in Beit Yosef that there are three disputes; and now one should be stringent regarding one who enjoys when he does not know which berachah, and in chazarat hatfilah even if he knows which berachah; but for fulfilling, certainly whoever did not hear from beginning to end does not fulfill — certainly. And Eliyah Rabbah sign 15, and with God's help in Magen Avraham sign 17 I will explain more on this. And there is a little lacking in what the Taz wrote; and if not possible in another matter, Rabbeinu Yonah's words must be thus — and this is why the Tur did not bring in Rabbeinu Yonah's words that case in Sukkah; and see with God's help Taz sign 6 from this, and Magen Avraham sign 13 from this, and see further nearby with God's help from this.

**And still** we need to be aware that Beit Yosef wrote here for Rashi and Tosafos: even though one is obligated in the berachah, whoever knows answers amen and is exempted through it — it implies for actual fulfilling as well. And in siman 46 he wrote that one must bless a hundred berachot every day; and on Shabbos one completes with the berachot of the readers and answers amen even though he did not hear the berachah — and this contradicts what is written here in Shulchan Aruch: all who are obligated in it must hear the berachah even if he knows, etc.; and contradicts what is written in Berachos 53b: if he ate with them he must hear the whole berachah. And I already saw Efrayim Chayyim stand on this here, and it appears he returned here from that; and in siman 284:3, as the reader will see there, one must have kavanah and answer amen — it implies he must hear from beginning to end; and Magen Avraham in 46 sign 8 hinted at this briefly, see there. And in my humble opinion the answer could be: on the contrary, a resolution was made from difficulty (2) on (1) — for certainly all who are obligated to fulfill for himself, like one who eats and must bless and the like — there is no dispute, and all agree he must hear and does not fulfill through answering amen even if he knows, all who did not hear the berachah from beginning to end; unlike one obligated to bless a hundred berachot — the law is he fulfills through completing by answering amen even though he did not hear the berachah; and this is what Rashi meant: exempted through it — such a case is discussed. And for actual fulfilling, all agree one must hear the whole berachah, as that case in Berachos 53b proves. And therefore if we were to say the author ruled like Rabbeinu Yonah that all who are obligated to actually fulfill — even if he knows he does not fulfill through amen all who did not hear the berachah — behold, one not obligated to actually fulfill answers amen even if he does not know which berachah; and there are those who are stringent — no leniency is derived from one who is stringent who agrees that for fulfilling, even if he knows, he does not fulfill — it is not comparable; unlike the Taz and other explanations — why did Mishna Berurah not write the leniency that derives for some when he knows? For this the Taz wrote that he adopted the stringency of both.

**I was in doubt** about this: that one must bless a hundred berachot, and on Shabbos have kavanah for the berachah of the readers as in siman 284, and answer amen — whether we need both specifically, or hearing the whole berachah alone suffices even if he did not answer amen — he fulfills. And what brought me to this: in siman 213:2 for fulfilling — from what is written: all who heard the whole berachah even though he did not answer amen — and all the more so here; and all the more so, for behold Mishna Berurah says so — the master of blessed memory in Magen Avraham in siman 46 sign 8: all who have maggidim — it does not help that he hears the berachot. And why — is he better than a berachah one is obligated in from what is written for fulfilling: hearing is like answering — it is better and better. Whereas for one obligated to bless on food or a tallit and the like — when he hears from another and has kavanah, hearing is like answering and as if he blesses himself; unlike for a hundred berachot — how can we say hearing is like blessing, and he did not read from the Torah, only when he answers amen; and we rule: greater is one who answers amen than the one who blesses — then it counts for him toward a hundred berachot. It turns out stringent to complete a hundred — one must hear amen as well, as Magen Avraham wrote in 46:8; unlike for fulfilling — from what is written it is lenient: when he heard he fulfills even though he did not answer amen — as stated; and in Rosh, Berachos siman 72, and in 284 below there it says one must have kavanah and answer amen — as we said.
