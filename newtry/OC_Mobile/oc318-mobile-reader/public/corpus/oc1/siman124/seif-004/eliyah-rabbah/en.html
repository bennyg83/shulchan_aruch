To remain silent, etc. This is unlike Nimukei Yosef, who says that one should recite together with the chazzan through the end of each blessing, and unlike the Beit Yosef in the name of Abudarham, who writes that such people should be rebuked even if they do not conclude the blessings together with the chazzan. Nevertheless, one who did not pray with concentration may recite together quietly with the chazzan without concluding the blessings, but should instead concentrate and hear their conclusions from the chazzan. Although I wrote in siman 112 that nowadays we consider ourselves nonproficient, that is a stringency; here it would produce a leniency. Moreover, if so, the entire congregation might recite along with the chazzan and cause confusion. Abudarham writes that each prayer is effectively like three prayers: three times nineteen equals fifty seven blessings. One set is the silent prayer; a second is hearing the chazzan, since hearing is equivalent to responding; and a third is answering Amen. At Maariv, where the chazzan does not repeat the Amidah, they instituted Baruch Hashem LeOlam Amen, whose mentions of Hashem and Amens total thirty eight; adding the nineteen blessings of the silent Amidah gives fifty seven blessings at Maariv as well.
Close to being, etc. The Perishah writes: 'Close,' but not actually so, because it was already explained at the beginning of siman 55 that if the chazzan began with ten and some left, he completes the prayer. The Beit Yosef also explains that even if some are sleeping or conversing, the chazzan nevertheless prays and completes the prayer. Accordingly, one who is speaking and not listening still joins the minyan. The Beit Yosef, Shulchan Aruch, and Levush state this explicitly in siman 55, and the Beit Yosef there in the name of the Agur states it even more clearly, unlike the Taz.
