It seems to me that the Mechaber also holds that if one heard the blessing and did not answer amen until the entire congregation finished answering, he may answer amen — only in the law of the Mechaber, which speaks of one who is still in his prayer when the prayer leader finished the blessing, it is not logical to answer amen when he finished his prayer, for he interrupted with speech in his prayer between the conclusion of the prayer leader's blessing and answering amen. And from this the Taz Derush learned from the passage in Berachos: if one finished his prayer before the answering of amen from the majority of the congregation finished, he may answer — for the congregation's answering of amen is part of the conclusion of the blessing, and it is as though the prayer leader just now finished the blessing and there is no interruption. Not so regarding the few who prolong — that is not called conclusion of the blessing, and therefore there is an interruption in what he is occupied with and praying. And this is clear.
