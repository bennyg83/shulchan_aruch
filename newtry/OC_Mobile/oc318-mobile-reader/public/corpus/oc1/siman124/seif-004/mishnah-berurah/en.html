(16) They should be silent — meaning: we need not say it is forbidden to say with the shaliach tzibur the entire blessing, for that is a blessing in vain; even to join with him a few words from the middle is also not proper, for one must be concerned lest shigra delishna cause a blessing to come from his mouth; and all the more those who raise their voices and sing with the shaliach tzibur, for that is like arrogance, and one should rebuke them, for it is like frivolity. Regarding the prohibition of idle chatter, it is written in seif 7.
(17) And he should intend — therefore one must beware of saying supplications or studying during the repetition of the shaliach tzibur; and even if they intend at the end of the blessing to answer amen properly so it will not be an orphan amen, as will be explained — still it is not proper what they do: if the learners turn to their learning, the amei ha-aretz will learn from them not to listen to the shaliach tzibur and will engage in idle conversation, heaven forbid — they will cause the public to sin.
(18) As if there were not nine, etc. — therefore ab initio one should intend to hear the entire blessing and not only the end of the blessing.
(19) And he should intend — and people are accustomed to join the minyan and pray the repetition of the shaliach tzibur even for those who are known to chat and do not listen to the repetition properly; and it seems that in such a case it is good to conduct oneself as written in Sefer Shulchan Shlomo: the shaliach tzibur should pray between himself and himself in the repetition of the prayer so they should not answer nine amen after him, and they should intend for his blessings so that prayer should be as a voluntary prayer.
(20) They should stand — their reason: since they intend and listen to the shaliach tzibur, and hearing is like answering, and it is as if they pray themselves — so it is; and such was the custom of the ancients; and now, heaven forbid, each does as he sees fit, and some of them sit and chat [Peri Megadim]. And within four cubits of the shaliach tzibur, according to all opinions it is forbidden by law to sit during the repetition of the shaliach tzibur.
