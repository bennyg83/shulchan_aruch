12) There, in the gloss: And if there are individuals in the congregation, etc., the shaliach tzibbur should not wait, etc. And now the custom is to wait for the av beis din, and it appears to me that the reason is that most people pray hurriedly, and if individuals pray word by word they would not be able to say Kedushah with the congregation; therefore they wait. And therefore it appears to me that even if there is no av beis din in the city, one should wait for one who prays word by word; but when he prays at great length, one should not wait for him. Magen Avraham, s.k. 7; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 6; Soles Belulah, ot 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 5; Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 137d; Chesed LaAlafim, ot 2; Mishnah Berurah, ot 13. And so wrote the Knesses HaGedolah in the Hagahos HaTur, siman 90, that the custom now is to wait for the chacham of the community and for all the notables of the community, and there is no burden upon the congregation in this, for they forgo their honor. End of his words. And the Eliyah Rabbah cited him there and wrote that nevertheless it appears that the rav should not pray at such length, as we find with R' Akiva, who would shorten [when praying with the congregation]; and all the more so in a place where they wait for the chacham who has not yet come, that he should come early and be among the first ten. End of his language. And the Chesed LaAlafim wrote there that if he prays exceedingly long, it is fitting for him to be considerate of the honor of the congregation and to instruct concerning himself that they not wait for him; see there. And see above siman 55, seif 7, and our words there, ot 50.
