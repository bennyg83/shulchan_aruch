(א) “pronunciation of the nun” — he cited a common matter; and the same applies if one omits pronunciation of the alef or the mem. Bach and Eliyah Rabbah.

(ב) “and the shaliach tzibur blesses it” — he cited shaliach tzibur in order to reject hereby the view of Orchot Chaim in the name of Rav Hai Gaon, brought in Beit Yosef, who holds that in a congregation one may answer even if he did not hear the blessing himself — see there.

(ג) “and this one did not hear it” — it is obvious that even if she heard it from the beginning, if she did not hear the end [she may not answer amen]. And behold, according to what is explained below in siman 213 seif 3, that where one wants to fulfill [his obligation] through a certain blessing he must hear it from beginning to end — ostensibly, if she lacked hearing even only the beginning of the blessing alone, again she is not permitted to answer amen on it. And one should not say: what deficiency is there in this blessing more than an ordinary blessing through which one does not want to fulfill [his obligation] at all, that he must answer amen on it — if so, even if she did not hear it at all, she certainly does not fulfill [her obligation] through it, yet it should be permitted? Rather, since he wants to fulfill [his obligation] through it and he made it in a manner that he does not fulfill [his obligation] through it, the Sages forbade answering amen on it; if so, in such a case as well — and it requires investigation.

(ד) “and some are stringent,” etc. — see in Mishna Berurah seif kaf-lamed-gimel, where we wrote that the Rama's intent is in all blessings; thus wrote several acharonim explicitly, and not like Taz. Nevertheless for practical law it requires investigation whether one should practice like these who are stringent, for Bach, Taz, and Magen Gibborim all alike wrote that in other blessings through which one does not intend to fulfill [his obligation], it is permitted to answer even if he did not hear and does not know which blessing [it is]. But from Magen Avraham, Eliyah Rabbah, and Chayei Adam it is proven that one should practice like these who are stringent; and it seems that sitting and not acting is preferable, because there is punishment for one who answers an orphaned amen, as our Sages received. Also, all the force of Bach and Taz is from the Yerushalmi brought in Taz — see there; and our version is: the law [is that one who] sits before the one blessing [may answer], and there is not so much proof. Again I saw that the Gra in his Biur hinted to this; and afterward I found in Azhenazi's Or Zarua newly printed that he wrote thus explicitly, that according to our version there is no proof for this; and it seems the main [point] is like our version — see there. If so, it is obvious that this is the reason Rashi and Tosafot did not divide on this.

(ה) “immediately when he finishes,” etc. — see in Mishna Berurah where we wrote that the measure of “immediately” is within the time [it takes to say] amen yehei shmei rabba (teikhleih), and this is from Peri Megadim, who wrote that it seems so and did not bring proof for it. And ostensibly this is not so simple, for behold the source of this law flows from Avudraham brought in Beit Yosef, and there he wrote: “Some say an orphaned amen after a long time, when the one blessing finished, answered amen per the opinion of that blessing” — end quote. Ostensibly from this it is not dependent on kedei dibbur; and it is possible they strained the Rama's wording that wrote “immediately.” And regarding the essential law it is ostensibly difficult, for we hold that until amen from the mouth of most of those answering has not finished, the blessing is not yet finished — as below in seif 9 and 10, and in siman 167 seif 16 regarding separating bread; and even in a place where they do not discharge their obligation through the blessing, the law is thus — see there in Peri Megadim. And we do not divide any distinction between whether the answering of those answering continued much more than kedei dibbur or not; rather, because he is considered as still standing in the middle of a blessing, one who did not answer amen [is as if he interrupted]. If so, the same applies in our matter regarding an individual who wants to answer amen on some blessing and continued after the blessing more than kedei dibbur — he too should be permitted to answer. And further, even if the congregation all begin immediately to answer within kedei dibbur of the one blessing's blessing, this one answering is certainly more than kedei dibbur after the one blessing's blessing, for they are permitted to begin within kedei dibbur, which is at least two words after the one blessing; and the measure of amen is the time to say amen — if so, when he begins, before they finish, he is more than kedei dibbur. And one may say that there it is different, because many people answer amen after the blessing, and certainly among them are some who began to answer amen within teikhleih after the blessing; therefore even one who began to answer amen afterward, as one and one, is within kedei dibbur of his fellow — similar to what we say in Shevuot regarding testimony (daf 32a): each and every one within teikhleih of his fellow; and in Nazir 84 — see there. Nevertheless the one separating [bread] need not wait except until most of those answering finish, for presumptively not all begin saying it afterward but begin immediately; and one who prolongs his answering excessively need not be waited for — unlike in our matter of an individual who wants to answer amen after the blessing, who must not delay more than kedei dibbur after the blessing. And it seems I can bring some proof for Peri Megadim's words from what we say in Shevuot 36a: amen in it, oath in it, acceptance of words in it, belief of words in it — see there; and as ruled in Yoreh De'ah siman 209 — see in the Gra's Biur; and in siman 237 seif 2. And just as regarding amen, which is acceptance of words, certainly one must answer amen within teikhleih — as proven in Nazir 84 in the Gemara and in the Gra's Biur cited, in note 1 — if so, the same applies in our matter regarding amen, which is belief of words, as brought in Beit Yosef: it must be specifically within this kedei dibbur — this is my humble opinion in our matter. But if so, if one waited and did not answer until most of those answering finished answering, and his answering was afterward within kedei dibbur of their answering, he joins with them and it is not an orphaned amen. And one should look into this below in seif 11 in the Mechaber, who wrote that if before amen from the mouth of most of the congregation finished, etc.; and according to our words, even after it finished, within kedei dibbur as well. But per what he wrote the reason on the Shulchan Aruch's words in Chiddushei Ra'ah and in the book Magen Gibborim — and we copied them below — everything is resolved.
