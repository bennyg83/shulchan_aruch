(29) Hastily — explained in Shulchan Arukh: and likewise one should not say amen with other vowel points under the shuruk or cholam, and the like — for in all these the meaning is not the language of affirming matters; rather the alef should be read with kamatz gadol.
(30) Before he finishes — meaning: he should wait until the shaliach tzibur finishes the last word entirely; and there are people who begin to answer while the shaliach tzibur is still in the middle of the last word — and this is forbidden.
(31) That he is obligated — the author's opinion: specifically if he wants to fulfill through some blessing in which he is obligated, such as if he wants to fulfill Shemoneh Esreh and kiddush, and the like; but if he is not obligated — such as if he already prayed for himself — although it is a mitzvah for every person to hear the repetition of the shaliach tzibur, nevertheless he has no obligation in the essence of the blessing; and all the more if the amen is from unspecified blessings of the world — per the author it is permitted to answer even though he does not know on which blessing he answers, only that he heard others answering; and the Rama in the gloss disagrees and forbids even in all the blessings if he does not know on which blessing he answers.
(32) Even though he knows — such as if he recognizes by the order of the blessings, or if he missed from hearing only the end of the blessing.
(33) If he does not know, etc. — and if he knows on which blessing he answers, he is permitted to answer in all the blessings even in the repetition of the shaliach tzibur, since he already prayed and is not obligated in the essence of the blessing. And there are among the later authorities who are stringent regarding the repetition of the shaliach tzibur and hold: since the rabbinic enactment that the shaliach tzibur should repeat the prayer — even if all prayed, we call them as if obligated in that blessing, and they may not answer even if they know on which blessing the shaliach tzibur stands, unless they heard the conclusion of the blessing from the shaliach tzibur himself; and one should be concerned for this ab initio and beware to hear all eighteen blessings from the mouth of the shaliach tzibur himself; and even without this it is a mitzvah to intend to hear the blessing of the shaliach tzibur, as above in this seif. But after the fact, even if he did not hear — only that he knows which of the eighteen blessings the shaliach tzibur concludes — he should answer amen, since he already prayed for himself.
(34) Immediately — and within kedei dibbur is considered like speech, and the measure of kedei dibbur is disputed among the poskim: some say three words and some say four words; beyond that one must not answer at all, as it falls under amen ye'utoma (orphan amen). And in a congregation, the accepted ruling is that until the majority of the congregation has finished answering amen, it is still within the blessing, and each individual may answer with them. See in siman 111 and in Biur Halacha.
(35) When the blessing has ended — and if the shaliach tzibur extends in the melody of "ve'imru amen," the congregation should say amen immediately, because the melody constitutes an interruption; and this is specifically if he extends much in the melody. And this is specifically regarding Kaddish, where the substance of the request has already ended after the word "be'zman kariv" or after the word "de'amiran ba'alma"; but if the shaliach tzibur extends at the end of another blessing with some melody, one should not answer amen while he has not yet finished the substance of the word of the blessing.
(36) A short amen — because it appears similar to a burden (as if saying "omem," heavy) rather than so that he can [finish properly], etc., because this is the meaning of amen, as above in siman 63, and it is the acronym thereof.
