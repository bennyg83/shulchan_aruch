One should not answer an Amen chatufah, meaning as though the alef were vocalized with a chataf, nor should he snatch and hurry to answer Amen before the one reciting the blessing has finished. He should also not answer an Amen ketufah, meaning that he omits the pronunciation of the nun and does not articulate it clearly. He should also not interrupt in the middle of the word. [Beit Yosef in the name of the Aruch] He should not answer an Amen yetomah, meaning that he is obligated in a certain blessing and the prayer leader is reciting it, but he does not hear the blessing. Even though he knows which blessing the prayer leader is reciting, since he did not hear it he should not answer Amen after him, for that is an Amen yetomah. {Rama: Some are stringent that even if one is not obligated in that blessing, he should not answer Amen if he does not know which blessing the prayer leader is reciting, for this too is called an Amen yetomah. [Tur in the name of Rashbatz] One should not delay answering Amen. Rather, immediately when the blessing concludes, he should answer Amen. [Abudarham]} One should not answer a short Amen, but should lengthen it somewhat, long enough to say 'El Melech Ne'eman.' He should not lengthen it excessively, because the word is no longer heard properly when it is prolonged too much.
