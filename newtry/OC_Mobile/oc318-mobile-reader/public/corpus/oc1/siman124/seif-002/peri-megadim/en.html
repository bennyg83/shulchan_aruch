**And if** (Taz) one does not hear, he does not have kavanah for the shaliach tzibur's berachah, and it was enacted in Yavneh together with the shaliach tzibur; but one who hears and does not speak joins in, even though he does not answer amen — and in siman 55:8 it is explained in the author that a deaf person who hears and does not speak, or does not hear and speaks, joins in; and similarly Eliyah Rabbah here sign 8. However, if the shaliach tzibur prays immediately aloud, there is no concern, as in seif 2 in the gloss: what Mishna Berurah wrote that they should stand when the shaliach tzibur repeats the prayer — thus was the custom of the ancients; and now, unfortunately, each one does as he sees fit, and some of them sit and converse. And know that within four amot of the shaliach tzibur it is forbidden by law to sit, as in siman 102 (Magen Avraham, Taz 102:3), for it is a holy place; even per the author's reason, nevertheless so that he not appear as a heretic; and even to lean directly — we wrote there that it is forbidden (and I am careful about this). Also, if there are those who are not proficient and do not hear from the shaliach tzibur, it is as if they prayed themselves — they are forbidden by law per the Taz there, and possibly also per the author there; and see siman 79:1 in the gloss.
