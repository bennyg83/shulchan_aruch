In a loud voice. And there is no concern here of small faith or raising one's voice in one's prayer, since due to pressure he does so (Kol Bo). At the end of Rosh Hashana in the Gemara it appears that even without pressure it is permitted, for they said to him according to your words, why do people pray — he said to them: in order to arrange the prayer leader's prayer. It appears that if not for the reason of arranging, the prayer leader would be permitted to pray immediately in a loud voice; however, one need not arrange except on Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur, as written in siman 100, see there. Nevertheless, there is a difficulty for Rabbeinu Gershom: why do people pray all year? And one must say that nevertheless it is good to arrange the prayer leader each time, as written in siman 139 and in Beit Yosef. And now they have practiced in most places that the prayer leader prays Minchah in a loud voice immediately because of this; but in these countries they have not practiced thus, and so it is correct. And so wrote R' Levi Chaviv in his responsum, and so wrote Radbaz siman 94, that if it is possible to abolish it without controversy, abolish it. And so wrote in siman 101 that their custom is that the prayer leader says the first three and last three blessings in a loud voice and the rest quietly, see there.
