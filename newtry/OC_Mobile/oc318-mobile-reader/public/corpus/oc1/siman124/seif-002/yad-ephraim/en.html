There — and therefore the Tur brings there to teach us that there is a distinction between knowing or not, which is not proved in the words of Rabbenu Yonah, etc.; see there. It appears his intent is that certainly the Tur is not accustomed to write proofs from the Talmud for from what did the decisor learn this ruling — and therefore Rabbenu Yonah brought here from the case of Sukkah; only Rav Cohen Tzedek cites it, that from this we learned that in Rav Cohen Tzedek's view, even ab initio it is permitted when he knows — since the proof is from that case of Sukkah, and there they answered amen ab initio. And since this advantage of knowing, that there not be orphaned amen, helps for us, one may say it also helps for answering ab initio — and we need not strain to establish that case of Sukkah for the reason that it was impossible in another matter. Not so for Rabbenu Yonah: according to him the distinction is from the side that he is not obligated in the blessing — if so, one cannot say that there in Sukkah, even ab initio and not in a time of pressing need, it is permitted; for it is obvious that there they were certainly obligated to hear repetition of the eighteen [blessings], and one may not permit for us ab initio from the side that he knows which blessing, since for us this distinction does not help regarding that there not be orphaned amen — it is obvious that it also does not help regarding hearing the repetition. Thus, necessarily, that case of Sukkah where they did so ab initio was because it was not possible for them to hear repetition of the prayer, since it was a very large synagogue — and nevertheless they properly answered amen and it is not called orphaned, since he is not obligated in the blessing. And thus, since according to Rabbenu Yonah there is no distinction at all between knowing or not regarding ab initio, therefore he did not bring that case of Sukkah, as it is not relevant to the law. The fundamental law, that there is a distinction between one obligated in the blessing, the Tur wrote in general, as is its way. But in the words of Rav Cohen Tzedek he cited his proof to teach us that per his view, even ab initio one may permit due to the advantage of knowing — and like that case of Sukkah.
