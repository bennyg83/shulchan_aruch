The chazzan should repeat the prayer. Olat Tamid writes: This applies even though some of them have already prayed with a congregation, because the majority have not yet prayed with a congregation. This is obvious, and so writes Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah. I am surprised at his calling it obvious, for in siman 55, seif katan 7, I cited a responsum of the Radbaz that even if none of them prayed with a congregation, if all of them already prayed individually the chazzan does not repeat the prayer. I examined Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah and saw that his words can be understood as referring to a case where the majority have not prayed at all, even individually. Although I wrote above that the prayer is repeated even for one person who has not prayed, that applies according to our practice, in which Pores Al Shema is done even for one individual, as above in siman 69. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah, however, follows the Rambam, according to whom Pores Al Shema is done only for a majority who have not heard it. Even according to our practice there appears to be a distinction: if a majority have not prayed, the chazzan first prays silently and then repeats aloud; for one individual he does not first pray silently, but only aloud. It also appears that if the chazzan himself has already prayed, he never now prays silently. See Magen Avraham, siman 69.
He should concentrate, etc. He must understand Hebrew; if he does not understand it, he does not fulfill his obligation through the chazzan. Beit Yosef and the later authorities. See below, simanim 185 and 193. They further write that an individual cannot discharge another's obligation even if the latter is not proficient, because the enactment applied only to the chazzan. See above, siman 5.
In a loud voice, etc. This does not constitute the conduct of those of little faith, because it is done under pressing circumstances. Magen Avraham asks from the Gemara at the end of Rosh Hashanah, which appears to permit it even without pressing circumstances. There Rabban Gamliel is asked: according to you, why does the congregation pray? He answers: so that the chazzan can arrange his prayer. This implies that were it not necessary for arranging the prayer, the chazzan could immediately pray aloud. But this is not correct, because the question concerns the congregation, and the congregation is not crying out aloud; rather, they listen to the chazzan. The chazzan may in fact first pray silently when there is no pressing circumstance. Magen Avraham further asks that according to our ruling in siman 100 that the chazzan need arrange his prayer only on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, why according to Rabban Gamliel does the congregation pray all year? In my humble opinion there is no difficulty. According to Rabban Gamliel the chazzan indeed must arrange his prayer all year. We, however, follow the Sages, who hold that he does not discharge the obligation of one who is proficient, and therefore we say that he need not arrange it. The congregation's prayer is necessary because the chazzan does not discharge the obligation of the proficient. Alternatively, throughout the year according to Rabban Gamliel there is no difficulty because it is forbidden to pray aloud. When the Gemara says it is to arrange the prayer, that refers specifically to Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, when praying aloud is permitted. This also resolves Magen Avraham's first question.
Until HaEl HaKadosh, etc. This is somewhat difficult, because above in siman 109, seif 2, it appears that one should pray the entire prayer word for word together with the chazzan, including the blessing Shomea Tefillah, and aim to reach Modim together with him. It is difficult to distinguish the cases. It appears that the ruling here teaches that they should recite the full text of Kedushah, including LeDor VaDor, etc., through HaEl HaKadosh. The same is true that they should pray the entire prayer with the chazzan. This is implied by Maharil, Hilchot Tefillah, the source of this law, and by Mateh Moshe, siman 375, in the name of Sefer Chasidim: 'He should say Kedushah and all the prayers aloud, and the congregation should say quietly everything that he says, Kedushah and all.' The Levush below in siman 233 implies the same. Lechem Chamudot writes that perhaps this applies only where the time is so short that if they do not begin they will not finish before the prayer time passes; otherwise it is preferable that the congregation not begin until after the chazzan says HaEl HaKadosh. From what I will write in the next seif katan, however, it appears that even when the time is not so extremely short they begin immediately. Maharil there implies this more clearly, and I believe this is the accepted practice. See above siman 109 and below siman 233 for more on this.
That there should be one person, etc. So writes the Rama. Olat Tamid writes that the Shulchan Aruch's ruling in seif 5, that if there are not nine people concentrating the blessings are close to being in vain, apparently disagrees with this and holds that one listener is insufficient. It is surprising that the Rama did not present his view as 'some say.' The Levush's statement that it suffices if children answer Amen also seems incorrect, because adults are required and one should not be lenient where a blessing in vain, involving the prohibition of taking Hashem's Name in vain, is at issue. According to this, it is even more surprising that the Levush himself writes in seif 1 that nine are required, following the Shulchan Aruch, yet here follows the Rama. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty. A blessing in vain is not applicable here because the chazzan has not already prayed silently. This is why the wording is merely 'it is good, etc.,' implying that strictly by law even one listener is not indispensable. This also explains the Levush's statement that children suffice, namely if there is no adult available. Moreover, as I wrote above, the authorities do not mean an actual blessing in vain, only that it is close to such a situation. Still, it is difficult why here the Levush follows the Rama and Tur that there should be one listener, whereas below in siman 233 he writes that children should answer and does not mention that here. I explain this based on Maharil, who writes that he instructed one of them not yet to pray so that he could answer Amen to the chazzan, or alternatively to call someone from the city who had already prayed. This necessarily refers to a case where the time is not so short that the prayer time will expire if they do not begin immediately, because otherwise the other person must also pray. Therefore here, where the Levush speaks of concern that the time may pass, it may be impossible for another adult to refrain from praying. In siman 233, by contrast, he speaks of a case where it is very close to evening and everyone must pray immediately, with no time to find someone who has already prayed; therefore children should answer.
