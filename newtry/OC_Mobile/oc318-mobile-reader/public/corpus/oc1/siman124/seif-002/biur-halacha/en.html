(א) "prayer leader who entered," etc. — from the conclusion of the poskim it implies that this law applies also in Shacharit, even though per that reasoning he cannot afterwards adjoin redemption to prayer, he need not be concerned about this, for all Israel are guarantors for one another and it is incumbent upon him to discharge them from their obligation with holiness and to fulfill the enactment of our Sages in the repetition of the prayer leader; and although one can push away and say that regarding Shacharit this law applies when the prayer leader already prayed with the congregation until Shema and left in between and until he came they finished their prayer — but if so they should have explained.

(ב) "that the time of prayer will pass" — see in Mishna Berurah in the name of Peri Megadim; and in my opinion one should look into this, for in Minchah if the time passes entirely it is better to skip the repetition of the prayer leader and discharge the congregation only with Kedushah; but in Shacharit, even if four hours' time passes, it is possible that our Sages did not uproot their enactment in order to discharge one who is not expert in the essential obligation of prayer, which is until noon.

(ג) “quietly until after El HaKadosh” — see in Peri Megadim, who agreed that lekhatchilah the congregation should pray with the chazzan word for word until after Shome'a Tefillah and Modim, and they [pray] quietly while the chazzan says everything aloud; and likewise the Levush in siman 233 implies that the chazzan finishes the prayer aloud. And from the Gra's explanation in what he hinted — see siman 109 seif 2 — it also seems ostensibly that he holds thus. And see in Eliyah Rabbah, who questioned from there: here [in our case] the world does not practice thus, but immediately after El HaKadosh the chazzan also prays quietly. And perhaps the reason for the custom is because in most places the congregation practices waiting until the chazzan finishes El HaKadosh, and it is difficult for them to catch up afterward to say Shome'a Tefillah and Modim together; if so, what benefit would there be if he prayed aloud?
