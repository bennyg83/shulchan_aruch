(23) That they not fulfill — meaning: although they fulfill now through the prayer of the shaliach tzibur, they are not like one who answers amen after his own blessings, for in any case they say nothing but listen [Levush].

(24) And with intention, etc. — see in Chayei Adam, general rule 6, who wrote that one should intend in his answering amen also on what the one who blesses said, "Blessed are You, Heaven" — meaning: when the shaliach tzibur said "Blessed are You, Heaven, Shield of Avraham," the one answering amen should intend: amen — that the Name of Heaven, may He be blessed, Who is Shield of Avraham, should be blessed; and so in all blessings.

(25) "It is true" — and this is in a blessing of thanks, such as Baruch She'amar, Yishtabach, Go'el Yisrael, and the like; but in prayer one must intend "it is true" and also "I pray that it be His will that this matter be fulfilled" (for example in the blessing Atah Chonen: the one praying asked "grant us from You," etc., in "Who graciously grants knowledge" he should intend: it is true that He grants knowledge, and may it be His will that He also grant us knowledge; and so in all blessings). And in Kaddish one must intend only on the future — that they believe his words, what he requests that His kingship be revealed speedily and in our days — for the essence of the matter will certainly be fulfilled in the end, as it is written "on that day Heaven will be One," etc. Peri Megadim wrote in siman 51: one who answers amen after the blessing "He Who restores" should not say together "amen, we thank You" — for amen refers to the blessing as above, and "we thank" is a matter unto itself; only pause a little after the word amen. One for whom it happened to answer amen on two matters answers two amen one after the other and should intend in each amen the matter about which he answers; and it is preferable to say amen, amen.
