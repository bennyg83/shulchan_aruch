“And with intention that he intend in his heart ‘it is true,’” etc. — see in Mishna Berurah in what he wrote; but regarding prayer, etc., this is from the words of Magen Avraham in the name of Bach. And it is astonishing that he changed Bach's wording, for there Bach wrote that one should intend this specifically in the middle blessings — which implies that in the first [blessings], which are designated for praise, one should not intend [this], only that “it is true” are the words of the one blessing, as written in Shulchan Aruch. And from his wording that he wrote this is in the blessing of thanksgiving, etc., it does not imply thus. And perhaps he disagrees with Bach on this and holds that in the blessing Mechaye Metim there are also two intentions — namely, “it is true” that He revives the dead, and “may it be His will that it be fulfilled speedily.” And perhaps this also applies in the first blessing — namely, that answering amen refers to the entire blessing that the one blessing blessed: He bestows kindnesses, creates all, remembers the kindnesses of the fathers, brings a redeemer, etc.; if so, two intentions also apply here, except in the blessing Atah Kadosh alone, where only one intention applies. Therefore Magen Avraham wrote [thus], and in Peri Megadim above in siman 51 he copied from Shelah that in the first three [blessings] one should not intend except the intention written in Shulchan Aruch — see there his reason. And it is astonishing that he did not mention at all these words of Magen Avraham, who copied Bach's words and changed the wording — which implies that he disagrees with this; and practically it requires investigation.
