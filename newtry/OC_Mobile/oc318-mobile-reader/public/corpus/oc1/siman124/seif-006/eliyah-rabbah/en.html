It is true, etc. For each individual blessing, when answering Amen one should intend that he acknowledges and believes the content of that blessing. After requests and petitions, such as the Amen after Kaddish, the blessing Rachem and the Harachaman passages in Birkat HaMazon, and after Zachreinu in Yaaleh VeYavo and similar petitions, he should intend: may it be Hashem's will that the requests of the person reciting them be fulfilled. Regarding the middle blessings of the Amidah, and Retzeh, Sim Shalom, and Shalom Rav, which combine requests with praise of the Holy One, blessed be He, in Atah Chonen he should intend: it is true that Hashem, blessed be He, grants knowledge, and may He fulfill the words of those making the request. The same applies to all the blessings according to their subject. Therefore a person should concentrate carefully on hearing from the chazzan what sort of prayer, request, or blessing is being said, so that he knows what to intend. The Shelah, folio 254, discusses this at length.
And I believe, etc. This is why the one who answers Amen is greater, etc. The Perishah writes that the reason is that by saying Amen he accepts the blessing upon himself, since Amen expresses acceptance, oath, and faith, as stated in Devarim Rabbah, unlike the one who merely recites the blessing. The Shelah, folio 255, concludes that when one recites the blessing with exceptionally deep concentration, the one who recites the blessing is greater.
And one who converses should be rebuked, etc. Kol Bo, folio 9, writes: Woe to those who speak idle words or engage in laughter during prayer, for because of our sins we have seen many synagogues destroyed because people treated them lightly. Sefer Vavei HaAmudim writes that every community should appoint people to supervise this matter and impose substantial penalties upon those who speak, even publicly embarrassing them. The Shelah, folio 255, writes that he saw those who tremble at the word of Hashem place the siddur before their faces during the repetition, keeping their eyes and hearts there and not looking outward, so that they concentrate on every word. Olat Tamid cites Responsa Menachem Azaryah, siman 102, that one who studies Torah during the repetition, although this is not proper conduct for the service, is nevertheless not called a sinner. One must be cautious with this, because Menachem Azaryah explains the reason as being that we presume ourselves proficient, and he concludes that since we have ruled concerning ourselves as nonproficient, if we did not concentrate sufficiently it is proper to fulfill our obligation when the chazzan descends to lead the repetition. It was already explained at the beginning of siman 101 that nowadays we presume ourselves to pray without proper concentration. Above in siman 1 I also wrote that one should not recite prayers and supplications while the chazzan repeats the Amidah. The Shelah there implies the same, and Sefer Vavei HaAmudim protests strongly against this practice. See siman 90, seif 18.
