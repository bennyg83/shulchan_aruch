Section 4. When the chazzan repeats the prayer, the congregation should be silent and intend the blessings the chazzan blesses, and answer amen. NB: We have the version in chapter 14 of Shabbat (daf 119): R. Eliezer said: Whoever answers amen with all his strength — they open for him the gates of the Garden of Eden, as it says: "Open the gates, and let a righteous nation enter, keeper of faithfulness" — do not read "keeper of faithfulness" but "who say amen." What is amen? R. Eliezer said: God, faithful King. In Yalkut Yeshayah siman 26 on the verse "Open the gates" he brought this statement, and brought the version of one who said: What is amen? You are a faithful King — end quote. To understand what they argue about: it seems they argue about what I saw in one book — it seems to me in Peri Megadim — who was uncertain whether one should answer amen after one praying quietly and one hears the blessing from him; for at the time of Shemoneh Esreh prayer it is as if the Blessed One stands before a person, as explained in the poskim. Therefore the one who holds "God, faithful King" holds one does not answer amen except on blessings aloud, not on blessings quietly; and if so, aloud he is not considered as if the Holy One stands before him — therefore he says in hidden language "God, faithful King." But the other view holds that on blessings quietly too one answers amen, and then the Blessed One is before him — then it is appropriate to say in present language "You are a faithful King." Or one may say the distinction is if one hears one blessing outside the bathhouse while he stands in the bathhouse — and similarly if it is permitted to answer amen, since it is explicit in Shulchan Aruch siman 87 that mundane matters may be said in the holy tongue — if so it is permitted to answer amen in the bathhouse, and similarly. But since amen is an acronym of "God, faithful King," and Tosafot wrote there it must be written thus — if so it is a name and forbidden to say in the bathhouse; but if amen is an acronym of "You are a faithful King," it is permitted to say in the bathhouse. But behold it is explicit in poskim that one does not answer amen in a place that interrupts feces or idolatry — all the more if the answering is in the place of his body, where it is forbidden to bless there. If so, this distinction cannot be said except perforce the distinction as I wrote initially — investigate well.
