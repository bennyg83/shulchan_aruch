1) [Seif 1] After the congregation has finished their tefillah, etc. And if one finished his silent tefillah and the shaliach tzibbur has not yet begun the repetition, it is an outright sin to speak between the silent prayer and the repetition. The disciples of the Arizal, of blessed memory; Kesher Gudal, siman 18, ot 21; Beis Oved, in the laws of the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur, ot 10; Pesach HaDvir, siman 111, ot 3; Chesed LaAlafim, ot 6; Kaf HaChaim of R' Chaim Palagi, siman 15, ot 52. And the Pesach HaDvir wrote there that all the more so the shaliach tzibbur himself is forbidden to speak between the silent prayer and the repetition; see there. So too wrote the Kaf HaChaim there. And so is implied from the words of Shaar HaKavanos, the fourth discourse on Yom Kippur, which wrote concerning the verse 'Hashem Hu HaElokim' that it would have been fitting to say it after the silent tefillah of Neilah and not after the repetition, and the reason they say it after the repetition is so as not to interrupt between the silent prayer and the repetition; see there. Accordingly, this implies that since there is a need to say this verse between the silent prayer and the repetition and even so it is not said, in order not to interrupt, then all the more so it is forbidden to interrupt with speech between the silent prayer and the repetition; and according to this it appears that even to study Torah is forbidden between the silent prayer and the repetition. So too wrote the Ben Ish Chai, parashas Terumah, ot 10. And see our words above in siman 53, ot 16.
2) There: For if there is someone who does not know, etc. This is according to the plain meaning; and it is known that all the words of the Sages, of blessed memory, although they contain wondrous secrets, they do not reveal them, and they clothe them in the language of the plain meaning. But in the days of our master the Arizal, of blessed memory, permission was granted to reveal, and he revealed the secret of the repetition: that its level is greater than that of the silent prayer, and both are obligatory, as explained in Shaar HaKavanos, the first discourse on the repetition of the Amidah, and in Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar Chazaras HaAmidah, chapters 1 and 2; see there. Therefore the Arizal, of blessed memory, during the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur, would close his eyes and listen and concentrate on the words of the shaliach tzibbur, as written in Shaar HaKavanos, folio 4a, and Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar HaKorbanos, chapter 2; and so wrote the Mishnas Chassidim in the discourse of She'eiris HaAtzilus, ot 1. Therefore one should never cancel the repetition, at Shacharis, Musaf, or Minchah, except specifically in a situation of duress; and so wrote the Ben Ish Chai there, ot 2; see there. And the order of the aspects of the tefillos I have already written in a responsum in my small holy work Be'er Mayim Chaim, with Heaven's help; take it from there.
3) There: For if there is someone who does not know, etc. But the one who is expert does not fulfill his obligation through the tefillah of the shaliach tzibbur. Beis Yosef; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 1. And it is implied that even after the fact he has not fulfilled his obligation. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, ot 1; Mishnah Berurah, ot 1.
4) There: For if there is someone who does not know, etc. That is, one who does not know how to pray, yet nevertheless understands what the shaliach tzibbur says. Beis Yosef. And the Bach explained: that is, one who does not know how to pray by heart, when there is no siddur in his hand; but if he does not understand what the shaliach tzibbur says, he does not fulfill his obligation through listening, as stated in siman 193. Beis Yosef; Levush; Bach. And the Bach wrote there: but one who prays by himself with the text of the tefillah, even if he does not understand what he utters with his mouth, fulfills his obligation; see there. And see our words above in siman 101, ot 16, and examine this well. Taz, s.k. 1; Olas Tamid, ot 2; Magen Avraham, s.k. 2; Pri Chadash, ot 1; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 2; Mishnah Berurah, ot 2.
5) There: One who does not know, etc. And an individual cannot discharge even one who is not expert, unless there are ten in the synagogue. The Rashba in a responsum, siman 126, in the name of the Yerushalmi; Knesses HaGedolah in the Hagahos HaTur; Olas Tamid there; Eliyah Rabbah there. And so ruled the Erech HaShulchan, ot 1, and wrote that it is as Maran, of blessed memory, wrote in siman 594, and not like the Rama there and the Pri Chadash in siman 62; see there. So too wrote the Eshel Avraham there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 1; Mishnah Berurah, ot 1.
6) There: And the one who fulfills his obligation through the tefillah of the shaliach tzibbur must concentrate, etc. And he does not interrupt, etc.; and he also does not say 'Baruch Hu uVaruch Shemo,' nor Modim d'Rabbanan, as we write below in ot 26 and ot 55; but Amen he answers, as stated below in seif 6.
7) There: And he steps three steps, etc. This is to exclude Mahari ibn Ghiyyat, whom the Beis Yosef cited in his name, who disputes and says that just as the shaliach tzibbur exempts him from the tefillah, so does he exempt him from the three steps. But it is not so, for the reason he exempts him from the tefillah is that one who listens is like one who recites; but from the steps he certainly does not exempt him, and as the Mechaber, of blessed memory, ruled. Pri Chadash, ot 1. And the Acharonim ruled like the view of Maran, of blessed memory. And according to this, he must also bow and straighten in Avos and in Hoda'ah, at the beginning and at the end, just as one who prays by himself.
