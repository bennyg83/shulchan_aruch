1) [Seif 1] After the congregation has finished their tefillah, etc. And if one finished his silent tefillah and the shaliach tzibbur has not yet begun the repetition, it is an outright sin to speak between the silent prayer and the repetition. The disciples of the Arizal, of blessed memory; Kesher Gudal, siman 18, ot 21; Beis Oved, in the laws of the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur, ot 10; Pesach HaDvir, siman 111, ot 3; Chesed LaAlafim, ot 6; Kaf HaChaim of R' Chaim Palagi, siman 15, ot 52. And the Pesach HaDvir wrote there that all the more so the shaliach tzibbur himself is forbidden to speak between the silent prayer and the repetition; see there. So too wrote the Kaf HaChaim there. And so is implied from the words of Shaar HaKavanos, the fourth discourse on Yom Kippur, which wrote concerning the verse 'Hashem Hu HaElokim' that it would have been fitting to say it after the silent tefillah of Neilah and not after the repetition, and the reason they say it after the repetition is so as not to interrupt between the silent prayer and the repetition; see there. Accordingly, this implies that since there is a need to say this verse between the silent prayer and the repetition and even so it is not said, in order not to interrupt, then all the more so it is forbidden to interrupt with speech between the silent prayer and the repetition; and according to this it appears that even to study Torah is forbidden between the silent prayer and the repetition. So too wrote the Ben Ish Chai, parashas Terumah, ot 10. And see our words above in siman 53, ot 16.
