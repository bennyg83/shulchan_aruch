(ט) [א] There — he goes down before the ark, etc. — Beit Yosef wrote that now in most places they practice initially having the chazzan pray Minchah aloud so that they answer Kedushah after him and he need not repeat and pray aloud — end quote; and this is that he says Kedushah and the blessing "Atah kadosh," and afterward says the middle blessings quietly with the congregation, and begins R'tzeh aloud and finishes his prayer aloud, as written in Beit Yosef siman 234 — see there; but our Rabbi the Rama z"l in Darkei Moshe note 3 wrote that we do not practice thus, but rather always the chazzan prays first quietly, except where the matter is necessary that we are concerned the time of Minchah will pass — then he immediately begins to pray aloud Kedushah so they answer Kedushah at its time — end quote; and likewise our master z"l later in Beit Yosef siman 234 that the Ashkenaz practice is correct; and so practiced the sages of the generation before us in Tzfat, etc., and they decreed excommunication on one who transgresses their enactment — see there; and Beit Din, chazzan siman 136 p. 73, and he wrote that all the more so in Shacharit and Musaf, for there is no situation of pressing time — it is forbidden to breach a fence and transgress the enactment of the sages — see there; Kavanat HaLev siman 8 note 1; Beit Yosef note 1; and so it is per the Ari z"l's teaching, that there must specifically be quiet prayer and then repetition prayer — whether in Shacharit, Musaf, or Minchah — and there is no distinction between them, for in each of these three prayers the repetition is greater in elevation than the quiet, as explained in Sha'ar HaKavanot and in our words above note 2 and note 8; and likewise in Sha'ar HaKavanot drush 2, that Minchah prayer — on the Ari z"l, who was extremely careful not to say it without repetition, but rather first quietly and then aloud as in Shacharit prayer, and all the more in Shacharit and Musaf; and even if he was close to sunset a little; and not only that, but even if it was on the eve before sunset he would say quietly and then say aloud — end quote; and so the custom spread now in all places as the Ari z"l stated, that one does not nullify any repetition — Shacharit, Musaf, Minchah — except only if it is a time of pressure, such as they fear the time of prayer will pass — meaning there will not remain time to say the repetition in the time of Minchah prayer; and as written below siman 132 — then when they begin to say the prayer quietly, the chazzan raises his voice in the first three until after El HaKadosh, and they say with him word for word in order to say Kedushah; and afterward the chazzan too completes his prayer quietly from Atah chonen, etc., until the end of the eighteen blessings — because it is forbidden to make a sound in quiet prayer, as written above siman 101 note 7 in the name of the Zohar and the Ari z"l; except only in the first three, since he raises his voice for the need in order to say Kedushah, it is permitted — see there; and see our words for siman 69 note 1.

(ט) [ב] There — he goes down before the ark, etc. — a chazzan who prayed individually and the congregation stumbled upon him and he is compelled to pass before the ark — Ya'avetz wrote in Sha'ilt Ya'avetz siman 21 that he should pray another time quietly and then pray aloud for the congregation; but Bach note 4 wrote about him that his words are not clear and his proofs are not decisive — see there; and likewise the later authorities, chazzan siman 136 p. 73; Beit Yosef note 3 at the beginning of this siman; Mishna Berurah note 5.
