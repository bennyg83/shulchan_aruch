9) There: Descends before the ark, etc. The Beis Yosef wrote that now the custom in most places is for the shaliach tzibbur to pray the Minchah tefillah aloud from the outset, so that they answer Kedushah after him and he need not go back and pray aloud. End of his words. That is, he says the Kedushah and the berachah of 'Atah Kadosh,' and afterward says the middle berachos silently with the congregation, and begins Retzeh aloud and finishes his tefillah aloud, as written in the Beis Yosef, siman 234; see there. But Moreinu HaRav Moshe [Isserles], of blessed memory, in Darkei Moshe, ot 3, wrote: We do not practice thus; rather, the shaliach tzibbur always prays first silently, except where the matter is urgent, when we are concerned that the time of Minchah will pass; then he begins immediately to pray aloud the Kedushah, so that they answer Kedushah in its proper time. End of his language. And Maran, of blessed memory, wrote below in the Beis Yosef, siman 234, that the custom of the Ashkenazim is the correct one, and so did the sages of the generation before us institute in Tzfas, may it be rebuilt and established, and they decreed excommunication upon one who violates their enactment; see there. And the Shalmei Tzibbur cited this, folio 136c, and wrote that all the more so at Shacharis and Musaf, where there is no pressing circumstance, it is forbidden to breach the fence and violate the enactment of the sages; see there. Kesher Gudal, siman 18, ot 1; Beis Oved, ot 1. And so it is according to the words of the Arizal, of blessed memory, that one must say specifically the silent tefillah first and afterward the repetition tefillah, whether at Shacharis, at Musaf, or at Minchah, with no distinction among them, for in each one of these three tefillos the repetition is of greater level than the silent prayer, as explained in Shaar HaKavanos and in our words above, ot 2 and ot 8. And so is written in Shaar HaKavanos, the second discourse on the Minchah tefillah, about the Arizal, of blessed memory, that he was exceedingly careful, in the utmost, not to say it without a repetition, but rather first silently and afterward aloud, as with the Shacharis tefillah, and all the more so with the Shacharis and Musaf tefillos; and even if it was slightly close to sunset. Moreover, even if it was on erev Shabbos before sunset, he would say it silently and afterward say it aloud. End of his language. And so has the custom now spread in all places, in accordance with the words of the Arizal, of blessed memory, that no repetition is canceled, at Shacharis, Musaf, or Minchah, except only if it is a pressed time, such as when they fear that the time of the tefillah will pass, that is to say, that no time will remain to say the repetition within the time of the Minchah tefillah, as stated below in siman 232. Then, when they begin to say the tefillah silently, the shaliach tzibbur raises his voice in the first three [berachos] until after 'HaKel HaKadosh,' and they say it with him word for word, in order to say Kedushah; and afterward the shaliach tzibbur as well completes his tefillah silently, from 'Atah Chonein,' etc., until the conclusion of the Shemoneh Esreh, because it is forbidden to make one's voice heard in the silent tefillah, as we wrote above in siman 101, ot 7, in the name of the holy Zohar and the Arizal, of blessed memory; only in the first three, since he raises his voice for a need, in order to say Kedushah, it is permitted, as seen there. And see our words on siman 69, ot 1.
9) There: Descends before the ark, etc. A shaliach tzibbur who prayed as an individual, and a congregation then happened to come to him and he is compelled to pass before the ark: the She'eilas Yaavetz, siman 21, wrote that he should pray another time silently and afterward pray aloud for the congregation. But the Birkei Yosef, ot 4, wrote about him that his words are not clear and his proofs are not decisive, as seen there. And so wrote the Acharonim: Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 136c; Beis Oved, ot 3; Shaarei Teshuvah at the beginning of this siman; Mishnah Berurah, ot 5.
