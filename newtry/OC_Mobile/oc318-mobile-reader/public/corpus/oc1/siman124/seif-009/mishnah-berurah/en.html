(37) A few of those who answer — but regarding the majority of the congregation, one is obligated to wait throughout the prayer not to begin the blessing that follows until they answer amen. And similarly in Kaddish, not to begin "yisbarach" until the majority has answered amen, yisrael (at the end). And similarly in everything like this; and unfortunately many people fail in this when praying before the amud — they rush to begin the next blessing immediately after finishing the blessing before it, and do not wait at all in between. See in the responsum he cited that this interferes even b'dieved, for it is forbidden to answer amen on it again once one has begun another blessing.
(38) The one reciting the blessing need not [wait] etc. — and this is because there is no obligation on everyone to hear the blessing; but if one is fulfilling the obligation for the many thereby, whether he is the shaliach tzibur or another person reciting a blessing, he must wait even for those who err and extend in amen, so that they hear and fulfill their obligation of the eighteen blessings as well.
