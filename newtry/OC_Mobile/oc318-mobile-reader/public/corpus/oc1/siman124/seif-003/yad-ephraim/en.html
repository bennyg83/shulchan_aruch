(There.) One may say that in forgetfulness, etc. — and it appears that Tosafot, etc., also intended this. That is what they wrote, to distinguish between one who did not pray at all, etc. It appears their intent is that Tosafot and the Rosh wrote proof from Rabban Gamliel, and on this they wrote that although Rabban Gamliel only exempted with those in the fields because they were coerced, one may say that this is when he did not pray at all; but where he already prayed, etc. Behold, at first glance it was possible to interpret that the main distinction of Tosafot is between where he did not pray — then he is not like one coerced — unlike when he already prayed but forgot, where we consider him like one coerced. This is difficult for the Taz: for even in coercion he does not exempt — and all the more so in forgetfulness. Therefore the Taz wrote that in truth, in forgetfulness the reason is not due to coercion; rather, the reason is that for one matter alone he may rely on the prayer leader — and on this he brought the words of Mordechai, who wrote that the halacha is like Rabban Gamliel even on the rest of the days of the year; and afterward he brought the words of Maharam regarding a complete blessing, that one repeats — and it is difficult: why should he repeat, since the prayer leader fulfills for Rabban Gamliel even the expert? Perforce one must say that forgetfulness is not considered coercion, and Rabban Gamliel exempts specifically in coercion. And if so, in Yaaleh veYavo, Behag holds that he should not repeat — because for one matter, such as Yaaleh veYavo, one may properly rely even when he is not coerced; not so for a complete blessing, per Maharam — and on this he wrote: and it appears that Tosafot and the Rosh, etc., also intended this (and one may say they intended this: that for a complete blessing one repeats — for in truth there is no decisive ruling in the words of Tosafot on this matter; one may say specifically when he did not pray at all; but when he prayed and forgot a blessing, it helps — and one blessing and Yaaleh veYavo are the same). And this refers back to what is above it, on what was written that forgetfulness is not due to coercion but only for one matter alone may he rely on the prayer leader — and that is what they wrote, to distinguish between one who did not pray at all: meaning, even in error of forgetfulness he may not rely on the prayer leader — that is, if he did not pray, forgetfulness does not help and we do not consider him like one coerced; but if he already prayed and only forgot one matter — although forgetfulness is not like coercion, nevertheless, since it was only one matter alone (such as Yaaleh veYavo per Maharam, and according to Tosafot even for a complete blessing it is possible that its law is also like Yaaleh veYavo — that specifically when he did not pray at all the prayer leader does not fulfill) — he may properly rely on the prayer leader.
