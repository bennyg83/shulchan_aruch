The chazzan should not wait, etc. Nowadays the congregation waits for the rav of the city. The reason appears to be that the congregation will not pray so hurriedly when they know in any event that they must wait. Magen Avraham writes even more strongly that even where there is no rav in the city one should wait for a person who prays word by word. I also saw in Knesset HaGedolah, siman 90, regarding the law in the next seif that one need not wait where a minyan is already present, that the current custom is to wait for the sage of the congregation and all its prominent members, and this is not considered a burden on the congregation because they waive their honor. Nevertheless, it appears that the rav should not prolong excessively, as we find that Rabbi Akiva would shorten his prayer. Certainly, where people wait for a sage who has not yet arrived, he should make an effort to arrive early and be among the first ten. Sefer Chasidim, siman 487, writes that a person should do whatever he can so that people not mock him. It relates that a sage told his students: when another person leads the prayer and prays quickly, so that I cannot shorten my own prayer enough, I make myself appear to bow at Modim even though I have not yet reached Shema Koleinu. When the chazzan begins the repetition, I step backward as though I had completed my prayer so that they will not mock me, although in truth I do not finish until after they have already said Kedushah. The Holy One, blessed be He, knows that I stepped backward only to avoid mockery and that otherwise I could not concentrate.
