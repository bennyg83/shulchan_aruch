(1) "That there is no prohibition" — for what they said [in Berakhot 11]: "In a place where they told to lengthen, one may not shorten; where they told to shorten, one may not lengthen" — they said only regarding long blessings that open with Barukh and seal with Barukh: one may not shorten so as not to open or not to seal; and likewise the reverse — regarding short ones one may not lengthen them, for thereby one changes from the coin that the Sages stamped in blessings. But the rest of the wording of the blessing — the Sages did not give it a measure that one must say such and such words specifically; for if so, they would have enacted the wording of every blessing in counted words and would have taught us each blessing in its wording — and we do not find this. Rambam wrote in chapter 1 of Laws of Keriat Shema, rule of the matter: whoever changes from the coin that the Sages stamped in blessings — this one errs and returns and blesses as the coin. And Kesef Mishneh wrote in chapter 1 of Berakhot: only if one opened a blessing in a place where they did not enact to open, or sealed in a place where they said not to seal, or omitted from long blessings the beginning of the blessing that begins with the Name and malchut, or did not conclude with the Name; but if one changed the wording of the blessing and did not say that exact language but said in other wording the matter of the blessing — even if one omitted many words, one has fulfilled bedieved, since there was in it the Name and malchut and the matter of the blessing; only the words that the Sages specified that are me'akev, such as not mentioning covenant and Torah in Birkat haMazon, and Mashiv haRuach, and all similar — and all this was brought in Magen Avraham siman 64 and siman 187. And in Biur haGra he wrote that Rambam in chapter 1 of Berakhot returned from this and holds that even in a long blessing, if one did not complete the end of the blessing as required, nevertheless bedieved one has fulfilled — and like the first view brought in siman 187, seif 1 — see there.

(2) "All the time the congregation" — the same applies even if he already finished and the congregation [is saying] the piyyutim and the blessing, and he stands between one paragraph and another — likewise it is forbidden to interrupt.

(3) "That through this" — meaning: when the multitude see that he is looking in a book, they too will come to speak; but mere meditation in words of Torah, perhaps one may be lenient — and see Pri Chadash. However, at the time when the prayer leader says Kaddish, it is forbidden to meditate, for one needs much kavanah in the utterance of Kaddish.

(ד) Regarding “one has no right” — and Maharach wrote in the name of the Ari that he would not say piyyutim and pizmonim except what the early ones arranged, such as Kalir who arranged per true intent; and likewise he would not say Yigdal. Nevertheless, the son of Maharach testified about his father that when he was shaliach tzibur in the congregation on the Days of Awe he would say all the confessions and all the piyyutim — “let a wise man hear and learn from him wisdom and moral discipline” — not to change customs. And in Shlah he expanded in the name of geonim that it is a mitzvah to say piyyutim, and when the poet R' Elazar and “living creatures that stand here, square before the throne,” etc., “fire blazed around him” — see there. However, customs practiced at the roots of prayer for everyone should not be changed except from the custom of one’s place — such as Ashkenaz nusach to Sephardic or the reverse, and all the like — for there are twelve gates in heaven opposite the twelve tribes, and every tribe has a gate and a custom alone. And regarding the law: Ashkenazim who pray with Sephardim or the reverse have fulfilled prayer. And all this is only regarding customs practiced at the roots of prayer; but what is mentioned in the Gemara or in the words of the poskim that they learned from the Gemara is equal for all, and no person has permission to practice his own custom.

(ה) From the congregation — see Magen Avraham that on Pesach, when the prayer leaders extend greatly in melodies, it is better to say the piyyut before one begins the blessing Yotzer Or, for mostly the piyyut is not like the blessing, and it will be considered an interruption if one pauses long enough to finish all of it; and lekhatchilah one should be concerned about this, as above in siman 65 in Mishnah Berurah — see there. And if one forgot to say the piyyut until one finished the blessing, it is forbidden to say it, for it is not better than al hanissim on Chanukah and Purim.

(ו) With them — and if one prays alone one should not say any piyyut in the middle of a blessing [per the later authorities]. Incidentally, I saw fit to mention here what many among the masses err on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur: when the prayer leader chants at the beginning of the blessing Yotzer Or the piyyut “Orot me’ofel” (“Lights from darkness”), he says “and it was”; afterward the masses begin immediately “Melekh” or “Salach na,” and they do not begin the blessing from the start — and one should warn them about this.
