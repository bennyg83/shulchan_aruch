The Ari z"l would not say piyyutim and pizmonim except what the early authorities arranged, such as those of Kalir that were enacted per true intent. However, customs practiced at the roots of prayer should not be changed from the custom of one's place, for there are twelve gates in heaven opposite the twelve tribes, and every tribe has a gate and a custom alone; except what is mentioned in the Gemara, which is equal for all (kavanot). And thus the Jerusalem Talmud: although we sent you the order of prayers, do not change from the custom of your fathers — end quote. And likewise in Sha'arei Chaim siman 256. And he wrote that also the verses that they recite over the piyyutim need to be chanted, as they chant kerovatz. And in Shlah he expanded in the name of geonim that it is a mitzvah to say piyyutim, and when the poet [says] R' Elazar and "living creatures that stand here," etc., "fire blazed around him" — end quote. One should not separate oneself, etc. And likewise R' Moshe Mintz in siman 87 — only he wrote that his custom was to say the piyyut and afterward begin the blessing Yotzer Or, so as not to interrupt in the middle of the blessing; and if one prayed alone one should not say any piyyutim in the middle of the blessing — end quote. However, at the end of siman 54 Rama wrote that it is forbidden to interrupt between Yishtabach and Yotzer; for indeed, when one says it in the middle of the blessing, it is like the blessing. And see siman 119, seif 1, in the Rama gloss. However, on Pesach, when the prayer leaders extend greatly in melodies, it is better to say the piyyut before one begins the blessing, for mostly it is not like the blessing, as explained above. And it seems to me that if one forgot to say the piyyut until one finished the blessing, it is forbidden to say it, for it is not better than al hanissim on Chanukah and Purim — see siman 294. And further, for indeed when one says it in the middle of the blessing, it is like the blessing; but between one blessing and another it is forbidden to interrupt.
