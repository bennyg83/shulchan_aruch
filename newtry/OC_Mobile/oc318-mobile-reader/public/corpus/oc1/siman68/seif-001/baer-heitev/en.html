From the congregation. And the Ari z"l did not say piyyutim and pizmonim except what the early ones arranged, such as Kalir who arranged per true intent; and likewise he did not say Yigdal. However, customs practiced at the roots of prayer should not be changed from the custom of one's place, for there are twelve gates in heaven opposite the twelve tribes, and every tribe has a gate and a custom alone; except what is mentioned in Shas, which is equal for all kavanot. And in Shlah he expanded in the name of geonim that it is a mitzvah to say piyyutim, and when the poet R' Elazar and "living creatures that are here," etc., — fire blazed around him (Arizal). R' Moshe Mintz wrote in siman 87 that his custom was to say the piyyut and afterward begin the blessing Yotzer Or, so as not to interrupt in the middle of the blessing. And if one prays alone, one should not say any piyyut in the middle of the blessing — end quote. And Magen Avraham wrote: however, at the end of siman 54 Rama wrote that it is forbidden to interrupt between Yishtabach and Yotzer; but when one says it in the middle of the blessing, it is like the blessing. However, on Pesach, when the prayer leaders extend greatly in melodies, it is better to say the piyyut before one begins the blessing, for mostly it is not like the blessing. And if one forgot to say the piyyut until one finished the blessing, it is forbidden to say it, for it is not better than al hanissim on Chanukah and Purim — end quote.
