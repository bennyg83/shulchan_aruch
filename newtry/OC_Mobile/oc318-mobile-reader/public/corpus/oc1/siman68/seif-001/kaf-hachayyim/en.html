(1) [Seif 1] There are places that interrupt in the blessings of Keriat Shema to say piyyutim, etc. And thus Sha'ar haKavanot page 50b regarding pizmonim and piyyutim that the later ones enacted: know that my teacher z"l would not say any pizmon or piyyut, and he requested of those that the later ones arranged, such as R' Shlomo ibn Gabirol and the like, because these later ones did not know the ways of Kabbalah and do not know what they said and err in the order of their words without any knowledge at all; and in particular the pizmon Yigdal Elohim Chai, etc., and likewise Vidui Ashamnu in saying 'u'vefe'el,' etc.; and likewise on Yom Kippur he would not say the vidui of Rabbeinu Nissim and R' Shmuel ibn Adrutni and R' Yitzchak ben Yisrael, who each enacted a vidui in Maariv and Shacharit and Musaf and Minchah and Ne'ilah — and he would not say them at all; but he would say the prayers and requests that the early ones enacted, such as the prayer of R' Akiva and the prayer of R' Elazar ben Arach and the prayer of R' Nechunya ben haKana, and likewise he would say all the piyyutim and pizmonim that R' Elazar haKalir enacted — behold they are in the Ashkenaz machzor, for all these early ones enacted their words per the wisdom of truth and knew what they enacted; and even if they are in the middle of the blessings of Yotzer he would say them, because they were tannaim and knew what they were enacting, and all their words were arranged per truth. And I heard in the name of my teacher z"l that the Kalir who arranged kerovatz in the Ashkenaz machzor is R' Elazar bar Shimon, as in the midrash (and Beit Yosef brought it) according to 'from all the dust of the peddler' — this R' Elazar bar Shimon, who was a reader, tanna, poet, and kerovatz; but I did not hear this from my teacher z"l.
(1) And indeed in the roots of the customs themselves there are many differences and many variations in the matter of the nusach of the prayers themselves, aside from the matter of the pizmonim and piyyutim added within the prayers; but in the nusach of the blessings and prayers themselves there are many variations between prayer books — between the custom of Sepharad and the custom of Catalonia and the custom of Ashkenaz and the like. Here my teacher z"l told me that in the firmament there are twelve windows opposite the twelve tribes, and every tribe and tribe — its prayer ascends through one gate designated for it; and these are the twelve gates mentioned at the end of Ezekiel. And there is no doubt that if all the tribes' prayers were equal, there would be no need for twelve windows and gates, and every gate has its way by itself; rather it is certainly compelled that since their prayers differ, therefore they need special gates for every tribe and tribe, for per the root and source of the souls of that tribe, so must be the order of its prayer. Therefore it is fitting for each and every one to hold to the order of his prayer as the custom of his fathers, for we do not know who is of this tribe and who is of that tribe; and since his fathers held that custom, perhaps he is of that tribe to whom that custom is fitting — and now he comes to annul it, and his prayer does not ascend on high if not by that order. But you should know that this is only regarding varied words in the middle of prayer and the like — such as to precede Hodu before Barukh she'amar or afterward and the like; but what is founded per the explicit law in the Talmud — that matter is equal for every soul, and there is no difference among them in all the tribes — end quote. And likewise in Sefer Pa'ach, beginning of Sha'ar haTefillah; and thus the Jerusalem Talmud: although we sent you the order of prayer, do not change from the custom of your fathers — end quote. And likewise in Sha'arei Chaim siman 256. Magen Avraham beginning of the siman, siman 12 note 1. Kaf haChayyim siman 12 note 9.
