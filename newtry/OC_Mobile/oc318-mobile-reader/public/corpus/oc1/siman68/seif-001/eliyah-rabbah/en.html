[1] One should not separate, etc. And likewise Maharil wrote that he was very careful to say kerovatz, and he would be angry at the youths who studied in the synagogue and did not pay attention to say kerovatz — up to here. And likewise he wrote in the responsum of Maharam Mintz siman 87, and he concluded that he would not begin the blessing 'Barukh attah Hashem Yotzer Or,' etc., 'Or haOlam,' etc., with the prayer leader as the whole congregation says; rather he would begin without a blessing — 'Or li' or other Yotzer sections with the congregation; and after the Yotzer was finished, the prayer leader would begin 'haMe'ir la'aretz,' and then he began the blessing 'Barukh attah Hashem Yotzer Or,' etc., 'haMe'ir la'aretz,' etc.; and the reason is in order to reduce interruption. And see below siman 112, where I wrote of the virtues of piyyutim. And Magen Avraham wrote that in the middle of a blessing it is like the blessing; but between Yishtabach and Yotzer it is explained at the end of siman 57 that it is forbidden to interrupt. He further wrote that if one forgot to say the piyyut until one finished the blessing, it is forbidden to interrupt between blessing and blessing.
