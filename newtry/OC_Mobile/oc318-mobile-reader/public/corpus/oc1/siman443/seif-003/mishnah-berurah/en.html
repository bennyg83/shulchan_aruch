(15) After six hours. That is to say, even though at that point it is forbidden in benefit according to the Torah according to most poskim, and if so by buying it he transgresses a Torah prohibition, nevertheless after the fact it does not seize its proceeds. For we have not found that a prohibition takes effect on the item exchanged for it except regarding idolatry, hekdesh, and shevi'is. According to this, the same is true where the prohibition is only rabbinic, as explained in Chullin folio 4b in Rashi and Tosafos. It also stands to reason, according to this, that if he sold it at the beginning of the sixth hour, when its prohibition is also rabbinic, or even on Pesach itself, it likewise does not seize its proceeds.
(16) It is permitted. This means even for the very person who bought it, and all the more so that others may benefit from that item. Know that the view of the Mechaber is not agreed to by all, for there are among the great poskim those who maintain that even though others may benefit from the exchange of chametz, because chametz does not seize its proceeds after the fact, nevertheless the Sages penalized the owner of the chametz himself that he should not benefit from the money, since he committed a prohibition. It is permitted for him only where others sold or exchanged his chametz without his knowledge. One should be stringent like these poskim where there is no great loss. Know further that even according to this view, if he transgressed and bought some item with the money, it is permitted to benefit from that item, for it is an exchange of an exchange, and is permitted. The later authorities further wrote that even according to the stringent view regarding chametz, forbidding its exchange to the owner himself, this applies only when he took the exchange for the chametz at the time that he gave the chametz. But if he took the exchange for the chametz later, according to all opinions it is permitted even for him, for once the non-Jew took the chametz into his domain, the chametz was acquired by him immediately through his taking it, and what was given to the Jew afterward, whether its money or its exchange, is merely a gift and has no connection at all to the chametz, and is not called its exchange.
(17) It does not seize its proceeds. But the chametz itself always remains in its prohibition wherever it is, and it is forbidden for anyone to take it from the non-Jew, even if the non-Jew went back and exchanged it for something else, and even if the Jew sold it to the non-Jew after Pesach. Since the Sages placed a prohibition upon it, it is like a piece of forbidden food. The Chok Yaakov wrote that grain that became chametz, Pesach passed over it, and it was then planted, its growths are permitted, for growths are like exchanges. In the work Mekor Chaim he disagrees with him, and his view is that growths are worse than exchanges and are forbidden according to all opinions. See Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chayim siman 104.
