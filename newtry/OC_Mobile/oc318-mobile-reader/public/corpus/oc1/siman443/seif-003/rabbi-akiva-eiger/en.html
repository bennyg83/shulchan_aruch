The reason he did not compare it simply to stolen chametz is that there is an explicit Mishnah that he can say, 'Here is yours before you,' as we rule in Choshen Mishpat siman 363. One can say that a guardian is worse than a robber, as Tosafos writes in Bava Kamma 56b regarding where part of it rotted. Therefore our master learned it from the sugya in Bava Kamma folio 45, namely from an ox whose judgment was completed. According to Rabbi Yehudah, who holds that they complete its judgment even not in its owner's presence, he can say, 'Here is yours before you'; and the same is true for us regarding chametz. And the reason that, in truth, where it rotted a guardian is worse than a robber is that a guardian, upon whom it is incumbent to guard it, has negligence that is like active damage. Just as if he had rotted them with his hands he would be liable under the law of a damager, so too his negligence is treated that way. This is unlike a robber, who is included in the law of returning the item. But with an ox whose judgment was completed, and likewise with chametz, even if he is considered like a damager, it is nevertheless damage that is not visible, and we rule that it is not called damage. Although one who actively destroys such an item is liable as a penalty, nevertheless one can say that they did not impose a penalty on a guardian where the loss occurred on its own. However, in my humble opinion there is no proof, for one can well say that the guardian's negligence is like actual damage, and even for damage that is not visible he is liable rabbinically. An ox whose judgment was completed is different, because there the rabbinic penalty of 'so that each person should not go and...' does not apply. This is proven from the words of Tosafos there in their question, for if it were damage that is not visible, even if he handed it over actively he should be exempt. In Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 3 of the laws of theft, he indeed wonders about this, for rabbinically he is liable, as with one who renders another's food tamei or mixes terumah into it. Tosafos there in Gittin was careful about this, explaining that here the concern of 'so that each person should not go' does not apply; see there. Thus there, even if done actively, he is exempt. But in general one can say that whenever, through the guardian's negligence, the item is damaged with damage that is not visible, he is like a damager and is included in the enactment of Chazal to obligate him when done intentionally. In this respect a guardian is worse than a robber, as in the case where it rotted. And that which they say in Bava Kamma, 'If so, let them disagree regarding chametz on Pesach,' can be explained as referring to stolen chametz: if it were so that by an ox, where the concern of 'so that each person should not go' does not apply, they disagree about damage that is not visible and whether he can say, 'Here is yours before you,' then let them disagree regarding chametz and a robber. Therefore the Gemara establishes their dispute as concerning whether they complete its judgment not in its owner's presence. But regarding chametz in the case of a robber, everyone agrees that he can say, 'Here is yours before you.' Regarding a guardian and chametz, however, one can say that he is liable, for a guardian is liable by penalty for damage that is not visible, just like a damager. The language of Rashi is precise, for he wrote, 'Let them disagree regarding chametz in a case of theft.' See Even HaOzer. According to what was stated above, Rashi wrote this precisely. Nevertheless, it still seems to me that there is proof for the Magen Avraham that a guardian who was negligent is exempt for damage that is not visible, from what we rule in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah siman 127, that since the loss of his wage is small, he is not believed to say, 'Your wine was used for nesach.' If we were to say that a guardian who was negligent is liable for damage that is not visible, the ruling should have distinguished and said that where the wine was used for nesach through negligence he is believed, since he loses the whole amount. It must be, therefore, like the Magen Avraham. Examine this carefully.
