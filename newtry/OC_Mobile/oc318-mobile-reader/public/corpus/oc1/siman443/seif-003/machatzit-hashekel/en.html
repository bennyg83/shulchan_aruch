(s.k. 3) A third of the day, etc. Until five and a half hours. For the two hours that the latter view gives are equal hours, not proportional hours. See the Taz. So too it is implied in Tosafos, folio 2a, s.v. 'But we rule,' etc., and folio 11a, s.v. 'One says,' etc. So wrote the Bach in siman 451.
And there is only a small difference between them. Many understood that he means there is only a small difference between the Bach and the Levush and Lechem Chamudos, and therefore they ask that there is a great distance between them. I will incline my ear with an example so that it will be readily understood. If the day is sixteen hours long, meaning that alos hashachar is at the end of the fourth hour after midnight and tzeis hakochavim is at the end of the eighth hour after midday, then according to the Bach, a third of this is five hours and a third. Therefore the time for eating ends after nine hours and a third from midnight, for alos hashachar is at the end of the fourth hour, and when you add five hours and a third you find as stated. But according to the Levush and Lechem Chamudos, the time is extended further. If one says like the Rambam, who holds that from alos hashachar until sunrise is the walking-distance of five mil, and likewise from sunset until tzeis hakochavim is five mil, then a mil is two-fifths of an hour; if so, from alos hashachar until sunrise is two hours, and, as will be explained, the same applies from sunset until tzeis hakochavim. Subtract four hours from the sixteen hours mentioned above, and twelve hours remain in the day; take a third of this, which is four hours, and add it to the end of the fourth hour after midnight when alos hashachar occurs, that is, six hours, namely two hours until sunrise and another four hours from sunrise onward. If so, the time for eating ends at the end of the tenth hour after midnight, and there are two-thirds of an hour between the Bach and the Levush and Lechem Chamudos. Even according to the truth of the matter, that the Levush rules like the opinion that from alos hashachar until tzeis hakochavim is four mil, and likewise from sunset until tzeis hakochavim, so that a mil is a quarter hour and half a quarter, as will be explained below, then from alos hashachar until sunrise is an hour and a half; when alos hashachar occurs at the end of the fourth hour after midnight, add an hour and a half, so sunrise is five and a half hours after midnight, and the same applies from sunset until tzeis hakochavim. If so, the day from sunrise until sunset is thirteen hours. Take a third of that, which is four hours and a third; if so, add to the fourth hour after midnight, when alos hashachar occurred, an hour and a half until sunrise, and another four hours and a third, which is a third of the day, making altogether nine hours, one half, and one third of an hour. Thus the time for eating according to the Levush extends more than according to the Bach by half an hour. However it may be, how did the Magen Avraham call this a small difference? In my humble opinion, my heart does not understand it that way. Rather, what the Magen Avraham wrote, 'and there is only a small difference between them,' refers to the Levush and the Lechem Chamudos. His intent is to explain that when the Magen Avraham wrote that the Levush and Lechem Chamudos calculate from sunrise until the end of sunset, this sounds as though they are equal in this law; but in truth the Levush and Lechem Chamudos also disagree, as will be explained. About this the Magen Avraham wrote that there is only a small difference between them, meaning between the Levush and Lechem Chamudos only, and therefore the Magen Avraham was not concerned for that small difference and grouped the view of the Levush and Lechem Chamudos together. For the Lechem Chamudos wrote in the first chapter of Berachos, siman 10, s.v. 'Until three hours,' etc., that Terumas HaDeshen calculates the day from alos hashachar until tzeis hakochavim, but the Rambam necessarily holds that we calculate the day from sunrise until sunset, because the Rambam wrote that the measure of a mil is two-fifths of an hour, and according to Terumas HaDeshen this calculation does not work. Perforce, the Rambam holds that we calculate from sunrise until sunset, and then the calculation is exact. For it states in the chapter Mi Shehayah Tamei that Rabbi Yehoshua said: an average person walks on a day in Nisan and Tishrei ten parsaos, which are forty mil, since each parsah is four mil. He holds like the opinion there that from alos hashachar until sunrise is the walking-distance of five mil, and likewise from sunset until tzeis hakochavim is five mil. If so, from sunrise until sunset remain thirty mil. Since in Nisan and Tishrei the day is twelve hours, if the measure of a mil is two-fifths of an hour, the calculation is exact, for thirty mil require sixty fifths of an hour, which are twelve hours, since five fifths are one hour. But according to what the Levush wrote in simanim 261 and 267, it appears that he holds like Rabbi Yehudah there, that from alos hashachar until sunrise is four mil, and likewise from sunset until tzeis hakochavim; from sunrise until sunset, which is twelve hours, there remain thirty-two mil. If so, the measure of a mil is a quarter hour and half a quarter, for thirty-two quarters are eight hours, and thirty-two half-quarters are four hours, together twelve hours. These are the words of Lechem Chamudos. According to this, there is a small difference between the Levush and the Lechem Chamudos. The Lechem Chamudos rules like the Rambam, that from alos hashachar until sunrise is five mil and likewise at sunset, and therefore for him a mil is two-fifths of an hour. If alos hashachar is at the end of the fourth hour after midnight, as in the example, then from alos hashachar until sunrise is five mil, which is two hours, so sunrise is at the end of the sixth hour after midnight; and likewise from sunset until tzeis hakochavim is two hours. Thus from sunrise until sunset there are exactly twelve hours. Take a third of them, namely four hours, and add them to the end of the sixth hour, the time of sunrise from which they begin to count; the time for eating therefore ends at the end of the tenth hour after midnight. But according to the Levush, who holds like the opinion that from alos hashachar until sunrise is four mil, the measure of a mil is a quarter and half a quarter of an hour, as stated. It is implied there in the Gemara and in Rashi, s.v. 'the Rabbis,' that those who shorten the amount calculate the people as setting out earlier and later: the opinion that there are only four mil from alos hashachar until sunrise, and likewise at sunset, holds that although we see people walking five mil before sunrise until sunrise, that is because they set out before alos hashachar by the measure of a mil, and likewise in the evening they walk a mil after tzeis hakochavim. Therefore the Rabbis thought that from alos hashachar until tzeis hakochavim is five mil. In truth, alos hashachar is a mil later than the Rabbis thought, because people set out to walk a mil before alos hashachar; see there. If so, according to the opinion that there are four mil from alos hashachar until sunrise, for him alos hashachar is necessarily later by the measure of a mil than according to the opinion of five mil, and the measure of a mil according to the opinion of four mil is a quarter and half a quarter of an hour, as stated. Therefore if according to Lechem Chamudos alos hashachar is at the end of the fourth hour, then according to the Levush, who holds four mil from alos hashachar until sunrise, alos hashachar is later by the measure of a mil and will be at four hours, a quarter, and half a quarter. Then calculate four mil until sunrise, which for him amount to an hour and a half; combine them, and sunrise will be five hours, three quarters, and half a quarter after midnight. If so, until midday is six hours and a quarter and a half, and likewise from midday until sunset. Thus from sunrise until sunset there are twelve hours and a quarter. Take a third of this, namely four hours and one-twelfth of an hour, which is five minutes, and add it to the five hours, three quarters, and half a quarter after midnight when sunrise occurs. It comes out to nine hours and one-tenth of an hour minus one twenty-fourth of an hour, that is, two and a half minutes. This is the small difference between the Lechem Chamudos and the Levush: according to the Lechem Chamudos, the time for eating ends at the end of the tenth hour, so there are only two and a half minutes between them. Examine this.
One may rely on the latter view, etc. See the Taz and what he wrote regarding the latter view. The Bach wrote that one who is stringent follows the approaches of Tosafos at the beginning of the chapter Kol Sha'ah, who explain the words of Rabbi Yehudah ben Beseira that even according to Beis Hillel, anything on which its owner's name is associated, such as types of liquor, is forbidden to sell to a non-Jew thirty days before Pesach unless he knows that the non-Jew will finish that liquor before Pesach, so that people should not say that the Jew is selling it through a non-Jew on Pesach. One who is stringent like their words shall be blessed. Chok Yaakov disagrees.
