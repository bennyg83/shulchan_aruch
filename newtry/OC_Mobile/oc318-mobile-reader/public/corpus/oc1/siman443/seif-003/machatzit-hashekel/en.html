(א) (s.k. 3) Third of day until five and half hours — those two hours of "some say" are equal hours not proportional; Taz; Tosafot 2a, 11a; Bach 491.

(ב) And there is only a slight difference between them — many understood that it means there is only a slight difference between Bach and Levush and Levushei Serad; therefore it is difficult, for there is great distance between them. And I will give an example so it will be understood easily: if today the day is long, sixteen hours — that is, 75 is the end of the fourth hour after midnight. And 90 is the end of the eighth hour after noon. If so, per Bach, from this calculation the fifth hour and a third of an hour is completed; if so, eating time ends after nine hours and a third after midnight, for 75 is the end of hour four and he added five hours and a third — find as stated; but for Levush and Levushei Serad the time extends more: per Rambam, who holds from 75 until 90 is five mil, and likewise from sunset until 90 is five mil — therefore a mil is two-fifths of an hour; if so from 75 until 90 is two hours, and as will be explained the calculation below. And likewise from sunset until 90 is two hours; if so, subtract four hours from sixteen — twelve hours remain; take from this the third which is four hours and add to the end of four after midnight when 75 — that is six hours, which is two hours until 90 and another four hours from 90 onward; if so, eating time ends at the end of hour ten after midnight; if so, there are two-thirds of an hour between Bach and Levush and Levushei Serad. And even per the truth that Levush ruled like one who says from 75 until 90 is four mil and likewise from sunset until 90 — therefore a mil is a quarter hour and half a quarter; and as will be explained below — if so from 75 until 90 is an hour and a half; and when 75 rises, end of hour four after midnight, add an hour and a half — if so 90 is five and a half hours after midnight; and likewise sunset until 90; if so the day is longer from 90 until sunset — thirteen hours; subtract the third, four hours and a twelfth of twelve hours, five minutes; combine with five hours three-quarters and a half after midnight when 90 — totals nine hours and a tenth of an hour less a twenty-fourth of an hour, two minutes and a half; and this slight matter is between Levushei Serad and Levush — for Levushei Serad eating ends at the end of hour ten, if so between them two minutes and a half; examine. However in my humble opinion it does not seem so, but what Magen Avraham said — and there is only a slight difference — refers to Levush and Levushei Serad; and his intent is to explain what Magen Avraham and Levush and Levushei Serad wrote to calculate, etc. — implying they agree in this law; but in truth Levush and Levushei Serad also disagree, as will be explained; and on this he wrote Magen Avraham: and there is only a slight difference between them, meaning between Levush and Levushei Serad only a slight difference; therefore Magen Avraham did not account for that slight difference, and the rule of Levush and Levushei Serad together. And it is that Levushei Serad wrote in the first chapter of Berachos siman 10 d.h. until three hours, etc. — that 90 considers the day from 75 until 90; but Rambam therefore holds they count the day from sunrise until sunset. Because Rambam wrote a mil is two-fifths of an hour — for Levushei Serad this calculation does not work; therefore Rambam holds from 90 until sunset, and then the calculation works: it states in chapter Mi she'aya tamei: R' Yehoshua said an average person walks on a day in Nisan and Tishrei ten parsaos, which are forty mil, for each parsa is four mil; he holds like one who says there from 75 until 90 is five mil and likewise sunset until 90 five mil — if so from 90 until sunset remains thirty mil; and since in Nisan and Tishrei the day is twelve hours, if a mil is two-fifths of an hour the calculation works: thirty mil are sixty-fifths, which are twelve hours, for five-fifths are one hour. But per Levush siman 261 and 267 it appears he holds like R' Yehuda there that from 75 until 90 is four mil and likewise sunset until 90; and from 90 until sunset, which is twelve hours, then thirty-two mil remain; if so a mil is a quarter hour and half a quarter, for thirty-two quarters are eight hours and thirty-two half-quarters four hours — together twelve hours — now Levushei Serad: therefore there is a slight difference between Levush and Levushei Serad, for Levushei Serad rules like Rambam that from 75 until 90 is five mil and likewise at sunset; if so per him a mil is two-fifths; if 75 is end of hour four after midnight as in the example, from 75 until 90 is five mil which are two hours — if so 90 is end of hour six after midnight; and likewise sunset until 90 two hours; if so from 90 until sunset twelve hours align — subtract the third, four hours, add to end of hour six which is 90 time from which they begin to count — if so eating time ends at end of hour ten after midnight. But for Levush, who holds like one who says four mil from 75 until 90 — if so a mil is a quarter and half a quarter hour as stated; and it implies in the Gemara and Rashi d.h. the Rabbis that they cut off — they count early and late. One who says only four mil from 75 until 90 and likewise at sunset holds: although people are seen walking before sunrise until sunrise five mil — they advance before 75 by a mil, and likewise in evening they walk a mil after 90; therefore the Rabbis thought from 75 until 90 is five mil. And in truth 75 is a mil later than what the Rabbis thought, for the world advances to walk a mil before 75 — see there; if so for one who says four mil from 75 until 90, therefore for him 75 is later than for one who says five mil — a mil; and a mil for one who says four mil is a quarter and half a quarter hour as stated; if so if for Levushei Serad 75 is end of hour four, for Levush who holds four mil from 75 until 90, 75 is later by a mil and will be at four and a quarter and half a quarter hour; afterward calculate four mil until 90 and for him they are an hour and a half; combine — 90 is five hours three-quarters and a half after midnight; if so until noon is six hours a quarter and a half; and likewise from noon until sunset; if so from 90 until sunset twelve hours and a quarter; subtract the third, four hours and a twelfth of twelve, five minutes; add to five hours three-quarters and a half after midnight when 90 — totals nine hours and a tenth of an hour less a twenty-fourth, two minutes and a half; and this slight matter is between Levushei Serad and Levush — for Levushei Serad eating ends at hour ten, if so between them two minutes and a half; examine.
