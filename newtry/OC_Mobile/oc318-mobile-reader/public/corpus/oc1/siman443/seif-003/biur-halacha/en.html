If they bought, etc., it is permitted. See Mishnah Berurah, where we brought the view of those who are stringent in this. Know that according to them, this is not specifically after six hours, which is a Torah-law prohibition according to most poskim. The same is true after Pesach: if one sold chametz over which Pesach had passed, it is also forbidden for the seller to benefit from its money, even though the prohibition of the chametz itself is only rabbinic, as explained in Chullin folio 4b in Rashi and Tosafos. According to this, it stands to reason that the same applies if he sold it at the beginning of the sixth hour, when its prohibition is also rabbinic: he is forbidden to benefit from the money. So too wrote the Gra.
It does not seize its proceeds. Know that all of this applies if he already received the money or the item exchanged for the chametz. But if he has not yet received the money, he is forbidden to accept it from the non-Jewish buyer. This is not only where there was no acquisition besides the money, where it is certainly forbidden, for by receiving the money he transgresses the Torah-law prohibition of benefiting from chametz. Even if the non-Jew performed meshichah earlier, according to some poskim meshichah alone does not acquire from a non-Jew; consequently when he receives the money from him, his acquisition is completed, and he transgresses according to the Torah if it was on Pesach, or rabbinically after Pesach. Rather, even if the chametz was already acquired by the non-Jew with a complete acquisition, it is initially forbidden for him to receive the money according to all opinions, because the primary benefit occurs at the time of receiving the money, as is explicit below in siman 450 se'if 4. See Yoreh De'ah simanim 132 and 144 and the later authorities there. So too wrote Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 16 of the laws of sale, in his conclusion; see there, where he discusses this at length.
