Permitted. The Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Bach that this is specifically if a maidservant bought it without his knowledge; then the householder may benefit from it, but the person who bought it is forbidden to benefit from it, end quote. Some later authorities are lenient in this, and so appears to be the view of the Shulchan Aruch here. My brother-in-law the Rav wrote in his work Even HaOzer that after six hours until night, an exchange of an exchange should indeed be permitted even for him. It seems to me that even one who is stringent for him regarding exchanges during Pesach refers specifically to clothing, fruit, and utensils, which are recognizable items; so it appears from Tosafos in the first chapter of Chullin, folio 4b, and it is explained there in the rulings of Tosafos that this answer is the main one for halachah. But its money is permitted even initially; so is implied by Tosafos at the beginning of Kol Sha'ah. See Responsa Sha'ar Ephraim, siman 4. All this is specifically that the exchange of the chametz is permitted, but the chametz itself, even if it was exchanged and then given back to the Jew, nevertheless remains under its original prohibition, as implied by the Shas and poskim regarding chametz of a Jew over which Pesach passed, which is forbidden in benefit. This is unlike what appears there in Responsa Sha'ar Ephraim, to permit even the chametz itself in benefit in such a case. In his final kuntres, my brother-in-law the Rav also objected to him in this, for it is plainly forbidden.
For chametz does not seize its proceeds. Its exchanges are permitted. It seems to me that the same applies to grain that became chametz, Pesach passed over it, and it was planted: its growths are permitted, because exchanges are like growths. See Nedarim folios 47 and 52 and 57 in the sugya of the Shas, and the first chapter of Shabbos. Although growths of terumah are forbidden because perhaps he will keep it and come to a stumbling block, regarding chametz over which Pesach passed, whose prohibition is rabbinic, they did not decree this in a case where what he planted was entirely consumed. So too is written in Sefarim, and Tosafos in the first chapter of Pesachim writes that growths of benefit-prohibitions are permitted.
