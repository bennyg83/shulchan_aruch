He should sell it to a non-Jew, etc. If the owner were here, he would have to destroy it, as stated above at the end of siman 440. If a Jew lent money to another Jew on chametz as a pledge, it appears at first glance that the lender must destroy it. For specifically regarding a non-Jew we say in siman 441 that a non-Jew does not acquire a pledge from a Jew, and likewise the reverse; but a Jew from a Jew does acquire a pledge, as stated in the chapter Kol Sha'ah from the verse, 'And to you it shall be righteousness.' If he does not acquire the pledge, from where would there be righteousness? If so, it belongs to the lender. There is further proof, for if he betrothed a woman with a pledge upon which he lent money, she is betrothed, as written in Even HaEzer. However, in my humble opinion it seems that nevertheless the body of the pledge belongs to the borrower, for it says in the chapter HaParah, cited in Choshen Mishpat siman 72, regarding a Jew's pledge that was with a ger, and the ger died, and another Jew came and took possession of the pledge, that he is obligated to return it to the Jew, because the body of the pledge belongs to the Jew; the ger merely had a lien upon it, and once he died, his lien lapsed. If so, here too the borrower is obligated to destroy it, for the body of it is his. And if you will say that it is not in his power to destroy it, since the lender has a debt upon it and will not give it to him without money, nevertheless, if some circumstance occurred such that they did not destroy it and the time of its prohibition arrived, it appears that the borrower is obligated to pay what the lender lent him, and the loss of the chametz is upon the borrower, since its body is his. Similarly, if the lender sells it to a non-Jew on erev Pesach, when chametz is cheap, as Rashi writes in the first chapter, folio 14, in the incident of Yochanan Chakukah, the loss is upon the borrower, as appears to me.
