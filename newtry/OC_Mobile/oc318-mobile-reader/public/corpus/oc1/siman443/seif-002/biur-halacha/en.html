To a non-Jew. See Magen Avraham, who wrote that if it is something for which buyers will not readily be found in the fifth hour, it is permitted to sell it earlier. We did not copy it that way, because at first glance it sounds as though, out of concern that it may become cheaper, he permits selling it earlier; and some later authorities explained it that way. In truth this certainly cannot be said, as Mekor Chaim wrote. Therefore we copied the words of Mekor Chaim, that this applies specifically where he is concerned that he will not find buyers at all afterward; he explains there that this is the intent of the Magen Avraham. See there, for even in this case he remains in doubt, since from Choshen Mishpat siman 292 se'if 18 it is proven that even in a case where he will lose it completely he should not sell, but should wait for the owners. As the Mechaber writes there, one may not touch a deposit even if he knows with certainty that it will become cheaper at a given time or that the authorities will seize it, because perhaps the owners will come first and take it from him. In truth, Eliyah Rabbah already discussed this. See there, where he answers that perhaps the Magen Avraham's intent is specifically very close to the fifth hour, meaning that it is unlikely that the owners will come in such a short time. But in truth this does not fit the words of the Magen Avraham, who made the reason depend on not finding buyers in the fifth hour. Even so, we did not want to reject the words of the Magen Avraham entirely, for his reasoning is plausible. The words of the Mechaber there in Choshen Mishpat, in the law of seizure by the authorities, are difficult logically, for there he compares the law of seizure by the authorities to the previous se'if, whereas chametz is forbidden to sell until the fifth hour, and there too a Torah decree is like seizure by the authorities. In truth it appears to be a proof to the contrary, for from the fifth hour onward they permitted him to sell, and he need not wait for the owners until the sixth hour, when the Torah would seize it. This shows that where he will lose it entirely, he need not wait because perhaps the owners will come. If so, according to this one can also say further that if he estimates that he will not find buyers in the fifth hour, and if so he will lose it entirely, he need not wait even until the fifth hour, and in the fourth hour he may sell when he sees that the owners have not come, as the Magen Avraham says. See there in the Sema; perhaps he intended our point, namely that there too he need not wait until the last time when the authorities will come, because of the reason that perhaps the owners will come, but one also gains a little time earlier, as in our case, the fifth hour. According to this, there is no difficulty against the Magen Avraham from there. However, his language is unclear and requires analysis.
