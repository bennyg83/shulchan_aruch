Magen Avraham s.k. 3. And it seems to me that if he forgot and did not sell. At first glance there is room to discuss: although with respect to the prohibition of benefit it is a rabbinic hour, and in rabbinic matters we rely on the lenient position, nevertheless after the time of Torah-law hours arrives, regarding the obligation of tashbisu, perhaps we follow the relative length of the day, and his sale was not a sale, so that he is obligated according to the Torah to destroy it. Even though one could say that he does not transgress the prohibition of bal yera'eh, for one may say that if he threw it outside or sold it to a non-Jew on Pesach he does not transgress bal yera'eh since it is not in his possession; see Sha'agas Aryeh. Nevertheless, he does not fulfill the positive mitzvah of tashbisu when he does not destroy it from the world. It appears that this is not difficult at all. For it seems to me that if he throws it outside his domain in the manner of hefker during the sixth hour, the obligation of destruction does not take effect, since before the time of his obligation of destruction the chametz is not in his possession. Although if he nullified it in the sixth hour that does not help to remove his Torah-law obligation, that is because it is not his and the nullification does not take effect, so it is as though he did not nullify it. But if he threw it outside, so that the chametz is not within his boundary, it helps even in the sixth hour. Only if he threw it out after the sixth hour, when the obligation of destruction had already taken effect upon him, is he obligated specifically to destroy it from the world. One can also say that since in truth it is a rabbinic doubt regarding benefit, and a rabbinic doubt is treated leniently, it is like a definite case of being permitted in benefit, and it is a proper sale. Examine this carefully.
