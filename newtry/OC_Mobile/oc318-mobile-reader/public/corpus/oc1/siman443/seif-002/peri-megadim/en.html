The Taz wrote that here he speaks of actual chametz; the word 'actual' is missing. So too wrote the Bach. The Taz concluded that nevertheless what is written in Even HaEzer 28 requires analysis, for according to Rabbeinu Tam, who explains 'from six and onward' as from seven, the time is the main factor, and rabbinic hours even by actual chametz would make the kiddushin valid. This requires analysis. Perhaps this is what the Beis Yosef wrote here and in the name of the Ramban; see there. See Chok Yaakov s.k. 6 and Olas Shabbos. Regarding nukshah in the fifth hour, which is forbidden in eating, and in the sixth in benefit, he left the matter requiring analysis. See Chok Yaakov s.k. 3 and Rashi 7a: if we say that wheat of Kordanya is nukshah, this is explained, and see the Magen Avraham. See Rashi 21b, s.v. One can say that according to Rabbi Yehudah he stated the halachah like an anonymous Mishnah, and he holds that the halachah follows the Mishnah at the beginning of chapter Ein Ovrin regarding nukshah and mixtures, and that the Mishnah 'si'ur must be burned' follows Rabbi Yehudah; and he wrote well that if it meant from seven this would be obvious and what would he teach us? He already said the halachah follows an anonymous Mishnah. Nukshah, even on Pesach itself, is rabbinic for us, and even according to the view that a half-measure before its time is rabbinic, see Tosafos at the beginning of Pesachim, nevertheless they decreed six and five because of 'do not confuse.' A mixture that has less than sixty is certainly forbidden in eating in the fifth hour; whether one may add to make sixty, see Yoreh De'ah 99 in Taz s.k. 12 and below siman 677. From six until night, where there is less than sixty, if he sells it excluding the value of the forbidden part, this requires analysis; see siman 447 in the Magen Avraham s.k. 45. After Pesach we are lenient because it is a penalty, unlike before its time until night, where benefit is forbidden. Even with an item whose prohibition is rabbinic, one should say that it is forbidden, and this will be explained below, with Hashem's help.
And it is. One knows of two pots in the fifth hour: one contains a rabbinic prohibition and one contains permitted food, and chametz less than sixty fell in, and it is not known into which one it fell. In the fifth hour it is permitted to eat, for I say it fell into the prohibited pot. But in the sixth hour, when both are forbidden, for any small amount with which you spoil it - what did you see to distinguish? So too here in the sixth hour, one spoils it with respect to benefit. If the dish remained from the fifth hour into the sixth, it requires analysis whether we say it was already permitted. One can say that it is like a case where one walked on one path and handled taharos and ate them, and then walked on the second path; when both pure items still exist, both remain in suspense. Even though they had already been declared pure, they return to their prohibition. See the words of the Rama there, and this is not like the Pri Chadash.
