(1) Forbidden in benefit. This is according to the Torah. The Gemara derives it from the verse, 'You shall not eat chametz upon it,' meaning upon the Pesach offering, and Chazal received that this means: you shall not eat chametz from the time that is fit for slaughtering the Pesach offering, that is, from midday onward, which is bein ha'arbayim. From the time that it is forbidden in eating it is forbidden in benefit. However, there is still no liability of kareis for eating it until night. Likewise regarding bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei, several poskim agreed that one does not transgress by keeping chametz from six hours and onward so long as the days of Pesach themselves have not arrived, for it is written, 'For seven days leaven shall not be found in your houses,' etc., and, 'Leaven shall not be seen by you in all your borders for seven days.' Nevertheless, even though he does not transgress bal yera'eh, he transgresses every moment that he keeps chametz in his house the positive mitzvah of tashbisu se'or mibateichem, which refers to erev Pesach from six hours and onward, as explained in the Shas.
(2) And even a non-Jew's chametz. For in the verse the Torah did not write 'your chametz,' but simply 'chametz,' whether of a Jew or of a non-Jew. The main place this law is brought in the poskim is regarding Pesach, and the Rema copied it here to teach us that the same law applies, that it is forbidden also from six hours and onward.
(3) Two hours earlier. The reason is because of a cloudy day, for a person is liable then to err by two hours since the sun is not shining. Even chametz nukshah, which is only rabbinic chametz, is also forbidden during these two hours, and all the more so chametz through mixtures. In Responsa Noda BiYehuda, siman 21, he is lenient regarding eating nukshah in the fifth hour.
(4) A large amount together. Even something on which its owner's name is associated, such as where he sold a storehouse of liquor, we do not decree that people will suspect him of having sold it to the non-Jew on Pesach when they see the non-Jew holding it on Pesach, since in any event he sold it to him at a time when it was permitted to sell it. This is the view of the later authorities, unlike the Bach.
(5) Provided that he stands over them. This law also applies before these two hours, for from the night of the fourteenth and onward, when he is obligated in bedikas chametz, he is obligated to be careful about this.
(6) They forbade it also in benefit. This is a decree because of the seventh hour, when it is forbidden in benefit according to the Torah. Therefore at that point it is forbidden to sell it to a non-Jew, to give it to him as a gift, or to feed it to an animal, beast, or bird. If there is a deceased person to bury on erev Pesach in the morning, if there is time to bury him and time will remain for eating chametz, they should bury the deceased before eating, for it is not proper for the members of the burial group to eat before burying the deceased, as explained in siman 526 in the Taz, s.k. 6. But if the time is pressed and it is possible that no time will remain for eating, it is better to eat first, then perform the destruction of chametz at its time, and afterward attend to the needs of burying the deceased.
(7) According to the nature of the day. This means that we always calculate the day as twelve hours, and consequently on long days we reckon with long hours. Therefore, in a leap year, it is permitted to eat until a third of the day even though the day is longer than twelve of our hours, such as when a third of the day arrives after five hours. Know that according to this, the same applies in countries where the days before Pesach are short: the twelve hours are divided into small hours, and one is permitted to eat only until a third of that day.
(8) Until a third of the day. The day is calculated from dawn until tzeis hakochavim; so wrote many later authorities. Some say that it is calculated from sunrise until sunset, and until a third of that measure it is permitted to eat chametz; this is a leniency in our matter. The view of the Gra is like them. Initially, it is good to be stringent like the first view.
(9) And some say until two hours, etc. This means ordinary equal hours, each one being one twenty-fourth of a full day and night. Therefore, even if the day is long, it is permitted to eat more than a third of the day, namely until the end of the tenth hour from midnight. Likewise, regarding the sale of chametz, according to this view it is permitted until the end of the eleventh hour. The poskim wrote that the main law follows the first view, and one may eat only until a third of the day. Likewise regarding the sale of chametz, whose time is one hour longer, we calculate proportional hours. Nevertheless, after the fact, if he has still not sold and there is a great loss, one may rely on the latter view, and he has time to sell it until one hour before midday.
