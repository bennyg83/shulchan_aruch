1) [Se'if 1] Chametz from six hours onward. This means from the end of the sixth hour, which is the beginning of the seventh. Peri Chadash. Anyone who eats chametz on the fourteenth from the beginning of the seventh hour onward is flogged under Torah law. Rambam, chapter 1, halachah 8. Maggid Mishneh writes there that this is the opinion of the Geonim and is the primary ruling. See there. This is also the opinion of Rosh and Tur, contrary to the lenient authorities cited by Tur, as stated in the following subsection. Nevertheless, one does not violate the prohibitions against chametz being seen and found until nightfall; before then, one violates only the positive commandment to eliminate it. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 3, halachah 7; Magen Avraham, subsection 1. Rashi in Bava Kama 29b, however, maintains that from the sixth hour onward one also violates the prohibitions against chametz being seen and found. See there. Chok Yaakov, subsection 1, cites his opinion. Levush at the beginning of siman 431, however, agrees with Maggid Mishneh that one does not violate the prohibition against chametz being seen until nightfall. See there. This is also the opinion of the later authorities: Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Mekor Chaim, subsection 1; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, at the beginning of siman 431; Chayei Adam, rule 123, subsection 1. Chayei Adam writes that Rashi's words may also be explained this way. See there. Chemed Moshe writes likewise there.
2) There. It is forbidden for benefit. This prohibition is under Torah law. Rambam, there, halachah 9. Maggid Mishneh likewise agrees there in halachah 8. See there. The widespread custom throughout the Jewish people is likewise to prohibit benefit from chametz from six hours onward. One who arrogantly issues rulings before unlearned people and rules leniently on the basis of Baal HaMaor deserves rebuke. Even with matters that are permitted but that others have customarily treated as prohibited, one may not permit them in their presence. This is certainly so here, where all the Sages of the Jewish people prohibit it. The view of Baal HaMaor, who is an individual authority, does not stand against the majority. Furthermore, this prohibition has become accepted throughout the Jewish people because their ancestors ruled according to the prohibiting authorities. Beis Yosef. Levush likewise rules that benefit is prohibited under Torah law. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1; Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Peri Chadash; Chok Yosef, subsection 1; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1 in this siman and subsection 2 in siman 431; Chayei Adam, rule 123, subsection 1, rule likewise.
3) There. It is forbidden for benefit. Regarding whether one is flogged for deriving benefit without eating, see Peri Chadash's extensive discussion, and the comments of Mateh Yehudah and Nehar Shalom, subsection 1. See there. Eshel Avraham, subsection 1, likewise cites a dispute as to whether one is flogged for deriving benefit. See there.
