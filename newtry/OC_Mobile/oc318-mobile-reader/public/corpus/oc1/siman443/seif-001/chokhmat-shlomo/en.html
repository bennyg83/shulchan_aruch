Se'if 1. Chametz from six hours and onward on the fourteenth is forbidden in benefit, etc. Note: the Tur concludes, 'And if he betrothed a woman with it, she is not betrothed.' The later authorities discussed this at length; see Chok Yaakov, who elaborated on it. I am astonished at all of them: how did they not see the Yerushalmi on this matter? This is the language of the Yerushalmi in Pesachim chapter 2, halachah 4: Rabbi Meir says, from six hours and onward it is rabbinic; Rabbi Yehudah says, from six hours and onward it is Torah law. What is Rabbi Meir's reason? For it is written, 'But on the first day' - this means the fifteenth. Could it mean from when it gets dark? The verse teaches, 'but on the first day.' How is this? Give him one hour before sunset, etc. From this there is a proof for the Rambam's view that the positive mitzvah of tashbisu applies before six, for just as according to Rabbi Meir the positive mitzvah of tashbisu applies before night, before the time of its prohibition, so too according to us, where it is forbidden according to the Torah at six, tashbisu applies before the time of its prohibition. Rav said: according to Rabbi Meir, one who betroths from six hours and onward has done nothing. And it is proper: these are hard desert-wheats, which the Korban HaEidah explains as chametz nukshah, etc. From the Yerushalmi it is explicit that nukshah is only rabbinic, and it is also explicit that one who betroths with chametz during rabbinic hours is not betrothed. It is also explicit that Torah-level chametz during rabbinic hours and rabbinic chametz during Torah-law hours are equal regarding kiddushin, and in both she is not betrothed. I am therefore astonished at the Ramban cited by the Beis Yosef and the Chok Yaakov, who holds that with rabbinic chametz during Torah-law hours she is not betrothed, but with Torah-level chametz during rabbinic hours we are concerned for kiddushin. If so, what proof does the Yerushalmi bring from rabbinic chametz during Torah-law hours, where she is not betrothed, to Torah-level chametz during rabbinic hours, where she is not betrothed? One could distinguish as the Ramban did, and this requires great analysis. While dealing with this I saw the Korban HaEidah there and the Shiurei Korban, s.v. 'and consecrated things do not effect kiddushin, for we do not redeem consecrated items in order to feed them to dogs.' He wonders there that it is hekdesh for Temple maintenance, etc. I am astonished at him: how did he miss the words of our Shas in Pesachim folio 29b, which says there that they dispute whether one redeems consecrated items in order to feed them to dogs, and Tosafos there asked the Korban HaEidah's difficulty and answered that rabbinically one does not redeem. This is astonishing. I have another difficulty with the Korban HaEidah's words from what it says there: if he made it terumah, it is not terumah, because terumah was given only for eating. I am astonished how he will explain the Mishnah cited by the Shas in Pesachim chapter 2, folio 33b, which is a Mishnah in Terumos: one may not separate from the tamei for the tahor, but if he separated unwittingly, his terumah is terumah. The answer of the Shas there does not apply here, for it speaks of ordinary chametz and according to Rabbi Meir. This all requires analysis. See Chok Yaakov in this siman, where he discussed at length Rashi's language in Bava Kamma folio 98b, why Rashi says the Gemara should have disputed regarding theft and did not mention a guardian.
