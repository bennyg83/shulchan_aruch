Forbidden in benefit. This is according to the Torah, as the Rambam writes in chapter 1. Nevertheless, he does not transgress bal yera'eh until night; he violates only the positive mitzvah of tashbisu, as the Maggid Mishneh writes in chapter 3.
Two hours earlier. For we are concerned that on a cloudy day they may err by two hours; but the fourth hour is everyone's mealtime, and one will not come to err. If there was a death on erev Pesach, they took the deceased out to the cemetery, returned home and ate, and afterward buried him, as Maharil writes. See what I wrote at the end of siman 506, that some say it is forbidden to eat before burying the deceased; here it is different, because the hour is passing, and they had also already begun taking him out. Nevertheless, this is difficult to me, for regarding Keri'as Shema we rule that one does not take the deceased out close to Keri'as Shema, and if they began they do not interrupt, as written in siman 72 se'if 2. If so, how may they interrupt for eating? Therefore it seems to me that it is better to eat first and afterward take the deceased out. If they already began taking him out, they do not interrupt for eating; and if afterward they will be unable to eat, they should eat matzah ashirah, or meat and fish.
A third of the day. For example, if the day is fifteen hours, it is permitted to eat until the beginning of the sixth hour; and according to the latter view, it is permitted to eat until five and a half hours. The Bach wrote: we follow the stringent ruling like the first opinion, and calculate from dawn until tzeis hakochavim; so it appears from Tosafos, end quote. The Levush and Lechem Chamudos in siman 233 wrote to calculate from sunrise until the end of sunset; see there. There is only a small difference between them. It seems to me that if one forgot and did not sell his chametz, one may rely on the latter view, and if there is more than an hour until midday he may sell it. See Rabbi Levi ben Chaviv, siman 138, and Rabbi Moshe Alashkar, siman 61.
