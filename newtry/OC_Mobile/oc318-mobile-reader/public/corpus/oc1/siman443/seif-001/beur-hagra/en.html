Se'if 1, chametz from six hours, etc., in benefit. The Rambam and the Rosh; and the Maggid Mishneh wrote that all the Geonim likewise agreed. Although after its time we rule like Rabbi Shimon, before its time we rule like Rabbi Yehudah, for the anonymous Mishnah at the beginning of the chapter Kol Sha'ah follows him: once its time passed, it is forbidden in benefit. Rebbe also ruled like him, for he said: in the fifth hour go out and sell it to a non-Jew. This implies that from then onward it is forbidden, for otherwise he should have waited longer. Also, the Gemara there establishes it like the first Tanna, who says even if they are lost, etc. See the Rosh. A proof is that Rav Nachman and Rava ruled in chapter 2 like Rabbi Shimon after its time, while before its time they ruled like Rabbi Yehudah, for one may leave it pending the whole fifth hour; see there, 13a. Although Rav Nachman said it in the name of Rav, and Rav himself holds even after its time like Rabbi Yehudah, see there 29b, nevertheless Rav Nachman himself holds only like Rabbi Shimon; see chapter 3 of Shabbos 47a, 'But who said that Rabbi Yochanan holds so,' etc.
Even his chametz, etc. Yerushalmi, chapter 2, halachah 2: During Pesach, what is the law? Rabbi Yirmiyah said it is permitted; Rabbi Yose said it is forbidden. Rabbi Yose challenged: but was it not taught that a Jew may not rent his animal to a non-Jew to bring chametz upon it? He answered him: that is where he comes with him. But was it not taught that a Jew may not rent his house to a non-Jew to place chametz in it? You can say: that is where he lives with him. The sugya concludes like Rabbi Yose. Even according to the Tosefta in chapter 2, where it is taught there that a Jew may rent his donkey to a non-Jew to carry chametz on it from place to place, nevertheless it is evident that it is forbidden in benefit, for otherwise what would it teach us? After all, the wage for benefit-prohibitions is permitted, except for wine of nesach, as stated in Avodah Zarah 62; see there. So too it appears in the chapter Kol Sha'ah, 28 and 29, where it says there: who is this? If it is Rabbi Yehudah, he speaks of chametz generally, even of a non-Jew. The same is true according to Rabbi Shimon within its time, for within its time he does not dispute Rabbi Yehudah, and he too speaks of chametz generally. Rav Assi bar Yaakov, who holds there that a non-Jew's chametz is permitted during Pesach, retracted, as stated there: he retracted, etc. Rabbi Abba bar Yaakov said that according to all opinions, something that causes, etc. And the one who says that one commits me'ilah holds like Rabbi Shimon; hence even according to Rabbi Shimon, one commits me'ilah only because it is something that causes, but during Pesach it is forbidden.

And that which is written at the beginning of Kol Sha'ah: but regarding an animal, if it leaves something over he hides it for it, etc., means that perhaps one might think he will avert his eyes from it; it teaches us that we are not concerned.
