[Levush] The same applies if he concluded regarding a beverage, etc., while thinking it was wine, etc. This is difficult, because below in siman 487 the Levush writes that he knows it is Yom Tov, implying that if he did not know he has not fulfilled his obligation. Regarding Shulchan Aruch one could answer that here too he knew it was wine and merely erred in his wording. Magen Avraham explains it this way, and I found the same in Perishah. But regarding the Levush this is difficult, and it is even more surprising that they did not note the contradiction. I am further surprised, because if Shulchan Aruch speaks where he knew, then there is an a fortiori argument from the first case, where with water he began intending to say Shehakol and concluded Borei Pri HaGefen, yet fulfilled his obligation. All the more so if he corrects himself within the brief interval and says Shehakol. It must therefore be that Shulchan Aruch also speaks where he did not know. Magen Avraham's question that this should have been written in the name of "some say" is no difficulty, because it refers back to the earlier "some say" concerning one who took, etc. The main difficulty from siman 487 can be answered by saying that prayer is treated more stringently than blessings. Regarding blessings, a doubt is treated leniently because of the concern for a blessing in vain, unlike prayer. Alternatively, Shulchan Aruch there states the undisputed case where everyone agrees if he knew, while relying on our siman for the case where he did not know. Or he simply uses the wording of Tosafot in Berachot 13, who phrase it this way even according to Ri's stringent view, although according to our ruling he fulfills the obligation even if he did not know. This also resolves well the question of Maadanei Melech, page 9, concerning the Rosh's challenge to Rashi regarding one who began Yotzer Or; perhaps the baraita concerns one who knew and merely misspoke, in which case even Ri, who is stringent according to Rashi's explanation, agrees.
[Levush] And some further say, etc., or water, etc. Bach and the later authorities write that this applies specifically to beer made from grapes, but with barley beer it is obvious that he has not fulfilled his obligation, and all the more so with water. Rambam, chapter 8 of Hilchot Berachot, writes similarly that if fruits of the ground were before him and he began the blessing intending to say Borei Pri HaAdamah but erred and said Borei Pri HaEtz, he need not repeat the blessing. Likewise, if a cooked grain dish was before him and he began intending to say Borei Minei Mezonot but erred and said Hamotzi, he has fulfilled his obligation. Lechem Mishneh explains that the conclusion is not false: beer can be produced from grapes; produce of the ground can be called tree fruit, since according to Rabbi Yehudah wheat is a type of tree; and a grain dish can truthfully be called bread. But where the conclusion of the blessing is false, he does not fulfill his obligation. Mishpat Tzedek writes that if after the blessing over wine one began Borei Meorei HaEsh, then remembered and concluded with the blessing over spices, he must bless again over the fire but not over the spices, since at the beginning of the blessing he intended the spices. If he had intended the fire, however, he has fulfilled the fire blessing and afterward blesses over the spices.
[Levush] Since his blessing, etc. Darkei Moshe makes clear that even if the blessing was not intended to discharge others, if he himself intended to drink from the cup he need not bless again when he drinks. The same applies conversely where he did not intend to drink but the blessing was intended to discharge others. In seif 7 the Beit Yosef and Rama are questioned at length for not mentioning that Mahari disagrees, but there is no difficulty. Mahari speaks where he did not intend to drink, and it is surprising that there, at the end of siman 271, he himself makes clear that Mahari is discussing precisely such a case. See siman 206 seif katan 10. Orchot Chaim reports an incident in which one recited Birkat HaMazon over a cup of water, thinking it was wine. There was no cup before the diners and only wine remained in the pitcher. They ruled that if he wishes to drink wine he must recite Borei Pri HaGefen after saying "Baruch shem kevod malchuto le'olam va'ed" over the blessing that had been in vain, because neither he nor they had intended to drink anything except from that cup. See above, siman 182 seif katan 9. This requires study.
