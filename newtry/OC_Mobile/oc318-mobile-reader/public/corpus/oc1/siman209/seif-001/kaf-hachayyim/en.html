1) [Seif 1] One took a cup of beer or water, etc. This is from the Rambam in chapter 8 of Hilchos Berachos, where only a cup of beer is mentioned; Maran added, 'or water.' Raavad there, however, disagrees with this principal law and writes that we consider only what one actually articulated. Bach similarly writes that according to Rashi, Rif, Rosh, Rashba, and most Geonim, he did not fulfill his obligation and must recite the blessing again. Taz, paragraph 1, Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, Shulchan Gavoah, paragraph 1, and Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, rule likewise. Therefore, since there is a dispute and in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently, if he remembered within the time required for a short greeting after saying Borei Pri HaGafen, he should also say Shehakol Nihyah Bidvaro. He thereby fulfills his obligation according to most authorities, as stated in seif 2. If he did not remember within that time, he should recite the entire proper blessing in his heart.
2) The Rambam writes there: Likewise, if ground produce was before him and he began the blessing intending to say Borei Pri HaAdamah, but erred and said Borei Pri HaEtz, he is not required to repeat it. Similarly, if a cooked grain dish was before him and he began intending to say Borei Minei Mezonos but erred and said HaMotzi, he fulfilled his obligation. This is because when he mentioned the Name and kingship, which are the blessing's essential components, he intended only the blessing appropriate to that species. Lechem Mishneh there explains that although this is not the proper blessing, it is not false. Ground produce is called tree fruit, as stated in the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, where R' Yehudah holds that wheat is a type of tree. Likewise, saying HaMotzi over a cooked grain dish is not false, because grain is called bread. If, however, one makes a false statement, he does not fulfill his obligation; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, cites this. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1, writes that even in these cases he must repeat the blessing; see there. We have already written in the preceding paragraph that since there is a dispute, if he remembers within the time required for a short greeting, he should also add the conclusion of the blessing required for that food. If he does not remember within that time, he should recite the proper blessing again in his heart.
3) There: Some say that if one took, etc. This is presented as 'some say' because according to the Rambam he did not fulfill his obligation, since he intended to say an improper blessing. Kesef Mishneh on chapter 1 of Hilchos Krias Shema writes the same. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. Magen Avraham nevertheless writes that the primary ruling is that he fulfilled his obligation. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2, likewise writes that the primary ruling is that he fulfilled his obligation. Olas Tamid, paragraph 1, writes that although this opinion appears to contradict the author's earlier words, in both cases he rules that one does not repeat the blessing, because each interpretation has a sound basis in the Talmudic passage, and in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently. Nehar Shalom, paragraph 2, likewise writes that Maran, of blessed memory, intended to rule leniently in both cases.
