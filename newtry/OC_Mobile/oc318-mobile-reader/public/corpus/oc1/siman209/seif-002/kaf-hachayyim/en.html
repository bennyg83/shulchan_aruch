2) The Rambam writes there: Likewise, if ground produce was before him and he began the blessing intending to say Borei Pri HaAdamah, but erred and said Borei Pri HaEtz, he is not required to repeat it. Similarly, if a cooked grain dish was before him and he began intending to say Borei Minei Mezonos but erred and said HaMotzi, he fulfilled his obligation. This is because when he mentioned the Name and kingship, which are the blessing's essential components, he intended only the blessing appropriate to that species. Lechem Mishneh there explains that although this is not the proper blessing, it is not false. Ground produce is called tree fruit, as stated in the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, where R' Yehudah holds that wheat is a type of tree. Likewise, saying HaMotzi over a cooked grain dish is not false, because grain is called bread. If, however, one makes a false statement, he does not fulfill his obligation; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, cites this. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1, writes that even in these cases he must repeat the blessing; see there. We have already written in the preceding paragraph that since there is a dispute, if he remembers within the time required for a short greeting, he should also add the conclusion of the blessing required for that food. If he does not remember within that time, he should recite the proper blessing again in his heart.
