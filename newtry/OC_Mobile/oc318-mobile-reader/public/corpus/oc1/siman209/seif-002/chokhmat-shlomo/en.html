Seif 2. He took a cup of beer or water, etc., and within k'dei dibur he remembered he erred and said shehakol nihyeh bidvaro, etc. NB: One must analyze — what he wrote "such was his utterance" is superfluous, since it is already written that within k'dei dibur he remembered he erred and said shehakol — the matter is self-understood and need not add further explanation. In Hilchot Pesach siman 487 he wrote only briefly that within k'dei dibur he remembered and concluded for Yom Tov, and did not conclude at all with the language "and such was his utterance" as here. Therefore it appears he intended something important: Magen Avraham already sensed why he did not explain here that he knew it was beer or water as in Hilchot Pesach. Therefore it appears we should say: it is known what some poskim wrote — that even in matters where we hold within k'dei dibur is not like speech, nevertheless if it is literally one utterance, return helps — brought in Beit Yosef in Shulchan Aruch Even HaEzer siman 38 to resolve Rambam's words regarding an act before conditions; he answered there that there is a distinction between within k'dei dibur and literally one utterance, which helps return according to all — see there and in Bach there. If so, here too: certainly if he did not do everything in literally one utterance, then it is within k'dei dibur — nevertheless here we require at least that he knew it was Yom Tov, otherwise he has not fulfilled. But here it discusses literally one utterance; therefore there, where he explained he knew it was Yom Tov, he ruled simply that within k'dei dibur he fulfills and we do not require literally one utterance. But here he did not know it was beer — therefore within k'dei dibur alone does not help if he paused somewhat. Therefore he explained here that when it says "within k'dei dibur he remembered," the intent is not that there was a pause, only that such was his utterance — borei peri haGefen [shehakol] — and it is literally one utterance; therefore here he fulfills even though he did not know it was beer and water. With this it would appear to resolve what Magen Avraham challenged: why did he not write here in the name of "some say," since per the first view it depends on his intent — see there. But in my opinion one may say: those poskim hold the reasoning to the contrary — that we do not follow his intent except where he did not conclude at all as he intended; then the strictness is in the body of the blessing that the end is not the beginning nor the beginning the end — he initially intended the blessing of borei peri haGefen and afterward concluded shehakol; and if it were actual wine as he initially intended, he also blessed improperly — for he would fulfill by another reason, that on everything if he said shehakol he fulfills, but nevertheless this is not his proper blessing l'chatchila; and regarding anything where the conclusion in the body of the blessing was not his initial intent, we follow his intent. But if he concluded the blessing as he intended, the blessing in itself was said properly; and if here it were wine it would be an excellent blessing, head like tail, and the strictness is only from another side — this cup is not wine — and anything that prevents from another side we do not heed his intent; therefore since he concluded shehakol he has fulfilled. And one may further reconcile: since mitzvot require intent, it applies only to a mitzvah one is obligated in; but for a mitzvah one is not obligated in, intent is not required — as I wrote in my work Sefer HaChaim siman 60. And I was told again that Ran in his novellae to Chullin wrote thus. If so, here too: if they were strict in the body of the blessing that the end is not like the head and he did not initially intend for that — they hold that since he is already eating and drinking he is obligated in the blessing; therefore the blessing is considered obligatory and we require intent in it. But if there is no contradiction in the body of the blessing — that from the aspect of the blessing body it was said properly, beginning and end with one intent, only the lack is from the food and drink that are not as the blessing, and food and drink have no obligation, only permission — therefore for something one is not obligated in, intent is not required; therefore it is preferable if he concluded the blessing as he intended and afterward remembered and said shehakol: here, if it were wine as he intended, it would be an excellent blessing from beginning to end, unlike if he intended at the start for wine and concluded only shehakol — here, even if it were wine as he initially intended, the blessing is not proper from the aspect of the main law l'chatchila; here the disqualification is in the body of the blessing, and therefore he loses lack of intent; and since in truth it is not wine but beer he has not fulfilled. But if he concluded borei peri haGefen, the blessing in itself — if it were wine — would be excellent; only the lack is from the beverage that is not as the blessing; here we do not require intent, only the blessing; and since he concluded shehakol within k'dei dibur he has fulfilled. Therefore the change in Shulchan Aruch's wording fits well: the matter of being able to retract within k'dei dibur is that in this retraction he uproots the first utterance and renders it as if it never existed from the outset — and proof: he is struck in the Talmud in Nazir and Temurah, brought in Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 105: where one can ask about it, one can also retract within k'dei dibur; but where asking does not help, retraction within k'dei dibur does not help. If so, it is proven explicitly: just as in asking there is uprooting of the vow and consecration retroactively as if never said, so too retraction within k'dei dibur renders the first utterance as if never said and uproots it from the outset — for if it does not uproot from the outset, what similarity is there to asking, and what connection does this have to that; perforce within k'dei dibur also uproots the first utterance from the outset. But this is specifically if he paused at least a little — then it helps only by reason of retraction that renders the first utterance as if nonexistent; but where it is literally one utterance it helps without this, without uprooting the first utterance retroactively. Therefore the distinction in those matters that within k'dei dibur is not like speech — nevertheless, if as we said it uproots the first utterance retroactively, and these are great matters, one cannot uproot the first utterance retroactively — as Taz wrote in Even HaEzer siman 38 regarding a gift, since it is an act one cannot retract — see there. But where it is literally one utterance, here it is like actual speech without the reason of retraction and uprooting the first utterance; and since there is no need to uproot the first utterance, therefore it helps even in this case. Therefore Shulchan Aruch's words here and in Hilchot Yom Tov fit well: there it discusses that he did not immediately conclude for Yom Tov but paused a little — it is within k'dei dibur; nevertheless, since within k'dei dibur helps only by uprooting the first utterance as if not said — if so here too, if he uproots the first utterance as if not said, it is as if he concluded only for Yom Tov while initially he intended only for Shabbat — it does not help according to Rambam and his supporters who follow intent; therefore we require that he knew it was Yom Tov, and then within k'dei dibur he fulfills. But here it discusses literally one utterance, and here there is no need to uproot the first utterance — it is as if he said it; and nevertheless, since he concluded in one utterance shehakol it helps, and regarding intent there is no strictness since he also said borei peri haGefen; therefore we require literally one utterance. But if not one utterance, only within k'dei dibur — then it does not help since he did not know it was beer; perforce according to Rambam in seif 1 we require intent — perforce if we consider it as if he also said borei peri haGefen, afterward shehakol without intent does not help since he paused in the middle; and if he uproots the borei peri haGefen utterance retroactively as if not said, again it does not help according to Rambam since he initially intended for wine; therefore we require literally one utterance. And Shulchan Aruch's rulings fit well — think well. And further one could say to resolve Shulchan Aruch's words: as stated above, one should distinguish between a blessing one is obligated in and one not obligated in; if so, one may say here it discusses a blessing one is not obligated in; therefore here Shulchan Aruch decided the main point not like Rambam, only we do not heed intent here — since he concluded properly he has fulfilled. But below, regarding Yom Tov blessing which is obligatory, one may say the halachah is like Rambam that we require intent; or that he initially intended Shabbat — what he concluded for Yom Tov does not help; therefore there we require that he knew it was Yom Tov, only that he erred in speech. If so, it would appear regarding a Kiddush cup or four cups etc., if he is obligated in this cup — if he intended to bless borei peri haGefen thinking it was wine and it became known it was beer and he concluded shehakol, he has not fulfilled; here we hold like Rambam that intent is required. However, where it is literally one utterance, one may say even if obligatory he fulfills here as we explained — correct for practical law. And conversely, according to Magen Avraham he should have explained Shulchan Aruch here that he knew, as he explained in Hilchot Pesach; behold there is an a fortiori — in Hilchot Yom Tov it does not initially discuss the reverse and explained he knew, while here it initially discusses the reverse and did not explain. But as we said, without knowing he need not explain, since at the beginning it discusses without knowing — obviously it refers to the above — think well on this.
In the gloss there: And if others were also drinking and wine was before them — NB: see Magen Avraham below siman 213 who challenged this. And see what I wrote in my novellae to Hilchot Rabbeinu Tam in my work on Orach Chayim from the year 5599 in the laws beginning "siman 5599," to reject Magen Avraham's answer; and I wrote to resolve by another matter — not for naught did Rama write "and wine was before them": specifically in such a case where their thought is evident from their actions, then they fulfill and he fulfills through them; but otherwise they depend on him — since he did not fulfill, they also did not fulfill — only if their thought is evident from their actions, then in those cases they actually blessed, and then he depends on them — see there what I explained, word by reason, with God's help — think well.
