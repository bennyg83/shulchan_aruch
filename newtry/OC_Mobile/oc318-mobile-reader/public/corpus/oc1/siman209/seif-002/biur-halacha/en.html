And within k'dei dibur — see in Mishna Berurah what he wrote; and specifically regarding blessings that are d'rabbanan, so too Magen Avraham in the name of Ri'az, and the reason is that in the Gemara it is an unresolved question, and Rif wrote that we rule leniently because it is a doubt regarding a d'rabbanan matter — from which we infer that regarding a d'oraisa matter we rule stringently. And what I depicted — one who erred and thought he was eating fruit: if at the time he said the Name and kingship his intent was to say properly but afterward he stumbled in his wording and said "on the tree and on the fruit of the tree" — it appears that what he returned within k'dei dibur to say properly helps even regarding a d'oraisa matter. And proof: the view of R' Yehudah in Tosafot that regarding blessings, even d'rabbanan, in a doubt we rule stringently — nevertheless they wrote: however, the Rosh would say that if he knew clearly that he erred in his speech, within k'dei dibur he may retract, since he knows it is Yom Tov — see there; behold, this is obvious to Tosafot: where his intent at the time of saying the Name and kingship was proper but afterward he stumbled in his wording, he may retract within k'dei dibur; and the doubt of the Talmud is only if the intent was also improper. Know further: even though according to Rashi and Rashba and Rabbenu Yona, where at the time of saying the Name and kingship his intent was not to conclude properly and afterward he remembered and concluded properly [and this is the "some say" in seif 1] — it is an unresolved question in the Gemara, and because it was not resolved we rule leniently as the poskim wrote — if so, likewise if such a case occurred regarding a d'oraisa matter [for example in Birkat HaMazon where when he said "Blessed are You, the Master our Heaven" his intent was to say "on the tree and on borei peri haEtz" and he remembered and concluded with his mouth "Who feeds the world" properly] — here too he has not fulfilled and must return to the beginning, for it is a doubt regarding a d'oraisa matter; and so Ri'az truly wrote, brought in Shiltei HaGiborim and in Magen Avraham — nevertheless for practical law it requires study, for from many rishonim it appears they disagree with Rashi [Raavad and Ramban, and they explained that Rif's view is likewise] and hold it is obvious we do not follow thought alone since with his mouth he said properly — if so, according to them certainly even regarding d'oraisa he has fulfilled; and it requires study for practical law.
