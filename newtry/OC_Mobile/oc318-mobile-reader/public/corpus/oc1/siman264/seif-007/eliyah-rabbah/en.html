[7] Lest he take some of it, etc. The Gemara and poskim give another reason: it adheres to the walls of the house and sets the house aflame; we fear that, panicking over his property, he will extinguish it. Magen Avraham derives that one should not seal an oven with wood for this reason, though his text applies this to tar, which is an error. One can defend the practice: Rambam and Rav Ovadiah of Bartenura explain the Gemara as a concern for domestic peace (people will leave the room) while the other poskim cite danger. They do not appear concerned that he will extinguish it. Biurei Semag writes that both reasons are necessary: the concern he may take some would not apply if the lamp stood high, and the danger would not apply in a marble house. This is difficult, because there would still be concern that it could leap into his face, as Rabbi David Abudraham writes. Moreover, below in siman 275 the prohibition applies even two stories high.
