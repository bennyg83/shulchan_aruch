Paragraph 6: For this reason; Beis Yosef writes that everyone permits this. Shulchan Aruch rules like Rif that the law depends on intention. The Gemara's permission "to stiffen" therefore means that his intent is to stiffen the wick. Rashi, however, holds that even when his intention is to stiffen it, we decree lest he come to light the invalid material by itself, since it also burns. The Gemara's permission means that he places it beneath the valid wick so that the latter floats above, while the invalid material does not burn at all. Beis Yosef's phrase "for this reason" implies that only because we rule like Rif and follow intention may one wrap the wick around straw or reeds, where he likewise does not intend additional light. According to Rashi, even wrapping around straw or reeds should be forbidden, because we do not follow intention and decree lest he light them alone. Beis Yosef, however, writes that everyone permits wrapping around reeds or straw, even Rashi, because they are not considered a wick and there is no concern he will light them alone. Magen Avraham adds, "If so, even if he intended, etc." Initially Magen Avraham only scrutinized Beis Yosef's phrase "for this reason." He now adds a practical consequence for us: although we rule like Rif, Beis Yosef's explanation implies that even if one intended to thicken the wick (a case Rif normally forbids) he agrees that reeds and straw are permitted, because no decree applies there.
