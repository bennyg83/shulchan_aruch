Nor with tallow. Eliyah Rabbah and Pri Megadim are uncertain whether butter and animal fat are included in tallow. It appears to me there is a clear proof regarding animal fat that it has the same law as tallow. Shabbos 26a states: Sumchus says that one may not light with anything derived from flesh, etc. Sumchus and the first Tanna differ only regarding Rav Beruna's case; thus the first Tanna agrees with Sumchus on everything else. Sumchus certainly treats animal fat like tallow, because it is derived from flesh, and surely no one disputes this. Otherwise, the Gemara should have identified animal fat as a difference between them. There is also proof from the first Tanna himself: he permits only fish oil, which Rashi explains as a novelty despite its resemblance to tallow; he does not state the greater novelty of animal fat. I later found that Rabbi Akiva Eiger's novellae to the Mishnah, citing Sefer Beis David, likewise holds that permitted animal fat has the same law as tallow.
With tar. It is the waste product of pitch, as stated in Shabbos 20b. After pitch emerges from wood, a clear oil-like residue flows from it through fire and is called tar. It draws after the wick, but its odor is extremely foul; the Sages therefore decreed lest one leave the room and depart. Petroleum is another type of oil with an unpleasant odor, though not as foul as tar, and was not prohibited. Only white petroleum was forbidden by Chazal, even on weekdays, because it is volatile and can set the house aflame. It is astonishing that Tiferes Yisrael forbids lighting with ordinary foul-smelling petroleum and writes that Tur agrees. How will he explain siman 275, se'if 12? Tosafos 25b, s.v. "because," is also explicit. Accordingly, the oils commonly used for lighting today, called gas, whose odor is somewhat unpleasant, may be used on Shabbos. Although their odor becomes extremely foul when extinguished, there is no concern, because then the reason lest one leave the room does not apply. The Yerushalmi explicitly says likewise concerning fish entrails; see there.
