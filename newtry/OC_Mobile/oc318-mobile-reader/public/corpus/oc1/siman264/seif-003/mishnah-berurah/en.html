(13) With pitch, etc. This means placing these materials in a lamp and setting the wick within them as with oil. Candles like ours are permitted, as explained in se'if 7. One may not light with oil sediment, which is thick and does not follow the wick well.
(14) Nor with animal fat. Rendered animal fat is also included, as we established in Biur Halachah. Butter requires examination. Since butter and fat are normally melted before lighting, one solution is to add a small amount of valid oil, which certainly helps, as below in se'if 5. Nevertheless, initially it is best to avoid them when other oils are available, because animal fat sometimes remains in the lamp and is cooked together with butter, violating the prohibition against cooking meat and milk and against benefiting from it. This concern does not apply to poultry fat, for we rule that cooking and benefit are not prohibited in its case.
(15) He should leave it and depart. One is obligated to eat on Shabbos by the light of the lamp.
(16) With balsam oil. This is fragrant persimmon oil.
(17) He may take from it. The Gemara gives another reason: it is volatile, adheres to the walls, and can set the house aflame; a person panics over his property and may extinguish it. Magen Avraham writes that this teaches one not to seal an oven with wood in a manner that could lead to desecrating Shabbos.
