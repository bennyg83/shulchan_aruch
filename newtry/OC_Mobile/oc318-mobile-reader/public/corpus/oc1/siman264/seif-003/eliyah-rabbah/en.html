Taz to siman 673, paragraph 4, implies that studying is forbidden even when another person is present. Rema writes: Some say that if one lamp is made from permitted materials, the light of the others may be used. A task possible without a lamp may likewise be performed even beside the other lamps. For a Shabbos need one may be lenient after the fact. Levush omits this, perhaps because he found the proof difficult according to the prohibiting authorities cited in Beis Yosef. The proof is that invalid fuels may be lit on Shabbos Chanukah because using Chanukah light is forbidden, even though Chanukah lights were placed outside at the entrance. It appears to me that the Gemara says this for completeness: even if one wished to eat there, he could not. Malbushei Yom Tov writes that Levush omitted the leniency because most authorities in Beis Yosef prohibit it. Bach and Nachalas Tzvi likewise prohibit. Malbushei Yom Tov's explanation remains difficult: Rav Huna says invalid fuels may not be lit on Chanukah because use of the light is permitted. Why should that matter if it stands outside at the entrance? I found that Semag, siman 65, and Sefer HaTerumah likewise prove the law from Rav Huna. Rema may understand the Gemara as prohibiting initial lighting with invalid materials, but permitting use after the fact, like the questioner in Responsa Rashba, siman 171. Rashba disagrees, but close examination shows that the Gemara does not compel his view; it depends upon reasoning. Rokeach, siman 45, agrees with the questioner. Raavan, siman 342, likewise implies it: he explains that Bameh Madlikin is recited so one will learn which wicks are invalid and correct his mistake the next Shabbos. He should instead have said that one will refrain from using their light that very Shabbos, implying that use is permitted. Rema's words appear to mean that a task possible without a lamp is permitted even if no valid lamp is present, whereas when a valid lamp is in the house, even a task impossible without light is permitted, though the valid lamp is in another room; since a valid lamp exists somewhere, he will remember and not tilt elsewhere. Hagahos Mordechai, chapter Bameh Madlikin, indeed implies this. Agudah, however, states that even when a valid lamp is in the house, an invalid lamp may be used only for a task possible without light; a task impossible without light is always forbidden. When no valid lamp is in the house, even a task possible without light is forbidden. It therefore appears that Rema's permission to use the other lamps means within the same room. In another room he permits only a task possible without light. Although Agudah somewhat implies that even in the same room a task impossible without light is forbidden, Rema decides otherwise. This is unlike Magen Avraham. Hagahos Mordechai gives drawing wine from a barrel as an example of a task possible without light. Gedulas Mordechai explains this as drawing through a vessel such as a basin called "han" in German. Mordechai's statement that one should be careful when drawing wine at night, Gedulas Mordechai explains, refers to drawing through a spigot, which is impossible without light. This is difficult, for then Mordechai and Sefer HaTerumah do not disagree with Hagahos Mordechai, whereas Beis Yosef says they do; Rema's position is also explicit in Agudah, which understands Mordechai and Sefer HaTerumah to discuss even a task possible without light. Moreover, Mordechai also says to be careful when lying down in a room; shall we say that too is impossible without a lamp?
