Such as wool and hair. The Gemara there teaches this. Rambam selected the poorest materials as examples.
Such as flax. This is stated there on 27b.
Combed. This is stated there on 20b.
And a linen garment. This is stated there on 21a.
And cotton wool, etc. This does not follow Rashi, who writes that these fibers grow from a tree. A proof may be brought from Menachos regarding hemp and luf, which were prohibited, etc., and from Berachos 40 concerning “wherever, etc.”
If he lit. As stated above, siman 255, seif 1: “And if he did not, etc.”
Some say that if, etc. This is derived from the Gemara’s statement concerning the wicks and oils, the reason for the prohibition, and the prohibition against using their light. Rava states there that another lamp is required. They derive from this that whenever another lamp is present nearby, one may use the light, as stated in siman 673, seif 1. Nevertheless, it is permitted to kindle with wicks and oils that do not burn well. According to Rashi’s explanation there, however, the extra lamp is required only as a distinguishing sign, and no proof can be brought from that source. Rashi’s reason is that Rava holds that one may use the Chanukah light, while his statement that the lamp of one’s home takes precedence refers to the fact that the Chanukah lamp is kindled outside. Ran also explains that the extra lamp serves as a distinguishing sign, although he holds, like Rashba, that Rava forbids using the Chanukah light. Since it is placed upon one’s table, one will inevitably use its light, and another lamp is therefore needed as a distinguishing sign. See below, siman 673, seif 1, concerning the custom to make, etc. According to Rashi and Ran, however, the presence of another lamp does not remedy inferior Shabbos wicks and oils.
Likewise a matter, etc. The Gemara states that it is forbidden to inspect, etc. The commentators ask why this must be taught, since every use of the lamp is forbidden. They answer that an incidental use is permitted.
