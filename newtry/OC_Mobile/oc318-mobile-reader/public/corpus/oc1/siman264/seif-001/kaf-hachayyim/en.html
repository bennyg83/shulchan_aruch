1) [Seif 1] One may not make a wick, etc. The reason that these wicks and oils are invalid is that they do not produce a good light, and we are concerned that one might tilt the vessel so that the oil within it reaches the wick and burns well. Taz, paragraph 1.
2) There: This applies to every lamp that one kindles in the house, etc. This excludes the ruling of Hagahos Mordechai in the name of Maharam, that whenever there is one valid lamp in one location, it will remind him and he will not tilt the other lamps in the courtyard or house. It also excludes the ruling cited in the name of the officer of Coucy, that an activity one can perform without a lamp, such as drawing wine from a barrel, may be performed by the light of these lamps. Many halachic authorities dispute these rulings, as Beis Yosef writes, adding that this is Tur's view; see there. This also appears to be Bach's view. See below, paragraph 7.
3) There: And hemp, etc. It appears proper to be stringent about this, because I saw that Sefer Yere'im, Shibbolei HaLeket, Piskei Recanati, and Rokeach, siman 44, forbid hemp even though they permit cotton. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2.
4) There, gloss: If he kindled using forbidden materials, it is forbidden, etc. Accordingly, if he has only that room, he must leave it. Rambam's words, however, imply that if he transgressed and kindled it, he is not required to leave. This is also implied by Rokeach, siman 45, who writes that one who transgressed and kindled it may use its light. Nehar Shalom, paragraph 1. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, writes the same.
5) There, gloss: It is forbidden to use its light. This applies even to a use that does not require close examination. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2. An activity requiring close examination may not be performed on Shabbos by lamplight even when the lamp burns well, as stated in siman 275. Levush Serad.
6) There, gloss: It is forbidden to use its light. It is likewise forbidden to study by its light, and the presence of another person to remind him does not help. Taz, siman 673, paragraph 4; Eliyah Rabbah in this siman, paragraph 3; Machatzis HaShekel, paragraph 1. Similarly, all cases permitted in siman 275, such as for a distinguished person and the like, are forbidden here. Even if he has no valid wicks or oils other than these, he may not kindle them. So write the later authorities.
7) There, gloss: Some say that if there is one lamp made from permitted materials, etc. This is the ruling of Hagahos Mordechai cited by Beis Yosef, that whenever there is one valid lamp in one location, it will remind him and he will not tilt the other lamps in the courtyard or house; see there. We already wrote above in paragraph 2 that most halachic authorities dispute this, and that Tur and the Shulchan Aruch also reject it; see there. Levush likewise omits this ruling because most authorities forbid it. Bach and Nezer Yisrael also rule like those who forbid it, as stated in Eliyah Rabbah.
8) There, gloss: Likewise, an activity that can be performed without a lamp, etc. Such as drawing wine from a cellar. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3. This is the ruling of Hagahos Mordechai in the name of the officer of Coucy, cited by Beis Yosef and quoted above in paragraph 2.
9) There, gloss: One may be lenient after the fact. It appears that if one valid lamp stands beside an invalid lamp, one may use the light, because the case is no worse than one involving only a single lamp, as stated in siman 276, seif 4. Although Bach is stringent in this regard, he follows his own reasoning, for he writes in siman 673 that a shamash beside Chanukah lamps does not permit their use; see there. According to our practice, it is obvious that this is permitted. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, however, writes that one can distinguish between the cases. Here there is concern that he might tilt the invalid lamp even though he can see by the valid one, because he wants both lamps to burn well. There, by contrast, the matter depends upon benefit, and since he can use the light of one lamp, it is not considered benefit from the other. These are his words. Therefore, it appears that if a valid lamp is beside an invalid one, one may be lenient and perform in that room an activity that could be performed without a lamp. An activity impossible without a lamp, however, is forbidden. Note this well. See below, siman 275, paragraph 12.
