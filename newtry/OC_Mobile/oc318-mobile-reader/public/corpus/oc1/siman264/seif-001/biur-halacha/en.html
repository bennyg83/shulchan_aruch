For the Shabbat lamp - But for Yom Tov all wicks and oils are permitted except shemen sereifah; see the Gemara there, 24a. At first glance, with tar one also should not kindle on Yom Tov, according to what we hold above in siman 263 seif 5, that on Yom Tov as well one blesses over lighting; thus on Yom Tov too it is an obligation, and if so perhaps he will leave it and go out. Regarding balsam, I found in Peri Megadim in this siman that he is uncertain about this.
Of something that the fire, etc. - The Shulchan Aruch speaks of a lamp of a stand, called a lamp, but in truth the same applies to wax and tallow candles that we make: one also may not make them with invalid wicks, as Magen Avraham rules below in seif katan 13.
And the flame jumps - We are concerned lest he tilt it. Therefore, even if he has no other wicks and oils except these invalid ones, and because of this he will lose the mitzvah of lighting, it appears forbidden for this reason alone, except for tar, whose prohibition is only because perhaps he will leave it and go out; one should not set aside certainty because of doubt, as Peri Megadim writes.
It is forbidden to use - See the responsa of Rashba for the reason: since the entire prohibition of lighting is because perhaps he will tilt, the same concern of perhaps he will tilt applies now regarding using it. At first glance, with melted tallow, which in truth is drawn after the wick and is prohibited only by decree because of unmelted tallow, one must examine the law after the fact.
One may be lenient after the fact - See in Mishna Berurah the view of Magen Avraham, who follows his reasoning, for he holds that the first law of the "some say" refers even to another room; therefore he wrote that if there is, etc., it is permitted even not in pressing circumstances. But according to what Gra explains, that the leniency of the "some say" derives from the Chanukkah lamp, where it is permitted to use their light when the shamash stands with them, and there it stands actually with them, and nevertheless Rama wrote that it is specifically for Shabbat needs. This must be because there, regarding the Chanukkah lamp itself, the law is not clear according to Rashi and Ran, as Gra wrote. Therefore here, regarding Shabbat, we are stringent unless it is for Shabbat need. Or perhaps Rama holds as Eliyah Rabbah wrote, that one can distinguish: this is not comparable to siman 276 or to siman 673 regarding the Chanukkah lamp, for here there is concern that he may tilt even though he can see by the valid lamp, since a person wants all the lamps to burn well; whereas there the matter depends on benefit, and since he can use another light, it is not considered benefit.
