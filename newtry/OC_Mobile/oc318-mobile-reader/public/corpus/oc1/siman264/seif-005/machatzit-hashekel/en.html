Paragraph 5: He intended, etc.; when he intends, etc. This is Rif's view that the law depends upon intention. Magen Avraham's phrase "we do not decree lest he tilt" is imprecise. When he wraps, etc., there is no need for a concern lest he tilt, because in that configuration the flame catches properly. Rather, if he intends to thicken the wick, it is forbidden because we decree lest he light the invalid material alone. Magen Avraham's phrase means that we do not decree that he may reach the concern of tilting (namely, by later lighting it alone) for even now, when he wrapped it, he did not make it to illuminate.
