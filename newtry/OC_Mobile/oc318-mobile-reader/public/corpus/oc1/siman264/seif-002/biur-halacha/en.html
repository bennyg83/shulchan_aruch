If he intended to stiffen it, etc., it is permitted. See Mishnah Berurah. Its explanation that he does not intend the added material as a wick follows Levush. Magen Avraham, paragraph 5, says that when he intends to stiffen it we do not decree lest he tilt, but this is abbreviated wording; he should have said we do not decree lest he light that material alone, as Birkei Yosef writes. It appears to me that if the invalid material constitutes a minority, so that it does not draw after the wick; see Tosafos 21a, s.v. "simchas," which discusses one quarter, and it appears the same applies to any minority; one should not be lenient even when his intent is merely to stiffen, because he may later tilt. I subsequently found a similar ruling in Tosefes Shabbos.
Over reeds or straw. Magen Avraham writes that since reeds and straw are not fit to serve as wicks by themselves, there is no decree lest he light them alone; therefore, even if he intends to thicken the wick, it is permitted. Rabbeinu Yerucham, path 12, part 2, implies that according to Rashi it is forbidden. Tosefes Shabbos likewise questions the leniency according to Rambam.
