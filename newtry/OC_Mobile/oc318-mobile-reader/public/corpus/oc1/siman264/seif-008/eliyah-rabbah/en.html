[8] A little oil, etc. If he has no oil, he should squeeze a nut. Tashbetz. I was asked how people light with animal fat and butter. I answered that oil is certainly the preferred mitzvah, for it lengthens life. Moreover, butter may drip into meat food. Sometimes people light with animal fat and sometimes with butter while the lamp is still soiled, thereby violating the prohibition of cooking meat and milk; the mixture becomes forbidden for benefit. This certainly applies if actual meat is mixed in, except with poultry fat, whose cooking and benefit are not prohibited. Nevertheless, butter and animal fat are not included in the Mishnah's terms tail-fat and forbidden fat. Some proof is that the Tanna had to prohibit fat from the kik bird despite already listing forbidden fat and tail-fat. Furthermore, one may rely here upon the poskim who permit cooked forbidden fat, and butter is cooked before lighting. Therefore, he should at least place a little oil within it.
