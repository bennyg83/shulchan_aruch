9) There, gloss: One may be lenient after the fact. It appears that if one valid lamp stands beside an invalid lamp, one may use the light, because the case is no worse than one involving only a single lamp, as stated in siman 276, seif 4. Although Bach is stringent in this regard, he follows his own reasoning, for he writes in siman 673 that a shamash beside Chanukah lamps does not permit their use; see there. According to our practice, it is obvious that this is permitted. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, however, writes that one can distinguish between the cases. Here there is concern that he might tilt the invalid lamp even though he can see by the valid one, because he wants both lamps to burn well. There, by contrast, the matter depends upon benefit, and since he can use the light of one lamp, it is not considered benefit from the other. These are his words. Therefore, it appears that if a valid lamp is beside an invalid one, one may be lenient and perform in that room an activity that could be performed without a lamp. An activity impossible without a lamp, however, is forbidden. Note this well. See below, siman 275, paragraph 12.
