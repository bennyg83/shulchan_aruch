1) [Seif 1] We do not initially decree a communal fast on Thursday, etc. The Beit Yosef cites a dispute between the Ran and Rabbeinu Yerucham. According to the Ran, this was stated not only regarding a fast for rain, but regarding every distress that should not befall the community. According to Rabbeinu Yerucham, it was stated only regarding a fast for rain, for then the market price rises; see there. The Shulchan Aruch rules without qualification in accordance with the Ran. Nevertheless, it is possible that the Ran said this only regarding a continuing distress similar to the lack of rain, such as blight, mildew, locusts, and the like, where human life does not depend upon the matter from one moment to the next. However, regarding a distress in which human life is immediately at stake, such as when a great earthquake is heard repeatedly and there is reason to fear that, G-d forbid, their houses may become their graves, the Ran too concedes in such a case that there is no restraint requiring one to wait. I acted accordingly in practice, and I also relied upon what I saw in the Magen Avraham, that in a place where those who artificially raise market prices are not common, it is possible that it is permitted. Nehar Shalom. See below, os 5, and siman 576, seif 4.
2) Ibid. We do not initially decree a communal fast on Thursday, etc. This applies when Monday, Thursday, and Monday are proclaimed. However, if they decree only one day and the hour is pressing, for it is a time of distress, we do not delay until Monday. Rather, they should precede the distress with prayer by fasting on Thursday, for the circumstances require it according to the matter. Everything depends upon what the judges who sit in judgment deem proper. Ruach Chaim, os 1.
3) Ibid. We do not initially decree a communal fast on Thursday, etc. However, regarding an individual there is no concern. Maharam Mintz, siman 115; Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahos HaTur.
