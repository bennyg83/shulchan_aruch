Mechaber & Rama · Seif 11 (יא)

אַמְבַּטִּי (פי׳ כְּלִי שֶׁרוֹחֵץ בּוֹ) שֶׁל מֶרְחָץ שֶׁהִיא מְלֵאָה מַיִם
חַמִּין {אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁהִיא כְּלִי שֵׁנִי (תּוֹסָפוֹת וְרא״ש וְטוּר)} אֵין
נוֹתְנִין לָהּ מַיִם צוֹנֵן, שֶׁהֲרֵי מְחַמְּמָן הַרְבֵּה. אֲבָל נוֹתֵן הוּא
מִמַּיִם חַמִּין שֶׁבְּזֶה הָאַמְבְּטִי לְתוֹךְ אַמְבְּטִי אַחֵר שֶׁל
צוֹנֵן.
A bath-tub (meaning a vessel one bathes in) of a bathhouse that is full of
hot water {even though it is a second vessel (Tosafos, Rosh, and Tur)}—one
does not add cold water to it, because it greatly heats. But one may pour
from the hot water in this tub into another tub of cold water.

“But a basin with water…” is astonishing — Gemara concludes Abaye to Rav Yosef: R. Hiyya taught a basin is not like a bathtub; Rava too was not stringent because R. Beit Yosef suggested our author explains the basin permitted in the Gemara as kli shlishi — untenable. And what he wrote — forbidden to put cold items in them: since they are for bathing, generally they are very hot and cold items mixed in get cooked. He did not say the prohibition is because “lower heat overcomes” — for then a cup would also be forbidden for that reason. Rather certainly kal va-chomer: cup, basin, or tub — permitted by law because we hold kli sheini does not cook — except the Sages decreed for basin and tub because the water is very hot and an observer will say the cold items mixed in are cooking — definite lower heat — and he will come to pour [more] for lower heat; but for a cup there is no decree because everyone knows it is lower heat since the water inside is not that hot.

Bathhouse tub, etc. — the Tur wrote “but a bowl of hot water,” etc. — it is very astonishing, for he ruled against all the halacha in the Talmud: we say in Abaye’s explanation he brought a baraita: a bowl is not like a tub: for a tub we say there one may put hot into cold, not the reverse; but in a bowl one may in all ways; afterward we say Rava is not particular about water — in a bowl, hot to cold or reverse — permitted — everywhere we rule like Rava — all the more here where Rif and Semak explain like Rava — how did the Tur rule unlike Rava? Beit Yosef explained two interpretations — honor to him in his place — “these two verses do not teach.” First: because we do not know the shape of these vessels we forbid every second vessel made for washing — therefore our teacher wrote to forbid even a bowl — this cannot be written: then our teacher should have written the Talmud’s law and the distinction between bowl and tub and afterward written “now we are not expert — forbid every vessel”; at minimum he should not have mentioned bowl specifically but written generally every kli sheini with hot water for washing; further what expertise applies between bowl and tub? The second explanation — the Tur explains the bowl is a third vessel — against the whole sugya where we say Rava is not particular about water in a bowl — presumably following earlier that a bowl is kli sheini. SHULCHAN ARUCH KITZUTA — markers: עא, עב, עג, עד, עה [318_kitzur_he_en.txt]

      

    

    

      
(עא)

      
הקדמה לסעיף – כשמערבבים בשבת מים חמים עם מים קרים, יש חשש שהמים החמים יבשלו את הקרים. הדבר אסור בתנאים מסוימים, ויש להבחין בכך בין כלי ראשון לכלי שני, וגם בין אמבטיה שנועדה לרחצה, שעליה מדבר סעיף זה, לבין מיחם לשתייה, שעליו מדבר הסעיף הבא.

Introduction to the section: When hot water is mixed with cold water on Shabbat, there is concern that the hot water will cook the cold water. This is forbidden under certain conditions, and one must distinguish between a kli rishon and a kli sheini, and also between a bath intended for washing, which is discussed in this section, and a kettle used for drinking, which is discussed in the next section.

    

    

      
(עב)

      
מים חמים – לדעת המחבר מדובר רק באמבטיה שהיא כלי ראשון שאינו על האש, כגון אמבטיה ממתכת שהיה תחתיה אש או גחלים שכובו, והם עדיין חמים.

Hot water: According to the Mechaber, this refers only to a bath that is a kli rishon not on the fire, such as a metal bath that had fire or coals beneath it that were extinguished, while it remains hot.

    

    

      
(עג)

      
כלי שני – הרמ״א חולק על המחבר, ולדעתו יש חומרה מיוחדת באמבטיה, ואסרו לתת בה מים אפילו שהיא כלי שני. שהואיל והמים מיועדים לרחיצה הם חמים מאוד, וכן מפני שיש באמבטיה הרבה מים, ולכן הם שומרים את חומם זמן רב והאיסור הוא מדרבנן.

A kli sheini: The Rema disagrees with the Mechaber. In his view, a bath has a special stringency, and the Sages forbade putting water into it even when it is a kli sheini. Since the water is intended for washing, it is very hot, and because a bath contains a large amount of water, it preserves its heat for a long time; the prohibition is rabbinic.

    

    

      
(עד)

      
שהרי מחממן הרבה – כאמור, לדעת הרמ״א זו חומרה מיוחדת באמבטיה, שמבשלת מים גם בכלי שני. ולמחבר אין דין מיוחד לאמבטיה, אלא כבכל כלי ראשון.

For he heats them greatly: As stated, according to the Rema this is a special stringency regarding a bath, which cooks water even in a kli sheini. According to the Mechaber, a bath has no special status, but is treated like any kli rishon.

    

    

      
(עה)

      
של צונן – אף על פי שמחמם בכך את האמבטיה השנייה. לדעת הרמ״א הדבר מותר רק בעירוי מכלי שני, ולשיטתו לא החמירו אלא בנתינת מים לתוך הכלי השני. ולדעת המחבר הדבר מותר אפילו באמבטיה שהיא כלי ראשון, כיוון שעירוי מכלי ראשון אינו מבשל (מלבד במליח הישן, בקולייס האיספנים ובתבלינים). הלכה למעשה, כבר ראינו שהפוסקים אוסרים עירוי מכלי ראשון, ומסבירים כאן שההיתר הוא דווקא באמבטיה, מפני שיש בה מים מרובים.

Of cold water: Even though this warms the second bath. According to the Rema, this is permitted only when pouring from a kli sheini, because in his view the stringency applies only to placing water into the kli sheini. According to the Mechaber, it is permitted even when the bath is a kli rishon, because pouring from a kli rishon does not cook, except for old salted fish, Spanish mackerel, and spices. In practice, we already saw that the poskim forbid pouring from a kli rishon, and they explain here that the permission applies specifically to a bath because it contains a large amount of water.
